## The two passages the record already has

1. **To the seed.** Nothing admits no description, so the cancellation cannot complete (PF1: κ ≠ 2). The least
   description that remains is forced: two records, two moves, the rule a → ab, b → a (GENESIS §1–§2).
2. **To the weave.** The least description is not unique. Many threads are allowed, and no reason picks one. So none is
   picked; all are taken (`docs/THE_WEAVE.md`, the owner's rule).

Both passages run on one engine: an impossibility, then the refusal to add what no reason supplies.

## The passage to the field, in five steps

1. **What every thread shares is forced.** Exactly two points of the fibre's characters are fixed by every move. One
   is the trivial character, where the cancellation completes (κ = 2), and PF1 excludes it. The other is the common point a ↦ i,
   b ↦ j (W2). Every odd thread receives the same 2T there (W3). Shared by all, it is law: the symmetry.
2. **The shared point is the only unitary point on κ = −2.**
   - On unitary (SU(2)) points κ runs over [−2, 2]. κ = 2 is where the cancellation completes; κ = −2 is the farthest
     from that a unitary point can be.
   - Only the common point reaches −2 (B1235; the SM seat's W28 I5; checked again here). It is also the node of the
     surface κ = −2 (W24).
   - The cusp, κ = −2 with a parabolic puncture, is the condition every thread meets. It is a premise of the weave (W2:
     "with the puncture parabolic"; GM4 lists the puncture itself as CONDITIONAL), not a consequence of PF1.
   - The common point also has κ = −2, but its puncture is central (−𝟙). No unitary point has a parabolic puncture.
   - **A reading:** every thread carries the value of κ that is most frustrated for a unitary point. Only the shared
     point carries that value while staying unitary.
3. **Every thread has moved.** Each thread's own geometry is another point of the same surface: a genuine cusp, whose
   puncture is parabolic rather than central. This difference is not optional. If the threads did not differ from the
   common point there would be no threads, only the point. The weave would collapse back into one thing that nobody
   chose.
4. **Moving while keeping the cusp cannot be done unitarily.** The common point is the only unitary point on κ = −2
   (step 2), and no unitary point has a parabolic puncture. So a departure that keeps the cusp must leave the unitary
   locus. On a surface with a complex structure,
   that part has a name. Under the non-abelian Hodge correspondence a flat connection is a unitary connection plus
   Φ + Φ*, and the non-unitary part is carried by Hitchin's Higgs field Φ. Φ is the adjoint scalar that a gauge field
   leaves behind when dimensions are reduced, the kind of field gauge–Higgs unification calls the Higgs.
5. **So, given the cusp, a field cannot not exist.** The threads cannot coincide with the common point (step 3), and
   they keep the cusp (step 2). What carries the difference is a Higgs field in Hitchin's sense. Plurality forces it to
   exist, and the cusp shapes it. Whether it is the physical Higgs is the hypothesis H-RES (relay §3). The mathematics
   does not settle that.

**In one line:** the impossibility of nothing forces the seed; the plurality of the threads forces a departure; the
cusp, which every thread keeps, makes the departure a Higgs field.

## What the cusp does to the field (given H-RES)

- **On the cone.** With no weight on the flat side, keeping the cusp means the field's value at the puncture is
  nilpotent (O3). In the record's coordinates that is the isotropic cone a·a = 0 (O2). On the cone the coupling has rank
  one: one heavy generation and two massless (O4).
- **Read this way,** the cone that B1645 found every hierarchy needs, and that B1646 found nothing on record drives the
  Higgs to, would be the cusp's cone. That holds only given H-RES and the absence of a weight. A weight at the puncture
  breaks it (O3).
- **The zero modes.** The record's zero modes already carry such a field. Their own bilinear is a Higgs field that is
  nilpotent at every point of the fibre, and its residue at the puncture is R, the triplet that makes the first
  generation massless (O6). That part follows from B1642. What is new is the uniqueness: up to scale and the parity
  signs, no other Higgs at the puncture is nilpotent everywhere.

## What is not fixed: the field's state

- **Where on the cone.** A null direction is a point of the curve X(4), which R(τ) also traces (O7; the SM seat's
  W67).
- **How large.** The size of the Higgs, which is the scale, stays outside, as B151 and P010 found.
- **How far off the cone.** The puncture's departure from parabolic, or a weight at the puncture (O5). That is where the
  second generation's mass would live. The record's P012 already says that closing is where symmetry breaks.

Forced to exist (given the cusp), shaped by the cusp, free in state: that is what a field is.

## Against the record's earlier stance

S037 called *the object* more primitive. It placed Φ outside, as the "filling" that carries the scale. P010 placed Φ
on the Hodge side, with an external scale (B151, B167). This note keeps the scale outside. Two things have changed:

- **The setting.** The weave now supplies a shared point and many threads. Read through non-abelian Hodge, at a chosen
  τ and with no weight on the flat side, the departure between them is Φ. P010 spoke about one object, where "a selected
  point" was absent.
- **The space.** The space of B1644's Higgs at the puncture is the space of Φ's values there (O1). Whether the physical
  Higgs is such a Φ is H-RES, which no arc has tested.

The owner's words of 2026-10-13 have an echo here: "a field of maximality compared to the seed of minimality". The
shared point is the minimal one, the single unitary point on κ = −2. The departures range over everything the
threads are.

the puncture's parity already constrains it.
