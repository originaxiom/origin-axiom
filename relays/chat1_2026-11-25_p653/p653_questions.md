# P-653 (h): the sealed question set for a fresh reader of GENESIS and the README

Written and committed before the reader runs. The answer key is kept off the branch until the reader has answered; its
SHA-256 is e7731618fa3fb8372ead2109c997ddc0698dae4e5e5a7ef8643d48cc36da3bba, and it is committed after the run.

**The reader.** One cold reader: a fresh session that has seen nothing of the programme. It is given only two files,
GENESIS.md (v1.69, last changed at 57256bc23) and README.md (last changed at 711e936ad), both from main at 88a1e9474,
and this question set without the key. It reads them as this seat's mask shows them, so numbers on lines the mask
marks are replaced by #; the questions are chosen so that none turns on a masked number. It may not open any other
file, the repository, or the web.

**Grading, fixed here.**
- Each question is worth 1 mark. A "must contain" point of the key earns its share of the mark when present in
  substance; part earns ½; wrong or missing earns 0. An answer that asserts something GENESIS or the README
  contradicts loses that question's mark.
- The load-bearing questions are Q2, Q3, Q5, Q6, Q10 and Q16: whether a reader comes away knowing what is assumed,
  what is chosen, and what is not claimed.
- **GENESIS passes this test** if the reader scores at least 12 of 16 and full marks on every load-bearing question.
  It **fails** on any load-bearing question below full marks, whatever the total. Between, it is **PARTIAL**.
- Expectation, this seat's: PASS (60 %), PARTIAL (30 %), FAIL (10 %).

**What a failure means.** A load-bearing question missed by a reader with only these two files is a place where the
foundation document does not say clearly what it assumes or does not claim. The finding is about the document, not the
reader. One reader is one sample.

## The questions

Answer each in a few sentences, citing GENESIS's sections or line numbers, or the README's, where you can.

1. What is the programme's principle, and what is its status: derived, or assumed?
2. Which object is the root of the programme, by what criterion is it selected, and what is the status of that
   criterion and of the further choice between the two objects the criterion admits?
3. How many of the Standard Model's parameters does the record claim to have derived?
4. What is a "state" of the generated state space, and how many states are there up to word length 12, realising how
   many distinct manifolds?
5. What is a "frame", and is any frame derived from the foundations?
6. Which came first in the programme's history: the root object or the axioms that select it? What does GENESIS
   conclude from that?
7. Name the six gaps that GENESIS lists between the foundations and physics.
8. According to GENESIS, what can frames built on flat bundles reach, and what can they not reach?
9. How, according to GENESIS, may a frame's dictionary from geometry to particles be earned, and what grade does a
   value matched that way receive?
10. What is the claim PF5, and what is its status?
11. What is the difference between a "thread" result and a "weave" result?
12. Which statuses can an item of GENESIS have, and who may set a status?
13. According to the README, how is measured data compared with the record?
14. According to the README, has any review from outside the project taken place?
15. What is SE3, and what is its status?
16. Does GENESIS claim that some arrow of the state space is physical time, that all states are physically realised,
    or that a rule selects which state is physical?
