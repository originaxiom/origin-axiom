## 1. The card (sealed at 44c44a63e)

**The hand work, disclosed in the header before the seal** (H1–H8):
- **No atom (a theorem).** A flow-invariant probability measure gives no point positive mass.
- **Absolute continuity (cited, with a gap).** The lifted measure has dimension 1 + 2(0.8747) = 2.749 > 2, so its
  projection to the surface is absolutely continuous to area. That is Ledrappier–Lindenstrauss (2003), which is stated
  for compact surfaces. Its extension to the modular orbifold, non-compact and with cone points at i and ω, is assumed
  here; it is neither proved nor cited.
- **The density at i.** The geodesics crossing the edge (0, ∞) carry the section measure ν ⊗ ν on (ξ, ζ): the forward
  endpoint ξ and minus the backward one, ζ. ν is Minkowski's measure on (0, ∞).
  - The distance from i to such a geodesic is exact: sinh d = ∣ξζ − 1∣/(ξ + ζ). So it passes through i exactly when
    ζ = 1/ξ.
  - ν is invariant under x ↦ 1/x (L ↔ R). So the mass of histories passing within δ of i is ν's correlation integral,
    which goes as δ^(D₂). Here D₂ ≤ 0.8747 is ν's correlation dimension.
  - The τ-law's mass in a ball of radius ε about i then goes as ε^(1 + D₂), against the area's ε². So the density
    relative to area diverges at i like ε^(D₂ − 1).
- **The rest of the hand work.**
  - By hand: no point's density rises faster than at i (Cauchy–Schwarz), and Jensen's lower bound holds at each
    point.
  - Theorems: the level-5 walk and the line classes equidistribute.
  - On record: runs (B1629), and under area nothing concentrates.

**The instrument.**
- **Samples:** 4·10⁷ section samples. Each sample is one move: its segment inside the Farey triangle (0, 1, ∞), which
  covers the modular surface three times.
- **Points:** nine, fixed before any run. All nine are CM points.
  - Five of small degree: i; e^(iπ/3), in ω's orbit; i√2; i√3; (1 + i√7)/2. Their degrees are 1, 3, 2, 3 and 7.
  - Four of very large degree: Q1 = 0.42 + 1.23i, Q2 = 0.08 + 1.32i, Q3 = 0.12 + 1.86i and Q4 = 1.34i. They were chosen
    far from every CM point of discriminant ∣D∣ ≤ 50. Their degrees run from 6.8·10⁵ to 1.5·10⁸.
  - The degree is the determinant of the primitive integer matrix of the point's half-turn, the involution of ℍ fixing
    it.
  - **A slip in the sealed header, found by the referee.** H5 treats the Q's half-turns as irrational. The Q's have
    rational coordinates, so their half-turns are rational, of large degree. The sealed rules C4 and C5(b) do not use
    that rationale, and both hold.
- **The readout at each point:** the density ratio f_ε, the τ-law's mass in the ball of radius ε divided by the area's,
  for ε = 2^(−2) … 2^(−12). The slope of log f against log ε is a weighted least-squares fit over ε = 2^(−5) … 2^(−11).
  Its error is a jackknife over chunks and is statistical only. A point is called **spiked** here by C3's
  read-out rule: over the fit range its slope is below −0.03, and below zero by two standard errors.
- **Controls and side computations:**
  - a smooth-law control (a non-invariant current with a smooth density), 10⁷ samples;
  - D₂ from the Stern–Brocot pressure, an independent route;
  - Jensen's exponent at each point, at 60 digits;

## 3. What it means (as the card sealed it, and where it went beyond)

- **C2(b) and C4 hold.**
  - Under the move count the τ-law's density relative to area is unbounded at i: H3 says so, and the measured slope
    matches its exponent, D₂ − 1 = −0.155, set by the correlation dimension of Minkowski's measure. Of the points
    tested, i has the steepest rise.
  - The four points tested away from small CM points are flat over the tested range.
  - No mass concentrates. The fraction of a history's time within ε of i still falls to zero, as ε^(1.845).
- **The mechanism is the half-turn at i.** A history passes near i when ζ is near 1/ξ: its past, read backward, nearly
  equals its future with L and R exchanged. Any i.i.d. letters make the two halves independent. Fair letters make them
  copies of one law, since ν is invariant under x ↦ 1/x. So such pairs occur more often than area allows, by the factor
  ε^(D₂ − 1).
  - The principle's tick passes through i (B1630). The card chose i for the half-turn, not for the tick. Both single
    out i because it is the fixed point of S: the tick's axis has endpoints that S exchanges.
- **Not every CM point is spiked.** Of the nine tested, two are spiked (i and i√2) and seven are not. Nine points can
  neither show nor exclude a dense set of spikes.
- **Under area nothing is singled out** (H8). The referee ran an area control at five points (i, i√2, (1 + i√7)/2, Q1
  and e^(iπ/3)); f = 1 there within its errors.
- **Nothing τ-side concentrates in mass:**
  - there is no atom (C1);
  - Re τ is within 5% of area where it was read;
  - the level-5 position is uniform in the limit and asymptotically independent of τ, and the line classes
    equidistribute;
  - no run length is singled out (B1629).
