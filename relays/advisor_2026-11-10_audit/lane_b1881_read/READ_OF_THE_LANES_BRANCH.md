### 3.1 One angle, and a pair of mirror images

What the run gives that symmetry did not: **one real number,** the angle of the peak on the circle ∣z∣² = 1/5, where
the findings' own argument makes the circle a ridge. The peak is at 0.2515 + 0.3698 i.

**It comes as a pair of mirror images.** The lowest state is odd under the mirror, so its density is even: the two
peaks are exchanged by the mirror and are equally the maximum. The arc's second test shows the same from the other
side: the two points carry opposite signs of J, to every digit printed.

**By main's criterion that is the kind of outcome that delivers the owner's condition.** Main's B1881 § 6 landed while
this page was being written, and main asks this seat to read it. It sorts an outcome of the whole into two kinds: one
point that the mirror fixes, where there is no hand; and a pair that the mirror exchanges, where "the two members are
the two hands; naming one is a convention". "Only even ticks are observed" is delivered exactly by the second. The
lane's peak is inside the arc and not at one of its ends. **So it is of the second kind.**

**A first draft of this page said the opposite, and is withdrawn.** It read "it returns both hands, so the answer to
main's question is no". That confused not choosing between the hands, which the record has held to be a convention
since 2026-10-08, with not breaking the mirror.

**One clause the criterion needs, which neither main's section nor the lane's arc states.** The criterion speaks of
"an observer at" one member. The lane's state is one state of definite parity under the mirror, and it sits at both
peaks equally. What puts a world at one of them is a further step. The record allows it ("spontaneous breaking is not
a hand-pick", P-368) and nothing yet derives it. So the outcome is of the right kind, and that an observer is at one
member is still to be said. This is the seat's read of § 6, as main asked: the section is sound as a criterion on
outcomes, and this is the clause it is short of.

### 3.2 The same closed form at other levels: one half is the level restated, the other is not

The findings point to a "striking connection back to the golden ground": the lowest state's spectral parameter is
π / (2 ln φ). The same paragraph gives the reason: the character that makes a function odd under the twins at level 5
"cuts out" the field of √5, whose unit is φ. The acceptance page's seventh condition asks what the same recipe gives
at another level. The arc did not run that. With the state in the seat's own code it could be run here
(`the_same_closed_form_at_other_levels.py`, committed before its run).

**The unit: the level restated.** At each of the levels 13, 17 and 29 the same recipe gives a state of the same kind
(odd under the mirror and under that level's exchange, to 10⁻¹²), with π / (2 ln unit) for that level's own unit:
1.3147, 0.7499, 0.9536. So φ enters with the level and with nothing else. Level 5 was named from the golden ground in
the first place (5 = trace + 2).

**The kind of place: not the same at every level.** This part is post hoc and labelled so. The control looked for each
peak on an arc and, at 13 and 17, found only the arc's end: its "HOLDS" for the peak there is not established, and the
fault is the seat's test. A second search over a region then gave:

| level | where the density is greatest | what kind of point |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.2515 + 0.3698 i, on the circle | **no symmetry fixes it** |
| 13 | (7 + i√3)/26, a point of order 3, which reduces to ω | **fixed by a symmetry** |
| 17 | (4 + i)/17, a point of order 2, which reduces to i | **fixed by a symmetry** |
| 29 | a local maximum at 0.2814 + 0.0689 i, off the circle (the region searched does not hold the whole domain here) | no symmetry fixes it |

**The reason is exact arithmetic.** A state of this kind must vanish at a point of order 2 when the character is −1 on
that point's stabiliser. That is so at levels 5, 13 and 29, and not at 17. Level 13 has points of order 3, where the
state need not vanish; levels 5 and 29 have none. So at 13 and 17 the peak sits where a symmetry puts a stationary
point, at the two values of the modulus the record already holds are "not a world". At level 5 every such point is a
forced zero, and the peak has to be somewhere symmetry does not name.

**What this does and does not show.**

- **In the arc's favour:** the place at level 5 is not what the recipe returns at every level. That is more than the
  arc claimed for itself, and it is the first thing on record that the level 5 does here which 13 and 17 do not.
- **It is not unique to 5.** The arithmetic is the same at every level of this family that is 5 modulo 24: 29, 53,
  101 and on. Level 5 is the smallest.
- **It is still on one clock, for one state** (R6), and nothing yet puts an observer at one member of the pair (§ 3.1).
- **It is the seat's observation, made after the fact,** on four levels, with a search that at level 29 is incomplete.
  It should be sealed and run again by someone else before it is leaned on.

**How often an unselected point clears it.** The arc does not say. It was measured here with the lane's own function,

| 200 points spread evenly in hyperbolic area over the lane's domain, below height 3 | **39 of 200: 19.5%** | a quarter or more: **failed** |

**The bar is a threshold on one number.** In both samples a point clears it when its reduced height is above about
1.66 and not below: on the circle the lowest that clears is at 1.647 and the highest that does not at 1.661; in the
domain 1.665 and 1.668; above 1.9, 18 of 18. The lane's point is at 1.85. On the circle that is every angle above
about 48 degrees, two thirds of the arc. And where a point clears the bar it clears it, on average, with 4.6 of the 96
configurations; the lane's point does with 2.

**So H4's PASS says that the peak's reduced height is above 1.66,** and no more. The arc's prior of 80% for a fail was
a prior about that height. It is not evidence that the read-out point is special: one unselected point in five of the
domain clears the same bar, and two in three of the circle the peak lies on. By the reading the seat fixed first, a
quarter or more means that clearing the bar says little about the point; the circle is well above that and the domain
a little below it.

**What passed, by the arc's own caveats:** 2 of 96, both under the reading (S, S, S), which is not one of the two
readings of the menu behind the six's sealed cells; 0 of 96 under the reading the six's best row has; and with the
