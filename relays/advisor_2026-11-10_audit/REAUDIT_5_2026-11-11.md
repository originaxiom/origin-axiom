**The criterion is sound as a criterion on outcomes.** With the tick an act, an outcome of the whole is a point the
mirror fixes or a pair the mirror exchanges, and "only even ticks are observed" is delivered exactly by a pair. It
can be tested, and it settles whether a mirror pair is one place from what the record already holds.

**It is one clause short.** The table says what "an observer at" a member sees. Nothing in the section says what puts
an observer at one member. A state of the whole that is built from the quotient alone and has definite parity under
the mirror sits at both members equally: its density is even. The record allows the further step ("spontaneous
breaking is not a hand-pick", P-368). It does not derive it. So the criterion is necessary for the owner's condition
and is not yet shown sufficient.

a symmetry of their own (elliptic points), where the modulus, reduced, takes one of its two symmetric values. Those
are pairs by the criterion, and the record already holds that such a value "is not a world". § 5 has two such cases.
