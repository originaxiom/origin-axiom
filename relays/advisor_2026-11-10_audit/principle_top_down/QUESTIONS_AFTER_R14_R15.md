# The questions that remain, after R14 and R15

**From:** the advisor seat. **To:** the owner. **Kind:** `q:` — nothing here is ruled.
**Settled before these:** R14, the differences between distinctions are kept accounted for; R15, what exists is what
survives. Of attack A's eight questions, Q4 is answered and Q5 is withdrawn. The other six come down to the four
below, plus one piece of work that is not a question.

## 1. The account: one conserved quantity, or the whole ledger?

- **One quantity.** The difference between "a then b" and "b then a" is the commutator, and its trace κ is unchanged
  by every move of the grammar (checked by hand for one move: with x, y, z the traces of a, b, ab, the move a ↦ ab
  sends (x, y, z) to (z, y, zy − x) and x² + y² + z² − xyz − 2 is unchanged). The record's atlas calls κ "the one
  conserved first integral". Under this reading "kept accounted for" is a theorem the record already has.
- **The whole ledger.** Every difference between any two things that exist is kept — a record of all of them, with κ
  as its first line. Under this reading the object to compute is the ledger itself, and it is the weave: the joint
  data of all the threads.

*The seat's advice:* the whole ledger. The owner's words are plural ("theyre kept"), and W3 asks for the interaction
of every object.

## 2. Cancelling: a thing against its opposite, or the two sides against each other?

- **(a) A thing meets its exact opposite and both vanish** (an act, then its undoing). Then each cancellation is one
  stroke, a single distinction alone survives, and what cannot be exhausted is that there is always more.
- **(b) The two sides are set against each other and the smaller is taken from the larger, again and again.** Then one
  side alone is exhausted at once, only a pair that never comes out even survives, and what cannot be exhausted is
  the subtraction itself.
- **(c) Both, at two levels:** (a) for the pair of distinctions — that something remains at all — and (b) for each
  history — what remains in it.

*On record:* the principle's own form (κ: the two orders do not undo each other) is (a); the reason LR and the golden
ratio come first is (b), the subtraction that takes longest. *The seat's advice:* (c). It also answers the old Q3: a
single distinction is a side; alone it makes nothing.

## 3. Interaction: does it make a third thing, or is it a condition?

- **It makes a third thing,** which is itself kept and can interact again.
- **It is a condition inside one history:** both kinds of act occur in it.

*The seat's advice:* it makes a third thing — "should produce reality" is a making. With question 1's ledger, the
account is then closed under interaction: everything made by interaction is entered too.

## 4. The elementary act: two acts, or one tick?

- **Two separate acts:** add this to that; add that to this. The two sides never change roles. Then LR is the first
  thing that survives, and every history has a mirror history.
- **One tick:** add, and the two sides change roles (a ↦ ab, b ↦ a). Then the tick is first and LR is two ticks. At
  the root left and right are not told apart at all.

*On record:* the owner's own "a, ab, ba" rule is the tick (B1323, B1607); GM5c holds the exchange OPEN; SE2 takes the
two-act root and is graded "the most fragile link"; `GENESIS.md` says of the other choice "what dropping SE2 would
break is not computed". *Two things the principle already says:* the bare exchange, done twice, is nothing, so by
W1 it is not an act by itself; the tick is not excluded by that, because the exchange in it comes with an addition.

*The seat's advice:* do not rule yet. One computation is owed first — what the tick as the root gains and what it
breaks — and it is data-free.

## Not a question: the loop

After R14 and R15 the open construction reads: **the ledger must close.** What survives fixes the geometry, the
geometry fixes what is read from it, and what is read must be what lets exactly that survive. No reader closed it and
the record has no back-edge. It is work, not a ruling.
