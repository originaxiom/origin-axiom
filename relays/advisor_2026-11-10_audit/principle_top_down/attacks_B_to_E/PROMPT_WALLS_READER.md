# A reading task: eight premises against twenty-one statements

You are asked to act as a careful, sceptical mathematical reader. You need no tools and no files; everything is on
this page. Do not search for anything. Answer from the text alone.

## The premises

Eight clauses, to be taken as premises for this exercise. Do not judge whether they are true.

1. **What cannot be cancelled to nothing exists.** ("Existence is what remains when cancelling to nothing cannot
   complete.")
2. **By survival.** What exists is what survives. Nothing is picked by being least or first; an ordering may list
   what exists and makes nothing exist.
3. **Cancelling has two levels.** Between the pair of distinctions, a thing against its opposite: that something
   remains at all. Within each history, the two sides against each other: what remains in it. A single distinction is
   a side; alone it makes nothing.
4. **Everything allowed happens.** ("If something is allowed to happen it will happen.")
5. **Everything that survives is kept accounted for — the whole ledger.** Every difference between things that exist
   is kept, none dropped, none ranked.
6. **Interaction makes.** Two things in relationship make a third, which is itself kept and interacts again. Reality
   is read from the interaction of everything kept, never from one thing.
7. **The act is the tick.** Add, and the two sides change roles. The two distinctions are the two sides; they meet
   inside one tick. The bare exchange is not an act: done twice it is nothing. Histories of an even number of ticks
   and of an odd number are both kept.
8. **The ledger must close.** What survives fixes what is read, and what is read must be what lets exactly that
   survive.

## Words used in the statements

- **Two records:** two whole-number counts. **A move:** add one count to the other. **The exchange:** swap the two
  counts. **A history (a thread):** a repeating sequence of moves; each history is also a three-dimensional space
  built from it. **The weave:** the joint action of all the moves, on everything at once. **A subweave:** the joint
  action of some of them.
- **The object, the root:** one particular history, the simplest one that uses both moves once each. It is the tick
  done twice. It is its own mirror image.
- **The mirror:** exchanging the two counts turns a history into its mirror image. **A hand:** what tells a history
  from its mirror image. **Chern–Simons (CS):** a number attached to a space with a hand; it changes sign under the
  mirror.
- **The modulus (τ):** a point of the upper half-plane, the shape of a torus; in the record it is what would carry
  values. **The cusp, an end:** the place where the space goes off to infinity. **A measure, a weight, an atom:** how
  much each history counts; an atom is a single point carrying positive weight.
- **The nineteen:** the free numbers of the Standard Model of particle physics. **The value layer:** attempts to
  obtain such numbers from one chosen history.

## The task

For each of the 21 statements give exactly one label, with respect to the eight premises:

- **FOLLOWS** — a short argument from named premises yields the statement, or yields its content (for instance that
  nothing is selected, or that there is no hand). Name the premises and give the argument in one or two sentences.
  Be strict: an association of words is not an argument.
- **INDEPENDENT** — the premises neither yield the statement nor speak against it.
- **OPPOSES** — the statement contradicts what a named premise asserts or requires. Name the premise and say how.

A statement may FOLLOW from some premises and OPPOSE another. If so, give both, each with its premise.

Then answer two questions in a sentence each:

- **A.** Is there any statement that opposes premise 1?
- **B.** Premise 8 can be read in two ways: (i) the whole ledger is consistent with itself; (ii) the closing singles
  out one value. Which statements, if any, oppose premise 8 under reading (ii)? Under reading (i)?

## The form of your answer

A table with 21 rows: the number, the label or labels, the premises named, and the argument in at most two sentences.
Then the two answers. Nothing else.
