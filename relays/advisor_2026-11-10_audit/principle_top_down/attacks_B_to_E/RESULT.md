## 5. Attack D — the draft applied to what it produces

### 5.1 Every history as a ground (the record's rule: level = trace + 2; not detected when the trace is ±2 at that level)

- **Never mutual.** Two histories of different trace never fail to detect each other (H4). Of 3,306 ordered pairs of
  different traces to 60, 208 go one way and none goes both.
- **Four roots.** Traces 3, 4, 5 and 6 are departures of nothing: L·R; LLR and LRR; LLLR and LRRR; LLLLR, LLRR and
  LRRRR (H6).
- **Not an order.** 3 → 7 → 11, and 3 detects 11 (H7). From the golden ground, chains reach 52 of the 58 traces.
- **One history is alone at its trace: L·R** (H5). For every trace from 4 up, the words L^(t−2)R and LR^(t−2) are
  two. Counting a history with its mirror once, traces 3, 4, 5, 9 and 21 have one.

**These conclusions hold a fortiori for the record's stricter premises.** The trace rule says some rotation or
conjugate of the history lies in the ground's allowed group. The charge and line premises ask more, so they give
fewer arrows: at least as many roots, and still no mutual pair.

### 5.2 The same by primes

With "not detected" read as "has every prime of the ground's discriminant", the relation is a consistent order. It
has 17 minimal traces to 60 and so no single root either. Only two traces have a one-prime discriminant: 3 (the
prime 5) and 6 (the prime 2), with none further to trace 200 000.

### 5.3 The act as the ground

Two of the four atoms, L and R, have a rational fixed direction: done alone, the subtraction of the two sides ends,
and by clause 1 nothing remains. The other two are one act seen from the two sides. **So exactly one history is made of a single atom: the tick.** Its discriminant is 5,
and its double is L·R. Under clause 7 the ground and its level are named by what they are, not by a rank.

### 5.4 What the attack shows

| if a ground is | then |
|---|---|
| any history that exists | no single root, no order, and the record's level 5 is the level of one ground among infinitely many |
| the act itself | one ground and one level, with no ranking; the record's frame is then the canonical one, and the root is no longer a choice |

The second is available only if the tick is an act, which the owner has not confirmed to main (main's P-771).
Neither is in the owner's words: in attack A no reader reached a ground or a level at all.

## 7. The tick's two debts

**The first: what stands in place of a spin structure on a history with no hand.**

- A Pin structure, of either kind. On a bundle of this sort both kinds exist, and each has as many members as
  H¹(bundle; ℤ/2) has elements. *(Existence is theory, not computed here: Wu's relation for three-manifolds, and the
  orientation class being the reduction of a whole-number class.)*
- That number is twice the number of fibre structures the history keeps, among the four quadratic forms the record
  already uses for spin structures. Checked on all 16 histories with an odd number of runs to 6 letters (H16).
- The tick alone keeps one form, the odd one, and has two Pin structures of each kind. The record holds "two Pin⁻
  structures" for it (B1175).
- **For the root the record already holds the answer, as a lane's result registered on main.** The SM seat's B1382,
  in B1453's harvest: "the spin bit is the parent's Pin type: m004's two spin structures are exactly the pullbacks of
  the Gieseking manifold's Pin+ and Pin- structures". B1175's sentence, "one of m004's spin structures does not
  extend", is the same fact seen from the Pin⁻ side: the two Pin⁻ structures pull back to one spin structure. The
  sweep flagged the two as possibly opposed. They are not. The seat has not computed the pullbacks itself.
- **So no new choice appears with the tick.** Which kind of Pin structure on the tick's manifold is the same choice
  as which spin structure on its double. Under clause 2 both are kept.
- **What this run adds** is the count for the other histories with no hand, and that it is read from the same four
  forms the record already uses.

**The second: the record's structures at odd levels, with the exchange.**

- At level 5 nothing changes up to scalars: 60 elements with or without the exchange. The matrix group doubles
  only by the centre {±1, ±2}. The reason is arithmetic: −1 is a square modulo 5, so the exchange is 2 times
  something already present.
- By the same argument it holds at any level where −1 is a square. Every level s² + 4 that an odd history calls its
  own is one (checked for odd s to 59), and 13 is computed. At 3, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 11 the group doubles even up to
  scalars.
- The charge and line premises define the same departures as before (H12). The least are still (6) alone, and (6)
  with (2,2,2).
- What does change: the tick multiplies the mark by 3, so the mark is the golden line and not one of its two twins
  (main's remark in B1872, here as a count: four vectors kept up to a scalar, none up to sign).
