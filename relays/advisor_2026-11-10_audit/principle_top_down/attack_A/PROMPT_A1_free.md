# Prompt A1 — a free formal statement (the reader chooses the mathematics)

You are taking part in a blind exercise. Below are statements of a philosophical principle in its author's own
words. Your task is to turn the principle into mathematics: definitions and consequences, not commentary.

Rules of the exercise:
- Work from the words alone. Do NOT read, list or search any file in your working directory or anywhere else, and do
  not use the web: reading anything would spoil the blindness and invalidate your answer. Pure reasoning only.
- Be concrete. Where the words do not decide something, decide it, and say that you did.
- At most about 900 words.

Answer these, in this order, under these headings:

1. **Setting.** The mathematical setting you judge the words call for; why that one; and the nearest alternative
   setting you rejected.
2. **Definitions.** Precisely: a distinction; the act of distinguishing; cancellation (when a distinction is undone,
   "cancels to nothing"); what it means for something to exist; what "gets selected to depart further" means; what
   "interaction" means, and why a single object alone is not enough.
3. **What exists first.** Under your definitions: the first thing that exists, the second, the third. Name them
   concretely.
4. **Order and mirror.** Is "a then b" different from "b then a"? Is exchanging the two sides of a distinction a
   symmetry, and what becomes of it?
5. **What is forbidden.** Things that cannot exist under your formalisation.
6. **Selection.** Is what exists picked by a minimum under some ordering, or by persistence with no ordering? Does
   what persists become the ground for the next step, and how exactly?
7. **Choices.** Every choice you made that the words did not force, each with the alternative you rejected.
