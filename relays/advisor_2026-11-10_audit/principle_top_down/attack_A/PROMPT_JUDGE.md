# Prompt J — the judge (blind to how each statement was produced)

You are the judge in a blind exercise. Below are (i) statements of a philosophical principle in its author's own
words and (ii) several independent attempts, labelled F1, F2, …, to turn that principle into mathematics. You do not
know who wrote which or under what brief. Do NOT read, list or search any other file and do not use the web.

Do three things.

**1. Tabulate.** For each attempt, its answer to each of these, in a few words:
C1 the setting · C2 what cancellation means (is there an absolute sense, a sense relative to a ground, or both) ·
C3 the first existent · C4 the second and third existents · C5 does order matter ("a then b" against "b then a") ·
C6 the exchange of the two sides: allowed? what becomes of the mirror · C7 selection: a minimum under an ordering,
or persistence with no ordering · C8 does what persists become the ground for the next step, and by what rule ·
C9 what interaction means · C10 what is forbidden · C11 the choices the attempt itself declared.

**2. Rate faithfulness.** For each attempt, a score from 1 (strains the words badly) to 5 (a fair reader would
accept it as what the words say), with one or two sentences of reasons that quote the words. Judge faithfulness to
the words only — not elegance, and not whether the mathematics is interesting.

**3. Find the choice points.** List every item C1–C9 at which two attempts that you rated 4 or 5 give different
answers. For each, state the two answers and the exact phrase of the author's words that fails to decide between
them. These are the places where the words underdetermine the mathematics.

At most about 1200 words. Write your complete answer to the file {OUT} and also return the same text.
