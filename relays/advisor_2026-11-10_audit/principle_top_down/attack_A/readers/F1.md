# Formalising the principle

## 1. Setting

**The free group F = F(x, y) on two generators, its lower central series γ₁ = F, γₙ₊₁ = [γₙ, F], and the associated graded Lie ring gr F = ⊕ₙ γₙ/γₙ₊₁** (which is the free Lie ring L(x, y)).

Why: "cancelling to nothing" is free reduction (u s s̄ v → uv) taken literally. Order matters because the group is non-commutative. "Interaction" becomes the commutator, which is trivial for one object alone. "Cannot be exhausted" becomes the fact that L(x, y) is nonzero in every degree.

Rejected nearest alternative: Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form* (the primary arithmetic). There every finite expression evaluates to marked or unmarked, so cancellation always completes. The calculus is commutative and Boolean, so it leaves no inexhaustible remainder and no relational structure beyond truth values.

## 2. Definitions

- **Distinction:** a pair of sides {s, s̄} with an involution s ↦ s̄ and s ≠ s̄. Here these are the generators and their inverses: x, x⁻¹ and y, y⁻¹.
- **Act of distinguishing:** appending a letter. A history is a finite word in x^{±1}, y^{±1}.
- **Cancellation:** the elementary reduction s s̄ → (empty). A history *cancels to nothing* iff its free reduction is empty, i.e. it equals 1 in F. A side cancels only against its mirror, never against a repeat of itself.
- **Existence:** apply W2 to the whole of F: every cancellation that is allowed happens. At depth n, everything that differs only by reordering at higher depth cancels, which is the quotient γₙ → γₙ/γₙ₊₁. **What exists is gr F = ⊕ Lₙ**, and *a thing exists* iff it is a nonzero element of some Lₙ. Each w ≠ 1 has a finite depth d(w) = max{n : w ∈ γₙ} (because free groups are residually nilpotent). Its remainder is its leading term λ(w) ∈ L_{d(w)}, which is nonzero. Cancellation "cannot complete" because Lₙ ≠ 0 for every n (Witt: dim Lₙ = (1/n) Σ_{d|n} μ(d) 2^{n/d}). So the description has no last level.
- **Interaction:** the commutator [a, b] = a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹, which induces the Lie bracket on gr F. A single object alone is not enough: [a, a] = 1, so self-interaction cancels to nothing. With one generator, F = ℤ, every Lₙ with n ≥ 2 is zero, and the description is exhausted at level 1. Two distinctions are the minimum that keeps existence open.
- **"Selected to depart further":** Lₙ₊₁ = [Lₙ, L₁]. The *difference between distinctions*, i.e. their commutator, is bracketed again with the original distinctions. What gets selected is whatever survives antisymmetry and Jacobi, which is the nonzero part of the quotient.

## 3. What exists first

1. **x and y**: the two primitive distinctions in L₁ (rank 2), with four sides in total.
2. **c = [x, y]**: the unique interaction, since L₂ has rank 1. It is the difference between "x then y" and "y then x". It is the first relational existent, "reality" in the sense of W3.
3. **[c, x] and [c, y]**: L₃ has rank 2. They are the interaction departing further along each original distinction.

After that the ranks are 3, 6, 9, 18, 30, … In Lyndon names, L₄ is [[c,x],x], [[c,y],x], [[c,y],y].

## 4. Order and mirror

**Order:** xy ≠ yx in F. Their discrepancy, xy(yx)⁻¹ = [x, y] = c, is exactly what exists at level 2. So order is not a convention: it is the source of the second existent.

**Mirror:** exchanging the sides of x is the automorphism x ↦ x⁻¹. It is a symmetry of the laws, because it preserves reduction and γₙ. On existents it acts by the sign (−1)^{(x-degree)}. For example c ↦ −c mod γ₃.

Reversing order is also a mirror: [a, b]⁻¹ = [b, a], so in gr F we get [b, a] = −[a, b]. Exchanging x ↔ y acts on L₂ by det = −1.

So the mirror survives as a ℤ/2-grading (a parity, or chirality), but no individual existent at level ≥ 2 is mirror-invariant: c and its mirror image are distinct. The symmetry belongs to the law and is broken in each thing.

## 5. What is forbidden

- **Self-interaction:** [a, a] contributes nothing.
- **A one-distinction world with anything beyond level 1.**
- **A finished universe:** any truncation where Lₙ = 0 from some n on, i.e. a nilpotent world.
- **Cancellation by repetition:** torsion such as x² = 1 (*Laws of Form*'s "crossing twice") is impossible, because F and every Lₙ are torsion-free.
- **Infinitely deep existents:** an element lying in every γₙ and still ≠ 1 cannot occur, since ∩γₙ = 1.
- **Free-standing triple interactions:** [[x,y],z] + [[y,z],x] + [[z,x],y] = 0 (Jacobi).

## 6. Selection

**Selection is by persistence, with no ordering.** W2 says every allowed bracket happens. What vanishes under antisymmetry and Jacobi is cancelled, and Lₙ is what persists. No minimum is taken. An ordering x < y is needed only to *name* a basis (Lyndon or Hall words), not to choose which space survives.

**What persists becomes the ground of the next step:** Lₙ₊₁ = [Lₙ, L₁], so level n is the entire input to level n+1, apart from the fixed primitives.

**Closing the loop (W5):** define φ : F → F by x ↦ c, y ↦ [c, x]. The images do not commute, so they generate a free subgroup of rank 2 and φ is injective. φ(F) ⊆ γ₂, and φ(γₙ) ⊆ γ₂ₙ. The interactions produced at the end are therefore again a pair of independent distinctions, the same structure as the beginning. Each turn of the loop departs further, and ∩ φᵏ(F) = 1, so the loop closes without collapsing.

## 7. Choices (not forced by the words)

1. **Free group** rather than *Laws of Form* or a Boolean algebra (rejected because cancellation always completes there).
2. **Cancellation against the mirror (s s̄)** rather than against a repeat (s s).
3. **Exactly two generators.** Rejected: one generator, which is exhausted; n > 2 or countably many, which would be "chosen by hand", and F₂ already contains every Fₙ.
4. **Existence = nonzero in gr F.** Rejected: "nonempty reduced word" alone, which is exhaustible by appending the inverse; also infinite reduced words, i.e. boundary points of the Cayley tree.
5. **Interaction = group commutator.** Rejected: plain product (just juxtaposition) and symmetric pairings, which kill order.
6. **Convention [a, b] = a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹.**
7. **Departure = bracketing with the primitives** (lower central series). Rejected: the derived series [γ, γ], which skips levels and loses rank 2 at level 3.
8. **Selection = survival in a quotient.** Rejected: minimising length or "energy".
9. **Closure via the self-embedding φ.** Rejected: imposing a finite cycle such as γ_N ≅ γ₁ by quotient, which would introduce torsion or collapse. The specific φ is my choice; any injective map into γ₂ would serve.
10. **Integer coefficients** rather than rational ones, so that the torsion prohibition has content.
