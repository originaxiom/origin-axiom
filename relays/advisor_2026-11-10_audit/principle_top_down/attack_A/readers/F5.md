# F5 — ordering existence by what remains

**Outcome.** Same first existent (LR); different second step: **LLRR**, a run of *each* letter lengthened, not LLR. I found no fair reading that dislodges LR: bare letters and closed loops cancel, and the exchange only halves its period (§5, §6).

## 1. Definitions in the arena

- **Distinction.** One comparison of the two counts, "which is greater?", answered by a letter (L if a > b, R if b > a); a history records the answers.
- **Cancellation.** Subtract the smaller count from the larger (the inverse update the answer licenses); the difference is carried on and compared again. It completes when a count reaches 0. Inverses appear only here; counts stop at nothing, never below; exchange is not an update.
- **Existing.** A periodic history w (matrix M) exists when the ratio x it reproduces can never be cancelled out: both letters occur (tr M > 2). It then never returns a count pair v at its own ratio: Q(v) = det[v, Mv] ≠ 0. **What remains** is

  ρ(w) = min over v ≠ 0 of |Q(v)| / √(tr² − 4),

  the least distinction left once every allowed cancellation has happened (W2), scaled so that repeating w changes nothing. Equivalently ρ = lim inf q·|qx − p|: how far x stays in between all ratios of counts. More remaining means earlier in existence.
- **Interaction.** Composition of two existing histories, each entered anywhere in its cycle (a relative phase).

## 2. What exists first, second, third

| | history | matrix | ρ |
|---|---|---|---|
| first | LR | [[2,1],[1,1]] | 1/√5 = 0.4472 |
| second | LLRR | [[5,2],[2,1]] | 1/√8 = 0.3536 |
| third | LLRRLR and its mirror LLRLRR | [[12,7],[5,3]], [[13,5],[5,2]] | 5/√221 = 0.3363 |
| other reader's second | LLR (or LRR) | [[3,2],[1,1]] | 1/√12 = 0.2887 |

This is Markov's theorem (1879); I rechecked it by enumerating every primitive cyclic word up to length 12. Two further routes to the same second step (≅: the same cycle, started elsewhere):

- *Interaction.* The only new history the first existent makes with itself is LR·RL = [[3,4],[2,3]] = LRRL ≅ LLRR. Of all new products of two existents it is the shortest, of least trace and of most remainder.
- *Cancellation loop.* L R⁻¹ L⁻¹ R = RL exactly: the first existent is what is left when L and R try to cancel around each other; with either letter doubled, LLRR is left. (This needs inverses and a transient negative count; I do not rest on it.)

## 3. Order and mirror

- *Order inside a history is only a phase.* LR and RL are one periodic history started a step apart. Their matrix difference, LR − RL = diag(1, −1), is a reflection, not a history.
- *Mirror (L↔R).* My first two existents are their own mirror images. The other reader's second is not: LLR ↦ RRL is a different cycle with the same remainder, so a letter is chosen by hand or that step is already a pair.
- *Handedness is born at my third step, as a pair, by interaction.* LR·LLRR ≅ LLRRLR and LR·LRRL ≅ LLRLRR, whereas RL·LLRR ≅ LLLRRR leaves only 0.2774 and is not selected. Order becomes real only between existents.

## 4. Depart further

**Rule.** Relative to a ground G (what already exists), what exists next is the history outside G that leaves the most; equal remainders enter together.

From the empty ground: LR; LLRR; then, in mirror pairs, LLRRLR; LLRRLRLR; LLRRLLRRLR; LLRRLRLRLR; … with remainders 0.4472, 0.3536, 0.3363, 0.3338, 0.33342, 0.33340, falling towards 1/3.

- Every rung after the first composes earlier rungs (LLRR ≅ LR·RL; LLRRLR = LLRR·LR; LLRRLRLR = LLRRLR·LR). So from the second step the rule equals: of all new histories made by interaction within the ground, the one that leaves most.
- LLR, at 0.2887 < 1/3, lies below the whole ladder; and no interaction of existents makes it, since every way of splitting it leaves a bare run of one letter.

## 5. Faithfulness

Honoured better:

- **W1, "Existence is what remains".** I rank by the remainder itself. Shortest word, least trace, smallest edit — the rules I can see behind "one run lengthened" — use W1 only as a yes/no gate.
- **W3, "interaction … should produce reality, not single objects alone".** My second existent is the first interacting with itself, and each later one an interaction of earlier ones. Theirs is the first with a bare letter added, and a bare letter is a single object alone.
- **"none chosen by hand".** No letter is preferred (§3).

Strained:

- **"none emerging from another".** LLRR ≅ LR·RL does emerge from the first. The cycles that are no product of others are exactly LR and LR with one run lengthened: if this clause defines the objects, it describes their family, not mine.
- **"the difference between distinction gets selected to depart further".** I read it as Euclid's step: the difference a − b is what is carried on. If the phrase is about passing from one existent to the next — a difference between the two kinds of distinction, L and R, gets selected — their step fits it and mine does not. And my later existents hold *less* remainder, an odd sense of "further".
- Nothing in the words warrants my ladder's limit at 1/3, with infinitely many existents ahead of LLR.
- **W5.** Neither reading closes a loop beyond periodicity. A literally closed loop (with inverses, L R⁻¹ repeated six times undoes itself) is exactly what cancels to nothing, so W1 excludes it.

**Verdict.** The readings agree on the first existent, and mine adds an independent reason for it: LR is the unique maximum of what remains. On the second step I judge mine about as faithful as theirs, not clearly more. But as far as I can see, LLR needs three unforced choices all to fall its way: "next" means least changed rather than most remaining; a bare letter, not itself an existent, may be added to one; the elementary act excludes the exchange. Reverse any one of them and the second step is LLRR.

## 6. Choices the words did not force

1. "Next" means most remaining. Least changed (shortest word, least trace) puts LLR second; least discriminant of the equation for x (5, 8, 12) puts LLRR second.
2. Only existents interact; otherwise LLR = L·LR is an interaction too.
3. No exchange in the act (nor negative counts; inverses only as cancellation). With Euclid's own step, (a, b) ↦ (a + b, a), F = [[1,1],[1,0]], F² = LR, F is the first existent, and lengthening its one run gives [[2,1],[1,0]], whose square is LLRR: the same second step, by the other reader's own edit.
4. The normalisation √(tr² − 4) and the minimum over all pairs.
5. Ground = everything existing so far, not one history; ties enter together.
6. "First, second" as rank: by W2 every history with both letters exists; only the order is disputed.
