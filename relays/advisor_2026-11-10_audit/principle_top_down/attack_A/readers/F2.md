# F2: the principle formalised as free-group cancellation

## 1. Setting

**The free group F(S) on a set S of distinctions, with free reduction.** I chose this because W1 is about *cancelling to nothing*, and free reduction (deleting any adjacent pair xx⁻¹ or x⁻¹x) is the standard mathematical form of that. It also brings the other things the words ask for. Order matters, because the group is non-commutative. Each distinction has two sides, x and x⁻¹. Nothing is imposed by hand, because a free group has no relations. And "interaction" has a ready form in the commutator.

**Rejected alternative:** Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form* (the calculus of marks, with calling and crossing). It is the closest match for "the act of distinction", but its arithmetic is commutative. Every expression in it reduces to one of two values, so in that calculus cancellation always completes and nothing is left that "cannot be exhausted".

## 2. Definitions

- **Distinction:** a generator s ∈ S together with its formal opposite s⁻¹. These are the two sides: is/not, here/there, question/answer.
- **Act of distinguishing:** appending a letter s^±1 to a word, which is multiplication in F(S).
- **Cancellation:** one reduction step, w·x·x⁻¹·w′ → w·w′. A word *cancels to nothing* if reduction ends at the empty word e. Reduction is confluent (by the diamond lemma), so every word has a unique reduced form.
- **Existence:** w exists iff its reduced form is not e. *Inexhaustible* means w^n ≠ e for every n ≥ 1, so no finite repetition of the description ever uses it up. In a free group this holds automatically, because free groups are torsion-free. Existing and being inexhaustible therefore turn out to be the same thing, which is what W1 says.
- **Interaction:** the commutator [u,v] = uvu⁻¹v⁻¹. A single object is not enough because [a^m, a^n] = e for all m, n. One distinction together with its own powers always cancels completely. A non-zero interaction needs two objects that are not powers of a common element.
- **"Selected to depart further":** the lower central series G₁ = F and G_{k+1} = [G_k, F]. The "difference between distinctions" is the commutator. To *depart further* is to move one level deeper in this series. *Selected* means "persists": an element is selected at depth k if it lies in G_k and is not in G_{k+1}.
- **"If allowed, it happens" (W2):** every reduced word and every commutator that the definitions permit is present. No element is chosen and none is left out.

## 3. What exists first

I decided that the minimal world needed for interaction has two distinctions, S = {a, b}, giving F₂. Each layer L_k = G_k/G_{k+1} is free abelian, with rank given by Witt's formula M(k) = (1/k)·Σ_{d|k} μ(d)·2^{k/d}, which gives 2, 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18, 30, …

1. **First:** a. It is a single distinction and a non-trivial word, but on its own it does nothing, since it commutes with itself.
2. **Second:** b. It is a second, independent distinction, and it is forced because a alone produces no interaction (W3).
3. **Third:** [a,b] = aba⁻¹b⁻¹. This is the first interaction, the only basic element at depth 2, and the first thing that is neither distinction. After it, depth 3 contains [[a,b],a] and [[a,b],b].

## 4. Order and mirror

**Order:** ab ≠ ba. The amount by which they differ is exactly the interaction: ab = [a,b]·ba. So order is the source of everything beyond depth 1.

**Mirror:** exchanging the sides of one distinction, a ↦ a⁻¹, is an automorphism of F₂, so it is a symmetry of the whole world. On the layers, though, it does not leave each element in place:

- on L₁ ≅ ℤ² it acts as diag(−1, 1);
- on L₂ ≅ ℤ it acts as −1, since [a⁻¹,b] = a⁻¹·[a,b]⁻¹·a.

So the mirror maps the interaction to its opposite. Interaction has an orientation, and no non-trivial element of L₂ is fixed. The symmetry survives as an operation, but every element that exists at depth 2 has a handedness. The swap a ↔ b behaves the same way on L₂ ([b,a] = [a,b]⁻¹).

## 5. What is forbidden

- **Torsion:** a word w ≠ e with w^n = e. A thing that exists but is used up after finitely many repetitions would contradict W1.
- **Imposed relations:** any quotient F/⟨⟨r⟩⟩ chosen by hand. This would break W2 and W3.
- **The empty word as an existent:** e is complete cancellation, which is the opposite of existence.
- **Unreduced words as states:** a word containing xx⁻¹ is not yet a remainder.
- **Interaction of an object with itself:** this always gives [u,u] = e.
- **A purely commutative world:** abelianising would collapse every depth beyond 1 to nothing.

## 6. Selection

Selection is **by persistence, not by a minimum.** What exists is what survives reduction. Because reduction is confluent, the remainder is unique and does not depend on any ordering or on which cancellation is done first. Nothing is ranked.

What persists becomes the ground for the next step in a precise sense: G_{k+1} = [G_k, F]. The elements that survive at depth k are the inputs that are commuted with every distinction to produce depth k+1. So each layer is generated only by the one before it and the original distinctions.

**Closing the loop (W5):** free groups are *residually nilpotent*, meaning ∩_k G_k = {e}. Equivalently, F embeds in the inverse limit lim F/G_k. Every existent is detected at some finite depth, and the whole tower determines the original group. So the "end" (the full tower) reconstructs and constrains the "beginning" (F and its distinctions).

The Magnus map makes this concrete: a ↦ 1+X and b ↦ 1+Y, into non-commuting power series. Under it, [a,b] = 1 + (XY − YX) + higher terms. The leading term of the interaction at each depth is the Lie bracket, and the tower is the free Lie algebra. Each a⁻¹ = 1 − X + X² − … is an infinite series, which reads literally as a description that cannot be exhausted.

## 7. Choices

| # | Choice | Alternative rejected |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free group | Laws of Form; free monoid without inverses, in which there is no cancellation |
| 2 | Exactly two distinctions | One, which gives no interaction; infinitely many, which only adds more of the same pattern |
| 3 | Interaction = group commutator | Concatenation; symmetric product |
| 4 | "Inexhaustible" = torsion-free | Infinite reduced words on the boundary of the group |
| 5 | Departure = lower central series | Derived series [G,G], [G′,G′], …, which grows faster but skips mixed levels |
| 6 | Persistence = not in the next term of the series | Minimal word length, which is an ordering and is what W2 rules out |
| 7 | Mirror = inversion of one generator | Global inversion w ↦ w⁻¹, which is an anti-automorphism |
| 8 | Closure = residual nilpotence / inverse limit | Imposing a relation that loops back, which W3 forbids |
| 9 | Hall basis ordering, used only to name the basic commutators | The ranks M(k) do not depend on it |
