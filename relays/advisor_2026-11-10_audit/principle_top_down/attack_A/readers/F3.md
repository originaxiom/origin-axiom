# F3 — The principle formalised on (a, b) with L and R

## 1. Definitions in the arena

**State.** A pair of counts (a, b) with a, b ≥ 0, not both 0. **Nothing** is (0, 0), or a state with one count 0, which is the end of all cancelling.

**Distinction.** A single act, meaning one letter. L means "a absorbs b" and R means "b absorbs a". Each act decides which side is which. A history is a word w over {L, R}, and its matrix M_w is the product of the letters.

**Cancellation.** I find three senses in the words.
- **C1, subtractive (the main one).** Undo the distinctions: (a, b) → (a−b, b) if a > b, and (a, b) → (a, b−a) if b > a. Each step is forced, so cancelling reads off a unique word in L and R. It *completes* when a count reaches 0. Every integer state completes in finitely many steps (this is Euclid's algorithm). Applied to a direction, a real slope x = a/b, it completes iff x is rational. The word it produces is the continued fraction of x.
- **C2, algebraic.** A history cancels if its matrix is I. Without inverse letters, only the empty word does this.
- **C3, spectral.** A periodic history w^∞ cancels if repeating it adds nothing new: its eigenvalue is 1 (tr M_w = 2), so finite data describe it completely.

**Existence.** A periodic history w^∞ exists iff C1, run on its fixed direction (the eigenvector v with M_w v = λv), never completes. For a primitive word w, taken up to cyclic rotation, this holds iff w contains both L and R. Equivalently tr M_w ≥ 3 and λ > 1, and the fixed slope is a quadratic irrational whose continued fraction repeats w. "The remainder" is that infinite word. It "cannot be exhausted" because no finite prefix determines it, yet it closes on itself: after one period, cancelling arrives back at a multiple of its start (M_w v = λv). That fixed-point equation is the closed loop W5 asks for, where the end constrains the beginning. A finite history is a one-way line. It always cancels, so it never exists.

**Interaction.** An L meeting an R, that is, a junction LR or RL in the cyclic word. A single update alone is not enough: L^∞ has matrix L^n = [[1,n],[0,1]], trace 2 and eigenvalue 1. Its fixed direction (1, 0) is already "nothing", so it cancels in every sense (C1 immediately, C3 by definition). Interaction is exactly the condition for existence.

## 2. What exists first

The ordering criterion is the growth λ, equivalently the trace (λ = (t+√(t²−4))/2). Ties are broken by length, then alphabetically. Classes are taken up to rotation, since a periodic history has no starting point.

1. **LR**, M = [[2,1],[1,1]], trace 3, λ = φ². Its fixed slope is the golden ratio, with continued fraction [1;1,1,…].
2. **LLR**, M = [[3,2],[1,1]], trace 4.
3. **LRR**, M = [[3,1],[2,1]], trace 4. This is the mirror of 2, and the two are distinct existents because the exchange is refused (§3).

Next come LLLR and LRRR (trace 5), then LLRR, L⁴R and LR⁴ (trace 6). I enumerated all primitive words up to length 8 to check this.

## 3. Order and mirror

As finite histories, LR ≠ RL. Their matrices are [[2,1],[1,1]] and [[1,1],[1,2]], and they send (1, 1) to (3, 2) and (2, 3) respectively. Their difference is LR − RL = [[1,0],[0,−1]] ≠ 0, so the order of distinctions is real. As periodic histories they are the same existent, because RL = L⁻¹(LR)L is a cyclic rotation. For longer words, order matters even cyclically: LLRLRR and LLRRLR are different classes.

**The exchange** J = [[0,1],[1,0]] has det −1. **I refuse it as an act.** If it were allowed, then by W2 it would happen, and JJ = I would be a history that cancels to nothing. It would also make "here" and "there" conventional, which erases the very distinction W4 calls the act of existence. What follows from refusing it:
- J still acts as a symmetry of the set of existents (JLJ = R), so the set is closed under mirroring.
- By W2 both chiralities happen.
- Existents come in mirror pairs, except the achiral ones. LR and LLRR are achiral, because their mirror is a rotation of the word itself.

If the exchange were allowed, LLR and LRR would merge into one existent and there would be no chirality.

## 4. Depart further

**Ground:** G = LR, the golden direction. Its cancellation is the slowest possible: every step is a minimal partial quotient 1.

**Rule.** For an existent w, let Λ(w) be the largest value over shifts of [aᵢ; aᵢ₊₁,…] + [0; aᵢ₋₁, aᵢ₋₂,…], where the aᵢ are the run lengths of w^∞. This is the Lagrange value of its fixed slope: how far cancellation by rational approximation is held off. The **departure** of w from the ground is δ(w) = Λ(w) − Λ(G), with Λ(G) = √5. Next, select the unselected existent with the smallest δ > 0, taking a chiral pair together.

**Result** (computed for all words up to length 8):

| # | word | trace | Λ |
|---|------|-------|---|
| ground | LR | 3 | √5 ≈ 2.2361 |
| 1 | LLRR | 6 | √8 ≈ 2.8284 |
| 2 | LLRLRR / LLRRLR (chiral pair) | 15 | √221/5 ≈ 2.9732 |
| 3 | LLRLRLRR / LLRRLRLR (chiral pair) | 39 | √1517/13 ≈ 2.9961 |

These are Markov's discrete spectrum, with tr = 3m for the Markov numbers m = 1, 2, 5, 13. The rule is a **minimum under an ordering**, not persistence. The ordering it uses is not the ordering of §2. LLR (Λ = √12 ≈ 3.46) is overtaken by LLRR, which departs less from the ground even though it grows faster. The selected chain is also generated by interaction. Each new term is the product of an adjacent earlier pair (Cohn's tree, e.g. a = LR, b = LLRR, ab trace 15, a²b trace 39), with b = L·G·R, the ground framed by the two elementary distinctions. So "their interaction matters" holds literally. The departures accumulate at Λ = 3 and never reach it. Beyond 3 the selection stops being discrete, and the rule has no unique "next".

## 5. What is forbidden

- Every finite history, and every state (a, b), because C1 completes on all of them.
- L^∞, R^∞ and the empty history (trace 2 or identity, cancels under C3 or C2).
- Non-primitive repetitions w^k as separate existents (they are the same as w^∞).
- The exchange J and any history containing it.
- Inverse letters inside a history. They appear only as the moves of cancellation.
- Negative counts.
- Any existent with Λ < √5. None exists below the ground, which is Hurwitz's theorem.
- A unique "next" past Λ = 3.

## 6. Choices

1. **Counts are non-negative.** Alternative rejected: all of ℤ, i.e. SL(2, ℤ), where cancellation is not unique.
2. **Inverses are only cancellation moves, never history letters.** Alternative rejected: free group words, where w·w⁻¹ cancels trivially.
3. **The exchange is refused.** Alternative rejected: allowing it, which merges mirror pairs.
4. **C1 is the main sense of cancellation.** Alternative rejected: making C2 or C3 primary.
5. **Existence means periodic histories up to rotation.** Alternative rejected: infinite aperiodic words. Those also resist cancellation, but they do not close the loop.
6. **A distinction is a letter, and interaction is an L–R junction.** Alternative rejected: a distinction as a state with a ≠ b.
7. **§2 is ordered by trace, with ties broken by length and then alphabetically.** Alternative rejected: word length.
8. **"Depart further" means a minimum of the Lagrange departure.** Alternatives rejected: a minimum of trace, and Christoffel/Farey persistence with no ordering.
9. **Mirror pairs are selected together.** Alternative rejected: separating them by alphabetical order.
