## 1. The question and the instrument

**The question.** a711bd189, § 3 has the joint action require that the allowed set be closed under composition, and
left "the lightest state is single" as "not known to be closed". If W₁ and W₂ are single, is W₁W₂?

**The instrument,** in exact form (the card's header).
- A history is a closed positive word, written by its runs r₁ … r_n: n is even and the letters alternate, L first.
- The height of run i is H_i = (r_i + x_i + y_i)/2. Here x_i = [0; r_{i+1}, r_{i+2}, …] is read forward round the
  cycle and y_i = [0; r_{i−1}, r_{i−2}, …] backward. The apex is the greatest H_i.
- The history is **single** when the apex is attained by exactly one run in one period of the word as written.
- Equality of two heights is decided exactly: H_i = H_j when r_i = r_j and the forward and backward run sequences at
  i are those at j, in the same order or exchanged. Numbers are at 60 digits; a tie is a difference below 10⁻⁴⁵.
- This is B1857's criterion. In a long golden background the formula gives (6 + 2/φ)/2 = 3.618034 for (6), and
  B1857's other three apexes to six places (cell C0).
- **One height per run is enough for the apex.** A primitive class that is not a run's pivot is crossed by the
  history's geodesic on at most one edge, so its height is below 1. Every history's apex is at least √5/2 = 1.118.
- **An integer form** (the referee's; the seat checked it on five words): H_i = √(tr² − 4)/(2c_i), where c_i is the
  lower-left entry of the word's matrix started at run i, that run's letter taken as the translation. A tie is then
  an equality of integers.
- **A word written twice is read over its whole length.** Its bundle is the double cover. The two copies of the
  deepest run are not identified by the monodromy there, and in this model the wells of different classes do not
  couple.

**The family,** fixed before the run. Write D_k for a departure D closed by k golden periods: (6)_k, (2,1,2,1,2)_k.
- The six single histories: (6)_k and (2,1,2,1,2)_k for k = 1, 2, 3. As words they are L⁶R(LR)^k and (L²R)³(LR)^k.
  They are B1857's two departures with a unique apex and the design's examples.
- Their 36 ordered composites W₁W₂, the six squares among them.
- The four histories of a711bd189's fact F8: L⁶R(LR)^k and LR⁶(LR)^k for k = 1, 2.

## 3. Why: a symmetry of the word moves the deepest run

**Squares, and all powers (H1).** The bundle of Wⁿ is an n-fold cyclic cover of the bundle of W. Its deck move
carries each fibre one period on. The cyclic word of W² is unchanged by the rotation by half its length, which
exchanges the two copies of the departure (C4, the six squares).

**Two copies of a palindromic departure with different golden gaps (H2).**
- The cyclic word D A₁ D A₂ has exactly one reversal. It exchanges the two copies of D and fixes the centres of the
  two gaps. Both departures tested are palindromes; a single run is one trivially.
- Reading a word backward is conjugation of W⁻¹ by g₀ = diag(1, −1). So the reversal is a g in GL(2, ℤ) with
  det g = −1 and g W⁻¹ g⁻¹ = W: it reverses the fibre and the base, and keeps the 3-manifold's orientation.
- C4 has this in integers for all 12 such composites: one reversal each, det g = −1, the relation exact, the two
  departures exchanged.
- **It keeps the line.** Mod 5, (L²R)³ ≡ I, so every word of the family is a power of LR and keeps LR's line
  v = (1, 3). For each of the 12, g v ≡ ±v and vᵀ g ≡ ±vᵀ (C4; the two signs are opposite).
- B1733's census (e·det g = +1 fixes the pair) covers classes with exactly one order-5 pair, and agrees in kind. Four
  of the 12 are ≡ −I mod 5 and lie outside it, as do 10 of the 36 composites. C4 checks all 12 directly.

**So in this instrument the tie is forced:** a symmetry that moves a deepest run makes a second run of the same
height. § 4 tests whether, over the family, that is the only way a tie arises.

**The record's own two "mirror-degenerate" departures are instances.**
- (2,1,1,1,2) closed by k golden periods is the run sequence 2, 1³, 2, 1^(2k+1): two copies of the single run 2,
  with golden gaps of 3 and 2k + 1 runs. The single run 2 does not keep the line by itself (L²R(LR)^k is
  L(LR)^(k+1), and L moves v); only the pair does.
- For k = 1 it is LLRLR twice over, as B1867 § 2 has it, and ≡ −I mod 5. Its deck move acts by LLRLR, which does not
  keep v. The reversal that exchanges the two runs does (the referee's check: g v ≡ −v).
- (2,1⁸,2) is different: its two runs of 2 are L² and R². The referee checked it closed by 1, 2 and 3 golden periods:
  one symmetry, a reversal with the swap, with det g = +1 and g W⁻¹ g⁻¹ = W. It reverses the 3-manifold's orientation
  and exchanges the line with its twin (g v ≡ −2v). So that tie belongs to the scalar instrument; an operator twisted
  by one line is not symmetric under it.

## 5. On the hyperbolic metric: what the same symmetries give, and what they do not

The card computes nothing there. The following is by symmetry alone, and it is conditional: a lowest state must
exist, and the operator must be one the symmetry keeps.
- **A symmetry of the cyclic word is a homeomorphism of the bundle, so it is an isometry up to homotopy** in the
  hyperbolic metric of the punctured-torus bundle (Mostow, Prasad). This is the direction of P-766 that needs no
  test. In the flat-fibre metric it is an isometry by construction.
- **An operator built from the metric and the line commutes with it,** up to complex conjugation where the line's
  sign is reversed. So the density of each eigenspace is invariant under it, whatever the eigenspace's dimension.
- **The profile along the base is invariant under a rotation or a reflection of harmonic time,** since the metric
  fixes that time. B1846 § 3 has this for the run of 6's own reversal ("By uniqueness, θ∘σ = −θ + c").
- **Powers have no one place.** The deck move has no fixed level. The profile repeats n times, so every mode has
  n − 1 equals and none carries more than 1/n of the total. Wⁿ then fails the design's § 2.4 for n ≥ 2: for any
  stated share of one half or more, and for any share if "one mode" means exactly one. For an operator twisted by
  the line this needs W itself to keep the line; (2,1,1,1,2)₁ = (LLRLR)² is a square whose root does not.
- **Two copies of a palindromic departure:** the reversal exchanges the two tubes, since it exchanges the classes of
  their core curves. Whatever sits at one departure sits equally at the other. One place is possible only at one of
  the two levels the reflection fixes. In the word those are the centres of the two golden gaps; on the hyperbolic
  metric the seat has not located them against the tubes.
- **This is not B335's claim** (P-310). Nothing here says an invariant or an eigenvalue is degenerate. A deck move
  leaves the density of each eigenspace invariant; the eigenvalue may be simple.
- **Not shown:** that a lowest state exists on these cusped manifolds for the design's operators; where it sits
  (P-744, open); anything for two different departures, where no symmetry acts; for the Dirac operator, that the
  symmetry keeps the spin structure; for the twisted operator, that it keeps the flat bundle's holonomy along the
  base, which a reversal inverts.

This bears on the design's test A6, which takes histories "with at most two departures": given a state, two equal
palindromic departures have one place only at a fixed level, and a power of a line-keeping history has no one place.
