## 3. How it could come out wrong, written before any run

1. **A number of main's does not come out of the rows.** The page's tables, the verdict line's counts and shares,
   the counts of the split by words. Each is recomputed before anything is written. One that differs goes back to
   main with the output, and that item stops; it is not repaired here.
2. **The page's table of walls lists five walls and the rows have eight classes.** Whether the page's 511 is the sum
   of all eight, and what the other three hold, is read from the rows first. If the page and the rows disagree it
   goes back.
3. **The commit the readers read is not in a fresh clone's history** if main's history is ever cut, and is in this
   one. The lock must not pass silently without it: it skips with a sentence, and a second test fails if the kept
   table says the quotations were not run.
4. **Main's script writes its kept table at every run.** A lock that runs it changes a tracked file in the checkout,
   which is the fault this seat reported twice in R80b's sixth part. The lock calls the script's functions and
   writes nothing; whether the script is changed to write only on request is main's, and is asked.
5. **The gate can turn the record red.** Verdict lines that say "selects", "picks", "singles out" or "forced" stand
   on main today, by the hundred. What stands is measured before the gate is written. The gate holds what stands by
   name as found, with a count that can only fall, or reads only arcs from B1905 on: which of the two is decided
   by what the measurement shows, and said. Main's word asks for the gate; it does not say which.
6. **"With a value or a member as its object" is a reading of a sentence,** and a pattern will miss shapes and catch
   honest ones ("no rule selects", "is not forced"). The denial is read in the claim's own clause, as the gate on
   sentences of arrival does (series 5, patch 13). What it still passes and still refuses is held in its test.
7. **The ledger's "first met" and "proved impossible" are not columns of the rows.** The rows have a date, a wall,
   kinds, an outcome and whether the arc settled its question. "First met" is the row of the wall with the lowest
   arc number: arcs are numbered in the order they were opened, and many rows give no date (counted before the
   card was committed). "Proves a route impossible" is the builder's reading of the rows' fields (settled, and an outcome that
   does not pick, by main's own two lists of words) and is said to be so on the page. If main means another
   reading, it is main's to give.
8. **The rows hold long quotations from the record's pages.** The ledger's page prints arcs and counts, and no
   quotation: a quotation could carry a sentence that another gate refuses on a living page.
9. **A new gate changes the count of gates,** which stands in several pages and tests. Each is found by running the
   gates and the tests that name the count, not by a list.
10. **The base moves again.** Main's head moved three commits while the sixth part was being finished. The series is
    said to be built on a named commit, and what main lands in between is main's to say.
