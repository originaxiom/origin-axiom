## 4. How it could come out wrong, written before any run

1. **"Main" is a word of English.** The gate cannot tell "main" as a person from "the main goal". It reads the list as
   M20 gives it with the senses that are words of the language excepted by a short list of phrases ("the main goal",
   "main line", "main result", ...), held in its test both ways.
2. **The foundation and `papers/` are most of the count, and hold statements of the mathematics,** dated entries,
   status cells read by locks (B1886, B1891, B1904) and quotations of verdict lines. A rewording there can move a
   lock. So they come last, one a patch, each with the locks that read it run before and after, and a version of the
   foundation for any change of its words, as v1.67 was.
3. **Some of `papers/` is a dated draft.** A draft that is kept as it was is the working record. Which of `papers/` is
   the public face is asked of main; until main says, the series reads the paper itself (`papers/P3_THE_PAPER/`) and
   lists the rest by count.
4. **The pages the README sends a newcomer to are not all the public face by their kind:** the status page and the
   register are the working record. The README points to them labelled as such, and they are not rewritten.
5. **A sentence on authority.** "The owner confirmed" can carry a fact (a wording was fixed on a date). The rewording
   keeps the fact and drops the authority: a postulate with its date and where its wording is fixed, as main's
   sentence for the principle does.
6. **The gate's count can hide a new word on a page whose count is above zero.** It is held by page and by word, and a
   new word on a page fails even if another was taken away in the same commit.
