## 3. How it could come out wrong, written before any run

1. **A label in prose cannot be held.** "Reproduced" stands in 1,182 files. The gate reads the field and no page. The
   note must say that a page can still say more than a field does.
2. **"Recomputed independently" cannot be read from two files.** That two scripts share no code is a judgement. The
   gate can hold only that there are two scripts and two outputs, that they are not one file, and that neither
   imports the other. It must say so in its sentence.
3. **A commit named as evidence may not be on main.** Work done on a session's branch is harvested by a pin. The
   gate then cannot read the order, and by the rule of series 1 it refuses and does not pass.
4. **Reading history moves `arc-data` into the table of gates that read the past.** That table is pinned by two
   locks. The change carries a dated note in each, or the order check stands in a gate that already reads the past.
5. **The wording gate reaches forms.** "Accepted by" beside a session's name is a form. A paraphrase is not.
   And "main verifies with its unseen cases" is a description of work and not a label; the pattern must not fire on
   it, and the twin for it must pass.
6. **A session's name is an address for work.** The gate must not refuse a name where it only says who built or
   who sent. Where main's builder still reads "verdict" and "accepted" in a session's file, those files are out of
   scope, as main's M3 says.
7. **Two lists must not be taken for one,** as in the first card: the five words of a grade and the nine labels.
