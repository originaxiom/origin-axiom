## 2. The record's candidate, generations-as-ends, on the fixed-point companions

- **FK14 (v1.15):** "does the genesis generate states with several ends, or are generations not ends?". It is OPEN. What
  would settle it: "a move of the grammar that adds punctures — none is named in §2, and a level (§3) adds none — or a
  frame whose count is not a count of ends".
- **v1.17**, the fixed-point companions (registered, not adopted): "the mapping torus of the monodromy with every fixed
  point punctured — with ∣2 − tr φ∣ ends, one per fixed point".
- **v1.20:** "Which word states have three ends: |2 − tr φ| = 3 ⇔ tr φ = 5 ⇔ L³R, the only one; its companion L8a15 is
  chiral with S₃ on its ends".
- **L8a15's ends are alike.** B1492's D5: "12, chiral, acting on the three ends as S₃ … the four order-3 elements fix no
  cusp … the three cusps have shape 0.642 + 2.365i". One shape for all three, so no end is singled out by its geometry.
  (A cusp shape is not FR-FIB's τ. P-244: B357's cusp-shape τ was "never identified with the weave's fibre modulus", and
  B1696, NEGATIVE as sealed, found that "the cusp shape does not select τ in this frame; open for other frames".)
- **Every fixed-point companion's ends are alike** (the seat's argument).
  - A companion is "the regular cover with deck group T in which the cusp lifts to |T| cusps" (B1494). For a
    fixed-point companion, T = coker(φ − I), of order |2 − tr φ|.
  - T permutes the lifted cusps simply transitively, and deck maps are isometries. So the ends are isometric.
- **The ends do not carry the generations.**
  - On L8a15, B1492: "So the three ends of L8a15 never enter a count: where an end could count, there is no member; where
    there is a member, no end is special."
  - On the companions read, v1.18: "the ends enter the floor, not the value". On o10_150729 some ends do carry a class:
    "the characters trivial on one or two ends *do* carry an interior class" (B1492).
  - L8a15's three members are one deck orbit, and v1.21 (B1495): "the reading "three generations = the deck orbit of a
    member" survives on the 10′ side only and stays a selection". A deck orbit is alike too.
  - From v1.24 FK14 reads the generations as the parity triplet T on the shared fibre: "READING, not adopted: the three
    generations are this triplet" (v1.24); "READINGS: that the triplet is the generations (FK14)" (v1.28). The flavour
    three is DERIVED, the gauge three a selection.
- **The ends are not points of a history.** They are the act's fixed points, punctures of every fibre together (v1.17:
  "one per fixed point"), so no τ separates them. And a companion covers a thread, a once-punctured-torus bundle, not a
  history (TERMINOLOGY: "A history has an endpoint law; a thread has a trace").
- **So, as the seat reads it, generations-as-ends does not put observables at several points of one history on any
  fixed-point companion.** FK14's other route, a grammar move that adds punctures, is not named on record and is not
  covered.

## 3. A sketch: the generations at three excursions of one history (speculation, below the gate)

**What it would say.** The three generations read three excursions of one history: three runs of letters, each rising to
a height of about a/2. Their mass ratios would then read the history's digits. That is what P-474 asks for, and its next
step names it: "the generations as successive letters".

**What it needs, and none of it is on record:**
- **A mass formula.**
  - FR-FIB's generations are the three parity blocks QJ, QI and QK applied to one shared doublet, at one τ (B1822).
  - Its hierarchy comes from τ's depth: near the cusp "the frame's m₂/m₃ falls like 18·e^(−π Im τ)" (B1691, as B1813
    § 3 reads it). The charges and the line set the twists (B1822 § 1).
  - A frame that reads heights would replace one τ by several.
- **A rule placing the parity lines at excursions.** The record's nearest pieces:
  - B1600's W1: "L fixes (1, 0), R fixes (0, 1), the swap fixes (1, 1)". It ties each letter, so each run, to a parity
    line, but gives no run of L or R to the third line.
  - B1621: "The tick σ² = LR cycles the parity lines". But B1621 itself: "the principle's own data, read by their means,
    fix no number". And it reads only the principle's own word.
- **An answer to the axis gauge.**
  - Harvest row 1450 (READ) summarises the SM seat's § 320: "a thread forces a curve on τ, not a point (the position along
    the axis is a gauge in the fibre's Sol geometry)". P-413 has the first clause.
  - § 320 states it for one thread: "in the flat fibre's Sol geometry the position along the axis is a gauge" (@
    0722f0ad). A note in the same commit's dossier (the_weave_2026-10-07) adds: "it holds for the flat fibre only, not
    for the cusped hyperbolic bundle".
  - Carried to a history (the seat's reading), a translation along the axis is a gauge. The excursions' absolute
    positions are not invariants; their heights and spacings are. So only heights and spacings could be read.
- **What its several points are under "all realised".** The premise map left this unfixed. Main's S274 took its line
  under P-439 with that scope.

**The record's nearest pieces, and why they are not it:**
- **The thread frame** (row 1161, the seat's, REGISTERED): the couplings are "cycle integrals … along the vacuum
  thread's closed geodesic", and "the hierarchy is parameter-free and its depth is the geodesic's height". It reads a
  closed thread, which has a trace, not a history.
- **The completion map's C1** (P-284; B1720, NEGATIVE as sealed): one apex per thread. There "masses and the mixing do
  not meet at one point".
- **B1694:** each sector at its own τ (up 3.1, down 2.5, leptons 2.1), but "one fibre has one τ".
- **The towers frame** (P-466 and P-415, QUEUED; B1812, NEGATIVE, its scope corrected after Review 73; the seat's row
  1477, READ, speculation).
  - In F-theory's 7-brane frame τ varies over the base: "a history is a path in the base and the clock becomes the
    choice of measure on paths".
  - "the breaking depth (a cluster's size) and the hierarchy depth (a matter point's distance to a brane) are both base
    distances". P-466 asks for them to be "one excursion".
  - But "a potential on the positions (none on record in this frame)" is missing, and P-415: "the object fixes the total
    class, not the positions". So it has no outputs either.

**So this is a sketch, not a frame with outputs.**

## 4. What such a frame could tell about the clock (the seat's check)

`excursion_evidence.py` uses the record's two dynamics models:
- the move clock's runs are i.i.d. Geometric(½), p_M(a) = 2^(−a) (Minkowski's measure; B1813, B1815);
- area's are Gauss–Kuzmin, p_G(a) = −log₂(1 − 1/(a+1)²) (P-438), for a start drawn from the stationary (Gauss) measure.
  From a Lebesgue start this holds only asymptotically.

**Two conditions.**
- **The runs are read at positions fixed independently of their lengths.** A placement by depth, which § 3 does not have,
  would change both likelihoods (order statistics). For example, the deepest of 64 runs being 6 has P_M = 0.234 and
  P_G = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶: 17.8 bits for the move clock (the check's part 5).
- **Successive runs are treated as independent.** That is exact for the move clock and approximate for area, whose digits
  are weakly dependent. With the exact Gauss-measure cylinder, three consecutive runs of 6 carry 2.43 bits (not 2.79),
  and three of 11 carry 12.48 (not 13.09).

| run length a (height ≈ a/2) | evidence ln(p_G/p_M), nats (positive favours area) |
|---|---|
| 1, 2, 3, 4 | −0.19, −0.39, −0.29, −0.06 |
| 5 | +0.26 |
| 6 (height 3) | +0.64 |
| 9, 10, 11 | +2.01, +2.51, +3.03 |
| 20 | +8.14 |

- **Three generations at the strip's depth carry 1.1–2.8 bits of likelihood ratio.**
  - Three runs of a = 5 carry 1.14 bits; three of a = 6, 2.79 (1.93 nats).
  - B1694's per-sector depths (a ≈ 6, 5 and 4 by the a/2 rule) give 1.22.
  - The a/2 rule is coarse here. The record's strip-depth thread L⁵RLR² (B1720) has runs (5, 1, 1, 2) and apex
    Im τ = 2.993, so a run of 5 reaches height 3. The range a = 5–6 covers it.
  - The per-run figure at a = 6 (0.64 nats) is of the same size as the plan's item 5.3 figure for one world in the strip
    (0.65 nats).
- **The yardstick.**
  - No bar between clocks is on record.
  - THE_BAR's gate is a gate on a null probability: Bonferroni over every look, "min(1, m p)", then "The positive counts
    only if p < 0.01" (its procedure, steps 4 and 5).
  - THE FINAL ACT'S BAR states it for the next look: "THE_BAR's gate at m ≥ 53 needs 12.4 bits". It applies the gate to a
    law against a clock: "For that statistic this is a genuine cannot-pass".
  - The plan's item 5.3 already says: "The bar's 6.0–6.9 and 7.7–8.7 bits are caps for a law against a clock, not
    between clocks."
  - So the gate is used here only as a yardstick. Three equal runs reach it:
    - read as a likelihood ratio of 12.4 bits: at about 11 letters (height ≈ 5.5);
    - read as P-479 reads p-values (Sellke–Bayarri–Berger, "p_F(A0) = 0.069 allows at most a Bayes factor of about
      2"): at p = 0.01/53 the bound is 227 (7.8 bits), reached at 9 letters (height ≈ 4.5);
    - read as the p-value of the likelihood-ratio statistic, with the move clock as the null: at 9 letters
      (p = 3.7 × 10⁻⁵). Three runs of 6 give p = 0.0066.
- **The expected evidence per run** (a part of P-479's ask, in this setting):
  - **A world on the move clock:** KL(M‖G) = 0.19 nats, uncapped. It favours the move clock (−0.19 in the table's sign).
  - **A world on area:** KL(G‖M) diverges like ln A, A the deepest run allowed, from rare deep runs. An area world's
    median run is a = 2 (P_G(a ≤ 2) = 0.585), which favours the move clock by 0.39 nats.
  - **Capped at A, two ways.** With both laws conditioned on a ≤ A and renormalised, KL(G‖M) is 0.02 nats at A = 6 and
    0.34 at A = 20. If runs deeper than A are seen only as "deeper than A", it is 0.34 and 1.01.
- **So door D through three excursions is weak at the strip's depth.** With the runs placed independently of their
  depths, it reaches the yardstick only from excursions about 1.5 to 2 times as deep (a ≈ 9–11).
