## 3. What it says

- **B1876's reading stands.** The tick and its square have the same discriminant, 5. Nothing is ranked in it.
- **The own-line rule, applied to F as written, gives the tick no line at any level.** B1733 states the rule for
  words in L and R, and P-721 for the ground's own map; the card applies the same condition to F. P-721's twisted
  fixed points are the points of coker(g + I), and for F there are none. H13 has this at level 5; the card has it
  at every level.
- **So the two routes agree on L·R and part on the tick.** For L·R the own-line route and the discriminant give
  the same 5, since det(L·R + I) = 5. For F the first gives nothing. The line of order 5 that P-721 reads is the
  line of the tick's square.
- **The tick is the exception among odd histories, in one line of algebra.** For determinant −1 and trace s,
  det(g − I) = −s and det(g + I) = s. So for s ≥ 1 there are own lines exactly when s ≥ 2, and s = 1 only for the
  tick and its other side. For determinant +1 and trace t the two orders are t − 2 and t + 2. At t = 3 the first is
  1 and the second is the whole discriminant.
- **Lines kept as sets: P-738's parenthesis needs one word.** L·R carries a line of order N to itself at 11, 19,
  29 and on, two lines each to 200. Only at 5 is there exactly one. So "a canonical invariant" selects 5 when it
  means the only invariant line, and singles out no level when it means any.
- **The card cannot tell "exactly one" from "least".** 5 is also the least N at which a line is kept. The
  difference is in the rule's form: "exactly one line at N" is a property of that level alone, and "least"
  compares levels. That only one level has the property is P2's fact and not part of the rule.
- **The twins.** At level 5 the tick carries v to 3v, which is −2v, the twin, and its four vectors are one orbit
  (P3). So one twin up to sign is kept by the tick's square and by the tick at no level (P1b). That the tick
  exchanges the twins at level 5 is on record already (H13; B1872 § 3). P3 and P1b stand beside it and change
  nothing in what main holds: two surfaces, both stated, and no ruling for either.
- **"Exactly one line" is not a rule for finding a ground.** L⁴R and LR⁴ name one level by it. L²R, LR², L³R and
  LR³ name three each, and L²R² names none.
