## The outcome: A, as the card wrote it — but decided on the three-tick object, not on m000

- **The theorem checks hold everywhere.**
  - K1 (twisted half lives, half dies) and K2 (the gap as a boundary quantity, with V* ⊗ w as the partner) hold on
    all 1 204 modules of the sealed run.
  - K4 (transfer to the orientation cover) holds on all 96 modules of odd n that have modules.
- **The objects are the right ones (K3).**
  - M_n is non-orientable exactly for odd n, and its volume is n times m000's.
  - Its H₁ in this seat's presentation equals SnapPy's.
  - The names: M₁ = m000, M₂ = m004, M₃ = m025, M₄ = m206, M₅ = m407 and M₆ = s961. The orientation cover of m000
    is m004, and that of m025 is s961.
- **Main's expectation (K5) holds in this seat's sealed reading.**
  - For even n, w is trivial, and the twisted and untwisted gaps agree on every module (100 on m206, 1 008 on s961).
  - For odd n, they differ on some modules: 6 of 96 on m025. With V* in place of V* ⊗ w, the duality identities fail
    on 24 of 96 (plant P1).
- **The plants fire:** P1 on 24 modules, P2 (a wrong twist) on 24 and P3 (the transfer without the twist) on 6.
- **So, where modules exist, the duality partner on an odd-tick object is V* ⊗ w, with w the tick parity.** There
  the bit that the boundary controls is the order combined with the tick parity.

## The scope: the one-tick object m000 shows nothing in either population

- **The sealed population is empty on m000, m004 and m407** (non-split abelian modules of character order up to 6).
  - On m000 and m004 this holds at every order. H₁ is generated by the tick, so every character factors through it.
    A non-split module needs a root of det(t − Jⁿ) among the character's values on t: t² − t − 1 for m000 and
    t² − 3t + 1 for m004. Neither has a root of unity.
  - So the one-tick object carries no module of the record's kind at all, and the sealed test is decided on m025.
- **Post hoc 1, labelled as such:** m407 at character orders 11 and 22, since its H₁ is ℤ/11 ⊕ ℤ.
  - K1, K2 and K4 hold on all 2 420 modules.
  - The twisted and untwisted gaps differ on 20, and P1 fails on 220.
  - On m407, as on m025, every module where the gaps differ has one character carrying the tick parity (t ↦ −1)
    and the other trivial on t. The twist shows only where a character sees the tick.
- **Post hoc 2, labelled as such:** the common point's representation on every M_n: x ↦ i, y ↦ j, and t ↦ c·gⁿ,
  with g in SU(2) carrying the act and c a root of unity of order up to 12. There are 46 modules for each n.
  - K1 and K2 hold everywhere. The gaps are 0 everywhere, and P1 never fails.
  - h¹ = 1 only at c = ±i, for V, V*, V* ⊗ w and V ⊗ w alike. The peripheral cohomology is 0, so the boundary is
    acyclic and duality constrains nothing there.
  - So on m000 the common point is blind to the twist.
- **What follows.** In these populations the tie between the order and the hand is visible on m025 and m407, the
  objects with 3 and 5 ticks. It is not visible on m000 itself.
  - Showing it on the one-tick object needs modules that m000 does not carry abelianly. Candidates are the record's
    levels taken on m000's own covers in the fibre's direction, or a deformation of the common point at which the
    boundary is not acyclic.
  - That is a widening for main's word, not a result here.

## What the wall teaches

The one-tick object m000 is too rigid for abelian modules: its only characters see the tick, and the tick's
polynomial t² − t − 1 has no root of unity. The twist in duality is there, as the theorem says. But it can be read only
on states with torsion in the fibre's direction. The first of these, among the tick's own powers, is m025. That is the
three-tick object, and its orientation cover is the record's s961.
