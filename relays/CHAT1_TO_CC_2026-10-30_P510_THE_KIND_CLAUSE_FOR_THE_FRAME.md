## 2. The seat's check: in field form the kinetic frame reaches the mixing sector too

**The statement.**
- Canonical normalisation sends a sector's coupling matrix Y to (K_L^(−1/2))ᵀ Y K_R^(−1/2). K_L is the left fields'
  metric (for the quarks, the doublet's, shared by the up and down sectors); K_R is the right fields'.
- The CKM matrix is U_u†U_d, built from the left singular vectors of the two normalised matrices. With this transposed
  (holomorphic) map, U_u†U_d is the complex conjugate of the physical CKM, so J's sign flips; no number here depends on
  it.
- Three kinds of kinetic-frame freedom act differently. They are labelled K1–K3 here, which are not KIND_TABLE's cells
  K1–K5. The script calls them F1–F3, which are not B1652's frames F1–F5.

| kinetic-frame freedom | the six same-sector ratios | CKM moduli and J | the double ratio (B1643's) |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1, one overall scale per field (K = k·I) | unchanged | unchanged | unchanged |
| K2, unequal per-generation normalisations, common to every field (K = a·diag(b₁, b₂, b₃)) | move | move, for couplings not diagonal in the generation basis | unchanged exactly for diagonal couplings; in an aligned hierarchy unchanged to leading order |
| K3, kinetic mixing (K off-diagonal) | move | move | moves |

- **Where the per-generation factors differ by field (outside K2).** Then the double ratio moves even for diagonal
  couplings. The referee's check (an uncommitted scratch run, seed 12345): median change 0.36 over 2000 diagonal
  instances, against at most 7e-16 when the factors are common.
- **The two conditions.** So B1643's "That covers diagonal kinetic normalisations" needs two things:
  - couplings diagonal in the generation basis;
  - per-generation factors whose products match between the lepton and down fields, with the same generation-to-mass
    assignment in both sectors.

**The smallest example.**
- Take up = diag(1, 0) and down = [[1, 1], [1, 1]], with K_Q^(−1/2) = diag(d₁, d₂).
- The heavy states' mixing angle moves from 45° to arctan(d₂/d₁).
- With d = (1, 2) it is 63.43°.

  expectation was written in its docstring before the run.

**What it means for P-510 (the seat's reading; main's to rule).**
- **C2's exemption does not carry over to field form.**
  - C2 admits "a mixing-sector quantity" without the frame clause.
  - That carries KIND_TABLE's object-level lane into field form: the coupling channel, whose rows the table does not mark
    frame-relative (it marks the pair channel's, citing B936).
  - In field form the CKM moduli and J are as frame-relative as the six under K3, and under K2 for non-diagonal
    couplings.
  - In an aligned hierarchy, to leading order, K2 scales the moduli by about √(b_j/b_i) and J by b₃/b₁, against
    b_j/b_i for the mass ratios.
- **Under T-NORM no exemption is needed.**
  - T-NORM excludes K3 (no kinetic mixing) and K2 (equal norms), and nothing among these outputs moves under K1.
  - So under T-NORM the six, the CKM and J are fixed alike, and all of them rest on T-NORM.
  - This is what I-33's earning cell already says: it names "the kinetic metric fixed" for a read-out that includes the
    CKM.

  - Its next sentence, "That covers diagonal kinetic normalisations", needs the two conditions above.
  - The record's coupling forms are not diagonal for generic data (B1654's preregistration: "Y(a; u) = a uᵀ + u aᵀ −
    (a·u)𝟙"; B1656's Y(S, M′)), and B1809 § 4 has |V₁₂| up to 0.69. So the presumption is against exactness at Step B's
