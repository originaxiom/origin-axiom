## 2. What went wrong first, and how it was found

- **The sealed run's A1 printed False.** At all twelve points the residual was the same constant, −4.355172, with no
  term in log Im τ (fit misfit 9 × 10⁻¹²). The c- and τ-dependence of B1919 held already there; only the constant did not.
- **Post hoc 1** (committed before its run) tested the reading that the statement's constant should be −2π(γ + log 2π);
  numerically that holds (1.4 × 10⁻¹¹). **That reading was wrong.**
- **Main's FINDINGS, read next,** define F_u as this seat does (u₁ multiplies τ, the pole π/(s − 1) removed), and main's
  code regularises the norm as this seat does. So this seat looked again at its own F_u and found the error by hand: in
  the Poisson form the first part carries √π Γ(s − ½)/Γ(s) = π(1 + (s − 1)κ + …), κ = ψ(½) − ψ(1) = −2 log 2, and it
  multiplies a pole, so the constant term gains πκ = −2π log 2 = −4.355172. The sealed code set that factor to π.
- **Post hoc 2** (committed before its run) tested the correction three ways:
  - C2: F_u from the general-s Poisson form, as [Z(1 + h) + Z(1 − h)]/2 with h = 10⁻⁴, agrees with the corrected closed
    form within 3.7 × 10⁻⁷ at all twelve points, and differs from the sealed one by 4.355 at each;
  - C3: B1919 then holds within 1.4 × 10⁻¹¹ at every point;
  - C1: the general-s form against the direct lattice sum at s = 3 failed at two points of c = (0.62, 0.09), where
    ∣m + u₁∣ is smallest and the Bessel series was cut at k = 30. **Post hoc 3** (committed before its run) cut it at
    150 instead: C1 then holds within 7.2 × 10⁻¹¹ at all twelve.

So the one disagreement was this seat's own: a negative from the sealed run that a second look found to be a dropped
term.
