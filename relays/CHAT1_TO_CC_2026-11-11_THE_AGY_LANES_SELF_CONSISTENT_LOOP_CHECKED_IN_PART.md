## 5. How the seat reads the lane's closures

These are readings of the parts of the lane's reports that were read, not results.
- **The lane's "Unique Winner: E_8 (1 of 95 Algebras)"** is the classical fact that, among the root lattices of
  the irreducible root systems, only E₈'s is even and unimodular. The lane itself says, on line 50 of its first
  report, which is unstarred, that it reduces a dependency to "one structural premise". The domain matters as
  well: E₈ ⊕ E₈ meets the same condition and is left out as
  reducible; D₁₆⁺ meets it and is left out because it is not a root lattice. The referee checked both.
- **The lane's "Closes m=1 & N=5"** is on record. That m = 1 is singled out among the metallic words is B251 and
  B591. The level is the rule of own lines applied to L·R (B1733, P-721). Main's B1884 has the seat's card showing
  that the same rule gives the tick itself no line. So this closure belongs to the two-act ground.
- **Phase 3's third gate, by symmetry.** The seat computed no value of it. The argument, with its hypotheses:
  - it is for scalar sections with a flat twist of odd order (here 5), and for overlaps that are plain integrals
    of products, with a real basis chosen in any degenerate eigenspace. The lane's gates also name spin, flux and harmonic forms, to
    which it is not applied here;
  - with no twist the operator is real;
  - if the twist is along the base circle, or has a base part and a fibre part that the symmetry sends to its
    inverse, a symmetry that reverses the base, composed with complex conjugation, maps the twisted operator to
    itself, and overlaps are then real;
  - the bundle of the run of 6 has such symmetries. In integers g = rows (1, 0), (6, −1) has g M g⁻¹ = M⁻¹,
    det g = −1, g² = 1 and v₀·g = −v₀ (post hoc), so g sends a fibre part to its inverse. The referee finds with
    SnapPy that two of the four isometries reverse the base, and that both keep the orientation of the
    3-manifold;
  - the bundle of L⁵RL²R² has only the fibre's half-turn, which does not reverse the base;
  - so, under those hypotheses, symmetry forces no phase in the first two cases and permits one in the third.
    That is the pattern the lane reports. The lane gives these reasons itself on line 83 of its third report.
    What the seat adds is the hypotheses and the manifold facts the reasons need. The size of the effect is not
    verified.
- **Two cautions on that reading.** The seat reads the lane's "cusp ramification" as a twist along the base.
  Line 83 glosses it as a peripheral holonomy; a character is trivial on the fibre's boundary, so a peripheral
  holonomy is along the base. The closed word L⁵RL²R² has M − I with invariants (1, 41), so a character of order 5
  on its bundle can only be a base character, if the lane's history is that closed word; with one golden period
  added the invariants are (1, 105). And if the twist were along the fibre only, conjugation with the half-turn
  would be a symmetry of every history and would forbid a phase in all three cases.
- **Not being its own reverse as a word is necessary and not sufficient.** The referee's examples: LLRLRR and
  LLLRLRRR are not palindromes, and their bundles still have an isometry that reverses the base; it carries v₀ to
  2v₀, so it serves for a twist along the base only. This does not correct the lane: its own condition on line
  83, read with reflections allowed, is exactly that no symmetry reverses the base.
- **Two asymmetries are in play, and they differ.** The run of 6 is not its own mirror, so it has a hand, and it
  is its own reverse. The lane's effect needs a history without a base-reversing symmetry.
- **Nothing in the parts read selects such a history.**
