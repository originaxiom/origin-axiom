## (b) Findings, by question

### Q1. Magnetized tori and orbifolds

**How theta functions with characteristics arise**
- **Cremades, Ibáñez, Marchesano 2004** (arXiv:hep-th/0404229; JHEP 05 (2004) 079). The abstract computes Yukawas for D = 10 SYM on tori with constant fluxes as overlap integrals. The couplings are "Riemann theta-functions" depending on the complex structure and the Wilson-line backgrounds, and different backgrounds give different textures. The abstract does not use the word "characteristics".
- **Zero modes.** ψ ∝ e^{iπMz Im z/Im τ} ϑ[(j+α₁)/M; −α₂](Mz, Mτ), j = 0…M−1, with SS phases (α₁, α₂) (2101.00826 eq. 44).
- **Wilson lines** enter through z̃ = z + ζ/M: in the theta argument and in an extra phase, with the characteristic still set by the flux integers (1204.5327, as read).
- **Normalization:** ∫ψψ* = (2 Im τ)^{−1/2} δ_ij (2209.07249 eq. 8).

**Lines ↔ characteristic shifts (standard identity, derived from the series).** With
ϑ[a; b](ν|τ) = Σ_ℓ exp(iπ(a+ℓ)²τ + 2πi(a+ℓ)(ν+b)):

  ϑ[a; b](ν + ατ + β | τ) = exp(−iπα²τ − 2πiα(ν + b + β)) · ϑ[a+α; b+β](ν | τ).

A theta function on the frame's line w = ατ + b is therefore a theta constant with characteristics shifted by (α, b), up to
an elementary factor. In magnetized language this is a Wilson line with components (α, b) in the τ- and 1-directions.

**Discreteness and modular action of the backgrounds**
- Continuous Wilson lines are "forbidden on orbifolds" because the relevant components are ℤ₂-odd (Abe–Kobayashi–Sumita–Tatsuta arXiv:1405.5012, as read).
- SS phases mix under the modular group. Under T, (α₁, α₂) → (α₁, α₁ + α₂ − M/2); under S, (α₁, α₂) → (1 − α₂, α₁), mod 1. "Closed sets" are only (0, 0) for even M and (½, ½) for odd M; T²/ℤ₂ allows only ℤ₂ phases (2101.00826).
- A Wilson line transforms as ζ → ζ/(cτ + d), like z (2005.12642).
- **[INFERENCE]** This is the analogue of SL(2,ℤ) permuting the frame's lines (B1724's laws): a line of order N is preserved only by a congruence subgroup.

**Three generations and multiple Higgs**
- There are |M| zero modes on T² with flux M (2005.12642). T²/ℤ₂ splits them into even and odd modes.
- Example: the model "4-4-8, (e,e,e), 5H" has three left- and three right-handed modes and **five Higgs modes**. The models in Table 2 of 2112.01680 have 5 to 9 Higgs modes.
- The U(8) model of 1405.5012 has "three generations of quarks and leptons and five generations of Higgs multiplets".
- Fujimoto et al. (arXiv:1605.00140) consider one or two Higgs pairs.

**Quark masses and CKM: what works and what does not**
- **1405.5012 (Gaussian Froggatt–Nielsen).**
  - The structure is Y_ij ≈ exp[−c(a_i + b_j)²]: Gaussian in the sum of flavour labels.
  - Sample fit: m_u/m_t 1.7×10⁻⁵ (data 1.5×10⁻⁵), m_c/m_t 5.7×10⁻³ (7.5×10⁻³), but m_s/m_b 6.8×10⁻² (2.3×10⁻²) and V_cb 0.07 (0.041). The authors call it "(semi-)realistic" with a 0.2–5 acceptance window.
  - Im τ is fitted in the 1.5–2.0 range with Re τ = 0.
  - **Up and down sectors need different Higgs VEV patterns to get nonvanishing mixing** (fitted ρ_u = 0.29, ρ_d = 0.38, ρ′_d = 0.1).
- **Kikuchi–Kobayashi–Ogawa–Uchida 2021** (arXiv:2112.01680).
  - At τ = i∞, T-invariance leaves "almost elements of Yukawa matrices" vanishing, each surviving matrix with one diagonal entry: "We cannot realize flavor mixing from these Yukawa matrices."
  - Realistic textures appear near τ = i (Fritzsch–Xing type) and τ = ω (Fritzsch type), and only with small deviations of τ and of the Higgs VEV from the exact rank-one directions.
  - **The Higgs VEV direction is fitted (polar angles θ, φ), and the up and down directions are independent: "Otherwise, we cannot derive realistic results."**
- **Hoshiya–Kikuchi–Kobayashi–Uchida 2022** (arXiv:2209.07249).
  - "There are 408 flavor models in total"; 24 can be realistic near the S-symmetric vacuum (τ = i); "no consistent flavor models for ST and T-symmetric vacua".
  - Higgs VEVs are assumed aligned with eigenvectors of the residual generator, and consistency requires the same fixed point for up and down (p_u = p_d).
- **Fujimoto et al. 2016** (1605.00140). Kinetic-mixing smearing gives hierarchies of about 10⁻²–10⁻³, but "it is very difficult to achieve the mass ratio between the up and top quarks" on T²/ℤ_N with N = 3, 4, 6.
- **Buchmüller–Schweizer 2017** (arXiv:1701.06935; PRD 95, 075024).
  - Setup: SO(10)×U(1) on T²/ℤ₂ with flux and two Wilson lines; two Higgs doublets with flat profiles; Yukawas live at fixed points and are dyadic (rank-one) per fixed point.
  - Result: the two heaviest families with a small CKM angle (sin θ₂₃ = 0.031) "because the up and down matrices are similar". "The lightest quark-lepton family remains massless."

**What fixes the light Higgs combination**
- In every fit found it is a free parameter: angles (2112.01680) or VEV ratios (1405.5012).
- 2209.07249 says the lightest combination is "not clear" in explicit models, assumes residual-eigenstate alignment, and suggests a D-brane-instanton μ-term could produce it.
- Kobayashi–Tatsuta–Uemura (PRD 93, 065029, 2016; intersecting D6-branes): one rigid instanton gives a rank-one μ-matrix; a full-rank one needs several instantons, left for future work. No light direction is singled out.

### Q2. Modular flavour symmetry and the quark sector

**The mechanism**
- **Feruglio 2017** (arXiv:1706.08749). SUSY, with "Yukawa couplings are just modular forms" and the only flavour breaking from ⟨τ⟩.
- **NPP 2021** (arXiv:2102.07488).
  - Hierarchies can arise "solely due to the proximity of the modulus" to a symmetric point, set by the decomposition of the representations under the residual group.
  - At i∞, ε = e^{−2π Im τ/N}, and M_ij is "expected to be O(ε^l) whenever Im τ is large", with l fixed by the Z_N^T charges.
  - Near τ = i the desired patterns cannot come from ε alone; only i∞ and ω work.
  - In a first read the fetched text listed (1, ε, ε³) for N = 4 near i∞ among the hierarchical pairs; the table itself was not visible in a second read.

**Does a single τ reproduce the CKM? Mostly only with extra structure**
- **Okada–Tanimoto** (arXiv:1905.13421, 2005.00775; A₄, near τ = i). CKM and CP are reproduced at Im τ ≈ 1.013–1.048, \|Re τ\| ≲ 0.007. This needs weight-2, 4, 6 forms and extra complex couplings g_u, g_d.
- **Petcov–Tanimoto** (arXiv:2212.13336; A₄, near ω). With a common τ, \|V_cb\| is "problematic" and CP arises only "as a higher order correction". The fix is complex g_u, g_d **with different ε in the up and down sectors**, or modified weights. The up sector needs \|g_u\| ~ 10.
- **Petcov–Tanimoto** (arXiv:2306.05730; A₄, near i∞). **The closest analogue to the frame's regime.**
  - ε = e^{−2π Im τ/3} with Im τ ≈ 2.4–2.5 (ε ≈ 5.8×10⁻³).
  - Common τ: \|V_cb\| ≈ 0.077 against 0.040.
  - With real couplings, **Re τ gives J_CP ≈ 2×10⁻¹⁰** against 2.8×10⁻⁵.
  - Two moduli fit (N_σ = 2.8): τ_d ≈ −0.309 + 2.401i, **τ_u ≈ 0.123 + 3.811i**.
  - Or **up-type right-handed weights (6, 2, 0) against down (4, 2, 0)** with one complex parameter (N_σ = 2.0).
  - The up hierarchy needs an ε about ten times smaller than the down.
- **de Medeiros Varzielas–Levy–Penedo–Petcov** (arXiv:2307.14410; S₄′, near ω — the paper calls ω "the (left) cusp"; it is not τ → i∞).
  - "Fitting quark data requires explicit CP breaking, unless a second modulus is introduced."
  - A shared τ makes the 1 : ε : ε² pattern hard for both sectors.
- **Abe–Higaki–Kawamura–Kobayashi** (arXiv:2301.07439; S₄′, Im τ ≫ 1).
  - Z₄^T acts as a Froggatt–Nielsen symmetry with \|ϵ\| ≈ 2e^{−(π/2) Im τ}; the texture works "only in the following four cases" of representations (u^c a triplet in all four).
  - Benchmarks: τ = 2.4956 + 2.2306i and 1.4944 + 2.6779i.
  - Needs coefficient ratios of about 23–53 and a cancellation for the charm mass; s₂₃ ~ ϵ² comes out an order of magnitude too small in model 1.
  - The CKM phase "is approximately vanishing if all of the coefficients are real", and the S₄′ modulus phase does not contribute at leading order.
- **Kikuchi–Kobayashi–Nasu–Takada–Uchida** (arXiv:2301.03737; Γ₆, near i∞). Couplings ±1, τ = 3.2i or 3.7i; \|V_cb\| 0.027 against 0.0405 (Type I); no CP analysis.
- **de Medeiros Varzielas–Paiva 2026** (arXiv:2604.01422; S₄′ near τ = i).
  - χ² ≈ 0.891 with 9 parameters against 10 observables; CP from Re τ ≈ 0.0046, with J linear in Re τ.
  - **Different modular weights per field** (via the Kähler factors (2 Im τ)^{k/2}) produce the hierarchies.
- **Non-holomorphic SU(5)** (Loualidi–Miskaoui–Nasri arXiv:2503.12594). One τ fits all quark observables within 3σ, with 21–24 real parameters.

**Kähler and non-holomorphic corrections**
- Chen–Ramos-Sánchez–Ratz (arXiv:1909.06910): the Kähler potential "is not fixed by the symmetries". Extra terms shift mixing angles by about 1–10°, and "the number of free parameters is generically larger than the number of predictions".
- Baur et al. (arXiv:2207.10677): Kähler corrections are needed for a simultaneous quark and lepton fit in the first string-derived eclectic model (modulus "in the vicinity of ⟨T⟩ = i∞").

**CP phase**
- CP-conserving τ are "the imaginary axis and the boundary of the fundamental domain", with τ → −τ* under CP (NPP–Titov arXiv:1905.11970).
- Near the cusp the Re τ effect is suppressed (2306.05730 above). **[INFERENCE]** Each leading entry is a single power of q^{1/N}, so its phase can be rephased away.

### Q3. Modular flavour symmetry and magnetized tori, and whether the frame is a known special case

**Magnetized tori as modular flavour models**
- Magnetized zero modes are weight-½ objects for Γ̃′_{2\|M\|} (2005.12642). M = 2 gives a group of order 96 (T′⋊ℤ₄); M = 4 gives order 384 (Δ(48)⋊ℤ₈); the even modes of T²/ℤ₂ with M = 4 form a triplet of Δ(48)⋊ℤ₈.
- On T² × T² with τ₁ = τ₂, the zero modes act as weight-1 forms for Γ(2 lcm(M₁, M₂)), and Γ′₄ ≅ S′₄ (order 48) is realized (arXiv:2007.06188); Γ′₅ is not discussed there.
- Three-generation groups include S′₄, Δ′(96), A₄ × ℤ₄, A₅ × ℤ₄ and A₅ × ℤ₈ (2101.00826).
- Almumin et al. (2102.11286) give closed-form Yukawas for arbitrary flux; finite metaplectic groups appear. The top-down origin controls the kinetic normalizations that bottom-up models leave free.

**Eclectic structure**
- Conventional discrete flavour symmetries of magnetized tori "do not commute" with the modular flavour symmetry; combined, they form a larger group with the conventional one as a normal subgroup (Ohki–Uemura–Watanabe arXiv:2003.04174).
- The eclectic programme (Nilles–Ramos-Sánchez–Vaudrevange arXiv:2001.01736): "Model building with finite modular flavor symmetries thus appears to be much more restrictive than previously thought."

**Level 4 and theta constants**
- M_{1/2}(Γ(4)) = ⟨θ₃(0\|2τ), θ₂(0\|2τ)⟩ (2007.13706).
- NPP's S₄′ building blocks are θ = Θ₃(2τ) and ε = Θ₂(2τ), with Y_3̂^{(1)} = (√2 εθ, ε², −θ²), Y_2^{(2)} = ((θ⁴ + ε⁴)/√2, −√6 ε²θ²) and Y_3′^{(2)} = ((θ⁴ − ε⁴)/√2, −2εθ³, −2ε³θ) (2006.03058).

**Barger 2026** (arXiv:2609.09262; single author; recent preprint, status unknown)
- Selects S₄ (level 4) from charged-fermion mass double ratios, with generations at T-charges (3, 1, 0). It uses weight-½ theta constants "on the metaplectic cover", following the "coupling dictionary of magnetized toroidal compactifications".
- It sits at Im τ ≈ 1.07, not at the cusp region.
- By its own account it writes "no modular-invariant Yukawa matrices". Several coefficients are inputs, and V_td comes from an empirical relation.

**Verdict on "known special case"**
- Each ingredient is known: theta constants with rational characteristics ↔ magnetized tori with Wilson lines and SS phases; the S₄ ≅ Γ₄ triplet and its residual Z₄^T counting at the cusp; weight-½ forms on metaplectic covers; Γ′₅ ≅ 2I as a finite modular group.
- I found no paper combining a modular-form Higgs triplet in CP², channel triplets from theta-function residues, the quaternionic coupling, hypercharge-proportional characteristic twists and the 2I/E₈ layer **[NOT FOUND]**.
- The frame is best described as "a level-4 (S₄/S₄′) modular model of magnetized-torus type, with a flavoured Higgs" **[INFERENCE]**.
- Relative to the literature, the frame replaces the usual 4–12 free O(1) couplings by fixed channel constants (c_s, L) **[INFERENCE]**. That makes it more predictive and harder to fit.

### Q4. Where τ sits: modulus stabilization

**The generic results put τ near Im τ ≈ 1**
- **NPP 2022** (arXiv:2201.02020; JHEP 03 (2022) 149).
  - Minima at τ ≈ 1.2i, at τ = i, and near ω, with \|u\|_min ≈ 0.0145/(m + 0.0625); e.g. τ ≈ ∓0.484 + 0.884i (m = 1) and ∓0.492 + 0.875i (m = 2).
  - CP-breaking minima occur near ω. "The potential under analysis diverges for large Im τ": no minima at Im τ ≈ 2–4.
- **Knapp-Pérez et al. 2023** (arXiv:2304.14437). Matter VEVs shift τ slightly off i or ω; all reported values have Im τ between 0.94 and 1.02.
- **Ishiguro–Kobayashi–Otsuka 2020** (arXiv:2011.09154). In the flux landscape of T⁶/(ℤ₂×ℤ₂′), τ is "clustered at a fixed point with the residual" ℤ₃, and CP-breaking vacua are "statistically disfavored".
- **Kobayashi–Nasu–Sakuma–Yamada 2023** (arXiv:2310.15604). A one-loop Coleman–Weinberg (CW) correction shifts τ near ω by \|δy\| ≈ 0.024–0.036.

**Mechanisms that reach large Im τ**
- **Higaki–Kawamura–Kobayashi 2024** (arXiv:2402.02071).
  - A CW potential from vector-like quarks with modular-form masses fixes Im τ ≃ 12–13, with Re τ an axion.
  - That A₄ model "can not be used to explain the hierarchical structure of the SM fermions".
  - Trivial singlets give a potential that grows with Im τ; non-trivial singlets are needed for a minimum.
- **Higaki–Kawamura–Kobayashi–Nasu–Sakuma** (arXiv:2412.18435). **The one published mechanism found that lands in 2–3.5.**
  - Radiative stabilization at Im τ ≫ 1.
  - Single-modulus Γ₆ examples: **Im τ₀ ≈ 2.4** (weight-10 singlet Y_{1_5}^{(10)}, μ/Λ = 10⁻², m²/Λ² = 10⁻⁸) and ≈ 22.6.
  - Two moduli: **(3.43, 3.43)** and (2.96, 14.8).
  - "Im τ ~ 3" is "moderate to explain the hierarchies". Re τ is flat to O(\|q\|²).
- **Wang–Zhang 2026** (arXiv:2601.09542; Jordan-frame supergravity).
  - A runaway at i∞ "can be properly stabilized at typical modulus VEV with large Im τ" using soft masses or racetrack terms; the analytic estimate is Im⟨τ⟩ ~ 10/k̃_N.
  - Numerical scans mostly give Im τ ≈ 1. Their flavour examples use τ ≈ 2.28i (S₄′ quarks) and −0.47 + 3.11i (A₅′ leptons).

**Upper bounds**
- Ishiguro–Kai–Kobayashi–Otsuka 2025 (arXiv:2508.12392; heterotic threshold corrections): "τ ≃ i∞ lies in the Swampland", with U₂ ≲ 34.3 for b = 10, Im S = 2, and O(10) stabilization "difficult".
- **[INFERENCE]** Im τ ≈ 2–3.5 is far inside that bound.

### Q5. Holomorphy and the reading question

- **SUSY.** Up-type Yukawas couple to H_u and down-type to H_d, distinct superfields, with holomorphic modular-form coefficients (Feruglio 2017 and its successors). Higgs fields are typically singlets of weight 0, as in 2301.07439, 2306.05730 and 2212.13336.
  - With a singlet Higgs, conjugation does nothing in flavour space. The frame's S/S̃ question exists only because its Higgs carries family indices.
- **Magnetized.** Opposite-chirality zero modes are complex conjugates (j → −j − α₁; 2209.07249 eq. 5). H_u and H_d come from different flux sectors, and fits need **independent** up and down VEV directions (2112.01680; 1405.5012).
- **Non-SUSY.**
  - Polyharmonic Maaß-form Yukawas (Qu–Ding 2406.02527) have non-holomorphic cusp terms: c⁻(0) y^{1−k} (ln y at k = 1) and incomplete-gamma terms.
  - The non-holomorphic SU(5) model (2503.12594) couples up quarks to 5_H and down quarks to 5_H* (plus 45_H*) and fits the quark data with many parameters.
  - **[INFERENCE]** The frame's m₁/m₃ ~ Im τ·\|q\| (B1691), with an explicit Im τ, looks like such non-holomorphic cusp terms (E₂-type, through the Serre derivative in DR).
- **A single non-SUSY doublet with modular Yukawas failing the CKM: [NOT FOUND].** The closest no-goes are for flavoured Higgs multiplets:
  - Three doublets in an A₄ or S₄ triplet: "not possible to obtain simultaneously non-vanishing quark masses and a non-vanishing CP-violating phase" for all quark representations and all vacuum alignments that can be global minima (González Felipe–Serôdio–Silva arXiv:1302.0861).
  - In general, "the only way of obtaining a physical CKM mixing matrix" with non-degenerate nonzero masses is "requiring the vacuum expectation values of the Higgs fields to break completely the full flavour group", apart from a baryon-number symmetry (González Felipe–Ivanov–Nishi–Serôdio–Silva arXiv:1401.5807; EPJC 74, 2953).
  - **[INFERENCE]** The frame's Higgs sits near a ρ(T) eigenline (a residual Z₄^T vacuum) up to departures O(r^½), O(r). That is the regime these no-goes describe, approached as Im τ grows. It fits B1739's "pinning" and THE READING TEST's failure.
- **Different modular forms for up and down.** No theorem found. In practice, successful cusp-region fits give the up sector its own structure: different weights, its own modulus, or \|g_u\| ~ 10 (2306.05730; 2212.13336; 2307.14410).
- **[INFERENCE, not from a paper]** In SUSY with a μ-matrix μ_ij(τ) built from modular forms, the light H_u lies in ker μᵀ and the light H_d in ker μ.
  - For symmetric μ the two kernels coincide: one holomorphic direction in both sectors, which is the frame's A1.
  - For μ = ε_ijk Y_k(τ), both light directions equal Y(τ): the Higgs direction would itself be a modular-form triplet.

### Q6. Georgi–Jarlskog 3 and b–τ from theta-function or modular Yukawas

- **Derivation of the factor 3 from theta functions or magnetized geometry: [NOT FOUND].**
- **Ding–King–Yao** (arXiv:2103.16311; modular S₄ × SU(5)).
  - Y^e = Y_5̄ − 3Y_45̄ and Y^d = (Y_5̄ + Y_45̄)ᵀ; the GJ relations "do not work if strictly imposed", so they are used in generalised form.
  - "Approximate b-τ unification is predicted": m_b ≃ 0.968 GeV against m_τ ≃ 1.302 GeV.
  - It uses several moduli at different fixed points (τ_TS = −ω² for up quarks, ω for down quarks and charged leptons).
- **Chen–King–Medina–Valle** (arXiv:2312.09255; JHEP 02 (2024) 160; S₄ near i∞ with ϵ = q^{1/4}, SUSY).
  - Down quarks and charged leptons share one mass-matrix structure, which gives relations such as R1: m_s m_d/[m_b(m_b − 3m_s)] ≈ m_μ m_e/[m_τ(m_τ − 3m_μ)].
  - These are deformations of the Golden Mass Relation set by Clebsch–Gordan and modular-form coefficients. F_R1 stays within ±10% from M_Z to M_GUT.
- **Font–Ibáñez–Marchesano–Regalado** (1211.6529; F-theory SU(5)).
  - Hypercharge flux "does not affect the holomorphic Yukawas but does modify the profile of the wavefunctions", which is linked to the down-quark/lepton difference at unification.
  - Naive Y_b = Y_τ, Y_s = Y_μ, Y_d = Y_e fails; non-perturbative effects plus flux "allows to describe the measured lepton and D-quark masses of second and third generations".
- **Barger 2026** (2609.09262). The plaquette m_b m_μ/(m_s m_τ) is **3.136 ± 0.027 at M_Z**, against the GJ value 3 at unification; running is deferred. It also gives m_b/m_τ = 1/√(2ε) at M_Z. These are empirical insertion rules.

## (c) Concrete, testable imports (data-free first)

1. **Is R the S₄′ weight-1 triplet?** (data-free, minutes of CPU)
   - Test whether the frame's R(τ) is projectively Y_3̂^{(1)} = (√2 εθ, ε², −θ²), with θ = Θ₃(2τ) and ε = Θ₂(2τ), in the frame basis (e₁, n̄, n), up to signs and phases.
   - Equivalent test: every null vector is (√2 st, s², −t²) up to scale, so check whether R's (s : t) equals Θ₂(2τ) : Θ₃(2τ) at a few τ.
   - Leading cusp coefficients (2√2, 4) and nullity already agree.
   - If it holds, B-2's "rule for h" can be phrased in NPP's ring of S₄′ forms (Y_2^{(2)}, Y_3′^{(2)}, …), and DR + λR becomes a statement about weight-raising.
2. **Give the up sector its own suppression.**
   - The literature's working cusp fits need an ε_up about ten times smaller than ε_down: different weights (up (6, 2, 0) against down (4, 2, 0)), a separate modulus (Im τ_u ≈ 3.8 against Im τ_d ≈ 2.4), or an O(10) up coupling (2306.05730, 2212.13336).
   - Frame analogue: different horocycles or weights per sector. A sealed test should say which one, and count it as fitted structure (PRECISION_BAR's 15.09 line).
3. **Treat two Higgs directions as the literature's default.**
   - Independent up and down directions are what the magnetized fits use (2112.01680, 1405.5012), and the flavoured-Higgs no-goes (1302.0861, 1401.5807) predict failure when the vacuum keeps a residual subgroup.
   - Data-free check on A1's stored optima: is the fitted h close to a residual-symmetric line (n, n̄, e₁ or an S-eigenline)? If so, 1401.5807 already explains a failure.
4. **Candidate Higgs rules from the literature** (for B-2):
   - (i) a residual eigenvector (2209.07249; the frame's n);
   - (ii) the null vector of a modular-form μ-matrix (my inference in Q5);
   - (iii) a fixed S₄′ multiplet such as Y_3̂^{(1)} or Y_3′^{(2)} (import 1).
5. **CP expectation.**
   - With nothing complex but τ near the cusp, the literature finds J suppressed by orders of magnitude (2×10⁻¹⁰ in 2306.05730; "approximately vanishing" in 2301.07439). A small J on A1's rows is the generic expectation, not a frame-specific defect.
   - Escape routes in the literature: a complex coupling, a second modulus, or a vacuum away from self-conjugate points. Record J against Re τ on A1's rows (B-3).
6. **τ's dynamics** (D-1).
   - Radiative CW stabilization with modular-form vector-like masses is the one published mechanism that lands at Im τ ≈ 2.4–3.4 (2412.18435).
   - Port it: build V_CW from the frame's heavy states with their τ-dependent masses (the channels' |Δ| = |u·u + w²| is a candidate mass function), check whether B1704's run to the cusp turns over, and note that trivial singlets give a growing potential (2402.02071).
   - The NPP-type SUGRA potentials will not do it: they diverge at large Im τ (2201.02020).
7. **Normalization and Kähler control.**
   - Physical couplings carry (2 Im τ)^{k/2} per field, and magnetized modes are normalized as ∫ψψ* = (2 Im τ)^{−1/2}. Check that the frame's channel weights (√(2πL + c_s) R_s) carry the Im τ factors consistently.
   - Kähler freedom adds parameters (1909.06910): a reason to keep c_s and L fixed by rule.
8. **Comparison scale** (A-4). The cusp fits quoted here compare at the GUT scale with SUSY running (2306.05730, 2307.14410, 2103.16311). Some down/lepton relations are stable within 10% under running (2312.09255).
9. **GJ and b–τ.** Compare the frame's D = 9 (order-5 lines, c = 3) and m_b/m_τ with the S₄ relations R1–R4 of 2312.09255, which carry an explicit 3, and with Barger's M_Z plaquette 3.136 ± 0.027.
