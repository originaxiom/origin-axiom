## The question

THE HOLOMORPHIC READING TEST (sealed at 110af4a13; running) asks whether one Higgs direction read S in every sector
(A1) brings the nine below the bars at M_Z. Its power control failed (33 of 36 against 34), so by its own rule a failure
of its H1 cannot be read as a negative. Separately, the plan's blind spot 3: the frame carries unification-scale
fingerprints (b–τ equality on the lines of order 1, 2 and 5; c = 3, the Georgi–Jarlskog factor; equal holomorphic
readings, as supersymmetry's holomorphy asks), and every value test so far compared at M_Z. Between M_Z and the
unification scale the up-quark ratios fall by about a quarter and V_cb and V_ub by about 8%, so a structure fitted at the
wrong scale can miss by several σ on those entries alone.

**Does one holomorphic Higgs direction bring the nine below the bars on rows nobody has computed, away from the look's
lines, against the unification-scale targets (arm G) or the M_Z targets (arm Z), with a search whose power is measured —
and is a fit as good as the data's rare for random hierarchies through the same procedure, including random hierarchies
that share the lepton–down relation c = 3 puts in by hand? And at the best such row, are J and m_b/m_τ — neither
fitted — right?**

The plan's C-2 also named A1 with τ at B1720's apexes (τ by rule). It is not part of this test (its 6 498 rows at
RIGID_TAU's points would double the cost) and is left for a later rule.

## The search (declared: two stages; `one_higgs_search.py` at c54c39d6f and `search_v3.py` at 2e98bdd47, sha256 `500f887f…d3b4`, its code unchanged since 2f0f61e31 where e12 ran it)

  random), once per row, shared by both arms, both stages and the calibration minima.
- **Stage A, every row and arm:** THE HOLOMORPHIC READING TEST's sealed search (`one_higgs_search.search`, `up_conj`
  False, `n_extra` 4): least squares (scipy trf, at most 400 evaluations) from the best 8 grid directions pairwise more
  than 0.05 rad apart and the best 4 of the record's 26 directions at τ (B1691's DR, B1731's ten F_a, B1700's h₂ and h₃,
  and their conjugates).
- **Stage B, only where stage A's minimum is below 150:** `search_v3.search3` — least squares from the best 16 grid
  directions pairwise more than 0.05 rad apart and the best 8 of the record's 26 directions, then basin hopping (rounds
  of 12 perturbations of the current best, three each at Fubini–Study angles 0.01, 0.02, 0.04 and 0.08 rad, each refined;
  another round while a round improves the best by more than 10⁻⁶, at most four). Seeds 7919 (2p + h) + a + 1 for row p,
  horocycle h, arm a. **The row's minimum is the lower of the two stages.**
- **Why two stages and why 150:** stage B costs about 4–8 s per row and arm on targets the model cannot reach; hopping
  matters only near a minimum. In every power test so far the sealed search's minimum on a planted row was below 70 (the
  largest, 66.6, on a noiseless near-conic row of THE HOLOMORPHIC READING TEST's C2, whose true minimum was 0). **Stated

  minimum is 150 or more while some direction lies below 21.67 would be missed; no measurement excludes it. C3′ below

## The calibration (a pre-registered price for choosing the row and the direction)

- **Targets, per arm.** Set 1: main's generator (`two_higgs_compare.py`'s `r_masses` and `r_ckm`, `random.Random(1743)`),
  the first 300 draws. Set 2 (lepton-tied): the first **10 000** draws, each target's lepton ratios set from its own down
  ratios by the arm's data factors. For arm G every random target is moved by the data's own ln(GUT/M_Z) in each of the
  nine (its hierarchy is taken to run as the data's; the factors, 0.763–1.000 at tan β 10, are small against the
  generator's spread of decades).
- **Row selection (in the scan, for every target):** on every eligible test row (the 264 lines other than (2/5, 0) and
  (2/5, 3/5), not the mirror set, not degenerate) the target's minimum over the calibration grid (every 17th direction of
  the search grid, 6 636 directions; `chi2_many`, the data's target in column 0); the target's single best row by that
  minimum.
- **The statistic, identical for the data and every target:** the declared two-stage search on that row (the grid stage
  computed once per distinct row; stage B's seed 104729 + 10 301 a + k for target k). **Random as good** = the fraction of a
  set at or below the data's statistic, with the upper end of its exact (Clopper–Pearson) 95% interval, two-sided as
  THE_BAR reads it. "The data's statistic below both bars" is read with χ²_six at the statistic's own direction.
- **Stated limit: the priced row.** The row is chosen by the coarse calibration grid, so for the data it can differ from
  the row the claim rests on (the H1′ row or the lowest H2′ row, found by the full search): in the third dry run arm G
  priced (2/5, 1/10) at 1 685.3 while the H1′ row, (2/5, 2/5) at 0.0625 + 2.1i on L₂, reached 1 120.2 and ranked third on
  the calibration grid. H3X and H3′X then price the statistic on the priced row, not the claim's row, and the first
  outcome bullet below fails if that statistic is not below both bars, whatever the H2′ row does: the limit can withhold
  a positive, never make one. Reported: the calibration-grid rank, among the eligible rows, of the H1′ row and of the
  lowest H2′ row, and whether the priced row is one of them.
- **When it runs:** for an arm only if that arm's H1′ holds (a row on the 264 lines below 21.67); otherwise that arm's H3
  cells are reported as not run and only the grid-level read-out (the data's best calibration-grid minimum and the
  fractions of each set at or below it, computed in the scan for every target) is given. The calibration prices
  positives; at five to six hours per arm it is spent only where there is one to price.
- **What it prices and what it does not.** It prices the choice of row (line among the 264, τ, horocycle) and of
  direction, through the identical procedure. It does not price the choice of A1, of c = 3 (set 2 prices the relation it
  builds in), of the frame, of the targets' paper and tan β, the 10% allowance, the record's directions as starts, or the
  grid.
- **THE_BAR's look count.** THE_BAR corrects by Bonferroni over every variant the claim's arc examined. This rule counts
  looks. **A look is one computation that compares a structure's values with the data's nine on rows or targets no
  earlier computation had compared it on.** The members of a menu compared together in one computation (several fixed
  directions or forms on the same rows) count as one look: the data were consulted once, and the choice among the
  members was made inside that computation (and priced there where it had a calibration). Dry runs against stand-ins,
  reproductions of a value already computed, and re-measurements on rows already computed (the referee's on TWO_HIGGS's
  rows; the 18.28 reproduction) are not looks. **The arc, m = 10:** every look at a structure that fits a Higgs direction
  per row — THE_NEXT_ATTACK's look (A2, A1 and the Standard Model's reading: three structures), THE READING TEST, THE
  HOLOMORPHIC READING TEST, main's S212 (H_d fixed, H_u free), main's P-357 tracking of A1 on the look's lines, B1748's
  disclosed A1 point, and this test's two arms. **The widest count, m = 21:** those ten and the eleven fixed-direction
  looks of the same two days' Higgs-sector work (ZERO_FREE_HOLOMORPHIC, TICK_LINE_HIGGS with its optimum read-out,
  RIGID_TAU_HOLOMORPHIC, TWO_HIGGS, the record pairs, the weight pencil, B1743, B1744, B1746, S213, S214); older value
  tests of the frame with main's reading are a different arc and not counted. H3′_BAR multiplies the upper end of set 2's
  interval by 10, H3″_BAR by 21, each graded at THE_BAR's p < 0.01: with 10 000 targets the first passes with up to three
  hits (0.88%; four give 1.02%), the second only with none (0.77%; one gives 1.17%). The widest grade is the same for any
  count from 18 to 27 (no hit passes, one does not), so splitting a computation's read-outs into separate looks (counting
  TICK_LINE_HIGGS's optimum read-out apart from its scan gives 22) does not change it; counting each member of a menu as a
  look would put the count above 27, where 10 000 targets cannot pass even with no hit.
- **Storage.** The per-row calibration-grid minima and directions (5 420 rows × 2 arms × 10 301 targets, about 0.9 GB)
  stay in the chunk folder (too large to commit; kept until the result is refereed). The per-target summary the
  calibration reads (`unif_calib_best.npz`: each target's best eligible row, its minimum and direction, the data's
  calibration-grid minimum on every row, and which rows are eligible) is committed before the calibration runs.
- **Cost:** the store about half an hour; C2 about 40 minutes on two workers; the scan about ten to twelve hours on two
  workers (about 12 s per row in the dry runs on one worker beside the other test's scan, before the calibration minima,
  about 1.85 s per row and arm), beyond the sandbox's two-hour limit on one job — the scan writes chunks of 200 rows
  atomically, refuses chunks made with other scripts, targets or store (a manifest of their sha256) and resumes from the
  completed chunks; the calibration, where it runs, about eleven hours of one core per arm (about 4 s per target,
  measured in the dry runs and by the referee), five to six hours on two workers — it writes each row's group of targets
  atomically under `chunks_unif/calib/`, refuses groups made with other scripts, store, rows or calibration summary (a
  manifest), resumes from the completed groups and runs one arm per call if asked (`--arm G` or `--arm Z`). Each resume of
  the scan or the calibration is disclosed with its log.
