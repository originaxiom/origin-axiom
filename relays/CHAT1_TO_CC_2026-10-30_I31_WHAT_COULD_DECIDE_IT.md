## 3. C10 cannot tell T from T̄ (the seat's argument, for main to verify)

**On a Riemannian four-manifold with the thread at its end.**
- **Hypotheses.**
  - (H1) The four-manifold is spin. A cone or cylinder over an orientable 3-manifold is.
  - (H2) The twist is unitary. The thread's bundle is the restriction of the moves' representation on T, whose image is
    finite, of order 96 (B1674).
  - (H3) The end condition used for ρ̄_T is the image, under the conjugation below, of the one used for ρ_T. APS and L²
    conditions are. C10's wording, "with the cusp's end condition", does not say which condition it means.
- **The argument** (from the first referee's derivation).
  - **The bulk.** In dimension 4 there is an antilinear map J on spinors that commutes with Spin(4) and with Clifford
    multiplication, and keeps the two chiral halves (the volume element has real eigenvalues). J ⊗ (conjugation on the
    bundle) carries the operator twisted by ρ to the one twisted by ρ̄, and D⁺ to D⁺. Under (H3) kernels and cokernels
    correspond, so ind(ρ̄_T) = ind(ρ_T). This holds under APS, under L², and under any end condition the conjugation
    carries. Curvature, of the bulk or of the bundle, does not change it.
  - **The 3d end.** There the same kind of J commutes with the Dirac operator, so η(ρ̄_T) = η(ρ_T) and
    h(ρ̄_T) = h(ρ_T), and APS's end term is even. GAP6 v1.12's parity half ("ind(V) ≠ ind(V̄) is possible only for
    n ≡ 2 (mod 4)") therefore survives a boundary. The end relaxes only its flatness half (GAP6 v1.11: "the index
    carries the boundary's spectral term").

**On C10's Lorentzian cone.**
- **The argument does not transfer.** In Lorentzian signature the spin group is SL(2,ℂ), and its two chiral halves are
  each other's conjugates, so every equivariant antilinear map swaps them. An end condition the conjugation carries
  would then reverse the index, not keep it (the second referee's reading).
- **Equality rests on the index formula** of Bär and Strohmaier (2019), with APS conditions on the Cauchy boundary. Its
  bulk term (Â·ch in four dimensions) and its Cauchy surfaces' η and h (in three) are each even under the conjugation.
- **(H4)** That such a formula holds on a slab of C10's cone. It is proved for a compact spacelike Cauchy boundary. W126's
  closed universes meet that hypothesis; a cusped thread's Cauchy surfaces do not. Over a thread it is an extension, not
  on record.

**Checks (scratch, not committed).**
- **The first referee's.**
  - The Spin-equivariant antilinear maps commute with Clifford multiplication for n = 3 and 7, and anticommute for n = 1
    and 5. They keep chirality for n = 4 and 8, and swap it for n = 2 and 6. These are the KO-dimension signs (Connes
    1995).
  - On five lens spaces S³/ℤ_p, computed from their spectra, η(χ_m) = η(χ_−m) to 5 × 10⁻¹³, with η ≠ 0.
- **The seat's.** Donnelly's equivariant formula (Donnelly 1978) on S^(2k−1)/ℤ_p gives η(χ_−m) = (−1)^k η(χ_m): even
  in 3d, odd in 5d, with non-zero values in both. On the four of the referee's lens spaces it was run on, its 3d values
  are exactly half the referee's spectral ones.

**What it means, if main agrees.**
- **C10 can give a count but never a difference between T and T̄.** That holds on a Riemannian four-manifold, and on
  C10's Lorentzian cone granted (H4). It is on I-26's side (P-330's "first count"). THE WEB SEAT'S ATTACK's 4.1 serves
  I-26, not 3.1.
- **The only Riemannian escape** is an end condition the conjugation does not carry: a fixed unit of end data, not
  conjugated with the bundle (FK10's kind). The asymmetry is then put in by hand.
- **The record's own four-dimensional ±3 is of that kind, as the seat reads it.** On M₁,₂ "the triplet's
  four-dimensional index is n times the fibre's index, where n is the cusp's units" (FK11 v1.37; I-34). The unit is
  FK10's, a tagged hypothesis. B1794 places it "at M₁,₂'s cusp, not at m004's torus".

**A caution on the 6d end term (the seat's own argument, first offered here; not claimed).**
- **The setting.** The cusped 6d frame X = Γ\PSL(2,ℂ): the APS index on a truncation of its end, for flat unitary
  twists, with a metric near the cut that the half-turn below keeps. Left-invariant metrics are kept.
- **The half-turn.** Left translation by h = diag(i, −i) is the half-turn about a vertical axis at the cusp. It
  normalises the cusp group and inverts it.
- **What it does on the end.**
  - It is an orientation-preserving isometry, and it keeps the end's spin structures.
  - The end's fundamental group is abelian, and the half-turn acts on it by inversion. So it pulls a flat unitary
    bundle on the end back to its conjugate.
- **The consequence.** With the 5d parity, η(V|end) = η(V̄|end) = −η(V|end), so η(V|end) = 0. The bulk term vanishes
  for flat V, so the APS index is −h/2 for V and for V̄ alike.
- **The second referee's checks (scratch, not committed).**
  - It confirmed each step analytically.
  - On T² × SU(2), with the metric induced by a left-invariant, Ad(K)-invariant metric on SL(2,ℂ), every block of the
    twisted 5d Dirac operator is symmetric to 1.1 × 10⁻¹³ at generic twists.
  - A control metric that the half-turn does not keep gives asymmetric blocks (about 10⁻³), while the 5d parity holds
    to 2 × 10⁻¹⁴.
- **Its limits.**
  - **L² is not covered.** On the complete frame the conjugation reverses the index, since in 6d it swaps chirality:
    ind_L²(V̄) = −ind_L²(V) for every unitary V. The half-turn forces a flat L² index to zero only when that index is
    the bulk term plus a term local to the end. Otherwise scattering data enter that the half-turn does not control.
  - **Non-unitary flat twists are not covered,** B1794's canonical bundles among them.
  - **The metric.** Under a metric the half-turn does not keep, ind(V) − ind(V̄) = −η(V|end), an integer that only
    spectral flow moves. That is not a topological difference between T and T̄.
- **If it holds,** the flat unitary end term cannot tell T from T̄ on any cusped member of the family, and the 6d APS
  end route needs a bundle that is not flat unitary. P-403's design would need it checked before its seal.

## 5. C11, read by B1674

- **The QCD condition binds whenever the moves are an exact symmetry, gauged or not.**
  - B1674 § 2: "If the moves are a symmetry of the fermions, they must act on left- and right-handed quarks alike."
  - Its table: free of the mixed anomaly with QCD "exactly when s_u = s_d = −s_Q: 16 of 64 assignments, the moves acting
    on left- and right-handed quarks by the same representation". Q carries one of T and T̄, and u^c and d^c the other.
    That freedom is at the level of the character.
- **I-31 is B1674's "every quark in T".** W101's s_f labels the left-handed fields Q, u^c, d^c, L, e^c and ν^c, with
  "s_f = +1 for T". With the SU(3)² weights Q 2, u^c 1 and d^c 1, and e = 9, I-31's exponent is
  n = 9(2 + 1 + 1) ≡ 12 (mod 24). It is not free of the QCD anomaly.
- **If the moves are an exact symmetry, I-31 needs the Dedekind counterterm.**
  - W101's A4, under exact moves: "Its price: either the weights cancel, or a τ-dependent θ term couples Re τ to GG̃, an
    axion-like input." That term is the counterterm θ(τ) = N·arg η(τ) (W101's H-θ).
  - It cancels I-31's anomaly only when the coupling weights satisfy k_u + k_d ≡ 2 (mod 4). B1674 writes this
    "K ≡ 2 (mod 4) with every quark in T"; its K is the coupling weights' sum, not C11's frame datum K.
  - θ̄ is then a free constant: "With the counterterm it is a free constant θ₀" (B1674 § 2).
- **Without the counterterm, the moves are anomalous with QCD.** They are then not an exact symmetry, and B1674's
  condition does not bind. θ̄ is tied to τ: "Without it, it is θ − π Re τ at large Im τ".
- **Gauging adds SU(2)² and gravity².**
  - SU(2)²: "never free: n ∈ {6, 12, 18}. This binds only if the moves are gauged".
  - Gravity²: "given SU(3)-freedom, free exactly when s_e = s_ν = −s_L (4 of 16); never without ν^c".
  - W101's A1: "No assignment is free in SU(3)², SU(2)² and gravity² at once."
  - So gauged moves obstruct every dictionary, the vectorial one included. That holds at the level of the character,
    with no counterterm for SU(2)² or gravity² on record. A Green–Schwarz-type term, as in the string literature B1674
    cites, is not examined.
- **P-290's gauge question.** W44's frame F_c is the one "where the scalar c is gauge" (B1689). Whether B1674's
  conditions bind on a gauged subgroup of the moves needs its characters restricted to that subgroup. That is not on
  record.
- **So C11 prices I-31, and neither earns nor excludes it.** If the moves are an exact symmetry, I-31 costs the
  counterterm. If they are not, the condition does not bind.
