## 4. The four routes considered, and what each must supply

**(a) An extremum.**
- **What it needs:** a functional of τ that the weave fixes, with a minimum at finite height.
- **[E] What the record has.**
  - Every dynamics on record has a symmetric vacuum or none. The exceptions have chosen inputs: B1655's minima, whose
    coefficients were chosen, and B1673's restricted minimum (ingredients map § 0 item 3). What is missing is
    coefficients the weave fixes (P-163).
  - B1704 finds genuine minima on the axis, at Im τ* from 3.08 to 2.13.
    - They come from a content tuned in integers: matter and Higgs, plus 89 to 95 complex scalars in ρ_Q, with a nearly
      cancelled cusp coefficient.
    - B1704 notes that a discrete content would fix τ with no continuous number.
    - No principle on record selects the content.
- **[J; arithmetic] The racetrack form.**
  - Take two terms with exponents E₁ > E₂ and opposite signs, V = A ε^E₁ − B ε^E₂ with A, B > 0. When B E₂ < A E₁ they
    have one minimum at finite height, at ε*^(E₁−E₂) = B E₂ / (A E₁).
  - When E₁ − E₂ = 1/10, the coefficients' ratio is raised to the tenth power.
  - The form is that of the racetrack (Krasnikov 1987; Casas, Lalak, Muñoz and Ross 1990). There the terms are in a
    superpotential and fix the dilaton, so only the stationarity equation is shared.
  - B1704's cusp coefficient belongs to the power-law term, which outweighs any ε^E term high in the cusp. So a
    racetrack in τ needs that coefficient to vanish, not merely to be negative. B1704's near-cancellation is the
    power-law analogue.
  - The exponents of such a functional need not be the table's.
  - Nothing on record supplies two forced coefficients.

**(b) Arithmetic.**
- **What it needs:** a rule from the programme's own arithmetic that picks τ among the CM points with no free integer.
- **[E; standard]** At a CM point of discriminant D < 0 with reduced form (a, b, c), τ = (−b + √D)/(2a). So
  Im τ = √|D|/(2a), and the scale becomes a pair of integers.
- **[E] The flux frame's list is infinite.**
  - In the holomorphic flux frame, LR-invariant fluxes force isolated quartic CM points in ℚ(√5, √−d), chosen by flux
    integers (B1804).
  - That is an infinite menu nothing on record selects (B1809 (S2)).
  - FR-FIB's values at CM points are not computed (B1809 § 7).
- **[E] A selection by symmetry is excluded.** A function unchanged by a group is greatest on a set the group carries to
  itself (B1881 § 1; B990's law). B1327's form of the wall is cited, not established (W-R1).
- [J] A selection must separate orbits by some invariant, and arithmetic is one kind.
- **[E] The record's finite lists, and what each trades away.**
  - **P-425** asked whether requiring CM by ℚ(√5, √−3) cuts B1804's fluxes to a finite set. Its first computation
    withdrew the reading "a finite set of points with no free integers". Requiring the field is itself a choice, so the
    integers are traded for the choice of field.
  - **This seat's relay of 2026-11-11 (§ 5)** named the rule "the torus is isomorphic to its quotient by the
    marked line".
    - The rule holds at ten points of the line's quotient, all on the arc |t|² = 125, where t = (η(τ)/η(5τ))⁶.
    - Its self-dual form holds only at the dual involution's two fixed points. They have discriminant −20, and the
      mirror fixes them.
    - The relay judged neither rule forced: the dual involution is not a move, so either rule would be a new postulate.
      Neither selects level 5 or the golden background.
  - **B1881 § 2** identifies the ten as the tori with an endomorphism of norm 5. Main predicted their discriminants
    (−4, −11, −16, −19 and −20) before its run.
