## 3. What the joint action requires

**Requirement 1: the allowed set is closed under composition.**
- The joint action of a set of histories is the action of everything the set generates: every composite, and the
  inverse of every history (GM5b).
- So under J, "all allowed histories together" already contains every composite of allowed histories. If the allowed
  set is not closed, J replaces it by the group it generates.
- As manifolds two allowed histories do not compose: they are two mapping tori, and the record's doors for joining
  such units do not select (B182, B185). As acts on the shared records they do compose. The weave rule takes the second
  view, and meaning P the first.
- Which conditions on record are closed:
  - **"The history keeps the frame's data"** (a line, a level): closed, since a stabiliser is a group. The design's
    "the frame's line extends over the history" is of this kind.
  - **"One departure"** (P-717's: one run that is not 1): not closed. Two such histories compose to one with two such
    runs, which still keeps the line (F6).
  - **"Least", under any count:** not closed. It names one member.
  - **"The lightest state is single"** (the design's second constraint): not known to be closed. Nothing on record
    says that a composite of two single-state histories has a single state.
- So, among the conditions on record, only "keeps the frame's data" is known to be closed. "One departure" and "least"
  are selections made afterwards; R10 already places "least" on a thread. "Single" can be part of what is allowed
  under J only if it is shown closed. Otherwise J sees only the group the single-state histories generate, and what
  that group is, is not known.

**Requirement 2: the data sit on the shared records.**
- B1733 gives each thread a line of its own. A thread is a cyclic word, so its line moves with the word's base point.
- The threads that each keep some line of order 5 are those of trace ≡ ±2 mod 5 (F4). Jointly they keep none. Even one
  thread read from its two base points does not: LR and RL each keep a line, and together they generate SL(2, 𝔽₅)
  (F11).
- So for the allowed histories to form a subweave, the line must be one mark on the shared records. All six eigenlines
  of order 5 are equivalent under the moves, so which one is marked is a convention.
- Being allowed is then a property of a based word: it keeps the mark, read from its own base point. Of the 330 cyclic
  words to 12 letters with trace ≡ ±2 mod 5, 23 have no rotation that keeps the mark (F10). Each of those is allowed
  only after conjugation by a move.
- The design's "the frame's line" is such a mark. "The thread's own line" is not, until it is placed on the records.

## 4. The allowed subweave at level 5 (exact; F1–F11 are in the two outputs)

**Words** (B1733's and P-718's, with P-725's "twins"). A line of order 5 is a point v of (ℤ/5)² up to sign. Its twin is
2v. An eigenline is a cyclic group of order 5; it holds one pair of twins.

- **The golden word's eigenline.** LR and RL are −(unipotent) mod 5. Each fixes exactly one eigenline: LR's is
  {(x, 3x)} and RL's is {(x, 2x)} (F1; P-718 names RL's, and so does the design, in its own letters).
- **Three nested stabilisers** (F2). Mod 5 the histories that keep a vector v, a line ±v, or an eigenline form groups of
  orders 5, 10 and 20, of index 24, 12 and 6 in SL(2, 𝔽₅). The middle one is ⟨LR⟩ mod 5 (F3; B1856 has it as ±⟨LR⟩).
  - Classically the middle one is conjugate to ±Γ₁(5), with quotient X₁(5), and the largest to Γ₀(5), with quotient
    X₀(5) (B1733, B1845).
  - The design's sign rule (the line extends as a line when W v = +v, and only as a pair χ ⊕ χ⁻¹ when W v = −v) is
    the step from the first group to the second.
- **The two readings of the level-5 premise allow different words, and generate the same group up to −I.**
  - On the record's definition (B1853) a history is a departure E on golden periods, and both premises are conditions
    on that one split: the line reading asks that E commute with LR mod 5, the charge reading that E ≡ ±I.
  - The sets of words differ. B1853's own counts are the witness: "643 of them rung-invisible", against "the 3 494
    line-invisible defects".
  - A plain check with its own convention agrees: of the 694 positive words to 12 letters that keep the line, 204 are a
    positive word ≡ ±I, possibly empty, followed by golden periods (F9).
  - Every history of either reading keeps ±v, since LR does.
  - In PSL(2, ℤ), four charge-invisible histories already generate the whole stabiliser of the line: L⁶R(LR)^k and
    LR⁶(LR)^k for k = 1, 2 generate a group of index 12 (F8). This uses the inverses of whole histories and no inverse
    letter.
  - As matrices the groups can differ by −I. The referee finds index 24 in SL(2, ℤ) for the charge reading with
    E ≡ +I only (B1862's correction), and 12 for the line reading and for E ≡ ±I. GENESIS calls −I "the negation of
    both records", so it is not nothing.
  - So under J the two readings give one subweave up to −I. Their difference is in which words are counted, and that
    count is a matter of each thread's word.
- **The joint action keeps both twins, never one.** Every history that keeps ±v keeps ±2v (F3). This is B1733's
  sentence at the weave's level: the twin bit is not read by J (P-724's "a CONVENTION"; P-725).
- **It fixes no τ.** No move of finite order other than ±I keeps a line of order 5: S exchanges the twins of each
  eigenline it fixes, and ST fixes no eigenline (F5; B1809's lemma). B1809 adds that a reflection geodesic can still be
  fixed; a reflection is not a move.
- **With the twins taken as one datum, the only points with a non-trivial stabiliser lie over i.** The group is then
  the eigenline's stabiliser, a larger one. It contains moves of order 2 and none of order 3. S fixes exactly two of
  the six eigenlines, LR's and RL's (F5). So there are two such points, both over τ = i. No single point is fixed by
  the whole group.
- **By level** (F7): for N from 2 to 13, S fixes an eigenline of order N at N = 2, 5, 10 and 13, and ST at N = 3, 7
  and 13. So where such points lie moves with the level.

## 5. The loop under the joint action

- **Read through what it fixes, the joint action closes the loop trivially at every level, and selects none.** The
  links are then: the data (a level and a mark) → the stabiliser of the data → what that stabiliser fixes. A stabiliser
  is always the stabiliser of what it fixes.
  - The step from a datum to what its stabiliser fixes only completes the datum. One twin returns both (F3). At N = 4
    a line's stabiliser fixes a further line (the first script's dropped check).
  - Among completed data it selects nothing: every level and every mark returns itself. So "exactly one" cannot hold
    for the joint action read this way. The claim page's tests T2 (uniqueness, over every level to a stated bound) and
    T4 (the second thread) would refute it.
  - This parallels the advisor seat's result that its selection rule "is empty" (not verified on main). Main says the
    like of the line premise on one history: "It does not by itself pick one."
- **So a back-edge needs a read-out that fixed data do not determine,** taken on the allowed subweave as a whole.
  - M is the one form THE_WEAVE's text admits for a selection on the whole: the minima of the functional the joint
    action defines. None is on record, and 4f7af67b6 found that no functional on record qualifies on τ. Whether a
    minimum would be a back-edge is not settled: it is a forward selection, and B1867 § 4 keeps the two apart ("a
    forward characterisation can also single out one object").
  - The design's read-out, the lightest state, is read on one history. Under J its second constraint leaves
    admissibility unless it is shown closed (§ 3). Then the design's back-edge would have to be restated on the
    subweave, and nothing on record does that.
- **The joint action does supply the space for such a read-out:** the allowed subweave's quotient, τ and the marked
  line together, X₁(5) at level 5. That is one of the joint doors 4f7af67b6 listed. No point of it is fixed by a move
  (§ 4).
- **Two limits stay.** A loop fixes an orbit, not a point (B1867 § 4; B990): here the orbit holds at least the twins.
  And one unit (the advisor seat's page): only dimensionless numbers can come out.

## 6. Verdict

- **"Together" has two meanings stated in THE_WEAVE's own text.** One is the joint action of the allowed histories, an
  allowed subweave. The other is the set of minima of the functional that action itself defines, and none is on
  record.
- **The joint action has two requirements.** The allowed set is closed under composition, and its data sit on the
  shared records. Among the conditions on record only "keeps the frame's data" is known to be closed.
- **At level 5 the allowed subweave is the stabiliser of the marked line up to sign.** The line reading and the charge
  reading allow different words and generate this same group in PSL(2, ℤ), that is, up to −I.
- **What its fixed data give:** both twins, the level of its data, and no τ. With the twins as one datum, its only
  points with a non-trivial stabiliser lie over i.
- **Read only through what it fixes, it selects nothing.** The loop closes trivially at every level.
- **The record's sentence on subweaves applies:** the allowed subweave is "a subweave the weave does not select: a
  selection, located". It is located at its data, the level and the mark. Whether only the background's own level
  counts for the premise is not ruled (the advisor seat's table; P-721); the ladder's rule takes every finite rung at
  once.
- **The other meanings** are not built (U), bring a chosen ingredient (Σ a counting rule, S an action), do not select
  (G), or read one history (P).
- **So the read-out the design called "not yet defined" is still undefined,** and by main's opinion R10 closes nothing
  until it is. What this relay defines is "together".
  The read-out would be a quantity taken on the allowed subweave as a whole that its fixed data do not determine.
  Nothing is selected here, and no value is read.
