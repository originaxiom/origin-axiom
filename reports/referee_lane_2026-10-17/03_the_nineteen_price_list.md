# The nineteen — a price list, and what the grammar says about two of them

An outside referee's answer, 2026-10-17, to the owner's challenge "derive the 19 parameters of the Standard Model".
The answer is no, with proofs; the first page of the price list below is the seed of arc B1668.

## The frame

No one has derived any of the nineteen parameters of the Standard Model from a parameter-free principle, and this
document does not pretend to. What exists, and what is listed below, are relations: each buys one parameter, or a
relation among several, at the price of stated inputs — a group, a particle content, a boundary condition, a texture, a
modulus. The nineteen are renormalised couplings at a scale, so any derivation must produce scale-dependent real
numbers; a discrete structure can only give algebraic relations at its symmetric points, and the data sit near none of
them. This is why the program's N5 fails at ω, why the parameter-free Yukawa sector of its triplet is closed by theorem
(N8 below), and why the strong CP angle in its framework comes out as a function of the modulus rather than zero (N10
below). The one framework that computes rather than fits — compactification with fluxes — pays in integers and a
modulus and lands in a landscape, which is the current best evidence that several of the nineteen are environmental
rather than derivable.

The standard is therefore not "derived or not" but the price. Every row below names the price, and the last section
proposes that producing this list in sealed form, for the whole literature, is the one problem on this subject the
program is uniquely equipped to solve.

The honest reading of the table: three of the nineteen have relations that predicted something before it was measured
(sin²θ_W with SUSY, m_H, Koide's m_τ), two have texture relations good to a per cent (θ_C, the Georgi–Jarlskog ratio),
one is set to zero in several frameworks (θ̄), and thirteen are inputs with hierarchies described but not explained.
Every success is priced, and the prices are not small.

## Two theorems about what the grammar says

Both hold on the ruled branch with the modular-flavour ansatz: the triplet T irreducible, Higgs a singlet of the
modular group, couplings vector-valued modular forms for ρ = χ_η²¹ ⊗ ρ_u. Both are statements about two of the
nineteen — the second-generation masses and the Cabibbo angle, and θ̄ — and both are negative.

**N8 (the half floor).** Every parameter-free Yukawa sector of T has m₂/m₃ ≥ ½ and a vanishing Cabibbo angle. There
are exactly two parameter-free sectors: L ∈ T, R ∈ T̄ at weight 0, where the unique form is the identity (three equal
masses), and L, R ∈ T at weight 1, where the unique form is

    Y_1(τ) ∝ ( 0     θ₄²   θ₃² )
             ( θ₄²   0     θ₂² )
             ( θ₃²   θ₂²   0   )

For any complex symmetric 3×3 matrix with zero diagonal, s₂ ≥ s₃/2: with Y⁺ the matrix of absolute values, ‖Yx‖ ≤
‖Y⁺|x|‖, so s₃ ≤ μ, the Perron root of Y⁺; Y⁺ is traceless with determinant 2|abc| ≥ 0, so its other eigenvalues are ≤ 0
and sum to −μ, giving μ² ≤ (2/3)tr(Y⁺²) = (4/3)N with N = |a|²+|b|²+|c|²; then s₂² ≥ N − s₃²/2 ≥ N/3 ≥ s₃²/4. Equality
needs |a| = |b| = |c|, which the theta constants satisfy exactly at ω (the spectrum there is (½, ½, 1); at i it is (φ⁻²,
φ⁻¹, 1)). With one form shared by the up and down sectors, M_u ∝ M_d and V = 1; with one Higgs in the fibre frame the
paper's own Theorem 12(4) gives V = 1 ⊕ U(2). Either way θ_C = 0. The data need m₂/m₃ ≤ 0.06 and θ_C = 13°. The first
free coefficients appear at weight 3 (one complex ratio per sector); a 400-start fit of that model to the eight quark
observables bottoms out at χ² ≈ 3.8·10³, because the forms carry one small parameter and give spectra (ε,1,1) or
(ε,ε,1), never 1 : ε : ε².

**N10 (the strong CP angle is the Dedekind phase of the modulus).** Let M_u, M_d be Yukawa forms of weights k_u, k_d.

1. If L, R ∈ T: det Y = η⁶·f with f ∈ M_{3k−3}(SL(2,ℤ)). Proof: det(ρ̄ Y ρ̄ᵀ) = (det ρ̄)² det Y, and (det ρ̄)²(t) =
   e^{−2πi·22/8} = i = χ_η(t)⁶; a character of Mp₂(ℤ) is fixed by its value on t, so det Y has weight 3k and character
   χ_η⁶, and det Y/η⁶ is a level-one form of weight 3k−3, holomorphic at the cusp because det Y expands in q^{1/4+n}
   like η⁶. Checked: f is constant at weight 1 (det Y₁ = 8η⁶) and proportional to E₆ at weight 3. Hence θ̄ − θ_QCD =
   arg(c_u c_d) + 12·arg η(τ) + arg f_u f_d. With CP-real coefficients the first term is 0 or π, and 12·arg η(x + iy) =
   πx − 12Σ arg(1 − qⁿ) equals πx to 10⁻⁵ at y = 2.5 (checked at x = 0.1, 0.25, 0.4). So θ̄ ≈ π·Re τ: the strong CP
   angle is the phase of the modulus, and θ̄ < 10⁻¹⁰ forces Re τ onto the imaginary axis to that precision, where every
   form is real and the CKM phase vanishes.
2. If L ∈ T, R ∈ T̄: det Y is itself a level-one form of weight 3k (the characters cancel), proportional to E₆ at
   weight 2 (checked at three τ). Its phase, arg E₆(x+iy) ≈ −504·e^{−2πy}·sin 2πx, is exponentially small toward the
   cusp and falls below 10⁻¹⁰ for Im τ ≳ 4.7. But the same q-expansion makes the two weight-2 forms diagonal and
   off-diagonal respectively with the off-diagonal part of order e^{−π Im τ/2}, so the masses degenerate pairwise and
   the mixing vanishes in the same limit: a 300-start fit gives χ² ≈ 2.9·10⁴ with τ free and 3.3·10⁴ with Im τ ≥ 4.5 (J
   = −10⁻¹⁰ there). The suppression of θ̄ and the loss of CP violation and hierarchy are one limit.

Conclusion: on the ruled branch the irreducible three cannot have θ̄ = 0 together with δ_CKM ≠ 0 at tree level. The
escape known in the literature — determinants of modular weight zero (Feruglio, Parriciatu, Strumia, Titov 2024;
Penedo and Petcov 2024) — requires the quark fields in reducible, one-dimensional representations with unequal weights.
In the program's own terms: the three and the strong CP solution are exclusive; keeping T irreducible is choosing a
strong CP problem of size π·Re τ.

Everything in the right-hand column is a fit or an identification. That is not a failure of effort; it is the content
of N8 and N10, and of the record's own ledger.

## The problem worth solving

The nineteen will not be derived by this structure, or by any structure without dynamics; the two theorems above and
the ledger say so with proofs. The problem the program is uniquely equipped to solve is one no one has solved: a sealed
price list of the field. Every claimed derivation or relation among the nineteen — Koide and its descendants,
Froggatt–Nielsen charge scans, the finite-group Cabibbo angle, modular fits near fixed points and near the cusp, the
weight-zero-determinant solution of strong CP, asymptotic-safety and criticality for m_H, GUT relations with and without
supersymmetry, quasi-fixed points, environmental (selection) windows — entered in one ledger with the program's own discipline: inputs
priced, identifications graded earned or unearned, status against data with propagated errors, and a preregistered
rule for what happens when the data move. The table above is the first page; the literature holds a few hundred rows.

That ledger would do for the parameter problem what the identification ledger did inside the program: show, as a
measurement rather than an opinion, how much of the Standard Model's numerical content has ever been bought, and at
what price. The current answer, from the first page, is: about five relations, none free, no hypothesis set buying more
than a handful, and thirteen parameters untouched. If a hypothesis ever buys ten at once, the ledger will be the first
place it shows.
