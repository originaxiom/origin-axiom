# Closure with a charged ideal does not establish compact gauge reality

Authored pre-execution argument. The preceding cyclic lemma applies if
the tested L1 is maximal isotropic and preserved by k. Here take k to
be the supplied gauge sl5 plus p tensored with its ten-dimensional
charged gauge module. The charged line p is peripheral-parallel. Its
self-wedge vanishes, so the added ideal is abelian; the sl5 action on
it is the usual nontrivial exterior-square representation (or its dual,
depending on the parent's label convention). Thus k has dimension 34.

## Full parent checks

The E8 branching has the two adjoints and the paired E and F = Lambda2 E
sectors. Bracketing p with E, F and F-dual uses wedge, volume and contraction
respectively. Bracketing with E-dual has two neutral components: the
traceless rank-one matrix p alpha minus alpha(p) I/5 in End0 E, and a
gauge adjoint component proportional to alpha(p). Relative nonzero
normalization constants do not change these direct-summand inclusions.
Bracketing with structure neutral forms is matrix action on p; bracketing
with gauge neutral forms keeps p and acts on the gauge tensor factor.
Those are all summands of the parent, and are checked separately.

The forced neutral classes W must be isotropic. A finite symplectic
space admits a Lagrangian containing any isotropic W. Construct it by
repeatedly choosing a vector in the current symplectic orthogonal
complement outside the current space. The dimension rises by one and
isotropy is preserved, ending at half-dimension. The program also checks
the matrix-action inclusion directly; it does not rely only on a
dimension count or cyclicity. The old neutral restriction image is a
comparator, not a mandatory boundary rule.

If all these checks hold, the cyclic lemma gives bracket closure of
(k,L1,ann k) in full boundary cohomology. This is an existence statement
with supplied choices, not a smooth field-space or higher-operation lift.
All cone degrees remain in the computation. H1 differences alone do
not license deletion of other modes.

## The precise compactness obstruction

In a compact real Lie algebra an invariant positive definite inner
product exists by averaging. For an abelian ideal I and x in I, ad(x)
maps the whole algebra into I and vanishes on I. Therefore ad(x)^2 = 0.
But ad(x) is skew-adjoint in that positive inner product, so ad(x) = 0.
Every abelian ideal in a compact real Lie algebra is consequently central.

In k the radical is exactly its ten-dimensional abelian module: its
quotient sl5 is semisimple and its action on the module is nontrivial.
If a compact real form existed, conjugation would preserve this
characteristic radical. Its real fixed part would be an abelian ideal
whose complexification is noncentral, contradicting the preceding fact.
So this k cannot itself be the complexification of compact real boundary
gauge transformations. This is not merely a complaint that a chosen
basis is not real; it is independent of basis or positive metric.

The presence of a complex nilpotent element alone would NOT prove this:
sl2(C) contains one and has compact real form su2. The invariant radical
is essential. The control implements both cases.

This restriction concerns a proposed GAUGE algebra. It is not R61's
fermion reality map, which combines Hodge duality and conjugation and
permits complex one-form lines. A holomorphic gauge formulation can use
larger complex transformations together with a moment-map equation and
metric response. This test supplies neither that physical formulation
nor a proof that none exists. No universal chirality kill follows.
