## Transformed coefficients must still represent the group

The sep16 function sends a degree-one column basis to the rows of M,
so at degree one its matrix is M transpose. On homogeneous polynomials
the same convention reverses composition: S(AB)=S(B)S(A). A column-action
convention instead obeys H(AB)=H(A)H(B). Noncommuting matrices discriminate
the two. If c=ab is a relation, assigning S(A), S(B), S(AB) need not satisfy
it. Checking only the original SL2 relators cannot validate the transformed
coefficient. Degree zero is a deliberate nondiscriminating control.
This independently checks B1471's instrument finding, not its 112 census.

## Relative chirality does not follow from the bulk Chern character alone

Let X be S3 minus the interiors of N disjoint balls, with boundary split
into nplus positive and nminus negative spheres, both nonempty. X has
H0=C, H2=C to the power N minus one and no other absolute cohomology.
The N oriented boundary spheres generate H2 with one relation, their
sum zero. Any proper subset of nminus boundary spheres injects with rank
nminus. The H0 boundary map has rank one. The long exact pair sequence
therefore gives

    b1(X,Sigma_minus)=nminus-1,
    b2(X,Sigma_minus)=nplus-1,
    b0=b3=0,
    b2-b1=nplus-nminus.

The producer checks this using literal quotient/inclusion matrices for

## The torus obstruction still holds in its proper scope

For oriented compact three-dimensional X, doubling and odd-dimensional
duality give 2 chi(X)=chi(boundary X). If the boundary is a union of tori,
chi(X)=0. If Sigma_minus is a union of whole tori or annuli, its Euler
characteristic is zero too, so the relative Euler count is zero. Other
partitions, defects and domains require their own calculation. A cover,
partial filling or CS change alone does not supply the absent signed
boundary Euler topology. This is a topology/domain statement, not a
universal ban on chiral physics on the commensurability architecture.
