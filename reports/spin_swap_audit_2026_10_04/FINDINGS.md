## What the new checks establish

### The proposed odd part can miss a real spin difference

L246 Phase 1b proposes R_s minus the complex conjugate of R_tau(s) on
the unit circle, with an all-zero result as a kill. For coherently normalized
reciprocal torsions related by coefficient conjugation, this subtraction
is identically zero. It tests the symmetry law, not whether the spin-labeled
sectors differ.

An explicit exact comparator gives

    T_plus  = t + 2i sqrt(3) + t^-1
    T_minus = t - 2i sqrt(3) + t^-1.

Their proposed conjugate subtraction vanishes on |t|=1, but their
spin-exchange contrast (T_plus-T_minus)/(2i) is 2 sqrt(3), nonzero.
Both have the same modulus there. The contrast is not a physical coupling,
mass or chirality index. Perturbing one partner makes the consistency
residual nonzero, so the control can discriminate a broken symmetry law.

These polynomials arise at the explicitly supplied character
u=(1+i sqrt(3))/2 in the published m003 family. We independently rebuilt
that family's group relation, both sign lifts and full Fox determinant
over rational functions. The derived raw function is t times the
published normalized function in our declared presentation convention.
This rederives known mathematics; we have not independently certified
the supplied character as the complete hyperbolic holonomy.
[Dunfield, Friedl and Jackson, sections 2.6 and 8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1108.3045v3#S8.SS1).

### An unfixed unit can create a spurious result

Multiplying both torsion representatives by the allowed unit t^2 changes
the raw proposed subtraction to (t^2-t^-2)T_plus on the unit circle.
At t=(3+4i)/5 it is exactly

    -96 sqrt(3)/25 + 288i/125,

although the underlying normalized pair is unchanged. The same defect
appears on a real reciprocal FIX comparator. A nonzero raw value is
therefore not sufficient evidence of spin-dependent symmetry breaking.

The repair is to state normalization and geometric parameter transport
first, and then distinguish a symmetry-consistency residual from a
spin-sensitive contrast. The existence of refined torsions is not denied.
Do not equate the spin label of the holonomy lift with auxiliary
Spin-c/Euler data used to remove torsion's ambiguity.

### Moving one spin structure need not move every one

An affine action on the spin torsor is x -> Ax+delta. A fixed spin structure
exists exactly when delta is in image(I-A). A nonzero delta merely says
the chosen origin moves. For a two-dimensional example, let A exchange
the coordinates and delta=(1,1): the origin moves, while (1,0) and (0,1)
are fixed. Pure nonzero translations supply the opposite control.

All 1,370 affine bijections in dimensions one through three were checked,
with 10,852 basepoint changes. The fixed-point criterion and its basepoint
invariance held. These are comparator actions, not computed isometries
of the geometric family. The actual action remains an obligation of
Phase 1a; multiple mirrors also require a simultaneous fixed-point check.

The frozen B1471 data contain two torsion-matching characters on each of
s955 and s957, and three on s960, at both recorded odd degrees. Thus that
scalar instrument does not uniquely identify the geometric action on
these members. This confirms why the matrix route is useful. It does not
prove any actual SWAP label wrong. B279's one-dimensional torsor argument
for the two spin structures of an S3 knot complement remains intact.

## The physical priority after this audit

The useful developments occupy different parts of one possible construction:

| Development | Useful content | Connection still to establish |
|---|---|---|
| Exact silver nonsplit coefficients | Matching asymmetric interior class differences survive independent replay | Their physical fermion domain and all charged sectors in the same model |
| Compact Dirichlet admission and proposed closed join | Conditional ways to admit nontrivial backgrounds; the closed join is not yet a reported completed run | A physically generated end law; smooth closed admission alone retains balanced charged counts |
| Spin-dependent torsion | A candidate way to distinguish decorated sectors; a published exact comparator | Actual affine mirror action, normalized observable, and its connection to the physical operator or effective action |
| Frozen mixed quartic | A diagnostic of a specified ansatz | The relaxed, normalized effective potential of an admitted common model, not a coefficient transported from another model |

The best-supported next physical task is to take one already verified
nonsplit candidate and derive its charged operator and end law from the
same bulk-plus-end action. Require the action's variations, finite-energy
conditions, allowed gauge transformations and fermion domain together.
Then compute the charged modes, continuum contribution and anomaly balance
on that same background. Keep every supplied input visible. A favorable
cohomology count is not authorization to choose its boundary condition.

For the new spin route, the immediate repair is smaller: determine the
complete affine action, normalize a genuinely spin-sensitive observable,
and only then ask whether it enters that physical model. Neither a spin
phase nor the silver count can be transferred between carriers without
an explicit construction. Changing A5 is also a separate model assumption,
not a consequence of a promising phase calculation.

This leaves a concrete integration target, not a claim that one missing
identity guarantees a TOE. The silver cusp-polarization test considered
before this refresh remains unexecuted; no result or new obstruction is
claimed for it. It remains a bounded diagnostic within the primary
physical-domain task, not an alternative physical completion.
