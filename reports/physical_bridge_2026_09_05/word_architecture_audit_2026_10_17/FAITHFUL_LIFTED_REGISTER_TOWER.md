## The actual generated lift group

Retain F2, rho, the four positive wordings, H=Aut+(F2), and

    E={(phi,g): rho phi=Ad_g rho}

from the preceding note. All group completions here are mathematical;
they do not declare inverse operations legal forward events.

Choose either SU(2) intertwiner for each of the four elementary maps.
Let K be the subgroup of E generated by those choices. Its projection
onto H is surjective. Its kernel is either trivial or {1,epsilon}. If
trivial, the inverse of K->H would give a group section of E->H,
contradicting the inversion-square proof of nonsplitting in the preceding
note. Thus epsilon belongs to K. Surjectivity and inclusion of the whole
kernel give K=E. Therefore the double cover is generated by these lifted
operations once their inverses and all four wordings are admitted; its
second sign is not merely appended as an unrelated symmetry.

This argument uses the enlarged group H. On a restricted alphabet it
must be redone. In particular one must not replace the group generated
by the original two lifted moves with the full inverse image of the

## A finite compatibility map

Every phi in H induces an automorphism theta(phi) of Q8 through rho.
This is well-defined because each elementary word map has an
intertwiner and hence preserves ker(rho), as do composites and inverses.
The quaternion normalizer is the finite group 2O and its conjugation
map

    q:2O -> Aut(Q8)

has kernel {+1,-1}. There are 24 possible automorphisms of Q8: six
choices for the image of i and four perpendicular choices for j, after
which k is fixed by multiplication. The two quarter rotations from
L and R generate these choices. In particular theta is surjective.

Thus the central extension is the following compatibility condition,
not the entire Cartesian product:

    E = H x_(Aut(Q8)) 2O
      = {(phi,g): theta(phi)=q(g)}.

Only compatible pairs compose, and multiplication preserves
compatibility. This is an explicit relation between the based act and
its coefficient registration. It does not derive a simultaneous spatial
join between carriers.

## A separating family without a preferred finite quotient

For every finite-index normal subgroup N of H contained in ker(theta),
define

    E_N = (H/N) x_(Aut(Q8)) 2O.

The map E->E_N sends (phi,g) to (phi N,g). It is surjective: a
compatible coset and g remain compatible for any representative phi.
Its kernel is {(phi,1):phi in N}, and

    |E_N|=2 |H/N|.

If N' is contained in N, the quotient E_N'->E_N preserves both the
based coset and the same lift g; compositions of refinements commute.
No sign is chosen afresh at a lower level. Intersections of the N give
a directed system, so no chosen prime or truncation depth defines the
whole register.

For completeness, separation of based automorphisms can be proved
without relying on a bounded census. The free group F2 is residually
finite. One elementary proof for a nontrivial reduced word w uses the
finite ball of radius |w| in its Cayley tree: each generator is a partial
bijection on the ball, extendible to a permutation of that finite set.
The word's successive suffixes remain in the ball, so its permutation
sends the identity to w and is not the identity. This gives a separating
finite quotient of F2.

If phi is a nonidentity automorphism, choose x with d=phi(x)x^-1
nontrivial. Choose a finite-index normal subgroup B of F2 omitting d.
Let m=[F2:B], and intersect all subgroups of index at most m. There
are finitely many: a subgroup of index r gives a transitive action on
r points, and F2 has only finitely many maps into the finite symmetric
group on those points. The intersection C is characteristic, of finite
index, and contained in B. The induced action on F2/C distinguishes
phi from the identity. Hence H is residually finite.

Intersecting any separating finite-index normal subgroup of H with
ker(theta) preserves finite index and separation. It follows that the
maps to E_N separate elements of E: different g are distinguished at
every level, and different phi at some finite level. This is a proof
for the whole family, not a claim that the first few levels are faithful.

## The family includes every finite quotient of the lifted group

The above family is cofinal among all finite quotients of E, not only
separating. To see this, let K be any finite-index normal subgroup of E.
The subgroup

    E0={(phi,1): phi in ker(theta)}

is normal and has finite index in E. Let N correspond to K intersect E0
under E0=ker(theta). Conjugation by (psi,g) sends (phi,1) to
(psi phi psi^-1,1); since E projects onto H, normality of K makes N
normal in H. It has finite index and lies in ker(theta). The kernel of
E->E_N is exactly K intersect E0 and is contained in K. Therefore
E->E/K factors through E_N.

Consequently the inverse limit is the full profinite completion of E,
equivalently the compatible pairs in

    completion(H) x_(Aut(Q8)) 2O.

This statement uses all finite-index normal N contained in ker(theta).
Replacing them by a particular Magnus or congruence subfamily requires
its own coverage argument. Gate 10's all-odd-prime Magnus family
separates based automorphisms; separation alone would not establish
cofinality among every finite quotient.

    Cov(I_x,I_y)=delta_(x,y)/|Q|-1/|Q|^2.

For a quotient pi:Q'->Q, summing the upstairs indicators over each
