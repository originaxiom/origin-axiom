## 5. Explicit remaining-scale discriminator

The curved parent explicitly keeps g6^2 positive and supplied, with
overall trace factors absorbable into that coupling. Distinguish the
choice of trace convention from a remaining coefficient after that
convention and measuring data have been fixed.

Let S0 be this fixed interacting classical action and consider

    S_alpha = alpha S0, alpha>0.

Hold fixed the field coordinates, bundles, geometry, gauge action,
classical domain and boundary behavior. Its first variation is
alpha times that of S0. Therefore its stationary set and homogeneous
linearized equations are unchanged. The positive kinetic form, Hessian
form and Green boundary pairing all acquire the SAME factor alpha.
The operator obtained by using that kinetic form to identify the
Hessian with an endomorphism is unchanged. Thus neither the classical
vacuum equation, its index nor a response defined as an unweighted
ratio of the same field traces selects alpha.

This does not say that alpha is always physical or always a convention.
Its status requires the complete observing/measuring sector and action
in units of hbar. For instance, in a fixed four-dimensional perturbative
mode description with hbar held fixed, write the local action as

    alpha [ (1/2) phi K phi + sum_(n>=3) V_n phi^n/n! ].

After canonical rescaling Phi=sqrt(alpha) phi, the n-field vertex is
alpha^(1-n/2) V_n. Cubic and quartic strengths consequently retain
alpha^(-1/2) and alpha^(-1), respectively, while the quadratic
operator is the same. This elementary accounting is conditional on
that physical mode description and its retained fields. It is not
a computed SM coupling or a new compactification claim. Simultaneously
changing hbar or all reference units may instead describe an equivalent
description; that equivalence has to preserve the complete relational
observables rather than be assumed from the vacuum equation alone.

The same issue is visible without quantization in Green's identity.
In the response note's unscaled canonical convention, Gamma0=a,
Gamma1=i b and M=i b/a. Scaling the bulk kinetic pairing by alpha
scales its Green boundary form by alpha. If the external source a and
its trace-space metric remain fixed, use Gamma1_alpha=alpha i b.
Then M_alpha=alpha M and

    M_alpha' = alpha gamma^dagger gamma,

where the dagger on the right is the old bulk pairing with that fixed
trace metric. This is exactly the scaled exterior norm. Alternatively
rescale both trace coordinates and their identification consistently;
then M can retain its old matrix. One cannot keep an unscaled measuring
source, discard the scaled Green pairing and claim that M has fixed
the action coefficient. The raw response ratio and the source-normalized
response are different conventions, whose maps must be retained.

Hence a derivation of the exterior norm FROM a fixed operator is a
positive dependence result, not a derivation of the entire kinetic
normalization FROM foundational principles. This argument is an
author source-level discriminator awaiting separate review; it does
not conclude that any wider genesis derivation of alpha is impossible.
