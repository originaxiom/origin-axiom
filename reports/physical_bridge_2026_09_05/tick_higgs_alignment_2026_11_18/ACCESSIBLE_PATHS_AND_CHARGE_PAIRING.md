# Which sectors can the same action connect?

19 November 2026. Author hand derivations for review. Use the curved family at 5a94ecf14 and the complete physical norms of the same classical parent. There are three distinct issues: existence of static vacua, existence of finite-cost off-shell paths, and stationarity of a joining field.

## End holonomy fixes possible nonlinear growth

Take any smooth zero-energy solution in the hypotheses of c1d2bde1e, replacing the identity end reference by an annular flat reference of counterclockwise holonomy P. Define s=(1,Q,R), T=∫Δ log||s||² as there. Positivity, the spin norm and the affine-coordinate area argument still give 0≤T≤2π. The general phase rule is

    exp(i T/2) is an eigenvalue of ρ(P),      ρ=1 ⊕ Ad ⊕ Ad.

To see this, work along each circle in a unitary frame transported by the reference. Vectors representing the globally single-valued normalized s then obey e(2π)=ρ(P)⁻¹e(0). The auxiliary extending spin frame is single valued on the circle. The covariant angular ray energy and squared difference-connection size are integrable over the cylinder. There is a sequence of circles on which their angular integrals both tend to zero. In the reference-parallel frame the ordinary ray length tends to zero and ∫Im<e,de> differs from ∫Im<e,De> by o(1). The latter tends to −T/2 by the circle-mean identity. Project the initial vector onto the moving ray and normalize, as in the identity proof. This lift varies by o(1) in norm and has o(1) phase integral. The remaining scalar phase thus gives e(2π)=exp(−iT/2)e(0)+o(1). Take a convergent subsequence of starting unit vectors in a fixed reference fibre. The claimed eigenvalue follows. No pointwise end expansion of the Higgs field was assumed. The rule is necessary. A weight of ρ(P) alone supplies no solution.

The curved family has T=4πα and E is a ρ(Pα) eigenvector of eigenvalue exp(2πiα). The flat vacuum with zero spin fields has T=0 in the very same end class. Thus the phase rule has distinct realized branches at the same P. T measures growth of the field in its auxiliary flat spin frame. It is neither a vacuum energy nor by itself a conserved spacetime charge.

## Different end classes require infinite kinetic cost in finite external time

Let τ range over an external interval of length D>0. Use a conformal cylinder coordinate t=log(1/|w|), θ at the internal puncture. Denote the conjugacy class of the circular gauge holonomy by [Pτ(t)]. Use the compact-group norm of the parent and the quotient distance on conjugacy classes. Variation of holonomy around the circle is the integral of parallel-transported F_{τθ}, plus a commutator from transporting the basepoint. This follows by differentiating the parallel transport ODE in τ; the commutator disappears in the quotient. Equivalently choose temporal gauge on the interval. Cauchy–Schwarz first around the circle and then on the interval gives

    dist([P0(t)],[PD(t)])² ≤ 2π D ∫_0^D ∫_0^{2π} ||F_{τθ}||² dθ dτ.

The parent kinetic term integrates the right side over all t with a constant positive weight because its internal one-form norm is conformally invariant. At each endpoint the conjugacy distance between its circular holonomy and its own flat reference is at most the angular L¹ difference-connection norm. Thus both endpoint errors are in L²(dt) by the affine domain assumption. If the two reference classes differ by distance d>0, the triangle inequality bounds the positive constant d by the sum of those two L² errors and the left-hand distance. The latter cannot also be L² on the half-line. This excludes a smooth finite-kinetic-cost interpolation in finite external time between distinct limiting classes in this domain. The argument keeps the full gauge compensator. It does not address infinite-time weak convergence with charge escaping down the end, or a modified end action.

## Different flat and curved vacua with the same end class have a finite-cost path

For every 0<α≤1/2 the punctured fibre also admits a flat zero-matter solution with circular holonomy conjugate to Pα. The two free handle generators can be sent to a half turn J about a transverse axis and a rotation D about the H axis through minus half the desired angle. Then JDJ⁻¹=D⁻¹ and [J,D]=D⁻². Reverse the sign of the chosen D angle if the boundary marking is inverted. This gives a flat connection in the compact SO(3). There is a second useful choice for small α. The exact B67 return family at bf72f2a1e attains every sufficiently small positive angle by continuity and the nonzero one-sided angle slope. Use its flat restriction to the fibre and match its peripheral conjugacy class to Pα. Only this flat member currently has the specific two-tick extension. The gauge symmetry also matches: for nonzero small curve parameter the two spin-one handle rotations have distinct real axes and angles different from zero and π. They have no common real invariant line. A common nonreal complex line and its conjugate would give a real invariant plane and hence a real invariant normal line. The unitary complex three is therefore irreducible. Its traceless commuting algebra and its invariant vectors vanish. In the E6 × SU(3) branching only e6 remains, just as on the curved background.

Identify the annular flat connection with the reference (α/2)H dθ. Work in the common E8 bundle, matching this frame with the curved solution. The circle gauge identification extends over the core in E8 since π1(E8)=0. No extension in a prescribed smaller real subgroup is needed for the path. Write B for the flat connection and a=Acurved−B. On the tail a lies in the H direction and is L². The physical spin field qhat is bounded and the flat endpoint has q=0. With an external real coordinate τ and 0≤h(τ)≤1 take

    A(τ)=B+h(τ)a,     q(τ)=h(τ)qcurved,     r=0,     Aτ=0.

The derivative kinetic cost per unit external transverse volume is K(h′)² with a finite positive K fixed by the parent norms of a and q. The moment residual is h(1−h)(i[a_x,a_y]−Ω[qcurved,qcurved†]) and the holomorphic residual is i h(1−h)[a_x+i a_y,qcurved]. These follow by subtracting the two endpoint vacuum equations before squaring. The [q,r] residual stays zero. Hence the potential is exactly C h²(1−h)². C is finite: the a commutator vanishes on the tail, physical curvature and moment are L², and the derivative residual is bounded in norm by a constant times |a| |qhat|. All fields are smooth on the compact core. C>0 for this path because qcurved is nonnormal and the tail moment term is nonzero. Both constants include the supplied trace/coupling normalization. They are properties of this interpolation and its chosen identification.

A smooth h changing from zero to one across a finite interval has finite action. On the entire external line the one-coordinate minimum of ∫(K(h′)²+C h²(1−h)²)dτ has h′=sqrt(C/K) h(1−h) and cost sqrt(KC)/3. This last expression is an upper bound on the infimum over unrestricted Euclidean/static trial fields with those asymptotics. Spatial joins use energy per transverse volume. Solving the full field variations, including the Gauss equation and variations normal to this path, is still required for a physical stationary wall. No universal tension or transition probability is inferred. A common finite-energy domain can therefore contain both a flat and a curved zero-energy endpoint even though changing the limiting end class has infinite cost on a finite external interval.

## Does the least-action profile on the trial path solve the field equations?

It fails the gauge equation on the open cusp for every 0<α≤1/2. This can be tested locally without assuming that a formal inverse of a gapless operator exists. Any full stationary solution of the displayed trial form with Aτ=0 must also be stationary under varying h alone. For a finite-energy heteroclinic this gives the same increasing logistic profile up to translation, by the first integral K(h′)²=C h²(1−h)². In particular h′′/h tends to C/K>0 at the flat end τ→−∞.

Dividing the gauge equation by h and taking the limit on any compact subannulus therefore requires d_B†d_B a=λ a with a constant λ>0. Equivalently put G=d_B a and take d_B of the equation. On the chosen tail the connection difference is Cartan-valued, so G=Fcurved and lies in the parallel H line of B. The scalar f=−κ(H,*G)/κ(H,H)=exp(u)/Ω must satisfy the positive scalar Laplacian equation Δ_g^+ f=λ f on the tail. This is also obtained directly from d d† on a top form via the Hodge star. Curl removes any longitudinal exact linear term.

In the distinguished coordinates θ,y with w_disk=exp(−y+iθ) and hyperbolic metric (dθ²+dy²)/y², the angular mean v(y) would solve −y² v′′=λ v. The affine coordinate used to specify u differs from w_disk by a nonvanishing smooth factor near zero. For 0<α<1/2 the actual f is positive and uniformly bounded above by a constant times y exp(−(1−2α)y) for large y. No nonzero solution of this Euler equation decays that fast: putting y=exp(z) gives a constant-coefficient ODE with characteristic polynomial s²−s+λ. The argument uses only the tail, independently of how the two endpoint gauges were joined in the core. Allowing the field profile on the surface to change with τ remains a genuinely different problem.

## A chirality test before computing masses

There is a charge-exchange symmetry for the endpoints valued entirely in the chosen principal SO(3), in the parent with its full adjoint and gauge-natural complete domains. Here is a group-level construction.

In the compact E8 Weyl group −I is realized by the product of reflections in eight orthogonal roots e1±e2, e3±e4, e5±e6, e7±e8. Choose a representative on a maximal torus adapted to E6 × SU(3) (conjugate maximal tori as needed). It inverts all torus weights, preserves the two root subsystems, and exchanges the E6 27 with its dual. On SU(3) its automorphism is the outer complex conjugation up to an inner change: both invert a diagonal maximal torus in a defining unitary basis, and a residual automorphism fixing this torus pointwise is conjugation by a diagonal matrix, as is seen by its phases on the two simple root spaces. Choose a defining basis in which the principal SO(3) is real and use that basis's diagonal maximal torus for this construction. Composing with an SU(3) inner element thus makes the action on SU(3) precisely complex conjugation while preserving its charge exchange on E6.

The resulting E8 element fixes the principal compact SO(3) pointwise. Its complex-linear adjoint extension fixes the entire complexified triple, including the nilpotent E. It commutes with the transition functions, background fields and spin compensators of the curved ansatz; it also fixes SO(3)-valued flat histories. This element acts on gauge coefficients without conjugating four-dimensional Weyl spin or interchanging spacetime chirality. Invariance of the parent and its domains identifies the charged quadratic operators in the two exchanged sectors (transport the transformation at both ends of any gauge identification). In particular every normalizable left 27 is paired with a normalizable left dual 27 and the spectral measures are exchanged. The October endpoint's paired spectrum was already found by explicit mode counts; the present argument does not need to assume a gap or carry those multiplicities to the interior.

This is an obstruction for SO(3)-valued backgrounds and domains preserving the exchange.
