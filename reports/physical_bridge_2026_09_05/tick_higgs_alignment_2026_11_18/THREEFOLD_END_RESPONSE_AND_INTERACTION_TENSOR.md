# The charged end response, deck action and actual E8 interaction tensor

20 November 2026. Follow-up to THREEFOLD_PERIPHERAL_RETURN_AND_DOUBLET_CHANNEL.md. We distinguish the periodic trace on the cover, its descent to the base, the kinetic response of the complete end, and an interaction tensor evaluated on the same trace spaces. ## Deck symmetry and the tensor on surviving coefficients

On the degree-three cover with the cubic lift let U be the two-dimensional longitude-unit plane in the B five. Normalize its h eigenvectors b₊,b₋ with eigenvalues +s,−s, where s has order three. On each 10 of A the surviving boundary line is b₊ on even components (uᶜ,eᶜ) and b₋ on odd components (Q). Acting by one base meridian including A parity multiplies every surviving 10 coefficient by s. In the dual five only the weak doublet survives, paired with b₊∧b₋. Its one-circuit phase including the weak sign is s². The conjugate weak five has the inverse phase s⁻²=s. On cover-periodic tensors these are induced deck characters, up to simultaneous inversion for the opposite deck convention. Torus differential forms with constant coefficients in the parallel frame acquire no extra geometric sign under the deck translation. Every individual charged periodic coefficient here is nontrivial under the deck group, so its invariant descent to the original exterior is zero. The global restriction image in degree one is equivariant and transforms on its visible line by the same character.

The trilinear tensor Tr(X[Y,Z]) of E8 can be tested before choosing internal wave functions. For two (10,5) coefficients the relevant bracket lands in (dual 5,Λ²5). Its A part wedges two alternating two-tensors, and its B part wedges two vectors. To verify the bracket is actually nonzero, use the weight basis for the two regular A₄ factors (roots have squared length two). For disjoint pairs of A indices the two weights of Λ² have inner product −4/5. Distinct defining B weights have inner product −1/5. Their joint E8 roots thus have inner product −1, so their sum is a root and their root-space bracket is nonzero. A common orthonormal basis can use the two h eigenvectors in B. Choosing A indices Q=e₁∧e₄ and uᶜ=e₂∧e₃ (first three are colors) leaves weak index 5 for contraction with the Higgs-like five. Their B coefficients wedge to b₋∧b₊≠0. Pairing against the available conjugate determinant line gives a nonzero Q uᶜ H tensor on the boundary coefficients. All three one-circuit phases multiply to s³=1.

For the 10·dual-five·dual-five tensor the B indices must contract to a five-volume. Both available dual-five coefficients lie on the same decomposable determinant b₊∧b₋. Its exterior square is zero; the down-type tensor restricted entirely to these spaces vanishes. Its deck character s(s²)²=s² also forbids an invariant on the base. In particular the color dᶜ coefficient required for a down Yukawa has no periodic boundary channel in the first place. The corresponding 10³·dual-five deck character is s² as well; this forbids that invariant on just these coefficients and says nothing about bulk mediation and other charges. A five/dual-five bilinear is deck invariant. The doublet multiplicity is one per conjugate gauge representation and the tensor distinguishes up- and down-type interactions. It neither identifies a physical Higgs field nor protects a massless Higgs pair. The tangential wedge of three one-forms on a two-torus vanishes: a seven-dimensional form-action overlap would need its genuine interior profiles, form degrees and normal components. The nonzero Lie-algebra tensor is an algebraic prerequisite only.

## Static response of the entire hyperbolic end

Use the ordinary unitary flat connection and the supplied three-dimensional cusp dr²+exp(−2r/ℓ)g_T for r≥0, with the flat reference torus at the cut. At r=0 each joint torus character has momenta given by its shifted reciprocal lattice. For the periodic scalar and form coefficients found above, zero frequency occurs exactly on the stated lines. At the cubic third return all longitude phases are cube roots and all meridian phases are signs. Every nonzero shifted momentum has length at least one sixth of the shortest nonzero reciprocal momentum of this marked cut (include 2π in the reciprocal lattice). In particular the triplet has a strictly positive angular gap. No absolute energy scale is inferred.

For a scalar with ordinary gradient energy the radial quadratic form for momentum length ν is proportional to ∫(e^(−2r/ℓ)|u′|²+ν²|u|²)dr. At zero external momentum the finite-energy, L² continuation of prescribed trace u₀ solves u″−2u′/ℓ−ν²e^(2r/ℓ)u=0. At ν>0 put x=ℓνe^(r/ℓ). The decaying solution is u/u₀=xK₁(x)/(x₀K₁(x₀)) with x₀=ℓν; substitution gives the modified Bessel equation of order one. The other solution xI₁(x) grows. The outward normal of the end at its inner cut is −∂r, so the boundary energy per unit trace is

    −u′(0)/u₀ = ν K₀(ℓν)/K₁(ℓν) > 0.

This uses (xK₁)′=−xK₀ and positivity for x>0. At ν=0 the finite solution is constant, L² since the cusp volume is finite, with response zero. The threefold weak periodic trace can therefore be continued down the end with zero scalar-gradient cost. Every color-partner trace costs positive end energy. On the compact core the minimum Dirichlet energy of the same nonzero doublet trace is strictly positive: equality would require a parallel extension, impossible since the fibre handles have no invariant in Λ²5_B. Existence and uniqueness for prescribed trace follow from the compact coercive Dirichlet problem as in DIHEDRAL_COLLAR_SYMBOL_AND_HARMONIC_DOMAIN.md. Adding end and core gives no massless global charged scalar for this pure gradient action. This computes an end response after all angular modes have been included. A tangential *one-form period* obeys the different divergent norm in the companion report.

For a conventional massless spin Dirac operator the warped product gives D=c(dr)(∂r−1/ℓ+e^(r/ℓ)D_T), with D_T self-adjoint and sign fixed by this normal form. Multiplication of a spinor by e^(−r/ℓ) moves its radial norm to unweighted dr. For a D_T eigenvalue λ the zero solution there is exp[−ℓλ(e^(r/ℓ)−1)] times the initial coefficient. Thus the exact L² end traces are the positive spectral subspace, and no λ=0 constant belongs to L². This is a trace space, not a count of global eigenstates. The charged ordinary spin channels here never reach λ=0 for the extending longitude spin. The normal form agrees with the warped-product analysis in [Bär, The Dirac Operator on Hyperbolic Manifolds of Finite Volume](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0010233). With a unitary flat gauge coefficient the usual parallel quaternionic conjugation on three-dimensional spinors and complex conjugation on the gauge coefficients carry D_V to D_(dual V) on the complete domain, so this ordinary operator alone supplies no unpaired charged excess. The four-field surface parent does not specify passage to this particular bulk spin operator.
