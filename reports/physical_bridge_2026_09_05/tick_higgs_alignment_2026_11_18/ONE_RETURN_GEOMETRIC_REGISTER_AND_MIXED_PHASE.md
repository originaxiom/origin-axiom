# One figure-eight return, its finite geometric register, and a mixed phase

19 November 2026. Author hand checks for main following 910a57ec4. The modular torus's integral *fibre* representation and the geometric representation of the entire figure-eight *knot group* are two maps from different domains. Here is a field-of-definition test and a positive construction for one full return. It does not derive a spacetime action.

## Which prime-field representations can see a nontrivial unipotent puncture?

In the B67 marking let M act by a→a²b, b→ab. Suppose a pair in PSL(2,F5) has a projective conjugator implementing the return (a conjugator in PGL(2,F5) suffices). Choose determinant-one lifts A,B. The equations may have central signs on the two returned handles. Changing lift signs changes this discrepancy by M−I on the two sign bits. This is invertible over F2 because det(M−I)=−1. Thus one can choose A,B with unsigned return equations in GL conjugacy. Their determinant-one trace triple (x,y,z) is fixed by (xz−y,z,xz²−x−yz). The first two equations give y=z=x/(x−1); x=1 is inconsistent.

Let κ=tr[A,B]. If the projective puncture is nontrivial unipotent, κ=±2. When κ=−2, substitution into the commutator trace identity gives

    0 = κ+2 = x²(x²−3x+3)/(x−1)².

The quadratic has discriminant −3≡2, a nonsquare modulo five. Hence x=y=z=0. Cayley–Hamilton gives A²=B²=(AB)²=−I, and [A,B]=(AB)²=−I. The projective puncture is trivial. For κ=+2 the pair is reducible over the algebraic closure. For completeness, in a basis diagonalizing a nonscalar semisimple A, κ−2 is minus the product of the two off-diagonal entries of B times the squared eigenvalue difference of A. In a single nontrivial Jordan block for A it is a nonzero constant times the square of the lower-left entry of B. A scalar A already commutes. These identities give a common invariant line when κ=2. A nontrivial unipotent commutator has a unique fixed line, so this common line and its stabilizer are preserved by the conjugator. In upper triangular form the diagonal ratios of A,B must be fixed by the exponent map M, since upper triangular conjugation preserves ratios. The equations α=α²β and β=αβ give α=β=1. Both projective handles are now upper unipotent and commute, again impossible.

Therefore a one-return PSL(2,F5) holonomy of this type cannot carry a nontrivial unipotent fibre puncture. The proof permits changing both handle signs and changing the handle pair. It does not address arbitrary representations with conjugators outside the stated projective linear group, or a multi-return cover. AGY-002's dihedral example has trivial longitude and passes this test.

## Recover one return from the banked knot holonomy

Use B285/B742's Riley presentation aW=Wb, W=b a⁻¹ b⁻¹ a. These a,b are *knot meridians*, different from the preceding fibre generators. Write U(z)=[[1,z],[0,1]], L(z)=U(z)^transpose, a=U(1), b=L(t), t²−t+1=0. Put δ=2t−1, δ²=−3. Use the preferred longitude word λ=b a⁻¹ b⁻¹ a a b⁻¹ a⁻¹ b in B1149's longitude_lock.py. Then

    W = [[1+t,t],[t²,0]],     Wrev = [[0,t],[t²,1+t]].
    aW = Wb,                  λ = W Wrev = −U(−2δ).

Each equality is direct matrix multiplication with t³=−1. In particular λ commutes with a and the cusp translation ratio is ±2δ with the orientation retained in the word. The prime 5 is inert here (B1068's i3-res-p5). Reduction gives k=F5[δ]/(δ²−2), a field of order 25. The nonzero longitude survives the full knot relation. In SL(2,k) its order is ten and λ⁵=−I.

In fact the image is all SL(2,k). The meridian and λ² supply U(1) and U(−4δ). Their powers and products supply U(z) for every z in k. Conjugating this full unipotent subgroup by b supplies another at b∞, which differs from ∞ since t≠0. A translation U(z) takes that finite point to zero. In the resulting basis the two root groups are all upper and all lower elementary matrices. They generate SL(2,k) by Gaussian elimination (a diagonal determinant-one matrix is also a product of elementary matrices). This proof uses the actual peripheral word, without a numerical group-size enumeration. The finite quotient works for the full knot group, hence also for its mapping-torus relation. It does not identify the specific W130 fibre pair with this different geometry.

There is a direct size test for a complex register. Restrict any nontrivial linear complex representation of SL(2,k) to U(k). Some nontrivial additive character must occur: if U(k) acts trivially, so does its opposite by conjugacy, so the entire group would act trivially. Diagonal elements diag(s,s⁻¹) send its argument to s² z. The orbit of any nontrivial character has size 12: the 12 nonzero squares are distinct multipliers, and multiplication by their nonzero difference is bijective on k. Each distinct character needs its own complex line. Therefore every nontrivial linear representation has dimension at least 12. The same bound holds for a representation factoring through PSL(2,k). A single three- or five-dimensional complex coefficient space cannot carry this whole linear geometric quotient nontrivially. This argument does not exclude an instrument with more degrees of freedom or a physical register of a different source.

## What couples the two five-valued coordinates?

As a conditional positive alternative apply the ordinary finite Weil construction to k with nontrivial additive character ψ(z)=ζ^Tr(z), ζ=exp(2πi/5), and use B1951's unipotent convention ρ(U(s))f(x)=ψ(2s x²)f(x). In characteristic five the coefficient 2 equals −1/2. Write x=u+δv with u,v∈F5. The basis vectors identify functions on k with functions on F5 × F5, dimension 25. Here Gal(k/F5) fixes the first axis and negates the second. The two coordinate labels and their phase transformations are explicit.

* The meridian a is diagonal with phase ζ^(4u²+3v²), since Tr(2x²)=4(u²+2v²) in F5. The coefficients belong to opposite square classes. The Fourier kernel on the prime-subfield Weyl element separates too, by Tr(xx′)=2uu′+4vv′. At the level of these generators the two factors therefore see opposite five-level quadratic classes. All scalar phases must still be transported in the chosen Weil convention.
* The geometric longitude is minus U(−2δ). Its central factor is joint parity (u,v)→(−u,−v); for the standard linear finite-field Weil normalization ρ(−I)f(x)=χ_k(−1)f(−x)=f(−x). Its remaining diagonal factor is **ζ^(3uv)** since Tr(−4δ x²)=−32uv≡3uv. Reversing longitude inverts that phase. This factor cannot be a function of u times a function of v: its multiplicative cross ratio on the unit square is ζ³≠1. On the uniform product amplitude its coefficient matrix is a Fourier matrix, with five orthogonal rows, giving Schmidt rank five for this stated tensor decomposition. The inner product and choice of input amplitude matter for this latter interpretation. The operator factorization test needs neither a measured state nor a fitted coupling.
* The two individual coordinate parities invert the mixed phase under conjugation; their product preserves it. Consequently fixed even/odd spaces of a *single* five-coordinate need not be invariant under the longitude. Joint parity survives. The full even/odd dimensions are 13 and 12 because (0,0) is the only fixed basis point. On the even space the two factor-parities occur as even-even (dimension 9) plus odd-odd (4); on the odd space they occur as even-odd and odd-even (6 each). A sign-only charge reading in 25 dimensions would have centralizer S(U(13)×U(12)), a different group from B1951's five-dimensional one.
* As a multiplicity check there are nine ordered pairs with uv=0, forming one fixed point and four parity pairs. For each nonzero value of uv there are four ordered pairs, in two parity pairs. Thus the eigenvalues of ρ(λ) consist of +1 five times, −1 four times, and each +ζ^j and −ζ^j twice for j=1,2,3,4. They give 25 states and ρ(λ)⁵=joint parity.

This derives a joint transformation from the geometric word **after** choosing the finite Weil representation and its additive character. It is kinematic composition. It supplies neither an interaction energy nor a measurement law. Changing δ to its negative exchanges the sign of the second coordinate. Other additive characters must be transported or priced; allowing ψ_c(z)=ζ^Tr(cz) for arbitrary c∈k× can change which coordinate transformations separate. The statements above use the displayed Frobenius-invariant trace character. The 12-dimensional lower bound is independent of that choice.

## Consequence for the physical join

There are now two distinct paths to the same central sign in this calculation. The modular fibre over F5 gives the SM-sized parity split and conditional puncture angles, but its flat register does not return once in the B67 marking. The geometric one-return quotient over F25 preserves the full peripheral word and entails a coupling of the two register coordinates in the stated representation. It supplies joint parity with a larger multiplicity. Neither path derives a stationary physical projection onto a five-space. The lower bound rules out obtaining such a space by a nontrivial invariant linear subrepresentation of the whole geometric quotient. A five-dimensional effective observable or reduced state would need its own covariance, source, norm and domain. This is a concrete requirement for an input reduction; no SM family multiplicity or physical coupling is read from the phase.
