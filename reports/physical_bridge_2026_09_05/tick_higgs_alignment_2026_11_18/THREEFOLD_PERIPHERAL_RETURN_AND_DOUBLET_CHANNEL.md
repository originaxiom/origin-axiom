## The peripheral meridian is essential

Use 068043746's marked SU(5)_B handles X,D, their return g and the A-side parity P. Put c=[X,D]. For the B67 automorphism φ, [X²D,XD]=X c X⁻¹. Thus the peripheral base generator is represented by h=X⁻¹g; using the matrix g alone would not generally preserve the longitude eigenspaces. On the cyclic degree-n mapping torus cover the peripheral torus is generated by c,hⁿ. It is connected because the peripheral meridian surjects onto the cyclic covering group. The corresponding A-side matrices are I,Pⁿ.

In the notation of 068043746 write A=p/q, B=q and C=p⁻² for the three longitude phases, with multiplicities 2,2,1. Use the general return u=s(1,p,1,q⁻¹,q⁻¹) so s⁵=q²/p. In the basis e_j, h maps j to −j−1 with these coefficients. The longitude sectors and the meridian action are therefore:

On the three-family walls p=q=v or q=1,p=v the longitude multiset is (1,1,v,v,v⁻²) with v=exp(−2πiα), 1/4<α<1/2. Its unit two-plane has meridian eigenvalues ±s on either wall. The determinant lift obeys s⁵=v on the first wall and s⁵=v⁻¹ on the second.

## First simultaneous opening in the family

On one circuit neither the 10 nor the dual five has a peripheral invariant in either gauge parity. For the 10 one would need s=±1 and for the dual five s²=±1, both inconsistent with the open angle interval. On two circuits the gauge parity becomes trivial; a 10 would require s²=1. In fact the connected bulk gauge group then enlarges to SU(5)_A because the fibre is unchanged and P²=I.

If the criterion is the earliest circuit admitting a 10 coefficient while maintaining the SM connected stabilizer, then n must be odd and sⁿ=±1. For n=3, s⁶=1. In the specified interval the only allowed phase v among sixth roots is exp(−2πi/3)=ω. On either wall this fixes s through its fifth power uniquely among sixth roots, and s has order three. On p=q=ω it is exactly s=ω² from 068043746. The conjugate family gives the reversed-angle counterpart. The angle and determinant lift have thus been recovered **conditional on earliest charged return**; the action has not been shown to favor that criterion. Multiplying this cubic lift by a nontrivial fifth root changes the first odd 10 opening to fifteen. The value three does not count generations here.

At n=3 the meridian acts on the B longitude-unit two-plane with eigenvalues +1 and −1, and on its determinant with −1. For (10,5) each gauge component finds one B line with matching parity. For (dual 5,Λ²5) the color component has A parity +1 and the weak component −1. Exactly one weak-doublet multiplet and zero color-antitriplet multiplets have a covariantly constant peripheral coefficient. The conjugate representation has the corresponding conjugate doublet. All statements count multiplicities per indicated gauge representation. The full bulk connected gauge group remains the SM group: the sheet cover retains the entire fibre holonomy and the return in A is P³=P. This test takes place on the cusp of the unfilled cover; B1802 concerns the lifted strong inversion on two fixed circles of even fillings and imposes different conditions.

For an ordinary flat torus spinor, a zero momentum requires the joint gauge character to cancel the spin signs. At n=3 the surviving boundary modes are vector-like.

## Does a boundary coefficient communicate with the core?

Take the compact core X_n of the cover, with oriented torus boundary T, and any one of these finite unitary coefficient systems V (one A parity with its B coefficients). If the invariant multiplicity is r, the torus has H⁰,H¹,H² dimensions r,2r,r. This follows by decomposing into its simultaneous unitary characters; a nontrivial character contracts the torus complex along a cycle on which its eigenvalue differs from one.

The image L of H¹(X_n;V) in H¹(T;V) has dimension exactly r. In the long exact sequence its image is the kernel of the connecting map. By Poincaré–Lefschetz duality the latter map is adjoint, up to sign, to restriction for V*. Unitarity identifies the ranks by conjugation. Hence 2r=dim L+rank(connecting)=2 dim L. This proves one extending period line per charged representation with r=1, and zero when r=0. It does not identify its slope or compute additional interior modes. In particular the n=3 weak doublet has one global degree-one cohomology line visible on the boundary; the corresponding color triplet has no visible line. On n=1 neither is visible.

The norm matters. On the complete three-dimensional hyperbolic cusp g=dr²+exp(−2r/ℓ)g_T a nonzero harmonic tangential one-form period has squared norm bounded below by a positive constant times ∫dr. The slice-area shrinkage cancels the inverse tangential metric. A closed representative keeps its period, and orthogonal projection to the parallel coefficient and torus harmonic part gives this lower bound. Thus the extending period is a possible source datum, not a normalizable free particle. The calculation supplies a charge-selective channel for a boundary law and a definite kinetic price.
