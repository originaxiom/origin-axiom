# Identical peripheral holonomy with different spaces of bulk extensions

19 November 2026. C8 hand continuation of f2bbb7c94 and c86f37bd6. Independent review pending. All cohomology here is ordinary with complex local coefficients on a compact core. Infinitesimal flat deformations, kinetic zero modes on a complete cusp, and stationary nonlinear field extensions have distinct analytic requirements.

## Match the boundary experiment before comparing the bulk

Take the orientation-preserving return M=LR on a once-punctured torus F. The peripheral torus has a puncture circle c and a base loop b around the return. Its b can differ from a chosen mapping-torus generator by a fibre path. Use the parity flat SU(3) history of the preceding report. Its fibre holonomies commute, so c has image I. Let B be the actual holonomy of b. It is a three-cycle decorated by unit diagonal signs, with B³=I and three distinct eigenvalues. In E8 use the same regular SU(3) embedding and trivial spectators as in the compact calculation.

There is an identity-fibre history with precisely this peripheral pair: send the whole fibre group to I and the base generator to B. The semidirect product relations then hold identically. The peripheral representations agree on (c,b)=(I,B), while an interior fibre generator has different trace in the two SU(3) representations. In E8 its adjoint traces are 248 and 24, also different. Keeping the base holonomy is essential to this control. Holding it fixed to the identity in the first history would silently perform a different boundary comparison.

Consequently a gauge-invariant energy depending solely on the isomorphism class of the flat boundary bundle and the same intrinsic boundary fields and geometry has equal value at the two matched inputs. This applies even to arbitrary Wilson functions on that torus. Bulk-induced boundary response can still differ through extendability into the interior. Additional extrinsic data, sources or distinct boundary states could also distinguish the histories and would have to be derived.

## Compute the images on first cohomology

Let V be any of the coefficient spaces in the table below, with its unitary action, and W=V^B. On the peripheral torus, c acts trivially. Diagonalize B and use the inverse of B-I on the eigenspaces of B with eigenvalue different from one as a contraction in the torus Koszul complex. This gives H¹(boundary,V)=W dc + W db, where dc and db denote the two cohomology generators. The holonomy of the coefficient bundle is the same on both sides.

For precision, split the fibre coefficient space orthogonally into V0 fixed by the whole fibre image and V1 its orthogonal complement. Both summands are return-invariant. In the identity-fibre case V0=V. In the parity case the complement is the sum of the three nontrivial Klein-four character spaces. Write W0=V0^B and W1=V1^B. The actual B differs from the mapping-torus lift by a fibre element, which acts trivially on V0.

* On V0 the ordinary fibre H¹ is V0 tensored with the two handle classes. The eigenvalues of M on the handles have modulus different from one. The coefficient return is unitary, so their tensor product has no fixed vector. The mapping-torus exact sequence leaves only the base coinvariants of V0. Their representatives may be chosen in W0, and restrict isomorphically to W0 db. Such base classes vanish on c.
* On V1 the fibre H⁰ vanishes. Restriction from fibre H¹ to the c circle is an isomorphism by the commutator calculation in the preceding note, applied with multiplicities. The restriction is equivariant with the actual peripheral transport B, since M preserves the orientation of c. Thus restriction from bundle H¹ to the c component is an isomorphism onto W1. The db component is some linear map into W1, depending on the peripheral framing and the representatives modulo coboundaries. More precisely its cohomology class is obtained by projection to W1, and for a fixed framing the resulting map is intrinsic. No slope value is assumed here.

Hence the matched identity-fibre image is W db. The parity image is W0 db plus a graph over W1 dc. Their intersection is exactly W0 db. Restriction has zero kernel for both histories. A boundary variation with nonzero c component in W1 therefore has bulk extensions on the parity side for a suitable db component. It has none in the identity-fibre infinitesimal flat problem.

A gauge change commuting with the boundary data acts simultaneously on the observations. Even if independent centralizer changes of frame are allowed on the two sides, the identity image is the entire pure base subspace, so the distinction by nonzero c component persists.

## Remaining action test

There is a direct necessary normalizability test at this point. Give the three-dimensional end its complete rank-two hyperbolic cusp metric dr²+exp(-2r)g_T for a fixed flat cross-section torus. In radial parallel gauge the slice restrictions of a smooth covariantly closed one-form represent the same flat cohomology class at every r: differentiating the tangential part gives the tangential covariant derivative of its radial coefficient. Project each slice onto its harmonic representative in the fixed metric g_T. The projection has a constant norm h, strictly positive for a nonzero peripheral class. In the volume integral the inverse metric on one tangential covector supplies exp(2r) and the area element exp(-2r). The contribution from every slice is therefore at least h² dr. The integral diverges if h is nonzero. Adding an exact variation cannot lower this harmonic part.

Since both restriction maps above are injective, no nonzero ordinary bundle H¹ class in either row of adjoint histories has a smooth closed representative of finite unweighted one-form norm on this complete three-dimensional cusp. This is a statement about the map from such representatives to ordinary cohomology. Its proof leaves open exact representatives, the kernel of a different physical operator, and nonnormalizable sources with a derived renormalized variational principle. The original supersymmetric action uses a two-dimensional internal surface. On its complete circle cusp the same estimate for a nonzero invariant peripheral period gives a lower bound proportional to exp(r) dr, since the cross-section has dimension one. Thus the three visible fibre residues likewise need a source interpretation if retained on that complete surface. Ordinary restriction alone does not produce a finite kinetic norm. The closed-torus one-loop cancellation in c86f37bd6 uses a different, compact domain.

A discriminating source would have to couple to a variation in the boundary direction just exhibited, or to another observable whose actual bulk response differs. Its existence at linear order in flat cohomology does not supply a source strength, a quadratic energy, an inverse kinetic operator or a preferred sign. At the common undisturbed boundary input both static energies can still agree. Setting c=I as a rigid boundary constraint removes the distinguishing tangent directions by assumption. Conversely enforcing an arbitrarily chosen nonzero peripheral displacement would introduce an external datum. The outstanding calculation is the physical boundary variational equation, with its kernel, allowed sources and induced quadratic form, at the same matched input. The intersection number gives a test for its zero-frequency flat sector if a map from that sector to its physical domain can be established.
