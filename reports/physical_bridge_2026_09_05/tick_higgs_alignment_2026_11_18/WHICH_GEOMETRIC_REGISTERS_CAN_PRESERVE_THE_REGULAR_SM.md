# Can a different embedding of the geometric register preserve the regular SM?

20 November 2026. C8 author hand test. Fix K=S(U(3)×U(2)) inside one regular SU(5)_A of compact E8. This is an explicit placement of B1951's group, not a deduction of that placement from B1951. For *any* homomorphism G=SL(2,F25)→E8 with image commuting pointwise with this K, the homomorphism must be trivial. This strengthens the specific centralizer computation at a0b1aba92 without treating that example as a classification of all G subgroups.

## Entire centralizer of the SM torus

The diagonal maximal torus T_A of SU(5)_A lies in K. It has rank four, since imposing determinant one on the 3+2 split still leaves all traceless diagonal generators. Its centralizer H in E8 is connected (centralizer of a torus in a connected compact group). Its Lie algebra is the full rank-eight Cartan plus precisely the root spaces vanishing on T_A.

Take the regular A4 with roots e_i−e_j on the first five coordinates of the usual E8 lattice. Orthogonality to its Cartan requires the first five coordinates of a root to be equal. Among integer roots ±e_i±e_j this leaves twelve with both indices in {6,7,8}. Among the even-parity half-sign roots the first five signs must agree, giving four choices of last-three signs for each common sign: eight further roots. The twelve span the last three coordinates; one of the half-sign roots raises the rank to four. The twenty norm-two roots are closed under reflection and form A4 (the simply laced rank-four types have no other twenty-root system). Thus the derived subgroup of H has Lie algebra su(5)_B, orthogonal to A. It is an actual simply connected SU(5) subgroup: its coroot lattice A4 has determinant five. The intersection of its real span with the integral E8 cocharacter lattice is an integral overlattice of finite index d; its determinant 5/d² is integral, forcing d=1. The exponential kernel on this Cartan is therefore exactly the coroot lattice. H modulo SU(5)_B is a torus.

Any group centralizing K centralizes T_A, so is contained in H. Perfectness makes the image of G in H/SU(5)_B trivial. The entire image must lie inside SU(5)_B and so would furnish a complex five-dimensional linear G representation. The elementary bound at 4d379d485 excludes even a nontrivial, nonfaithful one: on the root group U(F25) a nontrivial additive character has twelve distinct torus conjugates; if U acts trivially, its conjugates generate G and the representation is trivial. Thus preserving even T_A pointwise rules out using a nontrivial image of this whole G as holonomy. The argument needs neither the Spin(13) classification nor a guess about how its subgroup sits relative to colour. It holds also for conjugate regular SU(5) placements and still permits transformations acting on K externally rather than preserving each gauge generator as a parallel vector.

In a flat zero-matter background the parent vector mass form ∫|Dλ|² requires unbroken gauge vectors to be parallel. With positive spin kinetic terms and nonzero matter a gauge symmetry must additionally preserve that matter. In particular adding a matter condensate while retaining the same connection and its full G holonomy cannot restore those absent parallel vectors. A new stationary connection with different holonomy, an independent boundary gauge field or a measuring register outside the connection changes the question. Neither this proof nor B1951 classifies all embeddings of the abstract SM subgroup in all candidate theories.

## Recovery controls with smaller registers

H contains SU(5)_B, which commutes with the entire SU(5)_A by orthogonality of the two root systems (orthogonal norm-two roots have sums of norm four). The faithful level-five Weil 3+2 representation of SL(2,F5) has determinant one by perfectness and may be placed in this second SU(5). The five-sheet D5 permutation representation also has determinant one and fits there. Both choices preserve all of K as bulk gauge vectors. They also preserve the rest of SU(5)_A; obtaining *exactly* K needs a further breaking law. An abstract nonabelian finite group or the mere presence of gauge holonomy is therefore insufficient to infer this obstruction.

There is an explicit intermediate check involving no continuous angle. Given the D5 sheet homomorphism d of the knot group, let ε(d) be its reflection parity, P=diag(1,1,1,−1,−1) in SU(5)_A and σ the sheet representation in SU(5)_B. The combined knot connection with holonomy (P^ε(d),σ(d)) preserves exactly K **within the first SU(5) after meridional return**. It preserves all of the first SU(5) on the fibre, because ε vanishes on commutators. This is a legitimate flat gauge background in the commuting product, with its common central quotient understood. It does not leave exactly the SM in the full E8: at least two independent block-phase generators commuting with the decomposition 1+2+2 of the D5 sheets in SU(5)_B also survive. No dynamics selects the graph embedding or forces the meridional parity to act as P on a separate gauge factor. This example checks the distinction between containment, equality and physical selection.

The path retaining all registered information need not discard the geometric quotient. It can retain it in the joint record while keeping it out of the particular bulk connection required to leave K unbroken. That is an allowed assignment, not yet a derived one. The valuable next physical dependency is the interaction that specifies which transformations are gauge holonomy, which are transport of a measuring system, and how charged matter couples to both. Hand result, no scientific code or new measurement. For review, check the global cocharacter lattice, perfectness and the change between fibre and return in the graph control.
