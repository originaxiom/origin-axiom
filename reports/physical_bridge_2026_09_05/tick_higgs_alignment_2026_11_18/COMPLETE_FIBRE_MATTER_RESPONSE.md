# Can the parent's matter zero modes drive the puncture?

19 November 2026. Hand derivation for review, continuing bf72f2a1e. Work on a single complete, curvature −1, once-punctured elliptic fibre. Take the same two flat backgrounds with puncture holonomy I, the extending spin lines, and the entire adjoint of the compact parent. A flat returning history and a stationary solution on an internal surface are distinct requirements. Here we impose the physical surface action and its complete-domain finite kinetic norm. No base return is imposed on a varying field.

## Which fields can enter a smooth bifurcation?

Use the translation coordinate w=x+iy on the universal cover of the compactified elliptic curve, ds²=Ω²|dw|², with arbitrary period lattice. Write A0 for the flat background connection, d0 for its covariant derivative. A0 extends flatly over the puncture. An extending spin square root of dw has constant signs s on the two handles. These are transitions in a holomorphic square-root frame. Its physical metric varies with Ω. The common spin line for q and r is part of the specified action.

In the distinguished cusp disk coordinate z, the conformal factor is 1/(|z| log(1/|z|)). This follows directly by writing z=exp(2πiζ) in the half-plane cusp metric. A holomorphic spin field with coefficient f in a regular square-root frame has squared kinetic norm proportional to the integral of Ω|f|² in Euclidean area. Its Laurent term of degree n has radial norm proportional to the integral of r^(2n)/log(1/r) dr. Angular orthogonality excludes every negative Laurent power, including an essential singularity with even one nonzero negative coefficient. Conversely regular holomorphic fields have finite norm. The same argument for harmonic connection one-forms uses conformal invariance of their norm and the ordinary integral of r^(2n+1) dr. Thus their holomorphic and antiholomorphic pieces also extend. The graph cutoffs on the complete end approximate these zero modes because their kinetic norms are finite and the derivatives of the cutoffs are bounded and escape to infinity. See [Wolpert, equation (1)](https://www.math.umd.edu/~saw/preprints/Cusps.pdf).

In each case all flat transitions are finite signs. On the finite unramified torus cover killing the signs the extended holomorphic coefficients in the affine coordinate are constant. Descend them. In the harmonic gauge slice the real connection tangents are X dx+Y dy with X,Y Hermitian and in the fibre-invariant algebra V0. Each spin tangent is constant Q or R in the complex gauge character space of character s. Taking the adjoint preserves each sign. This identifies the complete-surface spin kernels, per spin field, with 248 or 0 for identity, and 80 or 56 for parity, according as s is trivial or nontrivial. The compact multiplicities were obtained in c86f37bd6. The new ingredient is control of the puncture in the physical norm. Gauge tangents or the existence of extra solutions at finite amplitude are separate questions.

In particular both matter fields occupy the SAME sign space. Their brackets [Q,R], [Q,Q†] and [R,R†] all belong to V0. On the parity fibre the 56 base-invariant directions distinguishing the two flat restriction images lie orthogonal to V0. A pair of these physical zero modes has zero direct quadratic moment source in any of those directions. Pairing modes from two different spin structures to get a different gauge character would change the field assignment.

## Complete infimum of the leading static interaction

Put K=X+iY and m=[Q,Q†]+[R,R†]. Define three positive metric quantities over a fundamental parallelogram with its puncture removed:

    L = ∫ dxdy,       I = ∫ Ω dxdy,       H = ∫ Ω² dxdy.

L is the area in the chosen affine coordinate, H the hyperbolic area and I the spin kinetic normalization for a unit coefficient. All are finite. Reference-frame rescaling must carry fields and all three factors together. Consider a smooth expansion with these first derivatives, order ε² connection correction a, and spin corrections q2,r2. Each coefficient is kept fixed when taking the Taylor coefficient. Minimize the order ε⁴ coefficient over these second variations. In the positive trace metric of the curved parent the answer is

    g6² inf V4 =
        (L²/I) ( ||[K,Q]||² + ||[K,R]||² )
      + 2 L ||[Q,R]||²
      + ||−i L [X,Y] + I m||²/(2 H).

Here V(ε)=ε⁴V4+o(ε⁴). This is a coefficient infimum about a fixed field tangent. An infimum of the exact nonlinear energy at fixed finite trace involves a different order of limits.

To verify the spin terms, the two linearized holomorphic residuals at second order are Dbar0 q2−i[K,Q] and Dbar0 r2−i[K,R], with squared norm weighted by Ω⁻¹ dxdy. The orthogonal complement of the range on compactly supported corrections consists of target vectors h with Ω⁻¹h antiholomorphic in the flat affine frames, by integration by parts against arbitrary test sections. The dual coefficient has finite norm with weight Ω. Changing from affine to a regular disk coordinate near the filled point introduces only bounded smooth nonzero frame and Jacobian factors. The same removable-singularity test and finite torus cover identify all such h as Ω times constants of the matching sign. Equivalently these are the spin dual zero modes. A constant source J projects to Ω(L/I)J with squared norm (L²/I)||J||². Compactly supported corrections suffice to approach the infimum by the Hilbert space range-closure identity. Enlarging to the parent's graph closure preserves the same orthogonality. The term [Q,R] has zero linearization at the origin and contributes its full norm 2L||[Q,R]||².

For the moment term the source as a two-form is (−i[X,Y]+Ωm) dx∧dy. Its second variation includes d0 a. The orthogonal complement to the range of d0 on compactly supported one-forms consists precisely of the parallel adjoint sections times hyperbolic volume: the adjoint equation is d0(*h)=0 in distributions. These sections have finite norm since H is finite, and they lie in V0. The integral of the source, in the parallel invariant frame, is −i L[X,Y]+I m. Dividing its squared norm by 2H gives the last term. No inverse or spectral gap is assumed. The three minimizations use independent second variations. All compactly supported trial corrections have finite residuals. The formula asserts an infimum without asserting a minimizing correction for every positive value.

As controls, Q=R=0 recovers the one-handle curvature quartic of the 8 November flat-period calculation after integrating the constant area form. For a constant Ω each complementary projection leaves its source unchanged in the corresponding physical frame. For nonconstant Ω the spin projection factor satisfies L²/I ≤ ∫Ω⁻¹ dxdy by weighted Cauchy–Schwarz. Simply freezing the higher modes would generically overestimate the cost.

## Classification of the zero set and exact recovery

Set Z=sqrt(L/(2I)) K. Vanishing of the first two nonnegative terms gives pairwise commutation of Z,Q,R. The last term vanishes precisely when the sum of their commutators with their respective adjoints is zero, using [K,K†]=−2i[X,Y]. These two conditions force joint unitary diagonalizability in any faithful compact-unitary matrix representation. Indeed commuting complex matrices have a common eigenvector and a common invariant flag by induction on the quotient. Choose an orthonormal basis adapted to the flag, giving simultaneous upper triangular matrices. The first diagonal entry of the sum of self-commutators is the sum of the squared strict first-row entries of all three matrices. They all vanish. Repeat on the lower block. This proof is a matrix criterion and requires no change of gauge group to the full unitary group.

Consequently each self-commutator vanishes, [X,Y]=0, and X,Y commute with Q,R and their adjoints. Conversely any such commuting normal triple in the indicated character spaces makes the coefficient zero. Every one of these tangents integrates exactly on the single fibre: keep the coefficient fields equal to εQ, εR and the connection equal to A0+ε(X dx+Y dy). Each term of all three parent vacuum residuals vanishes pointwise. Square integrability and graph cutoff approximation hold as above. The fields have bounded physical spin norms at the cusp (in fact their orthonormal amplitudes tend to zero), so the nonlinear products are admissible there. The handle backgrounds of these solutions remain flat with trivial puncture commutator. They preserve arbitrary continuous amplitudes subject to the commutation and sign conditions. Flat energy alone assigns no preference to a magnitude.

A further control concerns the earlier curved condensate. A covariantly parallel nonzero spin field has constant physical norm. Transport it around a horocycle towards the matched end. The extending spin connection has limiting unitary holonomy −1 (in the regular disk square-root frame the angular connection integral tends to ±π). With limiting gauge holonomy I, combined spin and adjoint transport tends to −I, which has no fixed vector. Thus the October parallel-spin condensate with a compensating gauge phase cannot simply be imported into this end. It remains a stationary solution in its own sector. The holomorphic fields above can exist at the matched end because their physical amplitudes decay and they need not be covariantly parallel.

## What this settles and what stays open

The nominal massless fields of either flat background have an interaction fixed by the supplied action and metric. Every zero of its relaxed leading interaction has an explicit zero-energy representative with no puncture response. A smooth branch departing with a positive coefficient cannot be a branch of zero-energy solutions with that tangent. This leaves room for higher-order bifurcations along the flat directions, changes of domain, finite-amplitude nonflat solutions, fermionic and quantum effects, and different generated carriers. A local rank or character argument would not address these. No finite-dimensional reduction of the full excited spectrum is assumed here.

The target still requires a physical selection mechanism and its stationary domains, followed by the fermion and gravitational problems.
