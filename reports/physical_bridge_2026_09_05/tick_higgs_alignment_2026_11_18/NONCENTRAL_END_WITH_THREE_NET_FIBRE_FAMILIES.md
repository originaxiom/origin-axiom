# A noncentral flat end with three net SU(5) families on one physical fibre

20 November 2026. Author hand proof for review, following 196734dc9. The aim is to see whether three complete net charged families are possible for the previously specified four-field action and ordinary complete cusp domain when the peripheral commutator can act differently on different components. The example deliberately chooses the end and then checks the marked return. It is a stationary classical fibre construction and a test of the earlier scalar-end restriction.

## Signed kernel formula with several peripheral phases

Let V be a finite unitary coefficient system on the punctured elliptic fibre, with phases γ_j∈[0,1) of the deck action around its positive coordinate circle. Assume no phase equals 1/2 in V or V*. The value zero is allowed. Keep the four independent left Weyl fields and extending compact spin line S of 196734dc9. Define β_j=γ_j for γ_j<1/2 and β_j=γ_j−1 for γ_j>1/2. The physical spin extension uses precisely these exponents by the ordinary cusp norm. Its dual is the physical extension for V*, because the balanced exponents change sign, including zero. By S²=K, Serre duality and genus-one Riemann–Roch, the difference in left zero modes of one spin slot in V and V* is −Σβ_j. This signed statement needs no assumption that an ordinary extension with unequal weights is semistable.

To check degrees with possibly distinct weights, pull back to the finite cover killing the representation, compactify it and use a global parallel determinant frame. Locally the determinant of the generators is z^(Σβ_j) times that frame. At each ramification point of order e the determinant has divisor contribution −eΣβ_j; there are deg(cover)/e such points. Dividing the resulting degree by the covering degree gives the formula downstairs. Only the determinant is asserted to split this way globally.

For a holomorphic one-form the local exponents of its coefficient in the dz frame start at γ_j−1 if γ_j>0 and zero otherwise. Call this coefficient extension F(V). Its ordinary dual is the bounded extension for V*, with exponents 1−γ_j when γ_j>0 and zero otherwise. Bounded holomorphic sections lift to global holomorphic functions valued in a fixed vector space on the compactified finite cover and hence are constant. Downstairs precisely the invariant constants survive. Thus h⁰(K⊗F(V))=Σ_(γ_j>0)(1−γ_j)+dim V^Γ by duality and Riemann–Roch. The connection field is a (0,1)-form and takes this count for V*. Its signed difference is Σ_(γ_j>0)(2γ_j−1), since the invariant dimensions agree. The scalar slot consists of parallel invariants and contributes zero to the difference. Summing the two spins and the form, the total excess is

    N(V) = #{j : 1/2 < γ_j < 1} − #{j : 0 < γ_j < 1/2}.

This includes the boundary and graph-domain tests for the actual fermion norms in the parent at Q=R=0. A phase 1 is counted as γ=0 and contributes zero. A phase −1 would require the separate threshold analysis. For dual systems the formula reverses sign. For the four scalar ends it reproduces all four rows at 196734dc9. Since the exterior square has dimension ten, an odd net dual-five in this nonthreshold calculation requires at least one unit eigenvalue there. With five generic distinct phases having no inverse pair and no thresholds, the exterior-square excess is even.

## An explicit flat SU(5) background

Put η=exp(2πi/30). On five orthonormal basis vectors numbered from 0 let X be the cyclic shift and take D proportional to diag(1,η,η¹⁰,η²⁰,η). Its determinant before rescaling is η³²; choose any fifth root of η^(−32) for the proportionality constant. The resulting pair is special unitary. The group is finite because its matrices are monomial with entries in a fixed finite root group. Its commutator [X,D] has diagonal exponents

    (1, −1, −9, −10, −11) modulo 30.

Indeed each diagonal entry of the commutator is the preceding entry of D divided by the current entry. All five commutator eigenvalues are distinct. A matrix commuting with it is diagonal; commuting with X then makes it scalar. Hence the fibre five is irreducible. In its exterior square only e₀∧e₁ is fixed by the puncture. X moves that line, so no two-form is invariant under the handles. The gauge algebra of the complete regular E8 branching on the fibre is therefore exactly su(5)_A by the same invariant calculation as before. The invariant adjoint of A is real; its two spin slots depend on S and do not affect complex family counts. Coefficients in (1,24) are SU(5)_A-singlets. The supplied classical equations are satisfied at flat curvature and Q=R=0 for every compact spin structure and every complex structure of the given once-punctured elliptic fibre.

For the B five, four angles lie in the negative half and one in the positive half of the balanced interval: N(5_B)=4−1=3. For the exterior square the pair (1,−1) has angle zero. The six pair sums of ±1 with −9,−10,−11 are negative and greater than −15. The other three sums −19,−20,−21 are positive after balancing mod 30. Thus N(Λ²5_B)=6−3=3. There are no threshold angles in either system. Independently retaining the separate slots gives

| SU(5)_A representation | form minus conjugate | first spin minus conjugate | second spin minus conjugate | scalar difference | total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 3−2=1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 3 |
| dual 5 | 5−4=1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 3 |

The 10 and dual five have opposite unit cubic gauge-anomaly coefficients, so the net charged fermion content passes the SU(5) cubic anomaly test. Restriction to the conventional SM subgroup gives three net copies of its charged-family pattern. The gauge vectors on this fibre still generate SU(5). Signed counts allow vector-like partners, and singlet neutrinos, their number, and their masses are not determined by this test.

## The return fails for this pair

The marked knot return requires simultaneously gXg⁻¹=X²D and gDg⁻¹=XD. In the second equation D has a repeated eigenvalue η times its overall scalar. In contrast XD is a single weighted five-cycle of nonzero weights. Its characteristic polynomial is t⁵−det(D)=t⁵−1, with five distinct roots. Therefore this pair cannot return by conjugation in B. Even allowing a full E8 conjugator gives no repair for the untwisted marked return: the two transformed words generate the same fibre subgroup (X=(X²D)(XD)⁻¹, D=X⁻¹(XD)). A conjugator must normalize the group's connected centralizer SU(5)_A and its commuting SU(5)_B. To see the latter, the A-invariants in 248 are exactly (1,24); B is the connected subgroup with that algebra. On B its automorphism is inner or inner composed with complex conjugation. Both preserve eigenvalue multiplicities in the defining five. Thus neither can implement D↦XD. A scalar ambiguity from comparing lifts also preserves multiplicities.

This identifies the missing joint condition in this family. One-return gauge configurations with the SM centralizer are already known. On the present fibre the actual parent fermion operator admits three net anomaly-cancelling charged families. The two results still belong to different histories. The order thirty, angles, gauge placement and end were chosen to test existence; no minimization or foundational law has selected them. The same finite-cover proof applies to nearby rational angles in the cyclic-shift construction preserving the inverse pair, the determinant and the indicated strict inequalities; a generic perturbation destroying the inverse pair removes the parity condition for an odd exterior-square excess. The next dependency is simultaneous satisfaction of the two return equations and these index constraints, before any return of physical wave functions, interactions, boundary response, or gravitational dynamics can be claimed.
