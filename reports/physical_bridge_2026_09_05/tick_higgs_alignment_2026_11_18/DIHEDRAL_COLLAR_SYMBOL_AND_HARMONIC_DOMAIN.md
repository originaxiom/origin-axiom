## Test on the principal symbol

Take a Riemannian product collar with inward coordinate t, the full complex four-dimensional spin bundle, and internal E=Λ*(C^5) of rank 32. Write Γ for four-dimensional chirality, F for exterior parity and χ=Γ⊗F. Use skew-adjoint Clifford multiplication c(v)^2=−|v|². Up to an orientation sign the tangential symbol of the normal form D=c(n)(∂t+B) is b(ξ)=−i c(n)c(ξ) for a nonzero tangential covector. It is Hermitian, squares to |ξ|², and commutes with Γ because it has two Clifford factors. The internal F commutes with this symbol for any ordinary twisted Dirac principal part.

At frozen tangential frequency ξ the decaying solutions of the homogeneous half-space equation have boundary data in the positive eigenspace of b(ξ). An elliptic half-rank trace condition must have zero intersection with these data. Within either rank-two Weyl space b(ξ) has eigenvalues ±|ξ| once each: b(η) for a nonzero perpendicular tangential η is invertible, preserves that Weyl space and interchanges the two eigenvalues. Thus on spin ⊗ E the allowed space χ=+1 has rank 64 and meets the decaying data in rank **32** for every ξ≠0. The other choice of sign has the same defect. Local flat holonomy, masses and other zeroth-order terms cannot change the principal symbol. Although c(n) interchanges χ eigenspaces, so the boundary Green pairing vanishes on the selected subspace, this alone does not imply ellipticity. Plane waves parallel to the seam times exp(−|ξ|t) give explicit frozen counterexamples at arbitrarily high frequency.

It follows that applying χ=+1 to the entire trace is neither the APS condition nor an elliptic local replacement for this operator. This tests an ordinary four-dimensional Euclidean collar reconstruction. A separately defined lower-dimensional boundary field, a doubled transmission problem, a higher-dimensional reflection with different Clifford action, or a different principal kinetic operator has its own symbol test. The F-U1 projector remains a well-defined finite algebraic projector on the stated tensor product.

## What survives in an elliptic construction

For a product Dirac operator take its self-adjoint tangential operator B. It anticommutes with c(n); in the massless flat model it commutes with Γ and F. Allow all negative B-modes and on ker B allow only K+=ker B ∩ ker(χ−I). The negative spectral subspace excludes the decaying high-frequency half, and modifying the finite-dimensional kernel of B leaves that symbol intact. Normal multiplication carries K+ unitarily to K−. Thus K+ is maximal isotropic for the zero-mode Green pairing, giving a self-adjoint elliptic full-Dirac realization on a compact manifold with such conditions on every boundary component. This uses an actual B and imports the sign choice on its harmonic space. An ordinary massive collar, another tangential spin structure or boundary interactions require recomputing B and its kernel. See [Bär and Ballmann, examples 3.20–3.21](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.3021) for the distinction between boundary chirality and spectral conditions.

For an explicit control use a flat boundary three-torus, with the dihedral meridian m acting by sheet reflection s, longitude l acting trivially and a third circle z with trivial gauge holonomy. This is a product control geometry, not an asserted derivation of an extra circle. Let ε_m, ε_l, ε_z be the three spin transport signs. The tangential kernel consists precisely of parallel data by B²=∇*∇ on this flat torus. If ε_l or ε_z equals −1 there are none. Otherwise the internal data must be in the s-eigenspace with eigenvalue ε_m. On each exterior parity half s has trace zero: evaluating det(1±s) gives zero in both cases since the defining reflection has both signs. Each exterior half consequently has eight internal vectors of each s-sign. For either ε_m the boundary harmonic space has dimension 4×16=64. Its χ-positive part has dimension 2×8+2×8=32. There are eight allowed *left internal components* and eight right internal components in this harmonic trace, each with its two-dimensional spinor factor. These are ranks of boundary traces. With s absent entirely the analogous left internal count would be sixteen for periodic spin. Losing a factor of two here would be easy if only the Clifford volume were transported around the meridian.

Even fixing the actual B at one end does not determine a global zero-mode dimension. On the finite flat product cylinder [0,L]×T³ with the above periodic spectator spins use complementary APS high-mode cuts at the two ends (the inward normals reverse). Each nonzero B-mode solves a scalar exponential normal ODE and cannot satisfy both ends. At tangential eigenvalue zero the solution is constant in t. Choose the same allowed harmonic subspace K+ in a common bulk frame at both ends. There are 32 full Dirac zero modes. Choose K− at the second end instead and there are zero. Each end remains elliptic and self-adjoint in both examples. Reversing the normal reverses the Green form but preserves the Lagrangian property. This shows precisely which global matching information a one-end count lacks; neither number is a generation prediction.

## Which of the torus spin signs extends from the knot exterior?

Consider the specific product filling X×S¹_z where X is a truncated figure-eight knot exterior with its preferred peripheral marking. Its longitude l bounds an oriented, once-punctured elliptic fibre. Every ordinary spin structure of X restricts to a spin surface on that fibre, using the oriented normal. The boundary circle has the bounding spin structure. One elementary check is to cap the puncture by a disk: spin structures on the filled torus restrict onto all spin structures of the fibre since H¹ of the filled and punctured surfaces agree over F2 and spin structures are torsors for these groups. Each restricting spin structure agrees at the seam with an extending disk spin. A tangent frame turning once around a disk lifts to minus one in Spin(2). Use product frames along the corner: the fibre's outward normal is the collar normal of X and its other normal lies tangent to the peripheral torus. Interchanging these frame directions with the constant orientation correction adds no winding. Thus in the parallel frame of the flat boundary torus the longitude sign is ε_l=−1. All spin structures of X×S¹ restrict this way since twisting by the extra spin character affects only z. This argument uses the preferred longitude and ordinary extending spin. A Spinᶜ twist or a different bundle needs its own combined transport.

Consequently the sheet Fock bundle (trivial longitude) has **zero tangential kernel** on this product boundary, for *every extending spin choice*. On a flat torus, for every Fourier momentum k one has k·l in (2Z+1)π, hence |k|≥π/length(l) even for a skew marking. The two meridional half-rosters computed above remain controls for abstract boundary spin data. Neither is the zero-mode roster on this particular filling. In the sheet model an APS condition on this boundary has no kernel on which the extra sign χ could make a choice. This gives no vanishing theorem for zero modes supported in the interior. For related results on untwisted hyperbolic spin cusps see [Bär's original study](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0010233).

The longitude is therefore −1 there, the meridian +1. With periodic meridian and spectator spin the line gives harmonic trace on this product filling.
