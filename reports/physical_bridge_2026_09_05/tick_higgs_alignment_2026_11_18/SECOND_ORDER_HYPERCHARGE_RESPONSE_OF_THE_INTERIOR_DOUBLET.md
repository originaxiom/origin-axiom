## A computable self-pairing of the single interior line

Let W denote the distance-one B exterior-square block in the preceding proof. It has no longitude invariant. Its full fibre H¹ is parabolic and carries the nondegenerate Hermitian form i times the oriented intersection pairing with coefficient conjugation. The return before multiplying by A parity has five distinct unit eigenvalues, with −1 simple. Distinct eigenspaces are pairwise Hermitian orthogonal: invariance multiplies a pairing by the product of one eigenvalue and the conjugate of the other. A vector spanning the −1 line therefore has **nonzero** self-pairing, since otherwise it would annihilate the entire space. The weak A sign is −1 and cancels this eigenvalue in the total marked history. This argument determines nonvanishing; it does not supply the four-dimensionally normalized magnitude.

Represent the resulting global H¹ class by a compactly supported closed one-form η valued in the coefficient system including the weak sign, with one fixed weak component.     I = i ∫_Σ ⟨η∧conjugate(η)⟩ ≠ 0.

The integral is independent of the fibre by Stokes and closedness, and a relative exact representative change preserves it. ## Test the flatness equation

For A(ε)=A₀+εa+ε²b+… the second-order curvature is d_A b + [a∧a]/2 in the skew-Hermitian convention. Project onto the covariantly constant generator Y of hypercharge using the positive invariant trace form. For two real gauge generators the identity ⟨Y,[u,v]⟩=⟨[Y,u],v⟩ reduces the projection to a degree-two form pairing opposite charges. Within one weak doublet both components have the same nonzero charge. Orthogonality of the weak basis removes cross terms between distinct components. With a fixed normalization and choice of orientation this yields

    ∫_Σ ⟨Y,[a∧a]/2⟩ = C I (|H₁|²+|H₂|²),   C real and nonzero.

The reality relation makes the two opposite-charge cross terms add after antisymmetrizing the one-forms. The Lie algebra only fixes C once a trace, Y, the complex field convention, and η have been normalized. This expression depends on the length of H; there is no nonzero weak direction where it vanishes.

The projected derivative is the ordinary exterior derivative of ⟨Y,b⟩. Stokes on the fibre now says that second-order flatness requires a nonzero compensating longitude period for b_Y whenever H≠0. On a compact core with the physical end connection fixed up to gauge, one may choose the relative boundary gauge (a and the pullback of b vanish there). The doublet is then obstructed already at order ε². Equivalently the obstruction paired with the base-circle one-form μ, normalized to period one, is the same integral in relative degree two on the three-manifold. The nonzero class is invariant under relative gauge transformations. This proof tests a deformation whose sole first-order physical excitation is this doublet. Added matter backgrounds, changed sources or a different first-order deformation require their own terms in the equation.

## A nonzero obstruction does not fix a four-dimensional quartic coupling

Let S denote this projected two-form source including its amplitude. On a fixed compact truncated core with relative b_Y, the pairing with μ gives

    ‖d b_Y+S‖²_core ≥ |∫_core μ∧S|² / ‖μ‖²_core > 0   (H≠0).

This is an order-|H|⁴ lower bound for the hypercharge part of the curvature energy at that cut. On the full exterior the same μ has a peripheral meridian period and its norm diverges. Indeed the infimum of the *isolated projected* residual can be zero without a finite-norm solution of d b_Y=−S. For a construction, ordinary H² of the compact knot exterior with trivial real coefficients is zero (for example by Alexander duality for a knot in S³). Choose an ordinary primitive of −S on the core. On a collar where S vanishes, adjust by an exact term so the primitive equals its constant harmonic period form β. The longitude component of β is nonzero by the preceding identity. Continue across a cusp segment of length L by multiplying β by a fixed smooth cutoff χ(r/L), initially one and finally zero. The residual dχ∧β has squared norm proportional to ‖β‖²/L. The kinetic norm of the continuation grows proportionally to L‖β‖². This reconciles the nonzero fixed-end obstruction with the absence of a uniform positive quartic bound on the infinite end from this projection alone.
