## Determine the full centralizer, including components

The image of Z=−I in G is the spin scalar of the D8 subgroup in E8. Its centralizer is the semispin group Spin(16)/{1,ω}, where ω is the volume lift acting trivially in the chosen half-spin representation. Indeed its fixed Lie algebra is so(16), and in the compact simply connected E8 a single-element centralizer is connected (the standard connected-centralizer theorem); the semispin subgroup has that dimension and the stated kernel. Any element centralizing G is therefore in this subgroup. Lift it to Spin(16). Its commutators with G take values in {1,ω} and define a character since that subgroup is central. G is perfect, so the lift commutes with G in Spin(16) itself.

In SO(16) the vector decomposes as the absolutely irreducible real thirteen plus three trivial directions. Orthogonal transformations commuting with G have form (ε I_13, A_3) with ε=±1 and det A_3=ε. Modulo −I_16 these are represented by (I_13,B_3) with B_3∈SO(3). Multiplying a lift by ω makes this replacement without changing its class in E8. The inverse image of this SO(3) in Spin(16) is precisely Spin(3), containing both lifts of every element. It embeds in E8 since ω is outside this block. Conversely it commutes with Spin(13). Thus the full centralizer of G here is **Spin(3)=SU(2)**, whose minus identity is the very same E8 element as Z. The computation excludes hidden disconnected components for this particular embedding.

There is no faithful commuting copy of D5 inside this centralizer: SU(2) has exactly one involution and D5 has five. Its adjoint SO(3) does contain the dihedral rotations. Treating that action on three spectator vectors as an E8 gauge transformation without lifting it would omit its action on the 128 spinor coefficients.

## A lift supplied by the marked knot group

Conjugating by a quarter-turn in the SU(2) circle (an element of square −1) fixes r and sends j to −j.
