## The implication that needs an additional premise

PROOF says that, conditional on the admitted global half-line and the
prior total-domain hypotheses, bounded symmetric perturbation gives the
"SAME graph-domain class and a selfadjoint twisted realization."

Admission of a global half-line alone is NOT enough to apply the
bounded-perturbation theorem to an ordinary spin operator and a canonical
Spin-c operator on one global Hilbert space. Their bundles have different
transition functions. A local frame equality D_can=D_spin+B does not
supply a global unitary identification. Any reading that treats that
sentence as a proved global domain identity is withdrawn.

What the exact computation establishes is narrower: in each specified
unitary frame, B=3i c1/(4ell) is bounded and selfadjoint, with norm
3/(4ell), and commutes with the prescribed puncture trace projector.
It preserves the LOCAL trace and graph-regularity class after the
actual line and frames have been included. It does not equate sections
of two different bundles or transport their kernels.

## What would justify a global realization

There are two distinct routes, neither silently supplied here.

1. Exhibit a common global Hilbert space and a unitary bundle map,
   including every transition and end condition. Only then can a global
   bounded-perturbation argument compare operators on the SAME domain.
   A nonflat canonical line is not removable by merely changing a label.
2. Build the canonical Spin-c problem on its OWN actual global bundle.
   Check that the total-domain localization, critical trace, adjoint
   regularity and complete-end cutoff arguments apply on that bundle.
   This can establish a selfadjoint realization without identifying it
   with the ordinary spin realization. It remains an authored analytic
   duty, with outside review owed.

The local projector identities apply to arbitrary coefficient planes,
not just sampled ranks. The live rank0 through6 controls check explicit
representatives; tensor-factor commutation supplies the structural
argument. The global invariance of a chosen plane under all transitions
must still be checked.

## Why this does not settle the index

Even a genuine bounded perturbation on a common Hilbert space can change
a chiral Fredholm index by changing an end threshold. The preregistered
whole-line control exhibits exactly that change. The supplied surface
has noncompact horizontal ends and a retained vertical kernel, so a
same-parent effective operator, complement coupling and asymptotic
threshold analysis are required before making any global family claim.

The canonical connection has nonzero curvature. Its local geometric
shift is not an integral cusp-flux relabeling or a selected gauge
generator. The full E8 adjoint and the conjugate multiplet remain.
No physical chirality result, three-generation result, global anomaly
cancellation or programme-wide exclusion follows from this comparison.
