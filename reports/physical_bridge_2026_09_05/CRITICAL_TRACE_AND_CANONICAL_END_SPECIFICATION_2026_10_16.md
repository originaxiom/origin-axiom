## The free cutoff search does not certify a canonical cut exclusion

Chat1's new standing correction and hostile-read M5 call B1660's
failure an a-fortiori negative for either canonical cutoff. There are
two separate duties before that stronger inference is licensed.

First, B1660's preregistration explicitly says its 160-start search
gives only an upper bound on the global minimum. The findings retain
that limit and describe rare recovery of some planted worlds. Its
producer uses trust-region least squares followed by Nelder-Mead;
the repeated-basin criterion tests the reported search, not exhaustive
optimization. Write F for its free-cutoff parameter set and C for an
actual subset imposing a canonical cutoff. For the same objective,

    inf_C loss >= inf_F loss,
    best_found_free >= inf_F loss.

These inequalities do not imply inf_C loss >= best_found_free. An
unsuccessful local search is not a lower-bound certificate. This does
not exhibit a missed fit or refute B1660's sealed scoreboard. Its
scoped result remains: no fit below threshold was found in the
reported calibrated multistart search. A proof of constrained
exclusion needs an appropriate lower bound; direct canonical-cut
searches would provide additional, still scoped numerical evidence.

Second, the objective and model must actually be the same before set
inclusion applies. B1660's Ms uses sqrt(2 pi L+c_s) times each twisted
channel residue. Its imported cA subtracts the logarithmic divergence
and restores the small-disc correction to obtain the regularized
finite part. B1667 instead computes the unshifted three profiles'
finite norms outside the actual horoball. Its canonical_lift is not
a five-target fit of B1660's full twisted four-channel matter system.
Sharing a leading L and including it in an optimizer's range does
not establish equality of those norms, fields or objectives. That
same-model map needs to be shown, not silently assumed.

The older flat-regulator argument is therefore not restored, and
the newer canonical geometry is not rejected. Under either tested
cutting rule geometry determines its conditional cutoff. The record
does not thereby show that these two rules exhaust all physically
admissible ends or that one is derived from the principle.
