## Exact reduction of both nonlinear residuals

For real radial f let g=exp(fT) and D=gH g inverse. Direct multiplication
gives D=H cosh(2f)-J sinh(2f), and

    C_r=-f' T,                  C_link=(a/2)wD,
    F_C=0,
    I=[-2f''-4f'/r+lambda sinh(4f)/(2r squared)]T.

Here I=div(C+C dagger)+g inverse contraction of [C_i,C_j dagger],
and the supplied potential is integral(2 norm(F) squared+norm(I) squared/2)
times the positive coupling factor. Direct coordinate differentiation
checks flatness; it is not inferred from only the tangential equation.

Thus BOTH residuals vanish precisely when

    r squared f''+2r f' = (lambda/4)sinh(4f).                 (1)

The compact comparator g=exp(i fT) is instead unitary and preserves
both residuals for arbitrary smooth real f; its radial Higgs is zero.
For the positive g, Psi_r=-f'T, so tr(Psi_r squared)=2(f') squared.
A nonconstant solution of (1) is not compact-gauge-equivalent to C0.
This is local bosonic inequivalence, not a global zero-mode theorem.

## Existence with an explicit contraction

Set L=r squared partial_r squared+2r partial_r-lambda and
N(f)=(lambda/4)(sinh(4f)-4f). Its inverse on remainders smaller than
r^eta, without the divergent r^(-eta-1) homogeneous term, is

    K n(r) = 1/(2eta+1) [
      r^eta integral_0^r s^(-eta-1)n(s) ds
      -r^(-eta-1) integral_0^r s^eta n(s) ds ].

For 0<s<r its kernel is positive. For any p>eta,

    K(r^p)=r^p/[(p-eta)(p+eta+1)].

Solve f=b r^eta+K N(f) in the weighted continuous norm
sup |f(r)|/r^eta on (0,R], with continuous weighted extension at zero.
For b!=0 choose delta=2|b|R^eta<=1/8 and the ball norm(f)<=2|b|.
The elementary Taylor bounds give

    |N(f)| <= (8lambda/3)cosh(4delta)|f| cubed,
    |N'(f)| <= 8lambda cosh(4delta)|f| squared.

Since (eta+1)/(4eta+1)<=1 and cosh(1/2)<=4/3<2, the map's correction
has norm at most |b|/12 and its Lipschitz constant is at most 1/8.
It maps the ball into itself and is a contraction. Its unique fixed
point has f=b r^eta+O(r^(3eta)); differentiating the integral equation
gives the corresponding first two weighted derivative bounds and (1)
for r>0. For b=0 the zero solution is the small solution.

Substituting the first remainder into N and using the monomial inverse
improves the expansion, with derivatives, to

    f=b r^eta+
      [4(eta+1)/(3(4eta+1))] b cubed r^(3eta)+O(r^(5eta)).     (2)

This is an existence proof on a sufficiently small collar for each fixed
eta, not a global shooting computation or a finite-cutoff approximation.
The transformation tends to one at the tip; no radial logarithmic
oscillation from R63 is being used.

## Which fluctuation space admits the family

Let u=C-C0, U=cosh(2f)-1, V=sinh(2f). Direct metric contraction yields

    r squared norm(u) squared =
        2r squared (f') squared+(lambda/2)(U squared+V squared),

    r squared norm(u wedge u) squared =
        2lambda (f') squared (U squared+V squared).          (3)

For b!=0 the leading coefficients are respectively
2eta(2eta+1)b squared r^(2eta) and
8lambda eta squared b fourth r^(4eta-2).
Therefore u is locally L2 for every eta>0, but u is L4 exactly
when eta>1/4; equality has a logarithmic divergence.
Since F(C)=0, d_C0 u=-u wedge u. Thus the separate linear curvature
norm also diverges for eta<=1/4, even though the FULL residual is zero.

The direct background adjoint is

    d_C0 dagger u=(f''+2f'/r-lambda sinh(2f)/(2r squared))T
                 =lambda V U T/(2r squared)

on solutions of (1). It is Hermitian, so the R46 compact gauge-fixing
component vanishes for this PARTICULAR ansatz, while the moment
linearization has cubic order. For eta>1/4, both linear differential
and adjoint residuals are L2. Hence the family
lies in the local maximal graph-plus-L4 space used as a sufficient
nonlinear space in R46. This does not import R46's completeness or
its unique global operator domain onto an incomplete cone.

The unitary comparator also has bounded g and weighted first derivative
O(r^(eta-1)). For eta>1/4 the derivative is L4, so multiplication by
Ad(g) preserves this local graph-plus-L4 class via Holder. For smaller
eta neither this sufficient space nor gauge admission is silently enlarged.
Zero full residual alone does not select a physical variational domain.

The straight additive path C0+b u_linear has a nonzero quadratic
curvature term; its quartic action has the eta>1/4 threshold.
An intermediate EXACTLY FLAT path exp(b r^eta T) C0 has instead

    r squared norm(I) squared/2 =
      lambda squared [sinh(4b r^eta)-4b r^eta] squared/(4r squared),

whose leading coefficient is (256/9)lambda squared b sixth r^(6eta-2).
It has finite residual action precisely for eta>1/6. The relaxed solution
(1) has zero full residual action for every eta>0. These are DIFFERENT
paths; a straight-line divergence cannot kill the curved solution,
and the curved solution cannot override a fixed domain restriction.

## The outer boundary cannot be erased

For xi=r^eta T, direct integration gives

    integral_(0,R) norm(d_C0 xi) squared =
      2eta R^(2eta+1) = [r squared tr(xi dagger xi')]_(0,R).

The apex contribution vanishes. The nonzero norm is supported by the
OUTER datum, not a hidden contradiction with positivity of a global
self-adjoint Laplacian. Cutting the primitive off there changes the
equation and cannot preserve its zero residual.

Similarly, multiplying (1) by f gives the exact nonlinear identity

    [r squared f f']_(0,R) =
      integral_0^R [r squared (f') squared+(lambda/4)f sinh(4f)] dr.

Both integrands are nonnegative and the apex term is zero. Imposing
either f(R)=0 or f'(R)=0 forces f=0 within this radial ansatz.
For b>0 the positive-kernel construction gives f>0 and the equation
(r squared f')'=(lambda/4)sinh(4f) gives f'>0; b<0 is its negative.
Thus the constructed family necessarily supplies changed outer data.

Global matching, physical end law, full fermionic domain and normalized
four-dimensional dynamics remain necessary. The local nonabelian
continuation is a positive candidate for that task, not a selected
background, chiral spectrum, complete gauge quotient or TOE.
