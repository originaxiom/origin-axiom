## M1. The exact-axis test precedes the size of the quartic

For real x,y, supplied a,c,d and fixed independent sources,

    V=a(x^2+y^2)+c(x^4+y^4)+d*x^2*y^2-J1*x-J2*y,
    partial_y V(x,0)=-J2, partial_x V(0,y)=-J1.

Thus a smooth unconstrained stationary point on the x axis requires
J2=0, regardless of d. The same holds with axes exchanged. No finite
d>2c can return a generic nonzero transverse source EXACTLY to an axis
in this model. It can give small transverse displacement, not exact
selection. Near an axis minimum x0 at J2=0 with transverse Hessian
h=2a+2d*x0^2>0, the linear response is y=J2/h+O(J2^3), with x allowed
to move at order J2^2. This local expansion assumes a nondegenerate
longitudinal Hessian too; it is not an arbitrary global minimum formula.

The familiar zero-source comparison is retained: on x^2+y^2=r^2 the
quartic is c*r^4+(d-2c)x^2*y^2, so d>2c favors axes AT FIXED RADIUS
when J=0. The same criterion does not prove exact axes at generic J.
Constraints, nonsmooth/infinite penalties, non-linear/dynamical sources,
or different interactions can change this conclusion. They require a
declared law. This is not a universal no-chiral-phase theorem.

Separate kinetic norms G1,G2>0 give normalized c1/G1^2, c2/G2^2 and
d/(G1*G2); equality of the two self quartics needs a same-action symmetry
and normalization. d/(2*sqrt(c1*c2)) is invariant under positive separate
coordinate rescalings. Bare extension coordinates alone do not fix d.
