## 2. Why the finite-family qualifier is load-bearing

Gamma has finite quotients beyond these four spherical triangle groups.
An exact pencil-and-paper countercontrol is reduction modulo 7:

S = [[0,-1],[1,0]], U = [[0,-1],[1,1]].

In SL(2,F7), S squared and U cubed equal -I; SU is minus the elementary
upper unipotent. Projectively S and U therefore satisfy Gamma's
relations, while T=SU has order 7. Upper unipotents and their S-conjugate
lower unipotents generate SL(2,F7) by elementary row reduction.
There are (7 squared - 1) choices for its nonzero first column and
7 choices for its determinant-one second column. Quotienting by
{I,-I} gives 168 elements.

Thus the infinite group Delta(2,3,7) has a finite quotient of order
168. No contradiction with the spherical triangle theorem occurs.
W153's own disclosed scratch census already includes this 168.
The correction concerns its headline and “every finite quotient”
wording, not a refutation of the four-rung calculation.

The McKay route additionally requires a finite subgroup of SU(2)
with its natural representation; an arbitrary finite quotient of
Gamma does not automatically supply that datum. Even broadening the
finite subgroup class would require accounting for the cyclic and
binary dihedral ADE families. That is a separate completeness question,
not a reason to discard the useful exceptional chain.

## 3. R2 needs its scalar-theta hypothesis

The positive even lattice theorem concerns a single scalar theta
series transforming with its weight under the full modular group.
It is not a theorem that every modular-invariant theory has a
unimodular charge lattice.

For a positive even lattice L of rank r, the theta components indexed
by L*/L transform in a unitary Weil representation. Consequently

Z_L(tau) = (Im tau)^(r/2) sum_mu |Theta_mu(tau)| squared

is modular invariant even when L is not unimodular: the norm acquires
the weight factor |c tau+d|^r, cancelled by the power of Im tau.
This is a mathematical countercontrol, not an OA physical partition
function or a derivation of its action. Primary source read:
Manuel K.-H. Müller, “The basis problem for modular forms for the Weil
representation,” §§1–2, https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01205v1
(accessed October 24, 2026), for the vector theta transformation and
unitarity; the displayed invariant is the inference from those facts.

Even unimodularity would select E8 only within this ladder or with an
additional suitable rank/minimality condition, not among all even
unimodular lattices. The joint action must earn the actual scalar
chiral-lattice requirement before R2 can pay for a physical input.
