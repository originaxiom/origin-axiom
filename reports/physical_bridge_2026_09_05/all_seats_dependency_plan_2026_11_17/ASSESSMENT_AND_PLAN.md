## 2. The common weakness

The record has several useful structures and several physical models.
It has not established that the same generated registering architecture
forces one physical state space, statistics, gauge action, interaction,
domain and boundary response. Agreement of dimensions or finite symmetry
actions does not supply that map. A topology-dependent index, an allowed
charge table and a model's Yukawa matrices cannot be joined by naming them
as the same matter.

This is a missing derivation, not a theorem excluding the programme. It
also is not one missing number whose insertion would settle everything.
There are two immediate deciding questions: the existing physical
construction's complete-domain estimate, and the compatibility of the
new coupling operator with the proposed physical matter and gauge action.

## 3. A bounded first test of the coupling join

Reuse B1953's O, its two parity projectors P3/P2, and its already-computed
hypercharge component F = sum_w zeta^(-ab) Y(w). Do not first run another
mass census or select the best-looking readout.

**Conditional hand observation for planning, not a banked result.** Suppose
O is interpreted as a map from F_R tensor W to F_L tensor W, where the
family spaces are gauge-trivial and the unbroken gauge group acts on W
as its fundamental 3+2. In orthonormal physical frames, gauge intertwining
requires O to commute with every continuous gauge generator, not just P.
Schur's lemma then requires

    O = A tensor I3  direct-sum  B tensor I2.

Since every displacement D(w) in B1953 is traceless, tr_W O=0 and
3A+2B=0. The normalized spectra of the two family Gramians must agree.
B1953 reports different spectra, so its generic parity-block operator
cannot simply be identified with that gauge intertwiner. This does not
refute B1953; its physical-sector identification is explicitly unclaimed.

The orthogonal projection to gauge intertwiners, under the same hypotheses,
would be

    (tr_3 O3/3) tensor I3  direct-sum  (tr_2 O2/2) tensor I2
      = (F/6) tensor I3  direct-sum  (-F/4) tensor I2
      = -(F/12) tensor 6Y.

Here tr_3 O3=F/2 and tr_2 O2=-F/2, since P3=(I+P)/2,
P2=(I-P)/2, tr D=0 and tr(DP)=1. B1953's sentence identifying
F itself with tr O3 misses this factor two (MAIN-01). Its distinct
identity tr_W(O 6Y)=-(5/2)F is consistent. The original uncommitted
projection section that repeated the erroneous sentence is preserved
in preserved_draft_01/, with this correction made before delivery.
The component F was already computed; it is not a new readout discovery.
A physical argument is still needed for performing this
projection rather than keeping additional charged background fields.

For an actual Yukawa tensor, left, right and Higgs representations differ;
the correct condition includes the Higgs's transformation and is not the
simple commutator above. Therefore the first calculation must classify
the invariant trilinear maps and insert O into those maps. Determine
whether its noncentral parts are actual derived fields or merely supplied
spurions. Check whether the permitted projection reproduces B1949's
down/lepton relation, and whether an admitted breaking field changes it.
An assigned B-L holonomy is an alternative to analyze, not a derived fix.

This test could reduce the readout ambiguity under earned physical
hypotheses. It could instead show that an extra field, operator or
interaction is required. Both outcomes are useful; neither permits an
input to disappear from the ledger without a derivation.
