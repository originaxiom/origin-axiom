# R84: adjoint rigidity, finite twists and the cap's actual scope

Independent authored review of B1535(c1,c2), B1530 Lemma T and E'.
Classical rigidity is a cited input, not proved anew here.

## RT1. Two different coefficient systems

Let Gamma be a torsion-free nonuniform lattice in SO+(3,1). The complex
adjoint A=so(3,1)_C is Lambda^2 rho = Ad(h) + Ad(conj h), of rank6.
The Lorentz vector rho=h tensor conj(h) has rank4. Garland--Raghunathan's
PH^1(Gamma;A)=0 does NOT say PH^1(Gamma;rho)=0. Kapovich1994 p349
explicitly uses this distinction; Bart--Scannell2006 pp675--676 does too.
PH means cyclic-parabolic coboundaries, for EVERY parabolic element,
not vanishing of full H^1 and not just one selected cusp generator.

Sources: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/EPR/Kapovich_1994.pdf ;
https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-2006-028-1 ;
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1970/92-2/p05 .

## RT2. Local parabolic classes need not vanish

In basis (N,H,F) of sl2, Ad(U_z) is
[[1,-2z,-z^2],[0,1,z],[0,0,1]], U_z=I+zN. For z!=0 its fixed line
is C N and im(Ad(U_z)-I)=span(N,H). Thus the fixed nilpotent lies in
every nonidentity translation's coboundary image.

For P generated by U_1,U_i, the cocycle c(p)=0,c(q)=N is not a
common coboundary of P: (Ad(U_1)-I)v=0 forces v in C N, which also
kills (Ad(U_i)-I)v. Nevertheless c(p^r q^s)=sN is in the coboundary
image of Ad(U_(r+is)) for every nonzero integer pair. This is a genuine
local positive. It refutes PH^1(P;Ad)=0, NOT global lattice rigidity.
Each adjoint summand's invariant-valued part pi_P is one-dimensional;
the two together give dimension2 in H^1(P;Lambda^2 rho).

## RT3. Transport through the finite kernel

Let xi be ANY finite-order scalar character of Gamma, and S=xi A.
H=ker xi is finite index and is itself a nonuniform lattice. Restriction
H^1(Gamma;S) -> H^1(H;A) is injective over C: restriction followed by
corestriction is [Gamma:H] times the identity. No assumption that every
character of H descends is used. This is transport of specified classes,
not a census or a physical cover-selection rule.

A cuspidal class restricts trivially to every torus, hence to every
cyclic parabolic subgroup. On H its class is zero by the cited rigidity,
and injectivity forces it zero on Gamma. Consequently n(S)=0 (c1).

For (c2), suppose its boundary class lies in pi_P at each xi-trivial
cusp, and is zero at every other cusp. A nontrivial scalar times
unipotent is acyclic: one generator has invertible xi(p)Ad(p)-I,
providing a contracting homotopy for the torus Koszul complex. At a
trivial cusp choose an invariant-valued representative; changing this
choice adds a common coboundary. Every parabolic in H therefore has
its value in im(Ad(gamma)-I), by RT2. All lifted/conjugate cusps are
included. Rigidity and restriction injectivity again force the class
zero. Thus Lambda(S) intersect pi=0. This checks B1535(c2).

Explicit power transport is c(gamma^m)=sum_j S(gamma)^j c(gamma).
For a coboundary this equals (S(gamma)^m-I)v. If xi(gamma)^m=1,
S(gamma)^m=Ad(gamma^m); invariant-valued terms also remain in its
image. A cusp with nu=-1 and xi=nu^2=1 is NOT acyclic for S merely
because nu is nontrivial. The proof survives that case without deleting it.
For cover-count terms use xi=nu^2 chi, not just nu^2.

## RT4. The local rank-five table, independently derived

Put (a,b,c,d)=(e00,e01,e10,e11). Let Xc=a,Xd=b and Yb=a,Yd=c;
all other values vanish. These are N tensor1 and1 tensor N. Their
Koszul complex has H^1 representatives (c,0),(0,b). Extend by e with
Xe=p c, Ye=q b. X,Y still commute, for all p,q. Any nonzero class
is represented by (p,q)!=(0,0); coboundary changes conjugate the extension.

On W=span(a,b,c,d,e), the joint kernel is C a for nonzero (p,q);
the joint coinvariants are span(d,e), so (h0,h1,h2)=(1,3,2).
At (0,0) the split comparison is (2,4,2).

On Lambda^2 W, the joint kernel is span(ab,ac) for nonzero (p,q).
Indeed the X/Y equations force coefficients of ad and bc to be both
equal and opposite, then zero; also bd,be,cd,ce,de vanish and
p*coefficient(ae)=q*coefficient(ae)=0. The joint image is always
span(ab,ac,ad,bc,ae,be-p*cd,ce-q*bd), dimension7. Thus its
(h0,h1,h2)=(2,5,3); split gives (3,6,3).

Wedge with the invariant a sends the two classes to (ac,0),(0,ab).
Neither is a coboundary in Lambda^2 rho: ac is outside im(X) and
ab outside im(Y) on that module. They are invariant-valued. Hence
a wedge c is nonzero in pi_P for every nonzero cusp class.

For an actual cusp the logarithms are zX+conj(z)Y and
wX+conj(w)Y. Their span is the same precisely for non-real cusp
shape w/z; a real shape loses this implication. The group complex and
Lie Koszul complex have the same cohomology, since exp(T)-I=T f(T)
with invertible f(T) for nilpotent commuting T. The resulting table is
B1530's, rederived rather than merely inherited.

## RT5. Extension-index algebra and the cap

On a compact core with nonempty torus boundary, Euler characteristic0
and Poincare--Lefschetz duality give
n(E)=h1(E*)-h0(E*)-h0(boundary E)+h0(E).
Subtract the analogous expression for E*: I(E)=h1(E*)-h1(E)
+2(h0(E)-h0(E*))+h0(boundary E*)-h0(boundary E).
No self-duality or semisimplicity of E is needed.

Writing f(E)=h1(E)-2h0(E)+h0(boundary E), the LES gives
f(E)=f(S)+f(Q)+r0-r1-r0_boundary for 0->S->E->Q->0.
Subtract the dual LES: exactly B1535 E', including its signs.

With its specified unitary real pieces and vanishing bulk invariants,
RT3 makes H2(S)->H2(boundary S) an isomorphism. RT4 forces delta1
of Lambda^2 W to vanish locally, hence globally. Dual connecting maps
are adjoint under boundary pairing; RT3's intersection result makes
their boundary rank k. If K is the kernel in H2(Q*), its dimension
is n(Q*)=n(nu^3 rho). Thus I(Lambda^2 W)=-dim(im delta1 intersect K)
lies in [-n(nu^3 rho),0]. E' for W gives I(W)>=-b0-n(nu^4).
This is B1535's cap, conditional on the declared carrier/coefficient
and classical rigidity hypotheses; the census is not its universal proof.

## RT6. Preserve what this does not establish

Three in this dictionary needs BOTH n(nu^3 rho)>=3 and
b0+n(nu^4)>=3 at the same nu, plus one extension class attaining
compatible indices. Bending can give vector classes on covers; it does
not by itself give the line supply, the character, or saturation.
Infinity of immersed surfaces and number of cusps alone do not do so.
Bart--Scannell's nonvanishing example is over Q(sqrt(-7)); no transfer
to the silver Q(i) commensurability class is asserted here.

B1535 Lemma W's wording about every class restricting to a puncture
circle is too strong: the fibration class restricts to the BASE circle
of a cusp torus. Its stated zero-interior argument can use this base
circle along with the fibre restrictions; this is not a counterexample
to the lemma or a re-run of its541-row census.

Non-unitary twists, other coefficients/frames, off-hyperbolic points,
nonabelian covers and physical source/end changes remain separate.
This interior index is NOT a certified Fredholm/positive-norm physical
fermion count. R81 compact admission remains positive, not selection.
Act/register and both partners' currents remain in the physical duty.
No qualia mechanism, normalized SM observable or full TOE follows.
