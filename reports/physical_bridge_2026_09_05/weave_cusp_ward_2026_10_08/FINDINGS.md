# The cusp end survives in the regulated gauge current

The previous ultraviolet heat cancellation survives as a mathematical
result, but it does not complete the gauge-consistency test. For one
explicit covariant inverse-current prescription on the SAME supplied
curved E8 parent, the propagator carries an additional cusp end term.
Its slowly varying external-field limit recovers the nonzero physical
zero-mode color coefficient. The candidate current fails the necessary
integrability condition for an action at nonzero flux.

This is a conditional analytic result about that prescription and the
specified stationary family, not a no-go for the parent or architecture.
The fields, metric, spin, gauge embedding and complete domain remain
supplied; the nonzero-flux backgrounds remain bosonic saddles.

## The term the heat trace alone does not measure

With the actual doubled fermion operator D and compact internal cutoff
chi, the candidate phase variation is

    Omega(delta D) = -1/2 Str(chi D^-1 exp(-epsilon D^2) delta D).

At finite cutoff its gauge-direction value includes BOTH terms:

    Omega(delta_alpha D)/i
      = Str(chi alpha exp(-epsilon D^2))
        + 1/2 Str([D,chi] D^-1 exp(-epsilon D^2) alpha).

For internally parallel unbroken external fields, write a_R(s) for the
external chiral heat insertion and S_R(s) for the already derived cusp
supertrace. Removing the spatial cutoff at fixed epsilon and then
sending epsilon to zero gives

    Omega(delta_alpha D)/i = sum_R integral_0^infinity a_R(s) S_R'(s) ds.

The first heat term tends to zero. The second need not. PROOF derives
the finite-cutoff identity, supplies a uniform shell heat bound before
interchanging limits, and preserves the physical conjugate counting.
No new compensating fermion is inserted.

A smooth small color field on a spin four-torus provides an explicit
test. Every external Dirac operator is invertible; the integrated
topological density is zero, but its gauge-parameter-weighted insertion
is nonzero. Metric dilation proves the slow-field limit equals the
old index-weighted color coefficient: -1 at flux n=1, -3 for n>=2,
opposite signs for negative n, and zero at n=0. This is an analytic
large-scale limit, not a numerical external heat-kernel computation
or a selected spacetime vacuum.

## Why the prescription is not an action variation

The finite-cutoff current and its gauge-direction limits are gauge
equivariant. Their gauge-direction curl equals the current on the
commutator parameter, rather than zero. Explicit noncommuting SU3
parameters make that response nonzero on the same smooth background.
Consequently these gauge-direction values cannot be those of an exact
effective-action variation at nonzero flux.

Only the gauge-direction limits are needed; a renormalized one-form on
arbitrary variations has not been assumed or constructed. An exact
action counterterm cannot change this curl. A covariant-to-consistent
current correction or a different regulated action/end system is a
distinct task, and obtaining consistency would not automatically cancel
the resulting anomaly. The general distinction is explained in
[Bilal sections4.2 and9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/0802.0634v1); the end
term and test background here are derived for our own operator.
