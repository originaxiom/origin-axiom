## The useful distinction

The handoff asks the right action-level question: a symmetry of a LAW need
not fix each of its STATES. Keep four different statements separate:

1. A field configuration is fixed by a physical mirror, up to admitted gauge.
2. A set of configurations contains each configuration's mirror partner.
3. An ensemble or quantum state assigns mirror-symmetric weights.
4. Two actual charged operators, with their norms and domains, are intertwined.

None of these is interchangeable with the others. Under an involution
theta, an odd observable J(theta x)=-J(x) has zero expectation in a
theta-invariant, normalizable measure. Equal weights suffice but are not
necessary. For a finite menu, pair x with theta x; fixed points have J=0
in a torsion-free target. This says nothing about J(x) for a non-fixed
individual state. Summing possible configurations is not a derived
physical probability law or path-integral measure.

## The revised selection test

Keep the handoff's proposed test, with these gates BEFORE its outcome:

1. **Name the physical transformation.** Exhibit the base map, bundle and
   gauge lift, source transformation, four-dimensional spinor action and
   end/domain transport. Algebraic coefficient duality is not automatically
   that physical mirror. State whether it acts inside one supplied sector
   or transports to another member of the generated architecture.
2. **Test the complete theory.** Check action and kinetic norms, admitted
   variations and domains, interaction tensors and eventually the quantum
   measure/anomalies. A residual-square potential invariant by itself is
   a necessary partial check, not full physical parity invariance.
3. **Establish an attained stationary state.** Minimize or solve the
   equations in that same admissible theory. Finite norm is not enough.
   R76's bare nonsplit scaling has a nonattained split limit, not two
   exhibited nonsplit minima. Nonflat/source/end alternatives stay distinct.
4. **Quotient by actual gauge.** Test whether the partner is genuinely
   inequivalent, not a basis convention or gauge direction. A matrix
   transpose check alone cannot settle this, especially in the full E8.
5. **Compute the physical spectrum there.** Tie the partner's chirality
   sign to a normalizable, anomaly-consistent fermion spectrum in the same
   background/action/domain, retaining every added field and extra mode.

The possible dispositions include fixed minima, a genuine exchanged pair,
a mixture of fixed and exchanged minima, no attained minimum in the
declared sector, an explicitly asymmetric theory/domain, or an unearned
physical mirror map. The handoff's A/B/C menu is not exhaustive.
One exchanged pair proves existence, not a classification of all minima;
one fixed minimum does not prove every minimum is fixed. A negative must
name the theory and admissible sector it actually exhausted.
