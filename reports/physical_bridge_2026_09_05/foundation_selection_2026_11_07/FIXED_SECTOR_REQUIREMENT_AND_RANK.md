## Outcome and scope

For the precise registration commitments below, the existence of a nonabelian
common fixed character in SL2(C) is **independent**: a rank-two model has one
and a rank-three model does not, while both satisfy every listed commitment.
This strengthens the preceding discussion into an explicit model comparison.
It is not an independence theorem for every possible reading of PF1–PF3.

Two constructive positives sharpen the conclusion:

1. Every free rank n>=2 has a faithful, coherent, jointly irreducible
   act/register action on an infinite-dimensional register. Requiring the
   joined action to be indivisible does not force the local SL2 sector.
2. Every free rank n>=2 has a finite-image, irreducible unitary common
   character in some finite dimension, by the full A5-quotient tensor
   construction below. Thus merely replacing 'SL2' by 'some finite
   irreducible unitary register' does not select two records either.

These constructions are mathematical alternatives and controls. They do
not derive a physical Hilbert space, a tensor law, observed matter, Born
probabilities, time, a boundary condition or the Standard Model. The
dimensions of the finite tensor registers are not claimed minimal.

## 2. The exact commitments being tested

Let H be a countable finitely generated group of formal histories, let Gamma
act on H by automorphisms, and let R:H->{0,1} be a registering map. Consider:

| A2 noncommuting distinction | Some a,b in H have [a,b]!=identity, with R(identity)=0 and R([a,b])=1 | A precise proposed non-cancellation reading, not all of PF1 |

For the stronger free-word comparison, also grant a free marked carrier

## 3. What the common fixed-sector condition says

Write S2(H,Gamma) for the existence of an irreducible representation

    rho:H -> SL(2,C)

such that tr rho(g h)=tr rho(h) for every g in Gamma and h in H.
'Irreducible' concerns the H representation here. This is an auxiliary
coefficient-sector existence condition; it does not assert that this
sector is the entire contextual register or a subspace of the regular
Hilbert realization used later.

### Rank two: a positive model

Take rho(x1)=i, rho(x2)=j as the standard quaternion matrices in SU2.
Both square to -I, anticommute and have trace zero; their product is k.
Their image is Q8. The eigenlines of i are exchanged by j, so the
representation has no invariant complex line.

Every elementary left/right Nielsen move replaces the ordered pair by
another pair of perpendicular imaginary quaternion units, with signs.
Such a pair is obtained by a rotation of the imaginary quaternion axes;
the corresponding unit quaternion implements simultaneous conjugation.
The inverse moves work the same way. Therefore the character is fixed
by Gamma_2. This is the existing quaternion common point, preserved here
as a genuine conditional positive.

The full-context register remains R(h)=1 iff h!=identity in F2. It is
not replaced with identity detection only in Q8, which would erase
nontrivial kernel words. A faithful larger register and a finite local
coefficient sector can coexist.

### Rank three and above: rechecking the actual exclusion by hand

This is the B1679/W109 result, reusing its first four steps and giving an
elementary unipotent argument for its final step. It requires the actual
SL2 target and the common-character quantifier on every word.

Let A_i=rho(x_i), with character fixed under every R_ij:x_i->x_i x_j.
For i!=j, the test word x_i gives

    tr(A_i A_j)=tr(A_i).

Interchanging i,j shows that all generator traces and all distinct pair
traces equal one t. The word x_i x_j^-1 becomes x_i under R_ij, giving
tr(A_i A_j^-1)=t. The SL2 trace identity gives

    t^2=2t, hence t=0 or 2.

For three distinct indices i,j,k, applying R_ij to x_i x_k gives

    tr(A_i A_j A_k)=t.

If t=0, Cayley–Hamilton gives A_i^2=-I. On sl2(C), the nondegenerate
trace form -tr(XY)/2 makes any three A_1,A_2,A_3 orthonormal. A_1 A_2
is traceless, has norm one and is orthogonal to A_1,A_2, so it equals
plus or minus A_3. Consequently tr(A_1 A_2 A_3) is plus or minus 2,
contradicting the required zero. No fourth generator is needed.

If t=2, put A_i=I+N_i. Then N_i is traceless with N_i^2=0, and pair
traces give tr(N_i N_j)=0. If all N_i vanish the representation is
trivial. Otherwise conjugate a nonzero N_i to E12. Writing any other
N_j as [[a,b],[c,-a]], orthogonality gives c=0 and nilpotence gives
a=0. Thus every N_j is a multiple of E12. The entire image is upper
unipotent and abelian, so the representation is reducible and its
character is trivial on all words. A trivial character is not the
same as requiring the actual unipotent representation to be identity.

Therefore S2 is false on F_n for every n>=3. This proof changes neither
B1679's conclusion nor its target scope.

### Exact independence conclusion

The A1–A5 model with H=F2 satisfies S2. The A1–A5 model with H=F3
satisfies not-S2. Both grant the same free-word category and elementary
move rule. Hence neither S2 nor its negation follows from those
commitments. This is ordinary model-based independence relative to the
explicit package, not an undecidability claim.

It does not yet show that two records are inconsistent with the principle.
It shows that these particular meanings of non-cancellation, persistence
and contextual registration do not earn the SL2 fixed-sector requirement.

## 4. A faithful joined register can itself be irreducible at every rank

This strengthens the countercontrol against replacing the missing
fixed-sector premise merely with 'the whole act/register is indivisible'.
The following is a standard regular-action construction, not a claim of
new representation-theoretic novelty or a derived quantum dictionary.

On K_n=ell2(F_n), with orthonormal formal basis delta_h, define

    lambda(a) delta_h = delta_(a h),
    U_g delta_h = delta_(g h),       g in Gamma_n.

These permutation operators are unitary and obey exactly

    U_g lambda(a) U_g^-1 = lambda(g a),
    U_g U_k = U_(g k).

Thus (a,g)->lambda(a)U_g is an ordinary representation of the
semidirect product F_n rtimes Gamma_n, not a separately selected
intertwiner at each step. Its kernel is trivial: acting on delta_identity
first forces a=identity, and then its action on every delta_h forces
g=identity. Register projectors onto delta_h transport with the same
law. The binary cancellation reading can also be recovered from the
matrix coefficient at delta_identity. No Born interpretation is used.

Gamma_n contains all inner automorphisms. For each generator x_i,
the product over j!=i of L_ji R_ji^-1 conjugates every generator by
x_i (composition acts rightmost first). Every factor fixes x_i and
changes only x_j, so these factors commute and give the stated map.

This uses the explicitly enlarged elementary-move group. It cannot be
copied silently to the original two-move rank-two group. For L:b->ab,
a->a and R:a->ab,b->b, the nonidentity word c=a b^-1 a^-1 b is fixed
by both moves, by direct substitution. Right translation by c then
commutes with the left history action and both rewording operators;
it is a nonscalar element of the commutant. The corresponding joined
regular action for that restricted move group is reducible. This is
a concrete scope countercontrol, consistent with the existing ruled-
group versus enlarged-group distinction in the register notes.

Every nonidentity conjugacy class of F_n, n>=2, is infinite. Indeed,
for a nonempty reduced word w choose a letter c that is neither the
inverse of its first letter nor its last letter. At least one exists
among 2n>=4 letters. Then c^k w c^-k is reduced of length 2k+|w|,
so these conjugates are all different.

Now let T be a bounded operator commuting with all lambda(a) and U_g.
The vector v=T delta_identity is fixed by every U_g, hence its
coefficients are constant on conjugacy classes. Square summability
forces its coefficient on every nonidentity infinite conjugacy class
to vanish. Thus v=c delta_identity. Since T commutes with lambda(a),

    T delta_a = lambda(a) v = c delta_a

on a dense basis. So T=cI. Any closed invariant subspace for the
whole unitary action has a commuting orthogonal projector; only 0
and I are possible. The joined representation is irreducible.

Both the proof and the infinitude hypothesis matter. On a finite group,
the constant vector is square summable and invariant under both actions;
on an abelian group, inner conjugacy classes are singletons. The same
irreducibility conclusion therefore cannot be copied to those controls.

**Result:** faithful registration, all-context distinction, exact
composition and joint unitary irreducibility coexist at every free
rank n>=2, including n=3 where S2 is false. The register here is
infinite-dimensional. A requirement of finite local dimension would
be an additional premise, not a defect this countermodel conceals.

The related left-right regular irreducibility fact for groups with
infinite nonidentity conjugacy classes is stated in Bekka–Pillon–Valette,
*Irreducible affine isometric actions on Hilbert spaces*, section 6.1:
https://arxiv.org/html/1411.2267v1 . Only their underlying linear regular
representation observation is relevant here. This report neither imports
an affine cocycle nor applies their separate amenability hypothesis to
the nonamenable free group. The direct proof above is self-contained.

## 5. Finite-image irreducible fixed sectors also exist at every rank

This second strengthening prevents silently replacing SL2 with 'some
finite unitary irreducible target' and retaining the rank-two conclusion.
It does not claim a small-dimensional target or a minimal physical one.

Fix n>=2 and the finite nonabelian simple group G=A5. It is generated
by a 3-cycle and a 5-cycle, so F_n surjects onto it. Let K_1,...,K_m
be **all distinct kernels** of epimorphisms F_n->G. There are finitely
many since an n-generator homomorphism to G is specified by n images.
At least one exists. Put K=intersection K_i; it is characteristic because
every automorphism permutes the full set of these kernels.

For the finite-group facts used here, the even permutations on five
letters have order 60: besides identity there are 15 double
transpositions, 20 three-cycles and 24 five-cycles. The conjugacy-class
sizes in A5 are 1,15,20,12,12, by their permutation centralizers. No
proper union including the identity has size dividing 60: the possible
nonidentity additions are subset sums of 15,20,12,12, giving total
sizes 13,16,21,25,28,33,36,40,45,48 or 60, besides 1. Hence A5 is
simple. A subgroup containing both a 3-cycle and a 5-cycle would have
order 15,30 or 60. Order 30 is an index-two normal subgroup; order 15
has both Sylow subgroups normal and would be cyclic, but A5 has no
element of order 15. Thus, for example, (123) and (12345) generate A5.

Choose one epimorphism pi_i per kernel. Then

    pi=(pi_1,...,pi_m): F_n -> G^m

is onto. Here is the needed subdirect-product proof, rather than merely
multiplying projection orders. Induct on m. If a subdirect image onto
G^r and one further G has a nontrivial kernel on the last coordinate,
that kernel is normal in G and hence all of G; the image is the full
product. Otherwise the image is the graph of a surjection G^r->G.
Each factor maps to a normal subgroup of G, and two different surjective
factor images would commute, contradicting nonabelian simplicity. Thus
that surjection is an automorphism after a single factor projection.
The new kernel would equal a previous K_i, contrary to distinctness.
This proves F_n/K is G^m.

Use the standard four-dimensional A5 representation on the sum-zero
subspace of C^5. Its character chi has values 4,0,1,-1 on elements of
orders 1,2,3,5. The element counts are 1,15,20,24. Therefore

    inner_product(chi,chi)=(16+20+24)/60=1.

It is irreducible. Because the character depends only on element order,
every automorphism of A5 preserves it; no identification of Aut(A5)
with a chosen permutation group is required for this argument.

The external tensor product of these representations of the independent
factors G^m has character

    Chi(g_1,...,g_m)=product chi(g_i)

and dimension d=4^m. Character inner products multiply, so it is
irreducible on G^m and, by surjectivity, on F_n. Automorphisms of G^m
permute its minimal normal simple factors and act by automorphisms on
each. They therefore preserve Chi. Since K is characteristic, every
automorphism of F_n induces such an automorphism of G^m. Consequently
Chi composed with pi is a common fixed irreducible character of F_n.

Finite images make the representation unitary after a specified
averaging of the Hermitian form. Its determinant is 1 because G^m is
perfect, so it is an SU(d) example. Its nonabelian image follows also
from irreducibility and d>1: an abelian unitary image would split into
one-dimensional characters.

Changing the pi_i representatives only composes factors with automorphisms
of G and permutes factors; the resulting character is unchanged. Thus
no single preferred epimorphism or factor is required by this construction.
Choosing A5 and the tensor composition is supplied for the counterexample,
not derived as an OA physical law or as an E8-generating instruction.

**Lift caveat:** a fixed irreducible character provides intertwiners up
to phase. It does not supply a composition-preserving section. One can
retain the full group of pairs (g,U) satisfying U rho(h) U^-1=rho(g h).
With U in SU(d), its kernel is the finite scalar group mu_d. Nothing
here claims that extension splits. This is the same type of retained
lift duty already visible in the actual quaternion register programme.

The construction is compatible with B1679's theorem: d is not 2.
It is compatible with B1680's scoped census: its higher targets were
specified small dimensions, not arbitrary products with d=4^m.
It also does not resolve or refute the Baby Wiegold conjecture concerning
**simple** characteristic quotients: this quotient is an entire product
G^m, not a claim that any individual simple factor is fixed by all moves.

Lubotzky's *Dynamics of Aut(F_n) actions on group presentations and
representations*, section 6.4, explicitly distinguishes characteristic
products of simple factors from questions about a single factor:
https://arxiv.org/html/1109.0155v1 . Gelander's *Aut(F_n) actions on
representation spaces*, section 8, defines characteristic linear
representations and the separate simple-quotient conjecture:
https://arxiv.org/html/2212.11857v2 . I inspected those relevant sections
personally; the finite tensor proof above is given explicitly rather
than attributed to an unread theorem or claimed as a novel discovery.

## 6. The selector that would suffice, and its actual price

There is a clean conditional positive. If one **additionally requires
minimizing the dimension of a nonabelian common fixed irreducible complex
coefficient sector across free ranks**, the minimum is 2: a nonabelian
irreducible representation cannot have dimension 1, and F2 supplies Q8
in dimension 2. The rechecked SL2 theorem excludes that minimum at n>=3.
F1 has no nonabelian image. This selects rank two and its Q8 sector
within that precise optimization problem.

There is a small genuine dependency reduction here: for ordinary finite-
dimensional complex characters fixed under all Nielsen moves, the
**special-linear determinant condition follows**. Equality of characters
on h,h^2,...,h^d determines the determinant by the characteristic-polynomial
identities, so det rho is a fixed one-dimensional character. Applying
R_ij to x_i gives det rho(x_i) det rho(x_j)=det rho(x_i), hence
det rho(x_j)=1 for every j when n>=2. At d=2 this needs only
det A=((tr A)^2-tr(A^2))/2. Thus the preceding minimum argument also
works when it starts with GL(d,C) or U(d); at the two-dimensional minimum
the required SL2 condition is automatic. Complex linearity, finite
dimension, the fixed-character quantifier and the minimization objective
remain premises. Projective character invariance up to phase is a
different condition and is not covered by this determinant argument.

But the objective being minimized is a specific **local fixed-sector
dimension**. It is not the size of the full faithful register, a measure
on all histories, the least number of independent relations, a physical
action, or the information required by every interaction. A construction
cannot switch among these costs while calling all of them 'minimality'.
For instance, a smallest local coefficient quotient can omit words the
existing full tower distinguishes. Its local optimality does not prove
that omitted data are physically absent. A root/building block and an
entire generated architecture remain different objects.

The next earning duty is therefore precise: does the foundational
act/register principle require this particular local-dimension minimum,
with a single fixed complex sector, while retaining the rest of the
architecture? If so, prove that implication and account for the full
register; otherwise retain it as a selection postulate. No empirical
particle assignment or known Standard Model number was used to obtain
the conditional argument above.
