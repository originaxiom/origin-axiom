## Operational test for a proposed reduction

Before extending a calculation, write its prospective reduction card:

1. **Input and duty:** the exact independent choice and the physical
   earning condition it is meant to satisfy.
2. **Existing evidence:** original proof/producer, actual scope and
   review status. Search prior work before rebuilding an instrument.
3. **Proposed derivation:** which already admitted premises are used;
   identify any NEW condition separately rather than hiding its price.
4. **Discriminator:** what outcome would show that the reduction failed
   or that an inequivalent freedom remains under the same hypotheses.
5. **Before/after accounting:** which freedom is removed, shared with
   another dependency, retained as a convention, or still physical.
6. **Physical transfer:** the actual fields, operator, interactions,
   domain and registering/measuring data that must travel together.
7. **Verification:** substantive proof/code coverage and genuine
   execution evidence where computation is needed. Metadata is not
   scientific verification, and review access is not banking.

Classify the outcome as foundational reduction, conditional same-model
reduction, dependency clarification, unresolved freedom or scoped failed
route. Conditional reductions are genuine positives within their scope,
but do not pay an entire foundational row while their hypotheses remain
supplied. A local freedom never shown to extend globally must not first
be counted as an established global parameter merely to remove it.

Bookkeeping groups are not a proved minimal independent axiom count.
Assumptions, discrete choices, continuous parameters and measured SM
constants are different kinds; do not compare their raw totals as if
they were equivalent quantities.
