## 1. Identify the two neutral sections without picking their values

Let xi be the nontrivial compact flat order-two line with characters
(-1,-1) in the fixed marking. The actual native neutral modes are

    n0 in H0(K^2(p)), n1 in H0(K^2 xi(p)).

Each section space is one-dimensional. Since X is elliptic, K^2 is
trivial, with the inherited omega^2 frame. Its nonzero n0 section
has no meromorphic pole and no interior zero. As a section of O(p)
its divisor is p.

A nonzero n1 must have exactly a simple meromorphic pole at p.
Otherwise it would be a holomorphic section of a nontrivial degree-zero
flat line. Its section divisor in xi(p) has one zero q, with q!=p.
The actual value of q is fixed by that flat line and the curve.
No marked point is added to the action.

Let z0,z1 be the two neutral state amplitudes, including their actual
normalization. Up to the fixed nonzero gauge and slot constants,
the reduced maps f0,f1 are z0 n0,z1 n1. The maps between the compact
bundles have Hom degree two by the inherited end bounds. Thus their
actual divisors are

    div f0=2p, div f1=p+q                              (1)

when the corresponding coefficient is nonzero. One extra p in (1)
comes from the two-pole allowance of the Hom line. It is physical
end data inherited from normalizability.

Therefore their common divisor Z is

    Z=p if z0 z1!=0,
    Z=2p if z0!=0,z1=0,
    Z=p+q if z0=0,z1!=0.                               (2)

At z0=z1=0 the differential is zero and that case is evaluated
separately below. Neither the unitary coefficient Wilson line nor
charge conjugation changes the orders in (1)-(2).

## 2. Compute both cohomology sheaves directly

Write the compact complex as

    Abar --(f0,f1)--> Bbar+Cbar --(-f1,f0)--> Dbar,
    Abar Dbar=Bbar Cbar.

Assume at least one f is nonzero. Locally a smooth curve has a
discrete valuation parameter w. Write f0=h g0,f1=h g1, where h
has the common zero order and g0,g1 generate the unit ideal. The
first map is injective. Its saturated kernel in the middle is
generated by (g0,g1). Globally that kernel is Abar(Z), with Abar
mapping into it by the defining section of Z.

The last image is Dbar(-Z). The cohomology sheaves are therefore

    Hsheaf^0=0,
    Hsheaf^1=Abar(Z)/Abar,
    Hsheaf^2=Dbar/Dbar(-Z).                             (3)

Both surviving sheaves have finite support and length deg Z. Their
higher cohomology vanishes. The hypercohomology spectral sequence
of (3) consequently gives

    dim Htotal^1=dim Htotal^2=deg Z,
    Htotal^0=Htotal^3=0.                               (4)

This proof includes both singular members of (2). A nonzero map
still has injective first arrow when its companion is identically
zero. No regular-sequence assumption of length two is imposed on
the one-dimensional curve.
