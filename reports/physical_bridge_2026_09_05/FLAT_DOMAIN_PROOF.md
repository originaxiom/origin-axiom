# Operator domains in the generated architecture

## What the closed flat index statement actually says

For a smooth closed even-dimensional spin manifold X and a smooth
unitary flat bundle E, the usual twisted spin-Dirac index formula is

    ind D_E^+ = integral_X Ahat(TX) ch(E)
              = rank(E) integral_X Ahat(TX).

The dual flat bundle has the same real Chern character. On a closed
six-dimensional X the integral is zero because Ahat has only degrees
divisible by four. In this specified problem a flat gauge twist cannot
generate a nonzero six-dimensional bulk spin-Dirac index. This is NOT
an assertion about every operator on every space carrying a flat
coefficient bundle. Boundary terms, singular defects, end conditions,
non-flat Higgs data and different gradings must be included in their
own problems. Flat real characteristic classes also do not classify
flat holonomy or its secondary invariants.

Consequently GAP6's curvature duty is valid for a proposed smooth
closed bulk-index route with those hypotheses. Its unqualified “no
index” and “chirality is a statement about curvature” do not follow.
This is not a claim that a physical curved frame has already been
derived, nor that a complex representation alone is Lorentz chirality.

## The repository already supplies a conditional counterexample

Keep R19's actual m202 compact core Q and rank-one unitary flat local
system. Its covariant base cochains have dimensions (1,2,1,0),
v=(x-1,y-1)^t and f=(-(y-1)P/x,(x-1)P/x). Since f v=0 they form a
complex. If the character is nontrivial and P is nonzero, both maps
have rank one and all base cohomology vanishes.

For k disjoint contractible proper source neighborhoods N, restriction
to their degree-zero fibres gives the relative mapping cone

    d0(c)=(v c,r c),     d1(a,b)=f a,
    dimensions=(1,2+k,1,0),     r_j in U(1).

For k>0, d0 is injective even at trivial transport. Its Euler
characteristic is -k, and its odd-minus-even graded index is k. For
nontrivial nonexceptional transport the kernel dimensions are exactly
(0,k,0,0). At trivial transport, or at the nontrivial P=0 locus, they
are (0,k+1,1,0). These are R19's existing exact formulas, not newly
chosen three-generation data. The current verification derives f from
the relator and computes the ranks again.

In R19's separately specified strong-source problem, the maximal
weighted Hilbert complex is compared analytically to H*(C,T;L), and
the opposite charged sector to H*(C,E;L^-1). Complementary-boundary
duality reverses degrees and conjugates the coefficient, hence changes
the index sign. Conjugating L on the same relative domain alone does
not change the computed Betti numbers. Calling both “the dual” loses
the domain information. R19's singular sources, boundary choices,
field equations, multiplet dictionary and anomaly debt stay priced.

This is enough to disprove an unrestricted mathematical no-index
claim for flat coefficients. It is NOT a source-free chiral vacuum,
an anomaly-free Standard Model or a solved genesis-to-physics map.

## Reattaching cores is a changed problem

R30's resolved complex includes the k degree-zero core states s:

    d0(c,s)=(v c,r c+T s),     d1(a,b)=f a,
    dimensions=(1+k,2+k,1,0).

Its Euler characteristic and total graded index are zero for every
attachment T. At T=0, the cohomology is the relative cohomology PLUS
k even core states. For invertible T the maps
i0(c)=(c,-T^-1 r c), i1(a)=(a,0), projections p onto the base and
homotopy H(a,b)=(0,T^-1 b) give p i=id and id-i p=dH+Hd. Thus it is
chain-homotopy equivalent to the ordinary base, as R30 already proved.
The new checks do not assert that T is an actual stationary PDE
coupling or that its singular values are physical masses.

The relative and the resolved complex for k>0 CANNOT be related by
a grading-preserving chain-homotopy equivalence: their Euler
characteristics differ. Recovering the relative answer by throwing
away the even states is therefore not a faithful re-description of
this resolved complex. It does not kill the relative problem on its
own supplied domain, nor every other completion of it.

## The stronger duty on an actual physical join

Suppose Hilbert spaces with chiral gradings carry closed Fredholm
blocks D1 and D2 and a proposed equivalence U. If U is unitary,
grading-preserving, bijective on the graph domains, intertwines the
operators and the declared gauge action, and transports the adjoint
domains as well, it gives bijections on both kernels. The indices
are equal. The same claim with an anti-grading map reverses the index.
This is substitution in the intertwining equation and its adjoint,
not an assumption that all covers or fillings meet these hypotheses.

Equal indices are only NECESSARY. They do not certify any U, equality
of norms, interactions, anomalies or a physical equivalence. A cover
normally is not a bijective map of all Hilbert spaces; a filling
normally changes the domain. Those operations may be legitimate
physical changes, but their added/removed states and boundary fluxes
must be accounted for instead of being called lossless reductions.

At a bookkeeping cut, the two normals are opposite. For the full
form trace c(n)=epsilon(n)-iota(n), the Green pairing is diag(c,-c).
Matching all components with U=(I,I)^t is maximally isotropic: its
rank is half the trace dimension and U^dagger diag(c,-c) U=0. A
normal jump invisible to tangential pullback has nonzero pairing
against the matched scalar. Therefore matching tangential data alone
does not supply a self-adjoint transmission law. This is R30's prior
point, independently checked here with bit-mask exterior matrices.
