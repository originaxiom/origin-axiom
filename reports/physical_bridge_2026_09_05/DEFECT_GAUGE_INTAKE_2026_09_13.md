## Proposed next calculation, still unsealed and unexecuted

The question is whether a gauge-covariant finite-width source model can
both support the desired background and control the extra gauge sector.
The proposed H-singlet scalar has charge four, as in R21, and is defined
on contractible tubes rather than as a parallel field over the whole M.
Declare its internal and four-dimensional kinetic terms, potential,
coupling to the commuting residual, and tube/end boundary variations.
This adds a bosonic regulator model; a full-parent supersymmetric lift,
fermions and source selection are not supplied by that declaration.

The intended discriminating tasks are:

1. Derive gauge covariance, currents and ALL coupled first variations.
   Test a regulated Poisson/core construction with fixed smooth outer
   data. Do not call a prescribed external density dynamical.
2. Derive the actual transverse extra-U1 fluctuation operator from the
   quadratic action, including internal profile variation. A mass read
   from the constant-mode matrix element alone need not be its lowest mass.
3. Compare finite-width positivity with the shrinking-tube limit on the
   fixed bulk Hilbert space. Use two-sided controls: a no-Higgs case,
   finite cores, actual hyperbolic radial measure, full compact/cusp
   exhaustion and a codimension-one comparator.
4. Check whether a proposed positive line-trace mass term is a closed
   form on that Hilbert space. Distinguish this from a renormalized
   defect theory or one with an enlarged kinetic/state space.
5. Use an independently diagonalized finite Kaluza--Klein toy matrix
   to test a zero-mode-only truncation; never infer the full geometric
   theorem from that flat toy spectrum.
6. Only after a compatible source/gauge result, compute its actual
   fermion domain and full anomaly. Added bosonic fields alone do not
   cancel the existing chiral-fermion anomaly. Smoothing a singular core
   does not automatically preserve R18/R19's domain or R26's hypotheses.

Expected outcome before formal sealing: a finite-width positive
construction may exist, while a bare zero-radius gauge-mass argument
may fail. Both sides must be tested and their exact hypotheses preserved.
This expectation is NOT a new scientific result. Finite physical width,
renormalized end dynamics, nonabelian fields and differently sourced
fermions remain alternatives if they actually change the failing premise.
