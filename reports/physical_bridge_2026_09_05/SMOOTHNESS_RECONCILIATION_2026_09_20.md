## What the sources actually license

For the compact core M of an orientable finite-volume cusped
hyperbolic three-manifold, compose geometric holonomy with a
principal embedding into complex adjoint E6. For t torus cusps,
the adjoint coefficient system has H0=0, dim H1=dim H2=6t,
and injective restriction to boundary H1. Ishibashi--Mizuno's
Proposition 5.7, Corollary 5.11 and Theorem 5.12 explicitly extend
the cohomology mechanism to centerless semisimple groups, using
the principal exponents. Their Section 5.4 assumes trivial center;
we apply it to adjoint E6, not silently to every global form.
This is boundary-adjoint regularity, not itself a printed theorem
about four-dimensional particles or character-variety smoothness.
[Primary text, v2, Sections 5.4--5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00816v2).

Falbel--Guilloux Theorem 5 bounds each relevant character component
below by rank(G)t-dim(G)chi(M)+dim Z(G), provided it contains a
strongly irreducible, boundary-regular representation. Use Theorem 5,
NOT their earlier SL(n) theorem requiring Zariski density in the
whole target group. A principal SL2 image is not dense in E6.
[Primary text, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.00567v1).

For a good representation, the character-scheme tangent is H1.
Complete reducibility and a zero-dimensional centralizer establish
irreducibility here; equality of the centralizer with the center
must also be checked. Irreducibility alone does not imply that
equality in every reductive group. These distinctions are explicit
in Sikora's Proposition 8, Corollary 17 and Theorem 53(2).
[Primary text, Sections 3--4 and 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.2589v3).

## Application to the already-banked principal point

1. Every finite-index subgroup of the lattice has the same connected
   SL2 Zariski closure. Its principal image is reductive. The adjoint
   decomposition is V3+V9+V11+V15+V17+V23, with no trivial summand.
   Its Lie centralizer is therefore zero. If the image lay in a proper
   parabolic, complete reducibility would put it in a Levi with a
   positive-dimensional central torus, contradicting that centralizer.
   This establishes strong irreducibility, not full E6 density.
2. A nontrivial cusp unipotent maps to a regular unipotent. The
   peripheral centralizer thus has dimension six. For goodness,
   choose a principal triple with h=2 rho-check and e having every
   simple-root coefficient nonzero. Anything centralizing the triple
   centralizes the regular h, hence lies in its torus; centralizing e
   then forces all simple-root characters to be one. It is central.
   In adjoint E6 the stabilizer is trivial.
3. chi(M)=0, so the local component dimension is at least 6t.
   The character-scheme tangent dimension is exactly 6t by the
   cohomology calculation and goodness. Local dimension cannot exceed
   tangent dimension. Equality makes the local ring regular: the
   principal character is a smooth point of complex dimension 6t.
   For one cusp this is the existing six-dimensional smooth germ.

This supplies an all-orders argument without extrapolating from two
finite primes or from a cubic obstruction. A simply connected E6
lift, when specified, has the same local adjoint deformation problem
under the finite central isogeny. No new global lifting assertion is
needed for the adjoint statement above. The argument does not classify
all components, prove every deformed representation dense, or settle
every other claim in B265/B274.
