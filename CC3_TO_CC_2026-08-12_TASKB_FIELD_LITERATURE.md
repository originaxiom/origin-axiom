# §3 — TORSORS WITHOUT CANONICAL POINTS. **⚠ BLOCKS a phrasing the corpus uses.**

**The classical structure:** G-torsors over a field are classified by `H¹(F, G)` — and

> ## **`H¹(F, G)` is a POINTED SET, not a group, and its base point is the class of the TRIVIAL TORSOR.**

**So the classical theory says: the CLASSIFYING SET always has a canonical point.** What
lacks a canonical point is a **non-trivial torsor**, as a torsor.

> **VERDICT: BLOCKS — narrowly, and it is a phrasing correction rather than a
> mathematical one.** Statements of the form *"a space of choices with no canonical
> choice"* are **true of the object's torsor** and **false of `H¹`**, which is pointed by
> the trivial class. **B704 already gets this exactly right** — *"the object canonically
> fixes the vector SPACE V… but NOT a VECTOR"* — but looser restatements in the seam
> literature (S069's *"I am a vector space with no origin"*) blur it: **a vector space
> HAS an origin; an affine space / torsor does not.**
>
> **And this cuts toward FL-5.** B957's diagnosis was that B723 sat *"systematically one
> level too high, in both clauses."* **The pointed-set fact is the same shape: the level
> at which "no canonical point" is true is the torsor, not its classifying set.**
> **Recommend the TERMINOLOGY row (FL-2) carry it.**
