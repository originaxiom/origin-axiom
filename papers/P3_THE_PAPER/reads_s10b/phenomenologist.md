Scope of this lens: E6 GUTs and the E6SSM, chirality and vector-like matter, generations, anomaly
cancellation, seesaw/neutrino masses, the eta-model Z' and its collider/flavour bounds, gauge
coupling unification and the GQW relation, doublet-triplet splitting, Yukawa textures and

General finding before the itemised list: this is an unusually self-policing manuscript for the
genre. Most of the traps a phenomenologist would normally set — GQW presented as a prediction,
the anomaly-cancellation census presented as if it used the object, sin²θW's miss undersold,
chirality quietly assumed — are already defused in the text itself (Recognition section,
non-claims, three-column ledger). The defects below are the ones that survive that defense.

## FATAL

None found at the level of "a referee rejects the paper on this sentence." The physics content that

instead as the answer to (a) below, since it is a property of the whole paper rather than a repairable

## SERIOUS

**S1. The paper's only proposed mechanism for actually supplying the chirality bit is reported as
excluded — but that verdict is invisible from the abstract and the non-claims section, the two places
a phenomenologist reader will stop.**

Location: the verdict is delivered at line 988 (single long paragraph, "The closing that could carry
the bit, and what it costs"): *"no η-neutral Majorana mass exists, U(1)_η is exact above the soft
scale, and under the cubic's one coupling the neutrinos come out Dirac at the up-quark masses — six to
eleven orders of magnitude above the sub-electronvolt scale, from the up quark to the top,"* and,
further down the same line, *"every charged-fermion mass matrix is complex symmetric with zero
diagonal ... against factors of about 273, 50 and 17 in the up, down and charged-lepton sectors ...
The three-apex design is excluded as it stands."* This is reproduced only in compressed form in the
Wall section's third column (line 1432: *"a Majorana mass without a 27 sector of the opposite
chirality,"* *"a tree-level flavour hierarchy from the apex design (the hollow texture)"*) and in the
provenance appendix (lines 1524, 1527).

Contrast: "vector-like" and "no supercharge" — the other two headline admissions the task asks about —
*are* in the abstract, verbatim: *"they are vector-like: the chirality bit is what the object
withholds"* (line 67) and *"its carrier admits no supercharge, by three exact negatives"* (line 66).
The non-claims section (lines 528–546) also states the vector-like point explicitly as item 4
(line 533–534) but contains no non-claim of the form "we do not claim the object supplies a neutrino
mass mechanism" or "we do not claim a realistic Yukawa texture." A reader who reads only the abstract
and the non-claims section — which the paper's own introduction treats as the two places "a reader who
accepts nothing else" can audit (line 107–108 for the ledger; the non-claims section is offered as the
list of things not being claimed) — comes away knowing the spectrum is vector-like and unsupersymmetric,
but not knowing that the one candidate dynamical route to a chiral, flavour-realistic theory the paper
itself designed and computed has already failed on both neutrino mass and Yukawa hierarchy grounds.

Why it fails: this is exactly the placement problem the task calls out. The admission exists and is
honest, but its severity (a complete, stated failure of both the seesaw and the flavour hierarchy for
the paper's own best design) is diluted by depth of burial relative to admissions of comparable or
lesser weight that were promoted to the abstract.

Minimal repair, in the paper's own register: add one non-claim, e.g. *"that the closing's G2-apex
design supplies a neutrino mass mechanism or a viable Yukawa hierarchy: §6 designs the closing that
would carry the bit and reports it excluded, on both counts, by its own criteria"* — and one clause in
the abstract's third paragraph alongside the existing "no supercharge" sentence, since it is the same
kind of admission and already has a slot reserved for it.

**S2. The Z′ flavour/collider phenomenology paragraph gives specific numbers before the caveat that
voids them, and the paragraph never states the paragraph's most damaging consequence: keeping three
vector-like copies of the complete 27 light enough to be relevant to TeV–PeV-scale flavour physics is
in tension with gauge-coupling perturbativity, and the paper does not address this.**

Location, ordering: the numerical bounds — *"M_{Z'}/g_{Z'} ≳ 1.5×10² TeV and ε_K with an O(1) phase
≳ 1.7×10³ TeV"* (line 980), the third-family alternative and its comparison to *"the dilepton
searches~\cite{atlasdilepton} (about 5 TeV for universal benchmarks)"* (lines 981–982), and the Z′
normalisation *"Tr Q'² / Tr Y_GUT² = 6210/9"* (line 984) — are all given before the disclaimer that
begins on the same line: *"This analysis assumes a chiral Standard-Model fermion content with
independent left- and right-handed rotations; the closing's own spectrum is vector-like, so the bounds
constrain a supersymmetric model suggested by the closing, not the closing"* (lines 984–986), and
*"a vector-like pair admits a gauge-invariant supersymmetric mass and can decouple, so the model must
further assume the copies are kept light, an assumption we do not derive"* (line 986). A reader who
stops at the TeV numbers — which is how this kind of paragraph is normally read, by a phenomenologist
looking for the bound — has already left with a number before reaching the sentence that says the
number may not apply to anything the object supplies.

Location, missing content: nowhere in the paper (checked: no occurrence of "Landau," "perturbativ,"
"beta function," beyond the one-line mention at 1289 that the SUSY crossing with the extra 27-matter
"alters the one-loop coefficients" and "has not been run") is it stated that three light vector-like
copies of the complete 27 — each roughly the matter content of two chiral generations once mirrored —
push the gauge one-loop beta coefficients up sharply; keeping such matter at or near the TeV–PeV window
invoked by the Z′ bounds risks non-perturbative gauge couplings well below any GUT scale, which is the
textbook reason model-builders who add vector-like 27's (in the actual E6SSM literature this cites,
King–Moretti–Nevzorov) keep such exotics at the TeV scale in small numbers with matter content tuned
for one-loop unification, not at "three complete copies" as an unexamined default. This is precisely
the gauge-coupling-unification territory the paper elsewhere treats with care (the desert-unification
crossing, §Recognition), so the omission here is inconsistent with the paper's own standard.

Why it fails: a hedge that arrives after the numbers it should have gated, on a topic (vector-like
matter and gauge coupling running) squarely inside this lens's competence, and a genuine physical cost
of the "kept light" assumption that is never named even though the paper already flags the assumption
itself as underived.

Minimal repair: move the disclaimer sentence to the front of the paragraph, immediately after
"a family-non-universal Z′ with coupling g_{Z'}" and before any TeV number is given; and add one clause
to the same sentence that already says "an assumption we do not derive" naming the cost: e.g. *"...an
assumption we do not derive, and one whose cost is not small: three light copies of the complete 27
would need to be reconciled with one-loop gauge-coupling perturbativity between the Z' scale and any
unification scale, which this paper does not check."*

## MINOR

**M1. "excluded by the collider limits" is asserted without a citation, unlike its sibling sentence two
lines later.** Line 977: *"a Z′ broken only at the electroweak scale, with O(g) couplings to first- and
second-family leptons, is excluded by the collider limits"* — no reference. Compare line 981–982, where
the parallel third-family claim is backed by `\cite{atlasdilepton}`. A referee in this lens will ask
which limits (LEP contact-interaction bounds, or a dilepton search) and at what coupling. Minimal
repair: cite the specific bound (e.g. LEP2 four-fermion contact-interaction limits, or the same ATLAS
dilepton reference with its first/second-generation coupling assumption stated) at the point of use.

**M2. The hollow-texture identity is stated as if self-evident, with no citation and no indication of
whether it is a known linear-algebra fact or original to this paper.** Line 988: *"every
charged-fermion mass matrix is complex symmetric with zero diagonal, whose largest singular value
equals the sum of the other two exactly."* I checked this numerically (three independent
complex/real test matrices, including a generic case with off-diagonal entries 1, 2, 3): the identity
holds exactly in every case tried, so the claim itself is not wrong. But the paper's own
"Recognition" section (§Recognition) exists specifically to police the known/new boundary, and this
identity — a clean statement about Takagi singular values of hollow symmetric 3×3 matrices, which is
exactly the class of fact this lens is asked to check for prior art — is used without being placed on
either side of that boundary. If it is a known result (it has the flavour of a folklore fact from the
zero-texture literature), Recognition should list it; if it is original to this paper, it should be
stated as a lemma with a proof or a proof sketch rather than asserted in the middle of a design
paragraph. Minimal repair: one clause in Recognition, or a one-line footnote with either a citation or
"this identity for hollow complex-symmetric 3×3 matrices is elementary and we have not sought a prior
reference."

**M3. The specific G2 orbifold cone asserted to exist is not separately cited from the general
anomaly-inflow mechanism it is built to host.** Line 988: *"The local model exists — the G2 cone over
CP³/2T, the twistor cone of S⁴/2T, whose E6 and A1 lines meet only at the apex, where the enhancement
is to E7 (the E7 point); its geometry is exact and its count, one 27 per apex, is the literature's
rule and is cited, not re-derived."* Only Acharya–Witten `\cite{aw}` is cited nearby, and that paper
establishes the general mechanism (chiral matter at ADE-meeting conical points via anomaly inflow), not
this specific quotient of the Bryant–Salamon cone over CP³ by 2T. A referee in this lens will want
either a citation to a construction of this specific orbifold cone or an explicit statement that the
existence claim ("the local model exists") is being asserted by this paper rather than quoted from one.
Minimal repair: either add the specific reference, or reword to "we construct this cone as the further
quotient of the Bryant–Salamon cone over CP³ by 2T; it is not itself in the cited literature," matching
the paper's own discipline elsewhere (e.g. line 999, "this is the literature's rule and is cited, not
re-derived" is a model sentence for exactly this kind of disclosure, and its absence here is the
inconsistency).

**M4. "About fifty experimental standard deviations" (line 65, repeated line 1283) does not name which
experimental sin²θW extraction and uncertainty is being used, and the paper has already had to correct
this exact number once.** The MSbar-scheme sin²θ-hat_W(M_Z) global-fit value carries an unusually small
quoted uncertainty (of order a few ×10⁻⁵) precisely because it is extracted from a fit that assumes
Standard-Model radiative corrections; direct single-measurement extractions of sin²θ_eff (LEP A_FB,
SLD A_LR) differ from each other by several sigma and have individually much larger uncertainties. A
"fifty-sigma" statement is correspondingly scheme/source-dependent in a way that "0.9%" is not, and
this section has already required one correction (lines 1280–1283, the withdrawn "16σ" and "within
1σ" framing). Minimal repair: name the specific extraction and its quoted uncertainty in a footnote at
line 65 (the Recognition section at 1277–1289 is the natural place, and could simply add the source of
the "50" once, since the abstract's line 65 could then just say "about fifty experimental standard
deviations (Recognition states the source)").

**M5. The E6 tensor-product fact licensing "no adjoint expectation value can give any 27 fermion a
mass" (line 787) is standard (Slansky) but is not given a citation at point of use**, even though
Slansky `\cite{slansky}` is already in the bibliography and used for exactly this kind of fact
elsewhere (line 51). Minor, since Recognition's closing "what is not on this list" audit would likely
have caught it had it been flagged; it wasn't. Minimal repair: add `\cite{slansky}` at line 787.

## (a) The single strongest objection to the paper as a whole, and does the paper already answer it?

The strongest objection a phenomenologist brings to this manuscript is: a paper titled "Standard-Model
structure from the figure-eight knot complement" delivers, as its only unconditional matter-content
result, a spectrum that is vector-like — which means it lacks the one feature (parity violation via
chirality) that makes the real Standard Model a nontrivial physical theory rather than a classification
exercise in representation theory. Everything downstream that a phenomenologist actually cares about —
generations, a seesaw, a flavour hierarchy, an anomaly structure that does more than trivially cancel
on a real representation — either requires chirality as an input the object does not supply, or, in the
one place the paper tries to manufacture it dynamically (the G2-apex design), fails on both fronts it
tests (neutrino mass, Yukawa texture). Does the paper already answer this? Substantially yes, and more
honestly than almost any paper in this genre: the title's own second clause ("what is forced, what is
withheld, and what must be supplied"), the abstract's explicit "vector-like" sentence, an entire section
(§6, "The chirality bit") devoted to proving where and why the bit is withheld, a non-claim item
addressed to exactly this point, and a three-column ledger in §Wall that places "the chirality bit on
the member m004 itself" in the "not yet had" column rather than smuggling it into "parameter-free."
What the paper does not do — the residue this review's S1 targets — is put the sharpest version of the
failure (that its own best attempt at a mechanism also fails, not just that the base object is
vector-like) in the two places a skimming referee reads first.

## (b) What does the paper actually prove, in one sentence, and does the abstract say the same thing?

What a phenomenologist referee would say the paper proves: starting from one arithmetic invariant of
one hyperbolic 3-manifold, the anomaly-cancellation conditions alone (not the manifold) force the
Standard Model's charge assignment up to a relabelling and its global form up to two stated inputs, the
construction contains no route to any Standard-Model numerical value, and its matter content — on
every closing computed, including a supersymmetric one modelled on the exceptional supersymmetric
Standard Model — is vector-like, with the sole proposed route to chirality (anomaly inflow at a G2
conical point) designed, computed, and found to fail its own neutrino-mass and flavour-hierarchy tests.
The abstract says close to this — it states the vector-like result, the value-withholding result, and
the global-form derivation with its listed inputs — but, per (a) and S1, it does not carry the second
half of the one-sentence verdict above (that the paper's own attempted chirality mechanism was tested
and excluded); a reader of the abstract alone would believe the chirality question is simply open where
the object is silent, not that one specific, fully worked design for closing it has already been ruled
out by the paper's own hand.
