## 1. Lines 499–501 and 688–695 — the chirality Non-claim can be STRENGTHENED from a census to a theorem over an infinite family

> §Non-claims: *"that three \emph{chiral} generations are derived. Three complete generations are
> exhibited on the object's own closings, in mirror pairs; they are vector-like, and
> \S\ref{sec:chirality} **proves at every computed sector** where the chirality bit is withheld."*

> §14 line 690: *"the section exists to say \emph{where} the bit is withheld"* … *"the spectral wall
> has held **on every sector computed so far**."*

Both phrasings are **sector-by-sector** — a census, with the honest open-ended tail that a census
carries. The record now supports something stronger for the *metallic* statement, though **not** for
the spectral wall (see the scope line below).

**B1346 §4 (F5, generalised beyond the handoff's own claim) + B1341.** Every metallic unit `λ_m`
solves `x² − mx − 1 = 0`, so the product of its roots is `−1` **for every m** — hence:

> `norm(λ_m) = −1` ⟺ `det = −1` ⟺ the tick carries the swap ⟺ the double tick is `X·swap(X)`,
> self-mirroring ⟺ **chirality is zero** ⟺ there is no action at the half step.

One fact in five languages, holding across the **entire metallic family**, not the golden alone. And
the escape is identified rather than merely absent: `2 + √3` has norm `+1` and evades this precisely
because `x² − 4x + 1` is **not** of the form `x² − mx − 1` — it is *outside the family*.

**What this licenses.** Line 54 of the theorem table already reads *"the mirror is swap × arrow"*;
that can be recorded as an **identity over the whole metallic family** rather than a property of the
object. Where the Non-claim says *"proves at every computed sector"*, the metallic half of it is now
forced by an identity over an infinite family — a stronger sentence, and shorter.

**Scope, stated so the strengthening is not over-read.** This forces chirality-zero for the
**metallic family of grammars**. It does **not** close the spectral-wall sectors, and it does not
convert the Non-claim into a claim: three *chiral* generations remain **not derived**. If anything it
makes the Non-claim firmer — the object's vector-likeness is now structural, not circumstantial.

## 2. Line 775 — "anomaly-free by the conjugate generations" is TRUE and carries ZERO BITS

> §8: *"The tree-level vacuum of the ninth leaves one abelian factor beyond the Standard Model, a
> family-non-universal $Z'$, **anomaly-free by the conjugate generations** and neutral on every field
> that acquires an expectation value."*

**This is the one place where the record now supports LESS than the sentence implies.**

**B1340 Q3 (the load-bearing question; a finding about the RECORD, not about the joker).** On the
record's **vector-like** vacuum — `3×27 + 3×27̄` — **all six anomaly coefficients vanish identically
as polynomials in all six parameters.** Every SM-commuting direction is anomaly-free there. So
"anomaly-free" **cannot fail** on that spectrum: it is not a check this `Z′` passed, it is a check
nothing there could fail. The phrase attributes to *this* `Z′` a property belonging to *the vector-like
spectrum*.

The register already carries the corrected form — `docs/FALSIFIER_REGISTER.md` P9 now reads
*"anomaly-free **only on the record's VECTOR-LIKE spectrum, and that check carries ZERO BITS**"*. The
paper's sentence has not been updated to match. **Suggested minimal repair: attribute the property to
the spectrum, not to the `Z′`** — e.g. *"anomaly-free, as every SM-commuting direction is on this
vector-like spectrum"*. That is shorter than what is there and it is true.

Two vacuous falsifiers this bench itself proposed are owned as vacuous in the same arc, so the
paper is in good company: E₆ anomalies over a full **27** cannot fail (E₆ is anomaly-safe), and SM
anomalies over `N` chiral generations cannot fail (the conditions are linear in `N` and one
generation already cancels). **The coefficient that *can* fail is the cubic of a family-non-universal
direction.** Which is item 3.

## 3. Lines 773–780 — the counterpart, and it is sharp: imposing chirality KILLS P9's `Z′` as stated

identity:

> Over a **chiral** spectrum, the family part of any anomaly-free `U(1)′` has the shape
> **`(0, t, −t)`** up to permutation: **one generation exactly neutral, the other two exactly

**The record's family part is `(−10, 5, 5)`.** It satisfies `Σf = 0` but has `f₁f₂f₃ = −250 ≠ 0`, so
it is **not** of that shape. Stated plainly:

> **The `Z′` of §8 survives on the vector-like spectrum and dies the moment chirality is imposed.**

This is worth the paper's space because it is **the first place in the record where the anomaly
conditions do work rather than pass trivially** — six free parameters reduced to a one-parameter
family. It also answers, in the paper's own terms, why the vector-like/chiral gap is not a soft
mismatch to be closed later: chirality is a **declared input** (C3), and B1340 prices it. The
object's fixed-locus theorem gives `|det(A − I)| ∈ {0, 4}`, and **3 never occurs** — so every
consequence downstream of "three chiral generations" inherits that debt, by name.

**A `Z′` of the forced shape `(0, t, −t)` is a different `Z′`** — one generation exactly neutral — and
the scale regime of lines 776–779 would have to be re-derived for it. Not done here, and not to be
assumed transferable.

## 4. Lines 507 and 1208 — two bare Non-claims about *action* that can now be made PRECISE

**Correction to this note's own first draft:** it said the paper contains no occurrence of "action".
It does — four, and two of them are exactly the sentences this item is about. The word is bare
("Lagrangian", "symplectic" and "least action" are genuinely absent — `0` hits each):

> §Non-claims line 507: *"that any dynamics, rate, or **action** is supplied."*

> §Conclusion line 1208: *"There is no dynamics, no rate, and no **action** beyond the structural
> statements above."*

Both are unqualified. B1341 makes them **precise in the object's favour**, and it is a theorem the
corpus did not previously hold:

> **A discrete Lagrangian `L(a,b)` generates, through `∂₂L(a,b) + ∂₁L(b,c) = 0`, a map that ALWAYS
> preserves `ω = ∂₁∂₂L · da∧db`, hence has `det +1`. An orientation-reversing map of a surface
> preserves no area form. Therefore an ANTI-SYMPLECTIC MAP HAS NO DISCRETE LAGRANGIAN.**

Confirmed by exhaustive solve over the general quadratic ansatz (the DEL relation always reads
`q(a+c) + 2(p+r)b = 0` — `a` and `c` forced to share a coefficient where the recursion needs them
opposite). **And it reaches the object:** `T` pulls the invariant Gelfand–Leray form `dx∧dy/I_z` back
to *minus* itself on **every** leaf, not only the one where the map linearises.

**Provenance, stated because this bench got it wrong once.** B21 banked the anti-Poisson fact and
**B448** banked the `det −1` *and* the identification of `T₁` as the half-monodromy; neither drew the
Lagrangian consequence. An earlier version of this arc claimed B448's results as new; that was
**withdrawn and re-credited** in a B1341 addendum. If the paper carries this, **B448 is cited for the
half-monodromy and the determinant, B1341 for the Lagrangian obstruction alone.**

**What this licenses at lines 507 and 1208.** The blanket *"no action"* can become a **located**
statement: **an action exists exactly at the monodromy, and is provably obstructed exactly at the
formal square root of the monodromy.** That is strictly more informative than "no action", and it is
*more* honest rather than less — it replaces a blanket disclaimer with a theorem naming the boundary,
which is precisely what line 1209 says the paper is for (*"a boundary — drawn in both directions,
with the negatives proved rather than reported"*).

**Do not over-read it.** This supplies **no dynamics and no rate** — the other two items in both
sentences stand untouched, and "an action at the monodromy" is a discrete variational structure on
the object's own map, not a field-theoretic action. The Non-claims should be *narrowed to dynamics and
rate*, not dropped. It is also item 1's fifth language: "no action at the half step" **is**
