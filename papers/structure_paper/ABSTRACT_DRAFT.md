## The abstract (main draft, ~260 words)

> The four Klein invariants of the binary tetrahedral group 2T under a
> principal sl(2) in the split Chevalley algebra 𝔢₆ over ℚ — of degrees 8,
> 14, 16, 22 — span an abelian subalgebra u(1)⁴, the charge torus C. We prove
> that successive centralizers of elements of C ("measurements") force a
> rigid cascade. First, the plane of the degree-8 and degree-16 charges
> stratifies centralizer dimensions as 12/30/46, with exactly three
> dimension-46 lines forming the Galois orbit of an irreducible cubic with
> constant term 13³; each line's centralizer is so(10)⊕u(1), and the three
> enhanced centralizers tile 𝔢₆ over the triality core so(8)⊕u(1)² with a
> cyclic bracket law on three 16-dimensional sectors. This tiling is the
> Barton–Sudbery magic-square algebra L(𝕆_split, ℂ′_split) by an explicit
> structure-constants isomorphism (Jacobi verified on all 76,076 basis
> triples; the isomorphism on all 3,003 basis pairs). Second, an algebraic
> wall point of the same torus refines the centralizer to
> su(3)⊕su(2)⊕u(1)³ exactly; the centralizer ladder never passes through
> su(5)⊕u(1), and the wall point is complex in the split frame at every
> Galois root. Among all torus-stabilizing involutions in a 128-representative
> census, a sign-locking theorem kills twelve of the sixteen sign patterns
> and exactly two representatives — both outer-twisted — realize the wall as
> real, both naming the quaternionic form e₆(2). Finally, the Hermitian twist
> governing the cascade's derived scale data is characterized with no
> residual freedom as the second wall conjugation's sign character on the
> 27-dimensional representation, with exact norm laws −(953/2304)² and
> −(953/2304)³. All verdict-bearing computations are exact and re-runnable;
> verification is internal to the project, and novelty relative to the
> literature is not claimed pending specialist review.

## Short version (~110 words, for venues with tight limits)

> In the split Chevalley 𝔢₆ over ℚ, the four Klein invariants of the binary
> tetrahedral group span an abelian torus u(1)⁴ whose successive centralizers
> force a rigid cascade: three Galois-conjugate breakings to so(10)⊕u(1),
> tiling 𝔢₆ over so(8)⊕u(1)² with a cyclic law — identified with the
> Barton–Sudbery magic square M(𝕆,ℂ) by explicit isomorphism — then a second,
> algebraic wall point landing on su(3)⊕su(2)⊕u(1)³ exactly, skipping
> su(5)⊕u(1). The wall is real precisely in the quaternionic form e₆(2), via
> outer-twisted alignments only (a sign-locking theorem makes the pattern
> unique), and the associated Hermitian twist is characterized exactly on the
> 27. All computations are exact and re-runnable; verification is internal;
> novelty is not claimed pending specialist review.
