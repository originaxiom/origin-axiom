ABSTRACT  (1917 characters -- arXiv's limit is 1920)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let $R^mL^m$, $m\ge1$, be the metallic family of Sturmian morphisms. We characterize it intrinsically: the hyperbolic unimodular classes whose dominant eigenvalue has purely periodic continued fraction of period one are exactly those of determinant $-1$, with traces exactly the integers $m\ge1$. The characterization is by trace and determinant, not conjugacy class (at $m=6$ a second class occurs); period one is a declared, priced input.

Four exceptionality criteria select the golden member $m=1$; three collapse to one arithmetic condition, each reducing to $m^2=1$.

The golden member is the figure-eight knot complement. From there we add no new data: we measure, passing to centralizers, and track where the centralizer jumps. What we do add is a small number of declared choices, and we price them: of the 43 links in the chain, 39 carry no declared choice and 4 are axioms. We do not claim that list is provably complete. The target algebra comes from the manifold's own arithmetic -- reducing the trace-field representation at the ramified prime gives $\pi_1(4_1)\twoheadrightarrow SL(2,\mathbb{F}_3)\cong 2T$, and McKay gives $\mathfrak{e}_6$ with a canonical 4-dimensional charge algebra $C$.

We prove $C$ is toral, not merely abelian, so every cascade centralizer is a Levi subalgebra; the terminal type $A_2\oplus A_1$ is then forced by the classification of Levi subsystems of $\mathfrak{e}_6$. The rung dimensions form an explicit eleven-element set, every value attained. The charge field is $K\cong\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^3-12x-5)$: monogenic, totally real, discriminant $6237$, class number one.

The ceiling is a theorem about the method: every centralizer of a subset of $C$ contains a Cartan subalgebra, so every rung has rank $6$ and any lower-rank target is unreachable by measurements taken inside $C$ -- a hypothesis we flag, not drop. We make no physical identification and do not treat real forms.
