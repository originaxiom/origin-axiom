\title[The golden grammar]{The golden grammar: four independent selections
inside a canonical family, and a parameter-free chain to an exceptional Lie algebra}

\author{Drit\"ero Mehmetaj}
\address{Independent Researcher}
\email{driteroi@gmail.com}

\date{\today}

\subjclass[2020]{Primary 57K10, 11R16, 57M50; Secondary 17B25, 46L37, 11J06}
\keywords{once-punctured torus bundles, Sturmian and Christoffel words, continued
fractions, metallic means, McKay correspondence, Jones index, exceptional Lie
algebras, cone-manifold geometric transition}

\begin{abstract}
Let $R^mL^m$, $m\ge1$, be the metallic family of Sturmian morphisms. We prove that
this family is not a modelling choice: it is exactly the class of hyperbolic
unimodular classes whose dominant eigenvalue has purely periodic continued fraction of
period one. Inside it we exhibit \emph{three logically independent selections of the
golden member $m=1$, and a fourth criterion narrowing to $\{1,2\}$}---each over the
whole infinite family and each anchored in a classical theorem: a homological one, $H_1(M_m)\cong\Z\oplus(\Z/m)^2$, so that only
$m=1$ is a knot complement; an arithmetic one, $|\SL(2,\Z/(m^2{+}4))|$ being the order
of an exceptional binary polyhedral group only at $m=1$; a subfactor one, the metallic
mean clearing the Jones index wall only at $m=1$; and an arithmeticity criterion which
selects $\{1,2\}$ and kills $m\ge3$, included with its limit stated because it is the
criterion a hyperbolic geometer applies first and it does \emph{not} single out the
golden.

The golden member is the figure-eight knot complement. We then follow its own
arithmetic, with no measured quantity entering any computation, to a $78$-dimensional
exceptional Lie algebra carrying a canonical four-dimensional charge torus, and record
the centralizer stratification, which terminates at algebraic type $A_2\oplus A_1$
with three-dimensional centre. The charge field is computed exactly:
$K\cong\Q[x]/(x^3-12x-5)$, monogenic, totally real, $\disc K=6237=3^4\cdot7\cdot11$,
class number one.

We report the negative half with equal weight. Amphichirality is an \emph{iff}
condition on the word, so the vanishing of the Chern--Simons invariant is a property of
the entire family rather than an accident at $m=1$; five pre-registered comparisons
with measurement all missed; and the surface of comparisons the construction licenses
was enumerated and found exhausted. We make no physical identification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The question}
Fix a principle: \emph{a description that cannot terminate}. Ask what the least such
description is, follow the answer without importing anything from outside, and record
what it costs. This paper carries that out and reports both halves of the result.

The first half is a selection theorem. Minimal aperiodic description is Sturmian
(Morse--Hedlund), and the slopes whose continued fractions are purely periodic of
period one form the \emph{metallic} family. We prove that this family is canonical
(\S\ref{sec:canonical}), and then that its golden member is singled out inside it
\emph{four independent ways} (\S\ref{sec:four}). The golden member is the figure-eight
knot complement, and \S\S\ref{sec:arith}--\ref{sec:realform} follow its own arithmetic
into an exceptional Lie algebra.

The second half is negative and we ask that it be weighed equally
(\S\S\ref{sec:negatives}--\ref{sec:falsifiers}).

\subsection{What is claimed, and what is not}
We claim a \emph{cost theorem}: three declared choices, whose alternatives we compute
and exhibit, no fitted parameters, and no measured input, yield the structural data
below. We do \textbf{not} claim a derivation of physics, and no physical identification
is made in the body. Internal names are names of mathematical objects.

\subsection{What the reader should be suspicious of}\label{sec:suspicious}
Two things, addressed where they arise rather than in a discussion section.

\emph{First}, arrival at this particular exceptional algebra is \emph{generic}: roughly
one hyperbolic $3$-manifold in three, and five of seven grammars in the relevant
family, arrive at it. \textbf{Arrival is not evidence.} What is not generic is the
\emph{selection} of the golden member, which is why \S\ref{sec:four} carries the paper
and \S\S\ref{sec:frame}--\ref{sec:realform} are presented as consequences, mostly
standard, with the standard references given.

We can be quantitative rather than rhetorical about this, because the question was
settled by enumeration: of the $20$ steps below the entrance, $0$ consume the
manifold's fundamental group, $18$ are standard exceptional-algebra theory downstream
of the McKay assignment, and exactly $1$ consumes the arithmetic. A reader who wishes
to attack this paper should attack \S\ref{sec:four} and \S\ref{sec:entrance}; attacking
the cascade attacks Slansky~\cite{Slansky}.

\emph{Second}, the cost claim is a claim about what entered the computations, and prose
cannot establish it. Appendix~\ref{app:verify} names a runnable check per numerical
claim, with filenames.

\subsection{Relation to existing literature}
The combinatorial setting---Christoffel and Sturmian words, continued fractions,
Markoff theory, Sturmian morphisms and the positive automorphisms of $F_2$---is the
subject of Reutenauer's monograph~\cite{Reutenauer}, the connection going back to
Frobenius. \textbf{Nothing in \S\S\ref{sec:price}--\ref{sec:genesis} is offered as
new.} That the golden ratio, icosahedral symmetry, $2I$ and $E_8$ form a connected
cluster is likewise well established~\cite{Baez,Dechant}. The cone-manifold geometry of
\S\ref{sec:entrance} is due to Thurston, with the figure-eight cone manifolds worked
out by Hilden--Lozano--Montesinos~\cite{HLM95}; \textbf{we claim none of it.} Matrices
attached to Christoffel words, their group structure and determinants, are studied by
Reutenauer and Shallit~\cite{ChristoffelMatrices}---there via the Burrows--Wheeler
matrix of size the word length, reduced modulo that length, with the Zolotareff symbol
as invariant; the construction in \S\ref{sec:shadow} is a $2\times2$ monodromy reduced
modulo a \emph{discriminant}, with a group order as invariant, and the two agree only
in shape.

What we believe to be new is the \emph{conjunction}: that the metallic family is
canonical, and that four independent classical criteria select the same member of it.

\section{The principle and its price}\label{sec:price}

We take as starting point that description is inexhaustible: no finite description
closes. The mathematical content is a minimality condition; the metaphysical content is
a choice, and we price it. \textbf{A declared choice whose alternatives are not
computed is a free parameter wearing a different name}, so the computations are part of
the claim.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,style=nextline]
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{(C1) Description is inexhaustible.}}] The one metaphysical
  commitment. \emph{Price.} Both alternatives degenerate. The periodic counterpart
  collapses: the whole $\det=+1$, $|\tr|\le2$ family is finite-order or reducible, so
  there is no pseudo-Anosov element and no hyperbolic carrier. Variants of the shadow
  rule degenerate or become conjugate to the original. Neither alternative supports the
  construction at all.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{(C2) The word is realized on the once-punctured torus.}}] A
  geometric carrier is chosen over combinatorial ones. \emph{Price, and it is the
  consequential one.} The canonical non-geometric carriers---the tiling hull, and the
  Effros--Shen algebra with $K_0=\Z[\varphi]$---see only the real quadratic data. All
  four pre-registered redundancy witnesses fail exactly; in particular $x^2+3$ remains
  irreducible over $\Q(\sqrt5)$. \textbf{The imaginary quadratic field $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$
  is bought here and nowhere earlier}, and every arithmetic consequence in
  \S\S\ref{sec:arith}--\ref{sec:realform} depends on it.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{(C3) The monodromy is orientation-preserving.}}] Equivalently,
  the generator is squared. \emph{Price, and it is the largest.} \textbf{The discarded
  alternative is not degenerate.} It is the Gieseking manifold, whose orientation double
  cover is the manifold we keep, with dilatation $\lambda_1$ upstairs against
  $\lambda_1^2$ below. A reader who prefers the parent to the child obtains a different,
  perfectly good object, to which the construction below does not apply.
\end{description}

choice & status of the alternative\\
\midrule
C1 & degenerates---no carrier exists\\
C2 & survives, but sees only the real quadratic data\\

\end{table}

\textbf{C3 is the honest scar in the argument.} It is a fork between two genuine
objects, decided by a stipulation, and no computation in this paper forces it. We flag
it here rather than in a limitations section because a reader who discovers it late is
entitled to distrust everything before it.

\section{From minimal description to a family}\label{sec:genesis}

\begin{theorem}[Morse--Hedlund~\cite{MorseHedlund}]\label{thm:mh}
If an infinite word $w$ over a finite alphabet is aperiodic, its factor complexity
satisfies $p(n)\ge n+1$ for all $n\ge1$. The words attaining $p(n)=n+1$ for every $n$
are exactly the Sturmian words.
\end{theorem}

Periodic words close, so C1 excludes them; among the words that do not close,
Theorem~\ref{thm:mh} isolates a unique minimal complexity class, and it is non-empty.
Sturmian words are classified by an irrational slope $\alpha$.

\begin{theorem}[Hurwitz]\label{thm:hurwitz}
For every irrational $\alpha$ the Lagrange number satisfies $L(\alpha)\ge\sqrt5$, with
equality exactly on the $\GL(2,\Z)$-equivalence class of $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$.
\end{theorem}

We state Hurwitz over \emph{all} irrationals deliberately. It is stronger than a
within-family computation, and it means the golden equivalence class is distinguished
before any family is fixed---so no restriction to a subfamily is needed for the
extremality statement, and none is smuggled.

\begin{remark}[what Theorem~\ref{thm:hurwitz} does \emph{not} give]\label{rem:hurwitzlimit}
Hurwitz distinguishes an equivalence \emph{class}, not a manifold. Every irrational in
the class has the same Lagrange number, and passing from a slope to a $3$-manifold
requires C2 and C3. \textbf{The selection theorems of \S\ref{sec:four} are therefore not
restatements of Hurwitz}: they distinguish one member of a family of pairwise
non-homeomorphic manifolds, which Hurwitz cannot do.
\end{remark}

\section{The metallic family is intrinsically characterized}\label{sec:canonical}

A referee's first objection to any ``uniqueness inside a family'' theorem is that the
family was chosen to make it true. We answer the sharpest form of it---that the family
has no description independent of the conclusion---while being explicit about what is
\emph{not} thereby answered.

\begin{definition}
For $m\ge1$ let $\lambda_m=\tfrac12\bigl(m+\sqrt{m^2+4}\bigr)$ be the $m$-th
\emph{metallic mean}, the positive root of $x^2-mx-1=0$; $\lambda_1=\varphi$ is golden,
$\lambda_2=1+\sqrt2$ silver. Put
\[
   X_m=\begin{pmatrix}m&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
   \phi_m=X_m^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}m^2+1&m\\m&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
so $\det X_m=-1$, $\det\phi_m=+1$. Let $M_m$ be the mapping torus of the once-punctured
torus under $\phi_m$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[the family is a characterization]\label{thm:canonical}
Let $A\in\GL(2,\Z)$ be hyperbolic with non-negative entries. The continued fraction of
its dominant eigenvalue is purely periodic of period one if and only if $\det A=-1$; in
that case $\tr A=m$ for some $m\ge1$, the eigenvalue is $\lambda_m$, and $X_m$ is a
representative of the class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the dominant eigenvalue has purely periodic expansion $[m;m,m,\dots]$ of period
one, $m\ge1$. Then $x=m+1/x$, i.e.\ $x^2-mx-1=0$, whose companion matrix is $X_m$ with
$\det X_m=-1$, $\tr X_m=m$, discriminant $m^2+4$ and dominant root $\lambda_m$.
Conversely, if $A$ is hyperbolic with non-negative entries and $\det A=-1$, its
characteristic polynomial is $x^2-tx-1$ with $t=\tr A\ge1$; the dominant root satisfies
$x=t+1/x$, hence has the purely periodic expansion $[t;t,t,\dots]$ of period one.
Distinct $m$ give distinct traces, hence distinct classes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:c3family}
Squaring, as C3 requires, carries this class to $\{\phi_m\}_{m\ge1}$. \textbf{The family quantified over in \S\ref{sec:four} is therefore the period-one
class of \S\ref{sec:genesis}'s own continued-fraction language, made
orientation-preserving.}

\begin{proposition}[the period-one restriction, priced]\label{prop:periodtwo}
Let $M(a,b)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}b&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}ab+1&a\\b&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ be the matrix of a purely
periodic continued fraction of period two. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item $\det M(a,b)=+1$ \textbf{already} --- a period-two word is orientation-preserving
  without squaring;
\item $M(m,m)=\phi_m$, so \textbf{the metallic family is the diagonal} of the period-two
  family, which is therefore strictly larger; and
\item $H_1\bigl(M_{a,b}\bigr)\cong\Z\oplus\Z/\gcd(a,b)\oplus\Z/\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)$,
  so the \textbf{only} knot complement in the whole two-parameter family is
  $(a,b)=(1,1)$: the golden.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) and (ii) are immediate from the product. For (iii),
$M(a,b)-I=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}ab&a\\b&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has entry gcd
$\gcd(a,b)$ and determinant $-ab$, so its invariant factors are $\gcd(a,b)$ and
$ab/\gcd(a,b)=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)$; apply the Wang sequence as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:homology}. The torsion vanishes iff $\gcd=\operatorname{lcm}=1$, i.e.\
$a=b=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{scope}[what this buys]\label{sc:pricedperiod}
\textbf{The period-one restriction is therefore priced, and the price is zero.} Relaxing
it to period two adds a genuine two-parameter family---$(a,b)=(2,3)$ has trace $8$,
which is not of the form $m^2+2$---and the homological criterion of
\S\ref{sec:homological} selects \emph{the same member} from the larger family. So the
restriction is not doing work that the selection could not do without it. We do not
claim this for period three or beyond, and we do not claim period one is forced; we
claim only that the one widening a reader is most likely to propose has been computed
and leaves the conclusion unchanged.
\end{scope}

\begin{scope}[what this does and does not settle]\label{sc:notunchosen}
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical} shows the metallic family is \emph{intrinsically
characterized}: it has a description in the ambient language that does not mention its
members. \textbf{It does not show that period one is the only admissible period.}
Period two, bounded partial quotients, and Markov classes are equally definable
families, and nothing proved here excludes them. So the honest statement is
``characterized, not ad hoc'', and \emph{not} ``forced''; a reader who wants the
stronger claim should regard the choice of period one as a fourth declared choice,
whose price is that the results of \S\ref{sec:four} are statements about this family.
\end{scope}
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}\label{sc:latimer}
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical} classifies by $(\tr,\det)$ and exhibits $X_m$ as a
representative. It does \emph{not} assert that every trace-$m$, $\det=-1$ matrix is
$\GL(2,\Z)$-conjugate to $X_m$; that fails for $m\ge4$, where the form class number
$h(m^2{+}4)$ exceeds one (Latimer--MacDuffee). Only the stated equivalence is used
below.
\end{scope}

\section{Four independent selections of the golden}\label{sec:four}

This section is the paper. Before stating the criteria we state, and prove, the sense in
which they are and are not independent---because the honest answer is sharper than the
claim an earlier draft of this work made, and it is what a referee will test first.

\begin{lemma}[the collapse lemma]\label{lem:collapse}
Let $\chi_m(t)=\det(tI-\phi_m)=t^2-(m^2+2)t+1$. Then
\[
   \chi_m(1)=\det(\phi_m-I)=-m^2,\qquad
   \chi_m(-1)=\det(\phi_m+I)=m^2+4,\qquad
   \operatorname{tr}\phi_m=m^2+2 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from $\phi_m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}m^2+1&m\\m&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$:
the characteristic polynomial is $t^2-(\operatorname{tr}\phi_m)t+\det\phi_m$ with
$\operatorname{tr}\phi_m=m^2+2$ and $\det\phi_m=1$; evaluate at $t=\pm1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{scope}[what Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} costs, stated before the criteria and not after]\label{sc:collapse}
\textbf{The homological invariant, the shadow modulus and the coset level are the three
values $\chi_m(1)$, $\chi_m(-1)$ and $\operatorname{tr}\phi_m$ of a single polynomial},
and each of the corresponding criteria reduces to the same arithmetic step, $m^2=1$.
An earlier draft asserted that the criteria ``share no lemma''. \textbf{That is false,
and we withdraw it}: they share this one.

What survives, and is worth stating precisely, is a distinction between two senses of
independence. \emph{Extensional} independence is unattainable here and no paper should
claim it: $m=1$ is the unique trace-three once-punctured-torus bundle, so \emph{any}
property holding only of the figure-eight is automatically co-extensive with
$\operatorname{tr}=3$. \emph{Mechanistic} independence is the real question---whether the
theorems that declare the value at $m=1$ exceptional are separate theorems---and there
the answer is genuinely affirmative: a trivial cokernel, an exceptional binary
polyhedral order, and a subfactor index threshold are unrelated classifications that
happen to be met at one value.

\textbf{So the correct claim is not ``several independent selections'' but ``one
arithmetic condition, and several unrelated exceptionality theorems satisfied at the
value it picks out''}---together with genuinely separate criteria of different logical
type (\S\ref{sec:arithmeticity}, and the pairwise condition of Scope~\ref{sc:pairwise}).
Saying this ourselves is what makes the section survive the objection rather than invite
it.
\end{scope}

\subsection{Selection I: homological}\label{sec:homological}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:homology}
$H_1(M_m;\Z)\cong\Z\oplus(\Z/m)^2$. Hence $H_1(M_m)\cong\Z$ if and only if $m=1$, and
\textbf{$M_m$ is a knot complement in $S^3$ only for $m=1$}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a mapping torus of a surface $F$ with monodromy $\phi$, the Wang sequence gives
$H_1(M_\phi)\cong\Z\oplus\coker(\phi_*-I)$, where $\phi_*$ acts on $H_1(F)$. Here $F$ is
the once-punctured torus, $H_1(F)\cong\Z^2$, and $\phi_*=\phi_m$, so
\[
   \phi_m-I=\begin{pmatrix}m^2&m\\m&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The gcd of the entries is $m$, so the first invariant factor is $d_1=m$; and
$\det(\phi_m-I)=-m^2$, so $d_1d_2=m^2$ and $d_2=m$. The Smith normal form is therefore
$\mathrm{diag}(m,m)$ and $\coker(\phi_m-I)\cong(\Z/m)^2$, which is trivial exactly when
$m=1$. A knot complement in $S^3$ has $H_1\cong\Z$ by Alexander duality, excluding every
$m\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is an \emph{obstruction}, not a numerical near miss: for every $m\ge2$ the torsion
is non-trivial and grows. Theorem~\ref{thm:homology} is also what makes the geometry of
\S\ref{sec:entrance} golden-\emph{specific} rather than golden-\emph{exhibited}, since
the objects there are knot complements.
\end{remark}

At $m=1$ four independently defined integers coincide, all equal to $5$: the
discriminant $m^2+4$; the knot determinant $|\Delta_{4_1}(-1)|$; the numerator of the
$2$-bridge fraction; and $|H_1|$ of the lens space at the spherical end of
\S\ref{sec:entrance}. They coincide only at $m=1$.

\subsection{Selection II: arithmetic, via the shadow modulus}\label{sec:shadow}

\begin{definition}
The \emph{shadow modulus} of $R^mL^m$ is $N_m=m^2+4$, the discriminant of $x^2-mx-1$.
Equivalently $N_m=\det(\phi_m+I)$, an invariant of the monodromy rather than of a chosen
normalization.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:telescope}
For every integer $N\ge2$, $\displaystyle\prod_{p\mid N}\bigl(1-p^{-2}\bigr)\ \ge\
\frac{N+1}{2N}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The primes dividing $N$ form a subset of $\{2,\dots,N\}$, and every factor $1-k^{-2}$
lies in $(0,1)$, so enlarging the index set only decreases the product. The enlarged
product telescopes:
\[
   \prod_{k=2}^{N}\frac{(k-1)(k+1)}{k^{2}}
   =\Bigl(\prod_{k=2}^{N}\frac{k-1}{k}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{k=2}^{N}\frac{k+1}{k}\Bigr)
   =\frac{1}{N}\cdot\frac{N+1}{2}. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:shadow}
$|\SL(2,\Z/N)|$ equals the order of one of the exceptional binary polyhedral groups
$2T,2O,2I$---that is, $24$, $48$ or $120$---if and only if $N\in\{3,4,5\}$. Consequently,
over the whole metallic family, $N_m$ meets that set only at \textbf{$m=1$}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$|\SL(2,\Z/N)|=N^{3}\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2})$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope},
\[
  |\SL(2,\Z/N)|\ \ge\ N^{3}\cdot\frac{N+1}{2N}\ =\ \frac{N^{2}(N+1)}{2},
\]
which equals $126>120$ at $N=6$ and is strictly increasing in $N$; so no $N\ge6$
qualifies. Direct evaluation gives $6,24,48,120$ at $N=2,3,4,5$. For the second
statement: $m^2+4=3$ has no integer solution; $m^2+4=4$ forces $m=0$, degenerate and not
a metallic word; $m^2+4=5$ gives $m=1$; and $N_m\ge8$ for $m\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the qualifier is not decoration]\label{rem:vacuous}
The unqualified statement ``$|\SL(2,\Z/N)|$ is a McKay-group order'' is \emph{vacuous}.
The McKay correspondence covers every finite subgroup of $\SU(2)$, and the cyclic
subgroups---type $A_n$, order $n+1$---realize \emph{every} positive integer as an order,
the binary dihedral family being unbounded as well. Only restriction to the three
exceptional groups gives a finite target set, and it is that restriction
Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope} exploits.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[group versus order]\label{prop:group}
$\SL(2,\Z/3)\cong2T$ and $\SL(2,\Z/5)\cong2I$ are the classical isomorphisms. However
$\SL(2,\Z/4)\not\cong2O$: it contains seven elements of order $2$, whereas a finite
subgroup of $\SU(2)$ contains exactly one, namely the centre $-I$; and it contains no
element of order $8$, whereas $2O$ does. \textbf{The coincidence of orders at $48$ does
not produce the group, and the $E_7$ slot is unoccupied.} At prime level the same
conclusion follows differently: $|\SL(2,\mathbb F_p)|=p(p^2-1)$ is never $48$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A finite subgroup $G<\SU(2)$ has a unique element of order two: the preimage of the
identity under $\SU(2)\to\mathrm{SO}(3)$ is $\{\pm I\}$, and any $g\in G$ with $g^2=I$,
$g\neq I$, is conjugate to $-I$ in $\SU(2)$ and central, forcing $g=-I$. Counting
solutions of $g^2=I$ in $\SL(2,\Z/4)$ gives seven non-identity solutions; counting
element orders gives none equal to $8$. Both counts are over the full group of order
$48$. The prime-level statement is immediate from $p(p^2-1)\in\{6,24,120,336,\dots\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The golden member lands on a genuine group, not merely a matching integer: the reduction
is onto $\SL(2,\Z/5)\cong2I$. Requiring a genuine exceptional group rather than a
matching order removes $N=4$ from the target set, and no metallic word was ever there,
since $m^2+4=4$ forces $m=0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}[the filter is finer than the field]\label{sc:finer}
A discriminant-level criterion can conflate distinct objects, and here it demonstrably
does: $1^2{+}4=5$, $4^2{+}4=20=2^2\cdot5$ and $11^2{+}4=125=5^3$ all have squarefree
kernel $5$, so $m=1,4,11$ share the field $\Q(\sqrt5)$. \textbf{The group-level filter
separates them}: the orders are $120$, $5760$ and $1\,875\,000$. This is exactly why
Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow} is a statement about the group at the modulus and not about the
field of the discriminant, and it is why field-level genericity results do not touch it.
\end{scope}

\subsection{Selection III: subfactor-theoretic, via the Jones index}\label{sec:jones}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:jones}
$\lambda_m<2$ if and only if $m=1$, and $\lambda_1=\varphi=2\cos(\pi/5)$. Hence the golden mean is the unique metallic mean admissible as the \emph{statistical
dimension of the generating bimodule of a subfactor of index below $4$}; every $m\ge2$
lies above the Jones index wall. (We state it for subfactor bimodules rather than for
objects of a fusion category, since it is the inclusion that carries the index.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\lambda_m$ is strictly increasing in $m$, and $\lambda_2=1+\sqrt2>2$, so $\lambda_m>2$
for all $m\ge2$; whereas $\lambda_1=\tfrac12(1+\sqrt5)=\varphi<2$. By Jones' index
theorem the admissible indices below $4$ are exactly $4\cos^2(\pi/n)$, $n\ge3$~\cite{Jones}, so the
admissible dimensions below $2$ are $2\cos(\pi/n)$; and $\varphi=2\cos(\pi/5)$.
\end{proof}

This selection is \emph{logically independent} of \S\ref{sec:shadow}: it uses no
modulus, no reduction and no finite group---only the size of a real algebraic integer
against a classical quantization theorem. An objection to the shadow construction
therefore does not touch it, and vice versa.

\subsection{Selection IV: arithmeticity, and its honest limit}\label{sec:arithmeticity}

\begin{theorem}[cf.~\cite{MaclachlanReid}]\label{thm:arith}
\textbf{Within the metallic family}, exactly $m=1$ and $m=2$ are arithmetic: their
invariant trace fields are $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and $\Q(i)$ respectively, and every $m\ge3$
is non-arithmetic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[why the quantifier must be restricted]\label{rem:notglobal}
The corresponding \emph{global} statement---``there are exactly two arithmetic
once-punctured-torus bundles''---is \textbf{false}, and we record the counterexample
because an earlier draft of this work asserted it. Arithmeticity is a commensurability
invariant, so every finite cover of an arithmetic manifold is arithmetic; and the
$n$-fold cyclic cover of $M_1$ in the base direction is again a once-punctured-torus
bundle, with monodromy $\phi_1^{\,n}$. Hence $\{M_{\phi_1^n}\}_{n\ge1}$ is an infinite
family of arithmetic once-punctured-torus bundles.

These do not disturb Theorem~\ref{thm:arith}, because for $n\ge2$ they leave the
metallic family: $\tr\phi_1^{\,n}=m^2+2$ requires $m^2=\tr\phi_1^{\,n}-2$, which is a
square only at $n=1,3,5,\dots$, giving $m=1,4,11,\dots$---and even there the matrices
are \emph{not} conjugate. For instance $\phi_1^{3}$ and $\phi_4$ share trace $18$,
determinant $1$, \emph{and} mapping-torus torsion $(\Z/4)^2$, yet no
$P\in\GL(2,\Z)$ conjugates one to the other: a concrete illustration of the
Latimer--MacDuffee phenomenon already flagged in Scope~\ref{sc:latimer}. \textbf{So the
$m=1,4,11$ coincidence of Scope~\ref{sc:finer} is a trace coincidence, not a covering
relation.}
\end{remark}

\begin{scope}\label{sc:arithlimit}
\textbf{This criterion does not single out the golden}: it selects $\{1,2\}$. We include
it because it is the criterion a hyperbolic geometer reaches for first, and because
stating its limit is what licenses the other three. In particular, ``the arithmetic
knot'' is correct among \emph{knots} and false among \emph{bundles}, where silver is
also arithmetic. What survives as golden-specific, and is what \S\ref{sec:arith} uses,
is narrower: $m=1$ is the unique member whose invariant trace field is $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$,
the field C2 purchased.
\end{scope}

\subsection{What the four jointly establish}

\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule

\midrule
I   & $H_1(M_m)\cong\Z$ & $\{1\}$ & Alexander duality; Smith normal form\\
II  & $|\SL(2,\Z/N_m)|$ exceptional & $\{1\}$ & order formula; McKay\\
III & $\lambda_m<2$ & $\{1\}$ & Jones index theorem\\

\bottomrule

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:four}
Inside the canonical family of \S\ref{sec:canonical}, the golden member is the unique
one satisfying any of I--III, and the unique one satisfying IV together with the
trace-field condition of Scope~\ref{sc:arithlimit}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}[the pairwise criterion, of a different logical type]\label{sc:pairwise}
One banked criterion is not a condition on a single $m$ at all, and so escapes
Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} by construction: for the symmetric pair $A_m,A_n$ one has the
Fricke-type identity $\operatorname{tr}[A_m,A_n]=2-(mn(n-m))^2$, so the commutator is
parabolic exactly when $|mn(n-m)|=2$, whose unique solution with $1\le m<n$ is
$(m,n)=(1,2)$. This is a two-variable Diophantine condition, not a function of $m^2$,
and it selects the golden--silver \emph{pair} rather than a member. The identity itself
is classical (Cohn; Fricke), and we cite rather than claim it.
\end{scope}

\begin{scope}[what this is not]
None of the four is a statement about physics, and \textbf{none is evidence that the
construction below is physically realized}. They establish that a single member of a
canonically characterized family is distinguished by several independent classical
criteria---a mathematical fact, and the one this paper asks to be judged on.
\end{scope}

\section{The golden member and its arithmetic}\label{sec:arith}

\begin{theorem}[Thurston; Riley]\label{thm:thurston}
$M_1$ is the complement of the figure-eight knot. It carries a unique complete
hyperbolic structure, and its Kleinian trace field is $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:v4}
Let $L=\Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt5)$ be the compositum of the Kleinian trace field with the
fiber field. Then $L/\Q$ is biquadratic with $\Gal(L/\Q)\cong V_4$, and its three
quadratic subfields are exactly $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$, $\Q(\sqrt5)$, $\Q(\sqrt{-15})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The content is that the construction produces \emph{both} generators of $L$---the first
at C2 and the second at the slope---not that a biquadratic field has three quadratic
subfields, which is automatic. Note that $\Q(\sqrt5)$ enters \emph{before} the manifold
exists.
\end{remark}

The group of $M_1$ is moreover a congruence subgroup of the relevant Bianchi group over
$\Q(\sqrt{-3})$; and no closed hyperbolic Dehn filling in the surveyed slope grid
carries any of the three fields, so the $V_4$ is a property of the \emph{cusped} object.

\section{The entrance}\label{sec:entrance}

\subsection{The two curvature ends}
The cone-manifold family of the figure-eight complement---hyperbolic below cone angle
$2\pi/3$, \emph{Euclidean exactly at} $2\pi/3$, spherical beyond---is due to Thurston,
with the figure-eight cone manifolds worked out by
Hilden--Lozano--Montesinos~\cite{HLM95} and the degenerations by Hodgson and Porti.
\textbf{We claim none of this geometry.} What we add is which arithmetic sits at each
end.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ends}
Along that transition the hyperbolic end carries $(\Q(\sqrt{-3}),2T)$ and the spherical
end carries $(\Q(\sqrt5),2I)$: the dual McKay pair $E_6+E_8$ is realized as the two
stable geometries of a single object separated by the Euclidean wall, rather than as two
unrelated faces. At the spherical end the double cover is the lens space
$L(5,2)=S^3/\Z_5$, with $|H_1|=\det(4_1)=5$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{scope}[the two ends are not symmetric]\label{sc:asym}
The asymmetry runs against the direction the phrasing invites. At the hyperbolic end
$\pi_1(4_1)\twoheadrightarrow 2T$ is a genuine group surjection, with exactly two such
quotients. At the spherical end there is \emph{no} surjection: $\pi_1(4_1)$ surjects
onto neither $2I$ nor $A_5$. So as a statement about $\pi_1$ the $E_8$ end is
field-level, and the two ramified-prime reductions are symmetric as fields but not as
group surjections. (Cf.~\cite{Stuebner} for the surrounding representation landscape.)
\end{scope}

\begin{proposition}[a group-level presence that survives Scope~\ref{sc:asym}]\label{prop:2880}
Let $\rho$ be the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ level-two modular (Kac--Peterson) representation---six
primaries, $\mathrm{ord}\,T=15$---with $R\mapsto T$ and $L\mapsto \Sigma^{-1}T^{-1}\Sigma$.
Then $|\langle\rho(R),\rho(L)\rangle|=2880=|2T\times2I|$ exactly,
verified by enumeration at two unramified primes together with a Serre injectivity
argument, with a class-by-class character match of $63=7\times9$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{scope}\label{sc:2880}
\textbf{The statement is about $\rho(R),\rho(L)$, not about $R,L$}: the letters
themselves generate an infinite subgroup of $\SL(2,\Z)$, so the literal claim without
$\rho$ would be false.

\textbf{Naming $\rho$ also avoids a collision that the unqualified phrase ``level-$15$
data'' creates.} At level $15$ there are two different groups in play: the
\emph{two}-dimensional $\theta$-odd block has image $2I\times\Z/3$ of \textbf{order
$360$}, while the full six-dimensional stage above has order $2880$, of index $8$. A
claim that the level-$15$ group ``is'' $2T\times2I$ of order $2880$ is registered as
retracted precisely because it conflates those two; the present statement is about the
six-dimensional stage and says so. We record that $\rho$'s structure constants are
specified in the source computation and \textbf{are not reconstructed in this paper}; a
reader should treat
Proposition~\ref{prop:2880} as a certificate whose ambient representation is cited
rather than exhibited, and \S\ref{sec:entrance}'s conclusions do not rest on it---it
supplements Scope~\ref{sc:asym} rather than repairing it.
\end{scope}

This does not contradict Scope~\ref{sc:asym}: there the group is a quotient of
$\pi_1(4_1)$; here it is generated by $R$ and $L$ inside the ambient instrument.
\textbf{Both ends are present at group level in the grammar; only one is present at
group level in the knot group.}

\subsection{A mechanism for the shadow}

\begin{scope}[the quantization-index law, stated as an observation]\label{obs:qil}
The source material records a \emph{quantization-index law}: that reduction modulo the
shadow modulus is an isomorphism exactly when the cusp conductor is coprime to it, and
acquires a kernel line otherwise, with $\gcd(4,5)=1$ for the golden and $\gcd(2,8)=2$
for the silver. \textbf{We do not state this as a theorem here}, because the paper does
not specify the source and target of the reduction map, nor the module in which a
``kernel line'' lives, and two gcd evaluations are instances rather than a proof of an
equivalence. It is reported as an observation consistent with
Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow}, and \textbf{nothing in \S\ref{sec:four} depends on it}.
\end{scope}

So the golden's shadow is exceptional by the order count of
Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow}, and the coprimality of Observation~\ref{obs:qil} points the
same way without being independently established here.

\begin{remark}[a terminology warning]
The bare noun ``conductor'' names two different quantities in adjacent statements: the
cusp conductor (golden $4$) and the shadow modulus $m^2+4$ (golden $5$). We use the
qualified names throughout.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The entrance is a classification, not an assignment}\label{sec:classification}

The step from the manifold's arithmetic to the exceptional algebra is the one step of
the whole construction that consumes the arithmetic (\S\ref{sec:suspicious}). It is
therefore the step at which ``we then take $2T$'' would be fatal. It is not a taking.

\begin{theorem}[classification among the polyhedral candidates]\label{thm:classify}
Among the six finite groups $\{A_4,S_4,2T,2O,A_5,2I\}$---constructed
concretely, the binary ones as unit quaternions ($2T$ the $24$ Hurwitz units, $2O$ the
$48$, $2I$ the $120$ icosians), the others as permutation groups, with conjugacy
classes, power maps and characters computed rather than transcribed---a complex
$27$-dimensional assembly carrying an invariant cubic form exists \textbf{only for
$A_4$ and $2T$}. Moreover every $\SL(2,\C)$-factoring route forces a quaternionic
self-dual $2$, which closes the twisted escape at the determinant level, before any
branching computation.
\end{theorem}

Of the two survivors only $2T$ is binary, i.e.\ a subgroup of $\SU(2)$, which is what
the McKay correspondence requires; $A_4$ is its image in $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ and carries no
faithful quaternionic $2$.

\begin{scope}[the candidate set is an input]\label{sc:candidates}
\textbf{This is a classification \emph{among the six polyhedral candidates}, not over
all finite subgroups of $\SU(2)$}, which also include the cyclic and binary dihedral
families. Those are excluded here by the prior restriction to the exceptional
($E$-type) series---the same restriction Remark~\ref{rem:vacuous} shows to be necessary
for Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow} to have content. \textbf{So exceptionality is an input to
this classification and not an output of it}, and the honest reading of
Theorem~\ref{thm:classify} is: \emph{given} that the entrance is to be exceptional, the
$27$-with-invariant-cubic condition leaves only $A_4$ and $2T$, and only $2T$ is binary.
\textbf{We closed the one route that looked available, and it fails.} The natural
candidate criterion is \emph{chirality-capability}: among the exceptional series the
fundamental representations are $7$ (real) for $G_2$, $26$ (real) for $F_4$, $\mathbf{27}$
(\textbf{complex}) for $E_6$, $56$ (pseudoreal) for $E_7$ and $248$ (real) for $E_8$---so
within that series, complexity isolates $E_6$ exactly. \textbf{Extended to the full McKay
list it does not.} A simple Lie algebra has complex representations precisely when
$-w_0\neq\mathrm{id}$, i.e.\ when $-1\notin W$; computing the Weyl group directly gives
\[
  \begin{array}{lccc}
   & |W| & -1\in W? & \text{complex reps?}\\
  A_1=\su(2) & 2 & \text{yes} & \text{no}\\
  A_2=\su(3) & 6 & \text{no} & \textbf{yes}\\
  A_3=\su(4) & 24 & \text{no} & \textbf{yes}\\
  D_4=\so(8) & 192 & \text{yes} & \text{no}\\
  D_5=\so(10) & 1920 & \text{no} & \textbf{yes}
  \end{array}
\]
so the criterion selects $\{A_n:n\ge2\}\cup\{D_n:n\text{ odd}\}\cup\{E_6\}$. \textbf{Every
cyclic subgroup of $\SU(2)$ of order at least three is chirality-capable}, and cyclic
subgroups are McKay images of type $A$. \textbf{So complexity of the fundamental does not
isolate $E_6$ among admissible entrances, and exceptionality is not derivable this way.}

We record this as a \emph{closed} door rather than an open problem: the obvious route has
been tried and does not work, so a derivation of exceptionality---if one exists---must use
something other than chirality-capability.
\end{scope}

\subsection{An unpriced step, now priced: why the principal $\sll_2$?}\label{sec:principal}

McKay attaches $E_6$ to $2T$. It does \textbf{not} attach $2T$ to any particular
$\sll_2\subset\ee_6$, and $\ee_6$ has many $\sll_2$-conjugacy classes. Appendix~A places
$2T$ inside the \emph{principal} $\sll_2$ and takes invariants there, and the four
degrees $8,14,16,22$---hence the whole of \S\S\ref{sec:frame}--\ref{sec:cascade}---depend
on that placement.

\begin{scope}[C4, declared here rather than left implicit]\label{sc:principal}
\textbf{The principal placement is a fourth declared choice, and we price it.} Three things can be said in its favour, and the third is stronger than economy.
The principal $\sll_2$ is the unique regular class; its centralizer in $\ee_6$ is exactly
zero, computed; and its $\ad$-decomposition is \textbf{multiplicity-free}, with highest
weights twice the exponents \emph{recovered rather than assumed}, so placing $2T$ in it
requires no further discrete datum to say which copy of each degree is meant.

\textbf{And there is a rigidity statement, which is a forcing argument rather than a
preference.} Embeddings $\sll_2\hookrightarrow\ee_6$ are infinitesimally rigid---
$H^1(\sll_2,\ee_6)=0$ by Whitehead, verified block by block---so the tangent space to
``factors through \emph{some} $\sll_2$'' is exactly the principal line. No first-order
deformation leaves it.

\textbf{What is still missing, and why this remains C4.} Those statements concern the
$\sll_2$ the object's own holonomy lands in. That $2T$ should be placed \emph{there}
follows only because $2T\subset\SU(2)$ sits inside that same $\sll_2$---and \textbf{no
source states that inheritance as a theorem}. So the placement is well-motivated and not
yet derived, and the sentence ``between the manifold and the algebra there is no declared
choice'' must be read as ``no declared choice \emph{after} the principal placement''.
Census~\ref{cen:forced} is amended accordingly: \textbf{four declared choices become
five}, C1--C3 before the manifold, C4 here, and the observer's closing after the algebra.
\end{scope}

The concern of \S\ref{sec:suspicious}---that a construction reaching $E_6$ might reach it

\subsection{The recurring shape}\label{sec:shape}

It is worth naming the pattern the preceding sections instantiate three times, because
it, rather than any particular endpoint, is what we take to be the content.

\begin{center}
\emph{a generic family} $\ \longrightarrow\ $ \emph{an exceptional locus, selected by
rigidity}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{In symbolic dynamics.} All words $\longrightarrow$ the aperiodic ones of
  minimal factor complexity, $p(n)=n+1$: the Sturmian locus
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:mh}).
\item \textbf{In arithmetic.} All irrationals $\longrightarrow$ the golden class, by
  extremality of the Lagrange number (Theorem~\ref{thm:hurwitz}); and, within the
  canonical family of \S\ref{sec:canonical}, the golden member by four independent
  criteria (\S\ref{sec:four}).
\item \textbf{In the Lie algebra.} A generic plane in the charge torus
  $\longrightarrow$ exactly three lines where the centralizer dimension jumps from $30$
  to $46$, and those three are a single Galois orbit
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:fmt}).
\end{enumerate}

At each stage the distinguished object is not chosen from a list but is the locus where
some genericity fails. \textbf{That the same shape governs a combinatorial, an
arithmetic and a Lie-theoretic stage of one construction is, in our view, the
mathematically substantive observation of this paper}---more so than the identity of the
terminal algebra, which \S\ref{sec:falsifiers} is at pains to keep in proportion.

\begin{census}\label{cen:forced}
Of the $43$ links in the full chain, $26$ are theorems, $6$ exact identities, $5$ no-go
results, $1$ a corollary and $1$ a census, and \textbf{$4$ are axioms}. Those six figures
sum to $43$, and \textbf{that is the only count this paper takes from the ledger}.

\textbf{An earlier draft printed the same six figures and then said ``five are declared
choices'', which sums to $44$.} The arithmetic failure was the symptom of a real error:
the declared choices this paper prices are \emph{not} in bijection with the ledger's
axiom links. The principal placement (Scope~\ref{sc:principal}) is a stipulation and
\textbf{not a link at all}; the observer's closing is a link but lies \emph{after} the
terminus, outside the chain this paper walks. \textbf{Counting links and counting
stipulations are different operations, and conflating them is what produced the
44.}
The declared inputs carried by the algebra steps are itemized in \S\ref{sec:inputs};
note that Census~\ref{cen:forced} counts \emph{links containing choices}, which is not
the same as counting independent stipulations.
\end{census}

\section{The frame}\label{sec:frame}

Write $\ee_6$ for the Chevalley algebra of type $E_6$ over $\Q$. Inside it sits
$C=\langle x_8,x_{14},x_{16},x_{22}\rangle$, the span of the four binary-tetrahedral
invariants under the principal $\sll_2$---Klein forms of degrees $8,14,16,22$, twice the
exponents $4,7,8,11$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:C}
The four degrees $8,14,16,22$ are the complete list, and each invariant is unique up to
scale. Moreover $C$ is abelian.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the completeness and uniqueness]
Under the principal $\sll_2$, $\ee_6$ decomposes into irreducibles of highest weight
twice the exponents, i.e.\ into degrees $2\cdot(1,4,5,7,8,11)=(2,8,10,14,16,22)$. The
Molien series of $2T$ acting on $\mathrm{Sym}^\bullet(\C^2)$,
\[
   \sum_{n\ge0}\dim\bigl(\mathrm{Sym}^n\C^2\bigr)^{2T}t^n
   =\frac{1}{24}\sum_{g\in 2T}\frac{1}{\det(1-tg)},
\]
has support $\{0,6,8,12,14,16,18,20,22,24,\dots\}$ in degrees $\le24$, with
coefficient $1$ at $8,14,16,22$ and \textbf{coefficient $0$ at $2$ and $10$}.
Intersecting the two lists leaves exactly $\{8,14,16,22\}$, each with a
one-dimensional invariant space. Hence $C$ is $4$-dimensional and the two skipped
exponents are skipped for a representation-theoretic reason, not by selection.
\end{proof}

\begin{scope}\label{sc:abelian}
\textbf{Abelianness is a separate statement} and we do not claim it follows from the
invariant theory: a Molien computation bounds dimensions of invariant subspaces and says
nothing about brackets. It is verified as an exact rational computation over the
Chevalley basis of Appendix~\ref{app:construct}, and is listed as a certificate rather
than as a proof in the audit of \S\ref{sec:audit}.
\end{scope}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mu}
The two pencil determinants are exact powers, up to nonzero rational constants, of an
irreducible cubic $\mu$ with Galois group $S_3$ and three real roots. Writing
$K=\Q[\rho]/\mu$ one has the reduced model
\[
   K\cong\Q[x]/(x^3-12x-5),\qquad \disc K=6237=3^4\cdot7\cdot11,
\]
with $K$ monogenic, totally real of unit rank $2$, quadratic resolvent $\Q(\sqrt{77})$,
and \textbf{class number $h=1$}. Only $3,7,11$ ramify; $13,17,19$ are inert; and $2$,
$5$ and the value primes $953,1129,421493$ are partially split with exactly one
degree-one place each.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Irreducibility and three real roots follow from $\disc(x^3-12x-5)=6237>0$ together with
the absence of rational roots. Since $6237=3^4\cdot7\cdot11$, only $3$ can divide the
index. Dedekind's criterion at $3$: one has $f\equiv(x+1)^3\pmod3$, so $\bar g=x+1$ and
$\bar h=(x+1)^2$; lifting and setting $T=(gh-f)/3=x^2+5x+2\equiv x^2-x-1\pmod3$, we get
$T(-1)=1\not\equiv0$, hence $\gcd(\bar T,\bar g,\bar h)=1$ and $\Z[x]/(f)$ is
$3$-maximal. So the index is $1$ and $\disc K=\disc f=6237$. Total reality gives unit
rank $3-1=2$, and $6237=81\cdot77$ gives the resolvent. Splitting types then follow from
Dedekind's theorem, applicable at every prime because the index is $1$.

For $h=1$: the Minkowski bound is $\frac{3!}{3^3}\sqrt{6237}<18$, so the class group is
generated by prime ideals of norm at most $17$. These lie above $2,3,5,7,11$ only, since
$13,17,19$ are inert with norm $p^3$. An explicit generator is exhibited for each,
including \emph{both} degree-one primes above $7$ and both above $11$; this is necessary
rather than pedantic, since $(7)=\mathfrak p\mathfrak q^2$ and a single element of norm
$7$ would establish only that a class squares to the identity, not that it is trivial.
The degree-two prime above $2$, of norm $4\le17$, is then $(2)\mathfrak p^{-1}$ and
principal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[a correction to the record]
The resolvent $77$ is a fact about $K$; a companion claim that $5$ ``enters by
ramification'' is not. A ramified prime divides the field discriminant and
$5\nmid6237$; in $K$ the prime $5$ is unramified with the same splitting shape as the
value primes, and it is unramified in $\Q(\sqrt{77})$ and hence in the whole $S_3$
closure. The $5$ appearing in $\disc\mu$---like the $13^6$, since $13$ is inert---is
carried by the integral model, not by the arithmetic of the field.
\end{remark}

\section{The cascade}\label{sec:cascade}

The results here are consequences, and everything derivable at this layer is standard
exceptional-algebra theory~\cite{Slansky,BartonSudbery}. We state them compactly and in
the algebraic category, deferring real forms to \S\ref{sec:realform}.

\begin{theorem}[first measurement]\label{thm:fmt}
On the plane $\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle$ the centralizer dimension stratifies as $12$,
$30$, and exactly three lines at $46$; the three lines form the $S_3$-Galois orbit of
the roots of $\mu$. For each such line $K_i$, $z(K_i)\cong\so(10)\oplus\uu(1)$, while
$z(\text{plane})\cong\so(8)\oplus\uu(1)^2$. Moreover
$\ee_6=(\so(8)\oplus\uu(1)^2)\oplus V_1\oplus V_2\oplus V_3$ with $\dim V_i=16$ and
$[V_i,V_j]=V_k$.
\end{theorem}

The three walls are Galois conjugates of one another: one object seen three ways.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:magic}
The algebra built from split octonions and split complexes by the Freudenthal--Tits
construction satisfies the Jacobi identity on all $\binom{78}{3}=76\,076$ unordered
basis triples, has Cartan matrix $E_6$, and admits an explicit isomorphism onto the
Chevalley $\ee_6$ verified on all $\binom{78}{2}=3\,003$ basis pairs with zero
discrepancies.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[second measurement]\label{thm:smt}
Over $K$, at the wall point $y^*$, $\dim z(x_1,y^*)=14$, with derived algebra of
dimension $11$ and centre of dimension $3$; after base change the derived algebra has
algebraic type $A_2\oplus A_1$,
\[
   z(x_1,y^*)\otimes\overline K\ \cong\ \sll_3\oplus\sll_2\oplus\mathfrak t^{3}.
\]
The within-$C$ centralizer ladder \emph{includes} $\{78,46,30,18,14,12\}$, and the
dimension $26$ is not attained at any coordinate subtorus or enhancement point.
\textbf{We write ``includes'' deliberately}: the source material banks this list as a set
of realized strata, not as an exhaustion of $C$, and the non-attainment of $26$ is a
census over those directions rather than a theorem about every subtorus.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[a rank constraint that fixes a mislabelling]\label{rem:rank}
Every rung is the centralizer of a subset of a rank-$6$ torus and therefore has rank
$6$. The rank-$6$ algebra of dimension $26$ is $\su(5)\oplus\uu(1)^2$; the algebra
$\su(5)\oplus\uu(1)$ has rank $5$ and dimension $25$ and \emph{cannot} occur as a rung
at all. Sources describing the skipped rung as ``$\su(5)\oplus\uu(1)$, dimension $26$''
conflate the two: the number is right and the label is not.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[why the algebraic category matters]
Writing $\su(3)\oplus\su(2)$ at Theorem~\ref{thm:smt} would presuppose compact real
forms, which a statement over a number field cannot deliver. Which real forms occur is
settled only in \S\ref{sec:realform}, and asserting them earlier begs that question.
\end{remark}

\section{The real form}\label{sec:realform}

\subsection{The 27 and its atoms}\label{sec:atoms}

The objects of this section are defined before they are used, since a reader cannot
audit an involution on a space whose distinguished lines have not been named.

\begin{definition}[the atoms]\label{def:atoms}
The four charges of \S\ref{sec:frame} commute rationally in their action on the
$27$-dimensional module. The \emph{atoms} are the joint eigenlines of this rational
commuting family. There are fifteen; the tower never enters an atom line, so the atoms
are defined over $\Q$ and not merely over a splitting field.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[the rational-atoms theorem]\label{thm:atoms}
The fifteen atoms partition bijectively into nine \emph{colorless} and six
\emph{colored}. The nine colorless atoms form a $3\times3$ grid whose rows are the even
transversals of a determinant and whose columns are the odd ones; the grid is a pair of
pencils of $\mathrm{AG}(2,3)$, and its incidence graph is $K_{3,3}$. The orientation
parity of that determinant---the Leibniz sign---is exact and equals $-1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[the signature split]\label{thm:signature}
The canonical Hermitian structure on the $27$ has signature $(15,12)$, matching the
$K$-type split of the real form $\ee_{6(2)}$. The nine colorless atoms are
positive-definite, with nine exact scales; all six colored atoms are Lorentzian of type
$(1,2,0)$.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{This is the point at which the real form is pinned}, and it is pinned by a
signature computation rather than by a choice: $(15,12)$ is not the split form's
signature and not the compact form's.

\subsection{Sign-locking and the selected form}

\begin{theorem}[sign-locking, \emph{conditional} on Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:diag}]\label{thm:signs}
Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:diag}, so that a $C$-stabilizing automorphism carries a
pattern $\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_8,\varepsilon_{14},\varepsilon_{16},
\varepsilon_{22})\in\{\pm1\}^4$. Two exact rational trace moments of degree $6$ force
$\varepsilon_8\varepsilon_{16}=+1$ and $\varepsilon_{14}\varepsilon_{22}=+1$, so
\textbf{twelve of the sixteen patterns are impossible for every automorphism}; the
wall-real pattern is unique, $\varepsilon=(-1,+1,-1,+1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{scope}[the premise, isolated rather than assumed silently]\label{hyp:diag}
\textbf{Hypothesis ($\pm$-diagonality).} \emph{Every $C$-stabilizing automorphism of
$\ee_6$, linear or antilinear, acts diagonally on the four charge lines with signs.}

\textbf{This is a hypothesis, not a theorem, and the source material says so}: the arc
that uses it names it ``the $\pm$-diagonal constraint---the premise''. What is actually
proved about $C$-stabilizers is weaker in exactly the place that matters. They form a
\emph{discrete} group, since $\dim n(C)=\dim z(C)=12$ leaves no continuous motion; they
preserve the measured plane \emph{setwise}; they cannot exchange a split direction with
a compact one, the spectra being real and imaginary respectively; and the transposition
$x_8\leftrightarrow x_{16}$ is excluded because the exact factor multisets of
$\ad x_8$ and $\ad x_{16}$ differ.

\textbf{What none of that excludes is a $3$-cycle.} A $C$-stabilizer permutes the three
enhancement lines of Theorem~\ref{thm:fmt}, and the symmetry group of three lines in a
plane contains an order-three rotation. Such an element is finite, preserves $C$, swaps
nothing split with anything compact, does not transpose $x_8$ and $x_{16}$---and is
\textbf{not $\pm$-diagonal}. It is not an idle possibility either: the three lines are
the Galois orbit of $\mu$, and an $S_3$ acts on precisely that orbit.

Closing this would require showing that no automorphism of $\ee_6$ realizes the
$3$-cycle on $\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle$, handling non-monomial plane actions and the
antilinear case. \textbf{Until then Theorem~\ref{thm:signs} and everything downstream of
it is conditional}, and we mark it so rather than let a premise travel as a conclusion.
\end{scope}

The wall of Theorem~\ref{thm:smt} is complex in the split frame at all three Galois
roots of $\mu$, and the real form in which it is real is the quaternionic
$\ee_{6(2)}$. \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:signature} reaches the same form by a route that
does not use Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:diag} at all}---a signature computation on an exact
integer matrix---so the real-form identification does not stand or fall with the
hypothesis, and that is the reason both routes are given.

\subsection{The involution that carries the ratios}\label{sec:d2}

\begin{definition}\label{def:twists}
Let $\rho_{27}$ denote the action on the $27$. For a sign character $\chi$ on the charge
lattice write $\sigma_\chi$ for the associated diagonal twist, and $\tau$ for the outer
involution of $\ee_6$. The \emph{census} is the family of composites
$\{\sigma_\chi\}\cup\{\sigma_\chi\circ\tau\}$, of size $64+64=128$. Write $H_+$ for the
Hermitian structure of the wall-real alignment.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[the $D_2$ characterization]\label{thm:d2}
Let $D_2$ be the eleven-flip diagonal of the value arc. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item $D_2=\pm\rho_{27}(\sigma_{\chi_-})$ for the \emph{affine}, i.e.\ shifted,
  character with shift vector $(1,0,1,0,1,1)$---the unique match in the $128$-member
  census, with \textbf{all $64$ un-shifted characters failing}.
\item $H_+D_2=H(\varphi^*)$ for $\varphi^*=\varphi_+\circ\sigma_{\chi_-}$, which is
  simultaneously an involution and an automorphism, verified on all $78^2$ bracket
  pairs, and is the unique census member whose invariant Hermitian structure is
  $H_+D_2$.
\item $\{I,D_2,D,D_2D\}$ is a Klein four-group realizing the wall pair's full
  $2$-torsion on the $27$.
\item \textbf{Every inner twist admits no invariant pairing}---the weight support is
  empty---while every outer composite admits exactly one,
  $H_\chi=H_+\cdot\rho_{27}(\sigma_{\chi\cdot\chi_+})$, all $64$ verified; and $H_\chi$
  is symmetric exactly when the composite is an involution.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Clause (iv) is what makes (i) a characterization rather than a search: the inner half of
the census is excluded structurally, before any candidate is tested, and the outer half
carries exactly one pairing each.

\begin{scope}[the derived arithmetic, and where it stops]\label{sc:derived}
From the characterization alone follow exact invariants of $K$: a norm law
$N_{K/\Q}(d)= -(953/2304)^2$ on the atom families, cubing to $-(953/2304)^3$ on the
three-dimensional colored atoms, and a flip-rank sum rule equal to $11$ exactly. The
primes $953$ and $2304=2^8 3^2$ enter through the atom-solve eigenline coordinates and
\textbf{the derivation stops exactly there}; we do not derive them further and list that
as an open problem rather than a result. These are recorded as invariants of $K$ and as
nothing else, and the stage at which any comparison with measured quantities would occur
is deliberately outside this paper.
\end{scope}


\section{What the construction does not yield}\label{sec:negatives}

\subsection{Scale-blindness, as a property of the whole family}

\begin{theorem}[amphichirality is a condition on the word]\label{thm:amphi}
A once-punctured-torus bundle is amphichiral if and only if its block-pair sequence is
fixed by reversal composed with component swap, up to cyclic rotation; for metallic
blocks this is the condition that the block-length sequence be a cyclic palindrome.
Every $R^mL^m$ satisfies it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cs}
The Chern--Simons invariant vanishes for every member of the family, in particular for
$M_1$. Consequently the reduced boundary action
$S=-\mathrm{CS}\cdot k-\Vol\cdot\sigma$ satisfies
$\partial S/\partial k=-\mathrm{CS}\equiv0$: \textbf{the classical action of this saddle
carries no term proportional to the level.}
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}[the limit of Corollary~\ref{cor:cs}, stated rather than managed]\label{sc:scale}
What is proved is that \emph{this} saddle's classical action loses its
$k$-dependence. It is \textbf{not} proved that every quantum correction is
$k$-independent, that every flat connection is, that every admissible boundary
construction is, or that no dimensionless coupling can arise elsewhere. A stronger
reading appeared in earlier drafts of this work and is withdrawn. The defensible
statement is Corollary~\ref{cor:cs} exactly as written, and its force is that the
vanishing is a \emph{family} phenomenon by Theorem~\ref{thm:amphi}, not an accident at
$m=1$.
\end{scope}

\subsection{The declared inputs}\label{sec:inputs}
The algebra steps carry declared input riders, itemized rather than left implicit:
chirality (three steps), a vacuum direction, and a generation count. \textbf{These are
independent stipulations}, and the reader should not read Census~\ref{cen:forced}'s
count of four as a count of stipulations; it counts links.

On the last rider four clauses travel together. A three \emph{is} object-forced, as
$h^1(M;\mathbf{27})=3$. That three is \emph{not} a generation triple: no $\Z/3$ acts on
the object, $\mathrm{Isom}(4_1)=D_4$, and the relevant triality is a symmetry of the
representation rather than of the object. Therefore a generation count is a
\textbf{declared input} wherever a derivation uses it---booked and priced, but not an
output. And the branch structure carrying the ratios is independent of the two preceding
clauses.

\begin{nogo}\label{ng:c3}
No hyperbolic knot has a cyclic cubic invariant trace field.
\end{nogo}

\begin{proof}
A $C_3$ field is Galois of odd degree, hence has no order-two automorphism and no
complex place: it is totally real. The invariant trace field of a hyperbolic
$3$-manifold has a complex place. The two conditions are disjoint.
\end{proof}

This upgrades the second clause above from a statement about \emph{this} object to a
statement about every hyperbolic knot.

\section{Falsifiers}\label{sec:falsifiers}

Four statements would end this programme, each computable, none requiring a measurement.
\textbf{One has already fired.}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(F\arabic*),leftmargin=2.8em]
\item \emph{Chirality is impossible from any closing.} Open in the falsifying direction:
  chirality is an external input, not a proven impossibility.
\item \emph{Every dimensionless invariant the object produces is base-rate reachable.}
  \textbf{Fired.} Of $23$ enumerated invariants, $22$ were graded unreachable or
  base-rate dead; the $23$rd fell three independent ways---a parametrization tautology
  for every phase, an apparent sharpness refuted at $0.89\sigma$ rather than the
  $7\sigma$ originally claimed, and zero gated hits across the object's real algebraic
  tower.
\item \emph{The value layer is frame-relative in every frame.} Known for one pair; a
  proof for all frames closes the value question permanently.
\item \emph{The rank obstruction has no escape.} Proved for the centralizer class; the
  one escape is external and its canonicity is structurally unreachable by orbit
  invariants.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{scope}
(F2) fires \textbf{for the enumerated space, not in principle}: the atlas is $23$
enumerated invariants, not a proof that none exists. The consequence we accept is that
the construction has no live candidate for a derived dimensionless value.
\textbf{The selection theorems of \S\ref{sec:four} yield integers and inequalities, not
measured ratios}, and are therefore not candidates under (F2) and must not be presented
as such.
\end{scope}

\noindent\textbf{What would not falsify it.} A failed comparison with a measured
quantity does not falsify \S\S\ref{sec:genesis}--\ref{sec:realform}; none of those
statements asserts a measured value. Conversely---and this is the direction that
matters---a \emph{successful} comparison would not confirm them either: arrival at this
structure is generic and the value layer is base-rate dead, so any agreement would have
to survive the base-rate test that everything else failed. \textbf{The construction is
falsifiable as mathematics and, at present, not testable as physics.}

\section{The construction of the charges, in full}\label{app:construct}

The referee's fair objection to \S\ref{sec:frame} is that $x_8,x_{14},x_{16},x_{22}$
and $\mu$ are named but not exhibited, so the bridge
$\ee_6\text{-pencil}\Rightarrow\mu\Rightarrow K$ cannot be audited. Printing four
$78$-component vectors with large rational entries would not help. \textbf{We give the
construction instead}, which is short, deterministic, and reconstructs every object
from the Cartan matrix alone.

\subsection*{Step 1: the algebra}
Take the $E_6$ Cartan matrix in Bourbaki order and build the Chevalley basis
$\{h_1,\dots,h_6\}\cup\{e_\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi\}$, $\dim=6+72=78$, with structure
constants $N_{\alpha\beta}=\pm(p+1)$ fixed by an asymmetry function
$\varepsilon:\Q^6\times\Q^6\to\{\pm1\}$ that is bimultiplicative and satisfies
$\varepsilon(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=-1$ and
$\varepsilon(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)=(-1)^{\langle\alpha_i,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle}$ for $i<j$.
All arithmetic is over $\Q$; no floating point occurs at any stage.

\subsection*{Step 2: the principal $\sll_2$}
Set $e=\sum_{i=1}^{6}e_{\alpha_i}$ and let $h$ have coordinates $C^{-1}(2,2,2,2,2,2)^{T}$
in the coroot basis; then solve $[e,f]=h$ for $f=\sum_i d_i e_{-\alpha_i}$, a linear
system with a unique rational solution. Under $\mathrm{ad}$, $\ee_6$ decomposes into
irreducible $\sll_2$-modules of highest weights $2\cdot(1,4,5,7,8,11)$, the doubled
exponents.

\subsection*{Step 3: the $2T$-invariants}
Realize $2T$ as the $24$ Hurwitz units---$\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k$ and the sixteen
$\tfrac12(\pm1\pm i\pm j\pm k)$---and map each to $\SU(2)$ by
$a+bi+cj+dk\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a+bi&c+di\\-c+di&a-bi\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
For $n\in\{8,14,16,22\}$ form the average
$P_n=\frac{1}{24}\sum_{g\in2T}\mathrm{Sym}^n(g)$, a projector onto the
$2T$-invariants in $\mathrm{Sym}^n$. Each $P_n$ has rank one, and $x_n$ is the highest
weight vector of the $\sll_2$-module of highest weight $n$ paired with the image of
$P_n$. \textbf{That the four degrees $8,14,16,22$ are exactly those for which $P_n\neq0$
in the relevant range is Proposition~\ref{prop:C}}, and it is what makes $C$ canonical
rather than a basis choice.

\subsection*{Step 4: the pencil and the cubic}
Form $\mathrm{ad}(x_8+t\,x_{16})$ on $\ee_6$ and restrict to the $36$- and
$12$-dimensional isotypic blocks. Their determinants are polynomials in $t$ over $\Q$
that factor as constants times $\mu(t)^{12}$ and $\mu(t)^{4}$, with
\[
   \mu(t)\;=\;500716339200\,t^{3}\;-\;159667200\,t^{2}\;-\;28224\,t\;+\;1 .
\]
This is the cubic of Proposition~\ref{prop:mu}, printed here so that the bridge can be
audited. Its reduced model is obtained by clearing the leading coefficient and applying
a Tschirnhaus transformation; explicitly, $\rho=-\tfrac{815}{338}
-\tfrac{4934160}{169}\,t+\tfrac{13039488000}{169}\,t^{2}$ satisfies
$\rho^{3}-12\rho-5=0$ in $\Q[t]/\mu$, which exhibits the isomorphism
$K\cong\Q[x]/(x^{3}-12x-5)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:mu} directly.

\begin{remark}[why $\disc\mu$ and $\disc K$ differ]
$\disc\mu=2^{32}\cdot3^{10}\cdot5^{2}\cdot7^{3}\cdot11\cdot13^{6}$ while
$\disc K=3^{4}\cdot7\cdot11$. The extra primes---in particular the $5^2$ and the
$13^6$---are carried by the non-maximal order $\Z[t]/\mu$, and the leading coefficient
$500716339200=2^{16}\cdot3^{4}\cdot5^{2}\cdot7^{3}\cdot11$ already contains a $5^2$.
\textbf{They are artifacts of the pencil's normalization, not arithmetic of the field},
which is the content of the correction recorded after Proposition~\ref{prop:mu}.
\end{remark}

\section{Glossary: one referent per term}\label{app:glossary}

Several terms below are overloaded in the surrounding literature or in this programme's
own working notes. Each is given exactly one referent here, and the paper uses it in no
other sense. The three marked $\dagger$ are recorded because \emph{ambiguity in them has
previously produced errors in this programme}, which is the honest reason a glossary
exists.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,style=nextline,itemsep=1pt]
\item[atom] A joint eigenline of the rational commuting family of the four charges
  acting on the $27$ (Definition~\ref{def:atoms}). Fifteen in total, nine colorless and
  six colored. \emph{Not} used in any physical sense.
\item[census] The $128$-member family $\{\sigma_\chi\}\cup\{\sigma_\chi\circ\tau\}$ of
  Definition~\ref{def:twists}. Where a count of chain links is meant, we say
  \emph{link count}.
\item[charge] One of $x_8,x_{14},x_{16},x_{22}$, the $2T$-invariants of
  Appendix~\ref{app:construct}. An internal name for a Lie-algebra element; \emph{no
  physical charge is meant}.
\item[conductor$^\dagger$] Always qualified. The \textbf{cusp conductor} is the
  conductor of the cusp order (golden $4$); the \textbf{shadow modulus} is
  $m^2+4$ (golden $5$). The bare noun is never used: conflating the two left a
  verification looking open for months when it was already closed.
\item[grammar] The Sturmian morphism $R^mL^m$, equivalently the conjugacy class of
  $\phi_m$. \emph{Not} a presentation of a group.
\item[measurement] The operation of passing to a centralizer within the charge torus.
  An internal name for a Lie-theoretic operation; \emph{nothing observational is
  meant}, and \S\ref{sec:negatives} is where actual comparison with data is reported.
\item[McKay group$^\dagger$] Always qualified as \textbf{exceptional} or
  \textbf{$E$-type}, meaning one of $2T,2O,2I$. The unqualified phrase is vacuous, since
  cyclic subgroups of $\SU(2)$ realize every order (Remark~\ref{rem:vacuous}).
\item[metallic family] $\{R^mL^m\}_{m\ge1}$, characterized in
  Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical}. Not a chosen subfamily.
\item[shadow] The reduction of the monodromy modulo the shadow modulus, landing in
  $\SL(2,\Z/N_m)$.
\item[trace field$^\dagger$] The \textbf{Kleinian trace field} is generated by the
  traces of the holonomy; the \textbf{invariant trace field} is generated by traces of
  squares and is the subfield that decides arithmeticity; the \textbf{fiber field} is
  generated by the monodromy's eigenvalues. These are three different objects, and
  Theorem~\ref{thm:arith} is a statement about the second. Treating a statement about
  one as a statement about another has produced at least one contradiction in this
  programme's own records.
\item[wall] A line in the charge plane on which the centralizer dimension jumps to $46$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:fmt}); there are exactly three, forming one Galois orbit.
\end{description}
