\title[The golden grammar]{The golden grammar: one arithmetic condition and four
  exceptionality theorems
inside a canonical family, and a parameter-free chain to an exceptional Lie algebra}

\author{Drit\"ero Mehmetaj}
\address{Independent Researcher}
\email{driteroi@gmail.com}

\date{\today}

\subjclass[2020]{Primary 57K10, 11R16, 57M50; Secondary 17B25, 46L37, 11J06}
\keywords{once-punctured torus bundles, Sturmian and Christoffel words, continued
fractions, metallic means, McKay correspondence, Jones index, exceptional Lie
algebras, cone-manifold geometric transition}

\begin{abstract}
Let $R^mL^m$, $m\ge1$, be the metallic family of Sturmian morphisms. We prove that
this family is \emph{intrinsically characterized rather than ad hoc}: the hyperbolic
unimodular classes whose dominant eigenvalue has purely periodic continued fraction of
period one are exactly those of determinant $-1$, and their traces are exactly the
integers $m\ge1$, with $X_m$ realizing each. \textbf{The characterization is by trace and
determinant, not by conjugacy class}---at $m=6$ a second class occurs and we exhibit
it---so the family below is a proper subfamily of that locus. \textbf{That is weaker than saying it is not a choice},
and we do not say so: period one is itself a declared input, and we price it. Inside it we exhibit \emph{four exceptionality criteria on the family}---three selecting the
golden member $m=1$ and a fourth narrowing to $\{1,2\}$---
each over the whole infinite family and each anchored in a classical theorem.
\textbf{We prove first that three of them collapse to one arithmetic condition}: they
test the values $\chi_m(1)$, $\chi_m(-1)$ and $\operatorname{tr}\phi_m$ of a single
characteristic polynomial, and each reduces to $m^2=1$. What survives is not
extensional independence---unattainable here, since $m=1$ is the unique trace-three
once-punctured-torus bundle---but \emph{mechanistic} independence: a trivial cokernel,
an exceptional binary polyhedral order and a subfactor index threshold are unrelated
classifications that happen to be met at one value. The criteria are: a homological one, $H_1(M_m)\cong\Z\oplus(\Z/m)^2$, so that only
$m=1$ is a knot complement; an arithmetic one, $|\SL(2,\Z/(m^2{+}4))|$ being the order
of an exceptional binary polyhedral group only at $m=1$; a subfactor one, the metallic
mean clearing the Jones index wall only at $m=1$; and an arithmeticity criterion which
selects $\{1,2\}$ and kills $m\ge3$, included with its limit stated because it is the
criterion a hyperbolic geometer applies first and it does \emph{not} single out the
golden.

The golden member is the figure-eight knot complement. \textbf{From there nothing is
added}: we \emph{measure}---pass to centralizers---and \emph{track}---follow the loci
where the centralizer jumps---and no measured quantity enters any computation. The
target algebra is not chosen from a menu either: the regular $d$-simplex has rotation
group $A_{d+1}$, which lies in $SO(3)$ only for $d\in\{2,3,4\}$, and of those only
$d=3$ has a McKay image that is exceptional with a complex fundamental. \textbf{$d=3$ is
what the figure-eight is glued from.} This yields a $78$-dimensional exceptional Lie
algebra carrying a canonical four-dimensional charge algebra $C$.
\textbf{We prove that $C$ is toral and not merely abelian}---for a finite group acting
by automorphisms on a semisimple Lie algebra, an abelian fixed algebra consists of
semisimple elements---so every centralizer arising in the cascade is a \emph{Levi}
subalgebra. Three consequences: the terminal type $A_2\oplus A_1$ with three-dimensional
centre is \textbf{forced by the classification of Levi subsystems of $E_6$}, the exact
computation supplying only the integer $14$; the attainable centralizer dimensions are
confined to an explicit $14$-element set, so $22$, $24$, $32$, $34$ and $38$--$44$ are
impossible; and the second measurement can be enumerated over the whole of $C$ rather
than sampled. \textbf{The plane on which the cascade is measured is not chosen either}:
it is the fixed algebra $\ee_6^{2O}$ of the unique outer involution of the binary
tetrahedral group. The charge field is computed exactly:
$K\cong\Q[x]/(x^3-12x-5)$, monogenic, totally real, $\disc K=6237=3^4\cdot7\cdot11$,
class number one. One stratum of the cascade has Levi type $A_4$, i.e.\
$\su(5)\oplus\uu(1)^2$, at dimension $26$. \textbf{We record separately, and do not claim
as a consequence}, the textbook fact that $\SU(5)$ contains
$\su(3)\oplus\su(2)\oplus\uu(1)$ with kernel exactly $\Z/6$: \textbf{no step in this paper
connects the two}, since exponentiating a Levi subalgebra to a compact group needs a real
form, we treat none, and the $26$-points are not real in any case.
\textbf{We state plainly what the field computation does and does not
depend on}: $K$, the four invariant degrees, and the whole rung ladder are invariants of
the triple $(\ee_6,\,2T,\,\text{principal }\sll_2)$ and \emph{not} of the figure-eight
knot. Anything reaching $2T$ through the principal $\sll_2$ meets the same cubic. The
manifold's arithmetic is consumed at the entrance, once, and nothing downstream
remembers which manifold it was.

\textbf{We report the ceiling with equal weight}, because it is a theorem about the
method and not a limit of effort: every centralizer of a subset of $C$ contains a Cartan
subalgebra, so \textbf{every rung has rank $6$}. Measurement preserves rank, and any
target of lower rank is therefore unreachable by any sequence of measurements. Descending
in rank would require an operation the object does not supply. We make no physical
identification, and we do not treat real forms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The question}
Fix a principle: \emph{a description that cannot terminate}. Ask what the least such
description is, follow the answer without importing anything from outside, and record
what it costs. This paper carries that out and reports both halves of the result.

The first half is a selection theorem. Minimal aperiodic description is Sturmian
(Morse--Hedlund), and the slopes whose continued fractions are purely periodic of
period one form the \emph{metallic} family. We prove that this family is canonical
(\S\ref{sec:canonical}), and then that its golden member is singled out inside it
\emph{four ways, three of which collapse to one arithmetic condition}
(\S\ref{sec:four}). The golden member is the figure-eight
knot complement, and \S\S\ref{sec:arith}--\ref{sec:cascade} follow its own arithmetic
into an exceptional Lie algebra.

The second half is negative and we ask that it be weighed equally
(\S\ref{sec:negatives}).

\subsection{What is claimed, and what is not}
We claim a \emph{cost theorem}: a single commitment---that description is
inexhaustible---together with two operations
that add nothing, \emph{measuring} (passing to centralizers) and \emph{tracking}
(following the loci where the centralizer jumps), yield the structural data below.
\textbf{We claim that what is added along the way is one thing, that what the object
cannot supply is two, and that the method has a proved ceiling at rank $6$.} The addition is the geometric carrier, at which
$\Q(\sqrt{-3})$ is bought. The residue is one sign and one
scale, both emitted by a single closing (Scope~\ref{sc:residue}). Two steps that an earlier draft booked as choices---the
exceptional target and the principal placement---are supplied by the object and are
retyped in \S\ref{sec:nomination}. \textbf{This framing is more exposed than a list of
priced choices}: an unnamed addition anywhere below refutes it.
\textbf{We count stipulations, not chain links}: the two counts differ, and conflating
them is an error earlier drafts made. We do \textbf{not} claim a derivation of physics, and no physical identification
is made in the body. Internal names are names of mathematical objects.

\subsection{What the reader should be suspicious of}\label{sec:suspicious}
Two things, addressed where they arise rather than in a discussion section.

\emph{First}, arrival at this particular exceptional algebra is \emph{generic}: roughly
one hyperbolic $3$-manifold in three, and five of seven grammars in the relevant
family, arrive at it. \textbf{Arrival is not evidence.} What is not generic is the
\emph{selection} of the golden member, which is why \S\ref{sec:four} carries the paper
and \S\S\ref{sec:frame}--\ref{sec:cascade} are presented as consequences, mostly
standard, with the standard references given.

We can be quantitative rather than rhetorical about this, because the question was
settled by enumeration. \textbf{Counting the entrance itself, there are $20$ steps.} One
of them---the entrance, Proposition~\ref{prop:mod3}---consumes the manifold's arithmetic;
one is the McKay assignment it feeds; and the remaining $18$ are standard
exceptional-algebra theory downstream of that assignment, consuming neither the arithmetic
nor the fundamental group. \textbf{An earlier draft described the same $20$ as lying
``below the entrance'' while counting the entrance among them.} A reader who wishes
to attack this paper should attack \S\ref{sec:four} and \S\ref{sec:entrance}; attacking
the cascade attacks Slansky~\cite{Slansky}.

\emph{Second}, the cost claim is a claim about what entered the computations, and prose
cannot establish it. Appendix~\ref{app:verify} names a runnable check per numerical
claim, with filenames.

\subsection{Relation to existing literature}
The combinatorial setting---Christoffel and Sturmian words, continued fractions,
Markoff theory, Sturmian morphisms and the positive automorphisms of $F_2$---is the
subject of Reutenauer's monograph~\cite{Reutenauer}, the connection going back to
Frobenius. \textbf{Nothing in \S\S\ref{sec:price}--\ref{sec:genesis} is offered as
new.} That the golden ratio, icosahedral symmetry, $2I$ and $E_8$ form a connected
cluster is likewise well established~\cite{Baez,Dechant}. The cone-manifold geometry of
\S\ref{sec:entrance} is due to Thurston, with the figure-eight cone manifolds worked
out by Hilden--Lozano--Montesinos~\cite{HLM95}; \textbf{we claim none of it.} Matrices
attached to Christoffel words, their group structure and determinants, are studied by
Reutenauer and Shallit~\cite{ChristoffelMatrices}---there via the Burrows--Wheeler
matrix of size the word length, reduced modulo that length, with the Zolotareff symbol
as invariant; the construction in \S\ref{sec:shadow} is a $2\times2$ monodromy reduced
modulo a \emph{discriminant}, with a group order as invariant, and the two agree only
in shape.

What we believe to be new is the \emph{conjunction}: that the metallic family is
canonical, and that four classical criteria select the same member of it---three of
them through a single arithmetic condition, as Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} makes explicit.

\section{One commitment, one method}\label{sec:price}

We take as starting point that description is inexhaustible: no finite description
closes. \textbf{That is the paper's single commitment}, and everything after it is
obtained by two operations that add nothing:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.9em,style=nextline,itemsep=2pt]
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{Measure.}}] Pass to the centralizer of what has been observed.
  This is an \emph{observation} of what already commutes, not a construction: no
  generator, no relation and no parameter enters.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{Track.}}] Follow the loci on which the centralizer dimension
  jumps, rather than sampling generic points. \textbf{This is what following a structure
  means}; a generic point returns the floor and reports nothing. It is stated here once,
  as method, and is not re-decided at each step.
\end{description}

\noindent\textbf{The claim of this paper is that the two operations carry the
construction a long way, and that where they do not, the residue is small, named and
irreducible.} A residue is not a free parameter dressed up---it is a place where the
object genuinely fails to supply something and we say what.

\begin{scope}[what would falsify the framing, not just a step]\label{sc:framing}
\textbf{If any step below turns out to require an addition we have not named, the claim
of this section fails even if every theorem survives.} That is a stronger exposure than
a list of priced choices, which can absorb a new row without embarrassment. Two
candidates that an earlier draft did book as choices have since dissolved
(\S\ref{sec:nomination}), and one that we do not know how to dissolve is stated as
residue in \S\ref{sec:inputs}.
\end{scope}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,style=nextline]
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{(C1) Description is inexhaustible.}}] The one metaphysical
  commitment. \emph{Price.} Both alternatives degenerate. The periodic counterpart
  collapses: the whole $\det=+1$, $|\tr|\le2$ family is finite-order or reducible, so
  there is no pseudo-Anosov element and no hyperbolic carrier. Variants of the shadow
  rule degenerate or become conjugate to the original. Neither alternative supports the
  construction at all.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{(C2) The word is realized on the once-punctured torus.}}] A
  geometric carrier is chosen over combinatorial ones. \emph{Price, and it is the
  consequential one.} The canonical non-geometric carriers---the tiling hull, and the
  Effros--Shen algebra with $K_0=\Z[\varphi]$---see only the real quadratic data. All
  four pre-registered redundancy witnesses fail exactly; in particular $x^2+3$ remains
  irreducible over $\Q(\sqrt5)$. \textbf{The imaginary quadratic field $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$
  is bought here and nowhere earlier}, and every arithmetic consequence in
  \S\S\ref{sec:arith}--\ref{sec:cascade} depends on it.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{(C3) The monodromy is orientation-preserving.}}] Equivalently,
  the generator is squared. \emph{Price, and it is the largest.} \textbf{The discarded
  alternative is not degenerate.} It is the Gieseking manifold, whose orientation double
  cover is the manifold we keep, with dilatation $\lambda_1$ upstairs against
  $\lambda_1^2$ below. A reader who prefers the parent to the child obtains a different,
  perfectly good object, to which the construction below does not apply.
\end{description}

\textbf{C3 is a genuine fork and we price it, but we no longer call it ``the honest

real choice is preferring the child to the parent: the Gieseking manifold is a perfectly
good object to which the construction below does not apply, and no computation here
forces the preference. We flag it at the point of use rather than in a limitations
section.

\section{From minimal description to a family}\label{sec:genesis}

\begin{theorem}[Morse--Hedlund~\cite{MorseHedlund}]\label{thm:mh}
If an infinite word $w$ over a finite alphabet is aperiodic, its factor complexity
satisfies $p(n)\ge n+1$ for all $n\ge1$. The words attaining $p(n)=n+1$ for every $n$
are exactly the Sturmian words.
\end{theorem}

Periodic words close, so C1 excludes them; among the words that do not close,
Theorem~\ref{thm:mh} isolates a unique minimal complexity class, and it is non-empty.
Sturmian words are classified by an irrational slope $\alpha$.

\begin{theorem}[Hurwitz]\label{thm:hurwitz}
For every irrational $\alpha$ the Lagrange number satisfies $L(\alpha)\ge\sqrt5$, with
equality exactly on the $\GL(2,\Z)$-equivalence class of $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$.
\end{theorem}

We state Hurwitz over \emph{all} irrationals deliberately. It is stronger than a
within-family computation, and it means the golden equivalence class is distinguished
before any family is fixed---so no restriction to a subfamily is needed for the
extremality statement, and none is smuggled.

\begin{remark}[what Theorem~\ref{thm:hurwitz} does \emph{not} give]\label{rem:hurwitzlimit}
Hurwitz distinguishes an equivalence \emph{class}, not a manifold. Every irrational in
the class has the same Lagrange number, and passing from a slope to a $3$-manifold
requires C2 and C3. \textbf{The selection theorems of \S\ref{sec:four} are therefore not
restatements of Hurwitz}: they distinguish one member of a family of pairwise
non-homeomorphic manifolds, which Hurwitz cannot do.
\end{remark}

\section{The metallic family is intrinsically characterized}\label{sec:canonical}

A referee's first objection to any ``uniqueness inside a family'' theorem is that the
family was chosen to make it true. We answer the sharpest form of it---that the family
has no description independent of the conclusion---while being explicit about what is
\emph{not} thereby answered.

\begin{definition}
For $m\ge1$ let $\lambda_m=\tfrac12\bigl(m+\sqrt{m^2+4}\bigr)$ be the $m$-th
\emph{metallic mean}, the positive root of $x^2-mx-1=0$; $\lambda_1=\varphi$ is golden,
$\lambda_2=1+\sqrt2$ silver. Put
\[
   X_m=\begin{pmatrix}m&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
   \phi_m=X_m^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}m^2+1&m\\m&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
so $\det X_m=-1$, $\det\phi_m=+1$. Let $M_m$ be the mapping torus of the once-punctured
torus under $\phi_m$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[the family is a characterization]\label{thm:canonical}
Let $A\in\GL(2,\Z)$ be hyperbolic with non-negative entries. The continued fraction of
its dominant eigenvalue is purely periodic of period one if and only if $\det A=-1$; in
that case $\tr A=m$ for some $m\ge1$ and the eigenvalue is $\lambda_m$. \textbf{The
classification is by $(\tr,\det)$ alone}: $X_m$ realizes each attained trace, but is
\emph{not} in general the unique $\GL(2,\Z)$-class doing so (Scope~\ref{sc:latimer}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the dominant eigenvalue has purely periodic expansion $[m;m,m,\dots]$ of period
one, $m\ge1$. Then $x=m+1/x$, i.e.\ $x^2-mx-1=0$, whose companion matrix is $X_m$ with
$\det X_m=-1$, $\tr X_m=m$, discriminant $m^2+4$ and dominant root $\lambda_m$.
Conversely, if $A$ is hyperbolic with non-negative entries and $\det A=-1$, its
characteristic polynomial is $x^2-tx-1$ with $t=\tr A\ge1$; the dominant root satisfies
$x=t+1/x$, hence has the purely periodic expansion $[t;t,t,\dots]$ of period one.
Distinct $m$ give distinct traces, hence distinct conjugacy classes; the converse
direction is not injective on classes, only on traces.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:c3family}
Squaring, as (C3) requires, carries the classes of this locus into $\SL(2,\Z)$, and
$\{\phi_m\}_{m\ge1}$ are among the results---\textbf{but they are not all of them}, by
Scope~\ref{sc:latimer}. \textbf{The family quantified over in \S\ref{sec:four} is a proper subfamily of the period-one
class of \S\ref{sec:genesis}'s own continued-fraction language, made
orientation-preserving.}

\begin{proposition}[the period-one restriction, priced]\label{prop:periodtwo}
Let $M(a,b)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}b&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}ab+1&a\\b&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ be the matrix of a purely
periodic continued fraction of period two. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item $\det M(a,b)=+1$ \textbf{already} --- a period-two word is orientation-preserving
  without squaring;
\item $M(m,m)=\phi_m$, so \textbf{the metallic family is the diagonal} of the period-two
  family, which is therefore strictly larger; and
\item $H_1\bigl(M_{a,b}\bigr)\cong\Z\oplus\Z/\gcd(a,b)\oplus\Z/\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)$,
  so the \textbf{only} knot complement in the whole two-parameter family is
  $(a,b)=(1,1)$: the golden.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) and (ii) are immediate from the product. For (iii),
$M(a,b)-I=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}ab&a\\b&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has entry gcd
$\gcd(a,b)$ and determinant $-ab$, so its invariant factors are $\gcd(a,b)$ and
$ab/\gcd(a,b)=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)$; apply the Wang sequence as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:homology}. The torsion vanishes iff $\gcd=\operatorname{lcm}=1$, i.e.\
$a=b=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{scope}[what this buys]\label{sc:pricedperiod}
\textbf{The period-one restriction is therefore priced, and against the period-two
relaxation---the only one tested---the price is zero.} Relaxing
it to period two adds a genuine two-parameter family---$(a,b)=(2,3)$ has trace $8$,
which is not of the form $m^2+2$---and the homological criterion of
\S\ref{sec:homological} selects \emph{the same member} from the larger family. So the
restriction is not doing work that the selection could not do without it. We do not
claim this for period three or beyond, and we do not claim period one is forced; we
claim only that the one widening a reader is most likely to propose has been computed
and leaves the conclusion unchanged.
\end{scope}

\begin{scope}[what this does and does not settle]\label{sc:notunchosen}
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical} shows the metallic family is \emph{intrinsically
characterized}: it has a description in the ambient language that does not mention its
members. \textbf{It does not show that period one is the only admissible period.}
Period two, bounded partial quotients, and Markov classes are equally definable
families, and nothing proved here excludes them. So the honest statement is
``characterized, not ad hoc'', and \emph{not} ``forced''; a reader who wants the
stronger claim should regard the choice of period one as a declared choice; it is
carried as \textbf{(C5)} in the ledger of \S\ref{sec:inputs}, where its price against the period-two
relaxation is computed and turns out to be \emph{zero}; period three and beyond are
unchecked.
\end{scope}
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}[what Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical} does \emph{not} say, with a witness]\label{sc:latimer}
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical} classifies by $(\tr,\det)$ and exhibits $X_m$ as \emph{a}
representative. \textbf{It does not assert that every trace-$m$, $\det=-1$ matrix is
$\GL(2,\Z)$-conjugate to $X_m$, and that is false.} By Latimer--MacDuffee the classes
with a given irreducible characteristic polynomial correspond to the ideal classes of
$\Z[\lambda_m]$, and $h(m^2+4)>1$ for some $m$~\cite{LatimerMacDuffee}. \textbf{An explicit witness}, which an
earlier draft did not supply:
\[
   A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&5\end{pmatrix}
\]
has non-negative entries, $\det A=-1$, $\tr A=6$, and dominant eigenvalue
$3+\sqrt{10}=\lambda_6$ with expansion $[\,\overline{6}\,]$---so it satisfies every
hypothesis and the characterizing property---yet $A\not\sim X_6$: the associated forms
are $(3,4,-2)$ and $(1,-6,-1)$ of discriminant $40$, and $x^2-10y^2=\pm3$ is insoluble
modulo~$5$. The two mapping tori are genuinely different manifolds.

\textbf{The consequence, stated rather than absorbed.} The period-one class, made
orientation-preserving, \emph{strictly contains} $\{\phi_m\}$, so \S\ref{sec:four}
quantifies over a proper subfamily of what this section characterizes.
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical} therefore answers ``was the family reverse-engineered?''
only at the level of traces, which is weaker than an earlier draft implied by writing
``the class''. An earlier draft also placed the first failure at $m\ge4$; we do not
assert a threshold here, having verified only the witness above.
\end{scope}

\section{Four selections of the golden, and what they share}\label{sec:four}

This section is the paper. Before stating the criteria we state, and prove, the sense in
which they are and are not independent---because the honest answer is sharper than the
obvious claim, and it is what a referee will test first.

\begin{definition}[the letters, and the bridge to matrices]\label{def:RL}
Write $R$ and $L$ for the two elementary Sturmian morphisms of $\{a,b\}^*$,
\[
   R:\ a\mapsto a,\ b\mapsto ab,\qquad
   L:\ a\mapsto ba,\ b\mapsto b,
\]
whose incidence matrices are
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; we use the same letters for
the matrices. Every Sturmian morphism is a product of these two up to the exchange
$a\leftrightarrow b$, and abelianization sends a positive word in $R,L$ to the
corresponding product in $\SL(2,\Z)$. \textbf{The metallic word is $R^mL^m$}, with
\[
   R^mL^m=\begin{pmatrix}m^2+1&m\\m&1\end{pmatrix}=\phi_m .
\]
The bridge from a symbolic word to a matrix is half the title of this paper, so it is
stated as a definition rather than left to context.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[the collapse lemma]\label{lem:collapse}
Let $\chi_m(t)=\det(tI-\phi_m)=t^2-(m^2+2)t+1$. Then
\[
   \chi_m(1)=\det(\phi_m-I)=-m^2,\qquad
   \chi_m(-1)=\det(\phi_m+I)=m^2+4,\qquad
   \operatorname{tr}\phi_m=m^2+2 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from $\phi_m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}m^2+1&m\\m&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$:
the characteristic polynomial is $t^2-(\operatorname{tr}\phi_m)t+\det\phi_m$ with
$\operatorname{tr}\phi_m=m^2+2$ and $\det\phi_m=1$; evaluate at $t=\pm1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{scope}[what Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} costs, stated before the criteria and not after]\label{sc:collapse}
\textbf{The homological invariant, the shadow modulus and the coset level are the three
values $\chi_m(1)$, $\chi_m(-1)$ and $\operatorname{tr}\phi_m$ of a single polynomial},
and each of the corresponding criteria reduces to the same arithmetic step, $m^2=1$.
An earlier draft asserted that the criteria ``share no lemma''. \textbf{That is false,
and we withdraw it}: they share this one.

What survives, and is worth stating precisely, is a distinction between two senses of
independence. \emph{Extensional} independence is unattainable here and no paper should
claim it: $m=1$ is the unique trace-three once-punctured-torus bundle, so \emph{any}
property holding only of the figure-eight is automatically co-extensive with
$\operatorname{tr}=3$. \emph{Mechanistic} independence is the real question---whether the
theorems that declare the value at $m=1$ exceptional are separate theorems---and there
the answer is genuinely affirmative: a trivial cokernel, an exceptional binary
polyhedral order, and a subfactor index threshold are unrelated classifications that
happen to be met at one value.

\textbf{So the correct claim is not ``several independent selections'' but ``one
arithmetic condition, and several unrelated exceptionality theorems satisfied at the
value it picks out''}---together with genuinely separate criteria of different logical
type (\S\ref{sec:arithmeticity}, and the pairwise condition of Scope~\ref{sc:pairwise}).
Saying this ourselves is what makes the section survive the objection rather than invite
it.
\end{scope}

\subsection{Selection I: homological}\label{sec:homological}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:homology}
$H_1(M_m;\Z)\cong\Z\oplus(\Z/m)^2$. Hence $H_1(M_m)\cong\Z$ if and only if $m=1$, and
\textbf{$M_m$ is a knot complement in $S^3$ only for $m=1$}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a mapping torus of a surface $F$ with monodromy $\phi$, the Wang sequence gives
$H_1(M_\phi)\cong\Z\oplus\coker(\phi_*-I)$, where $\phi_*$ acts on $H_1(F)$. Here $F$ is
the once-punctured torus, $H_1(F)\cong\Z^2$, and $\phi_*=\phi_m$, so
\[
   \phi_m-I=\begin{pmatrix}m^2&m\\m&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The gcd of the entries is $m$, so the first invariant factor is $d_1=m$; and
$\det(\phi_m-I)=-m^2$, so $d_1d_2=m^2$ and $d_2=m$. The Smith normal form is therefore
$\mathrm{diag}(m,m)$ and $\coker(\phi_m-I)\cong(\Z/m)^2$, which is trivial exactly when
$m=1$. A knot complement in $S^3$ has $H_1\cong\Z$ by Alexander duality, excluding every
$m\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is an \emph{obstruction}, not a numerical near miss: for every $m\ge2$ the torsion
is non-trivial and grows. Theorem~\ref{thm:homology} is also what makes the geometry of
\S\ref{sec:entrance} golden-\emph{specific} rather than golden-\emph{exhibited}, since
the objects there are knot complements.
\end{remark}

At $m=1$ four independently defined integers coincide, all equal to $5$: the
discriminant $m^2+4$; the knot determinant $|\Delta_{4_1}(-1)|$; the numerator of the
$2$-bridge fraction; and $|H_1|$ of the lens space at the spherical end of
\S\ref{sec:entrance}. They coincide only at $m=1$.

\subsection{Selection II: arithmetic, via the shadow modulus}\label{sec:shadow}

\begin{definition}
The \emph{shadow modulus} of $R^mL^m$ is $N_m=m^2+4$, the discriminant of $x^2-mx-1$.
Equivalently $N_m=\det(\phi_m+I)$, an invariant of the monodromy rather than of a chosen
normalization.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:telescope}
For every integer $N\ge2$, $\displaystyle\prod_{p\mid N}\bigl(1-p^{-2}\bigr)\ \ge\
\frac{N+1}{2N}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The primes dividing $N$ form a subset of $\{2,\dots,N\}$, and every factor $1-k^{-2}$
lies in $(0,1)$, so enlarging the index set only decreases the product. The enlarged
product telescopes:
\[
   \prod_{k=2}^{N}\frac{(k-1)(k+1)}{k^{2}}
   =\Bigl(\prod_{k=2}^{N}\frac{k-1}{k}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{k=2}^{N}\frac{k+1}{k}\Bigr)
   =\frac{1}{N}\cdot\frac{N+1}{2}. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:shadow}
$|\SL(2,\Z/N)|$ equals the order of one of the exceptional binary polyhedral groups
$2T,2O,2I$---that is, $24$, $48$ or $120$---if and only if $N\in\{3,4,5\}$. Consequently,
over the whole metallic family, $N_m$ meets that set only at \textbf{$m=1$}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$|\SL(2,\Z/N)|=N^{3}\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2})$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope},
\[
  |\SL(2,\Z/N)|\ \ge\ N^{3}\cdot\frac{N+1}{2N}\ =\ \frac{N^{2}(N+1)}{2},
\]
which equals $126>120$ at $N=6$ and is strictly increasing in $N$; so no $N\ge6$
qualifies. Direct evaluation gives $6,24,48,120$ at $N=2,3,4,5$. For the second
statement: $m^2+4=3$ has no integer solution; $m^2+4=4$ forces $m=0$, degenerate and not
a metallic word; $m^2+4=5$ gives $m=1$; and $N_m\ge8$ for $m\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the qualifier is not decoration]\label{rem:vacuous}
The unqualified statement ``$|\SL(2,\Z/N)|$ is a McKay-group order'' is \emph{vacuous}.
The McKay correspondence covers every finite subgroup of $\SU(2)$, and the cyclic
subgroups---type $A_n$, order $n+1$---realize \emph{every} positive integer as an order,
the binary dihedral family being unbounded as well. Only restriction to the three
exceptional groups gives a finite target set, and it is that restriction
Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope} exploits.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[group versus order]\label{prop:group}
$\SL(2,\Z/3)\cong2T$ and $\SL(2,\Z/5)\cong2I$ are the classical isomorphisms. However
$\SL(2,\Z/4)\not\cong2O$: it contains seven elements of order $2$, whereas a finite
subgroup of $\SU(2)$ contains \emph{at most} one, necessarily the centre $-I$; and it contains no
element of order $8$, whereas $2O$ does. \textbf{The coincidence of orders at $48$ does
not produce the group, and the $E_7$ slot is unoccupied.} At prime level the same
conclusion follows differently: $|\SL(2,\mathbb F_p)|=p(p^2-1)$ is never $48$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A finite subgroup $G<\SU(2)$ has a unique element of order two: the preimage of the
identity under $\SU(2)\to\mathrm{SO}(3)$ is $\{\pm I\}$, and any $g\in G$ with $g^2=I$,
$g\neq I$, is conjugate to $-I$ in $\SU(2)$ and central, forcing $g=-I$; so a finite
subgroup has \emph{at most} one such element, and none when its order is odd. Counting
solutions of $g^2=I$ in $\SL(2,\Z/4)$ gives seven non-identity solutions; counting
element orders gives none equal to $8$. Both counts are over the full group of order
$48$. The prime-level statement is immediate from $p(p^2-1)\in\{6,24,120,336,\dots\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Requiring a genuine exceptional \emph{group} rather than a matching order removes $N=4$
from the target set, and no metallic word was ever there, since $m^2+4=4$ forces $m=0$.
The surviving target at $m=1$ is $\SL(2,\Z/5)\cong2I$, onto which $\SL(2,\Z)$ surjects.
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}[what lands, and what merely contains it]\label{sc:landing}
\textbf{An earlier draft wrote that ``the golden member lands on a genuine group'', which
overstates what the manifold contributes.} The reduction map
$\SL(2,\Z)\to\SL(2,\Z/5)$ is surjective, but the object attached to \emph{this manifold}
is the image of its monodromy, and that is small: $\chi_1(t)\equiv(t-4)^2 \bmod 5$, so
$\phi_1\bmod5$ is $4$ times a unipotent and generates a \textbf{cyclic group of order
$10$}. So $2I$ is the ambient group in which the monodromy's shadow sits, not the shadow
itself. \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow} is a statement about the modulus $N_m=m^2+4$,
which is a function of $m$ alone}, and Scope~\ref{sc:finer}'s group-level filter is
likewise a filter on $N$. We keep the criterion---it does discriminate, and sharply---but
it discriminates between \emph{grammars} through their moduli, not between manifolds
through their monodromy images.
\end{scope}

\begin{scope}[the filter is finer than the field]\label{sc:finer}
A discriminant-level criterion can conflate distinct objects, and here it demonstrably
does: $1^2{+}4=5$, $4^2{+}4=20=2^2\cdot5$ and $11^2{+}4=125=5^3$ all have squarefree
kernel $5$, so $m=1,4,11$ share the field $\Q(\sqrt5)$. \textbf{The group-level filter
separates them}: the orders are $120$, $5760$ and $1\,875\,000$. This is exactly why
Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow} is a statement about the group at the modulus and not about the
field of the discriminant, and it is why field-level genericity results do not touch it.
\end{scope}

\subsection{Selection III: subfactor-theoretic, via the Jones index}\label{sec:jones}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:jones}
$\lambda_m<2$ if and only if $m=1$, and $\lambda_1=\varphi=2\cos(\pi/5)$. Hence the golden mean is the unique metallic mean admissible as the \emph{statistical
dimension of the generating bimodule of a subfactor of index below $4$}; every $m\ge2$
lies above the Jones index wall. \textbf{The exclusion of $m\ge2$ is the elementary
inequality $\lambda_m\ge2$, not a subfactor theorem}: every real index $\ge4$ is
realised, so \cite{Jones} excludes nothing on its own. What Jones contributes is that
the admissible dimensions \emph{below} $2$ form the discrete list $2\cos(\pi/n)$ and
that $\varphi$ lies on it. (We state it for subfactor bimodules rather than for
objects of a fusion category, since it is the inclusion that carries the index.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\lambda_m$ is strictly increasing in $m$, and $\lambda_2=1+\sqrt2>2$, so $\lambda_m>2$
for all $m\ge2$; whereas $\lambda_1=\tfrac12(1+\sqrt5)=\varphi<2$. By Jones' index
theorem the admissible indices below $4$ are exactly $4\cos^2(\pi/n)$, $n\ge3$~\cite{Jones}, so the
admissible dimensions below $2$ are $2\cos(\pi/n)$; and $\varphi=2\cos(\pi/5)$.
\end{proof}

This selection is \emph{mechanistically} independent of \S\ref{sec:shadow} in the sense
of Scope~\ref{sc:collapse}---not extensionally, which nothing here could be: it uses no
modulus, no reduction and no finite group, only the size of a real algebraic integer
against a classical quantization theorem. An objection to the shadow construction
therefore does not touch it, and vice versa.

\subsection{Selection IV: arithmeticity, and its honest limit}\label{sec:arithmeticity}

\begin{theorem}[cf.~\cite{MaclachlanReid,BMR}]\label{thm:arith}
\textbf{Within the metallic family}, exactly $m=1$ and $m=2$ are arithmetic: their
invariant trace fields are $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and $\Q(i)$ respectively, and every $m\ge3$
is non-arithmetic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bowditch, Maclachlan and Reid prove that for a fixed fibre type there are only finitely
many \emph{cyclic} commensurability classes of arithmetic surface bundles, and that for
the once-punctured torus there are \textbf{precisely three}~\cite{BMR}, carried by
\[
   RL\ \to\ \Q(\sqrt{-3}),\qquad RRLL\ \to\ \Q(i),\qquad RRL\ \to\ \Q(\sqrt{-7}).
\]
\textbf{The classification must be applied power-closed}, and this is the step at which a
shorter argument fails. Arithmeticity is a property of the commensurability class, and
$M(S,\varphi^k)$ is a cyclic cover of $M(S,\varphi)$, hence commensurable with it; so
every \emph{power} of an arithmetic monodromy is again arithmetic. The arithmetic
monodromies are therefore exactly those conjugate to a power of one of the three words
above---not exactly those three words. \textbf{Concluding ``$R^mL^m$ is none of $RL$,
$RRLL$, $RRL$, hence non-arithmetic'' is unsound}: $RLRL$ is none of them either and is
arithmetic, being $(RL)^2$.

Argue on block sequences instead, which are cyclic-word invariants. The powers have
block sequences
\[
   (RL)^k:\ (1,1,\dots,1)\ \ [2k\ \text{blocks}],\qquad
   (RRLL)^k:\ (2,2,\dots,2),\qquad
   (RRL)^k:\ (2,1,2,1,\dots),
\]
while $R^mL^m$ has \textbf{exactly two} blocks, both of length $m$. Two blocks forces
$k=1$ in each family, leaving $RL$, $RRLL$ and $RRL$; of these only the first two have
equal block lengths, giving $m=1$ and $m=2$ with the stated fields, and $RRL$ has
lengths $(2,1)$ so is not metallic. Hence no $m\ge3$ is arithmetic.
\end{proof}

\begin{scope}[what is cited and what is checked]\label{sc:bmr}
\cite{BMR} supplies both the finiteness and the count of three; the word-to-field
dictionary we have \emph{also} verified independently rather than taken on authority: over all cyclic $R,L$ words of length $\le8$,
exactly three imaginary quadratic invariant trace fields occur, carried by the words
above, with $RL$ the figure-eight complement. The unit is the \emph{commensurability
class}: there are infinitely many arithmetic once-punctured-torus bundles, since every
power of an arithmetic monodromy is one, and any statement counting \emph{bundles} is
false. Direct computation confirms the conclusion independently for $m=3,\dots,10$,
where the invariant trace field has degree $8,4,18,8,>24,12,>24,18$ and is imaginary
quadratic in no case.
\end{scope}

\begin{remark}[why this is a proof and not a census]
\textbf{An unbounded quantifier cannot rest on a finite table.} The tail
``every $m\ge3$'' is earned here by the classification, not by the direct computations of
Scope~\ref{sc:bmr}, which cover $m\le10$ and serve only as a check.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[why the quantifier must be restricted]\label{rem:notglobal}
The corresponding \emph{global} statement---``there are exactly two arithmetic
once-punctured-torus bundles''---is \textbf{false}, and we record the counterexample
because an earlier draft of this work asserted it. Arithmeticity is a commensurability
invariant, so every finite cover of an arithmetic manifold is arithmetic; and the
$n$-fold cyclic cover of $M_1$ in the base direction is again a once-punctured-torus
bundle, with monodromy $\phi_1^{\,n}$. Hence $\{M_{\phi_1^n}\}_{n\ge1}$ is an infinite
family of arithmetic once-punctured-torus bundles.

These do not disturb Theorem~\ref{thm:arith}, because for $n\ge2$ they leave the
metallic family: $\tr\phi_1^{\,n}=m^2+2$ requires $m^2=\tr\phi_1^{\,n}-2$, which is a
square only at $n=1,3,5,\dots$, giving $m=1,4,11,\dots$---and even there the matrices
are \emph{not} conjugate. For instance $\phi_1^{3}$ and $\phi_4$ share trace $18$,
determinant $1$, \emph{and} mapping-torus torsion $(\Z/4)^2$, yet no
$P\in\GL(2,\Z)$ conjugates one to the other: a concrete illustration of the
Latimer--MacDuffee phenomenon already flagged in Scope~\ref{sc:latimer}. \textbf{So the
$m=1,4,11$ coincidence of Scope~\ref{sc:finer} is a trace coincidence, not a covering
relation.}
\end{remark}

\begin{scope}\label{sc:arithlimit}
\textbf{This criterion does not single out the golden}: it selects $\{1,2\}$. We include
it because it is the criterion a hyperbolic geometer reaches for first, and because
stating its limit is what licenses the other three. In particular, ``the arithmetic
knot'' is correct among \emph{knots} and false among \emph{bundles}, where silver is
also arithmetic. What survives as golden-specific, and is what \S\ref{sec:arith} uses,
is narrower: $m=1$ is the unique member whose invariant trace field is $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$,
the field C2 purchased.
\end{scope}

\subsection{What the four jointly establish}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule

\midrule
I   & $H_1(M_m)\cong\Z$ & $\{1\}$ & Alexander duality; Smith normal form\\
II  & $|\SL(2,\Z/N_m)|$ exceptional & $\{1\}$ & order formula; McKay\\
III & $\lambda_m<2$ & $\{1\}$ & Jones index theorem\\
IV  & arithmeticity & $\{1,2\}$ & Maclachlan--Reid\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:four}
Inside the canonical family of \S\ref{sec:canonical}, the golden member is the unique
one satisfying any of I--III, and the unique one satisfying IV together with the
trace-field condition of Scope~\ref{sc:arithlimit}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}[the pairwise criterion, of a different logical type]\label{sc:pairwise}
One banked criterion is not a condition on a single $m$ at all, and so escapes
Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} by construction. For the \emph{monodromies} $\phi_m,\phi_n$ one
has Fricke's commutator identity
\[
   \operatorname{tr}[\phi_m,\phi_n]\;=\;2-\bigl(mn(n-m)\bigr)^2 ,
\]
so the commutator is parabolic---$|\!\operatorname{tr}|=2$ and $\ne\pm I$---exactly when
$|mn(n-m)|=2$, whose unique solution with $1\le m<n$ is $(m,n)=(1,2)$. \textbf{The trace
there is $-2$, not $+2$}: $[\phi_1,\phi_2]=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}11&-24\\6&-13\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
with repeated eigenvalue $-1$. We say so because $\operatorname{tr}=+2$ would force
$mn(n-m)=0$, which is impossible for $1\le m<n$, and a reader who reads ``parabolic'' as
``trace exactly $2$'' will conclude the criterion is never met. This is a two-variable Diophantine condition, not a
function of $m^2$, and it selects the golden--silver \emph{pair} rather than a member.

\textbf{The identity must be stated for $\phi_m$ and cannot be stated for $X_m$}, and the
reason is the point rather than a technicality: Fricke's identity is an identity in
$\SL(2,\Z)$, and $\det X_m=-1$ while $\det\phi_m=+1$. For the generators themselves it
simply fails---$\operatorname{tr}[X_1,X_2]=1$, not $-2$. \textbf{An earlier draft wrote
the identity for an undefined symbol $A_m$}; under the reading $A_m=X_m$ it is false, and
the squaring of (C3) is exactly what makes the true version available. The identity is classical~\cite{Fricke,Cohn} and we cite rather than claim it.
\end{scope}

\begin{scope}[what this is not]
None of the four is a statement about physics, and \textbf{none is evidence that the
construction below is physically realized}. They establish that a single member of a
canonically characterized family is distinguished by several independent classical
criteria---a mathematical fact, and the one this paper asks to be judged on.
\end{scope}

\section{The golden member and its arithmetic}\label{sec:arith}

\begin{theorem}[Thurston; Riley]\label{thm:thurston}
$M_1$ is the complement of the figure-eight knot. It carries a unique complete
hyperbolic structure, and its Kleinian trace field is $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:v4}
Let $L=\Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt5)$ be the compositum of the Kleinian trace field with the
fiber field. Then $L/\Q$ is biquadratic with $\Gal(L/\Q)\cong V_4$, and its three
quadratic subfields are exactly $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$, $\Q(\sqrt5)$, $\Q(\sqrt{-15})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The content is that the construction produces \emph{both} generators of $L$---the first
at C2 and the second at the slope---not that a biquadratic field has three quadratic
subfields, which is automatic. Note that $\Q(\sqrt5)$ enters \emph{before} the manifold
exists.
\end{remark}

The group of $M_1$ is moreover a congruence subgroup of the relevant Bianchi group over
$\Q(\sqrt{-3})$; and no closed hyperbolic Dehn filling in the surveyed slope grid
carries any of the three fields, so the $V_4$ is a property of the \emph{cusped} object.

\section{The entrance}\label{sec:entrance}

\subsection{The two curvature ends}
The cone-manifold family of the figure-eight complement---hyperbolic below cone angle
$2\pi/3$, \emph{Euclidean exactly at} $2\pi/3$, spherical beyond---is due to Thurston,
with the figure-eight cone manifolds worked out by
Hilden--Lozano--Montesinos~\cite{HLM95} and the degenerations by Hodgson and Porti.
\textbf{We claim none of this geometry.} What we add is which arithmetic sits at each
end.

\begin{theorem}[the two ends, by Niven's theorem]\label{thm:ends}
At the $\Z/n$ orbifold of $4_1$---meridian a rotation of order $n$, cone angle
$2\pi/n$---the meridian trace is $x=2\cos(\pi/n)$ and the trace field is
\[
   \Q\Bigl(x,\ \sqrt{(5-x^2)(1-x^2)}\Bigr),
\]
from the character-variety relation $u^2+(5-x^2)u+(5-x^2)=0$. This is a \emph{clean}
quadratic field---a single McKay group---if and only if $x\in\Q$. \textbf{By Niven's
theorem $2\cos(\pi/n)\in\Q$ only for $n\in\{1,2,3\}$, together with the cusp $n=\infty$},
and those cases are exactly
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccccc@{}}
\toprule
$n$ & $x$ & geometry & $(5-x^2)(1-x^2)$ & field, McKay image\\
\midrule
$\infty,\,1$ & $\pm2$ & hyperbolic (complete cusp) & $-3$ & $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$, $2T\to E_6$\\
$3$ & $1$ & Euclidean (the transition) & $0$ & $\Q$, degenerate\\
$2$ & $0$ & spherical & $5$ & $\Q(\sqrt5)$, $2I\to E_8$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At the spherical end the double cover is the lens space $L(5,2)=S^3/\Z_5$, with
$|H_1|=\det(4_1)=5$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[$E_7$ is geometrically impossible here]\label{cor:noe7}
$E_7$'s McKay field is $\Q(\sqrt2)$, which would require $x=\sqrt2$, i.e.\ $n=4$. That
value is irrational, so by the theorem the $n=4$ trace field is the \emph{mixed}
$\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{-3})$ and never the clean $\Q(\sqrt2)$. \textbf{The middle member of
the exceptional trio is excluded by one arithmetic fact rather than by a coincidence of
several.}
\end{corollary}

This is the pattern of \S\ref{sec:shape} again and in a third category. Rational

separately.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[the step that consumes the arithmetic]\label{prop:mod3}
The discrete faithful representation of $\pi_1(4_1)$ has Riley parameter $u=\omega$, a
primitive cube root of unity and hence a \emph{unit} of $\Z[\omega]$, the ring of
integers of the trace field $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$. Reduction modulo the ramified prime
$(\sqrt{-3})$ gives $\Z[\omega]/(\sqrt{-3})\cong\F_3$, sends $u$ to a nonzero element,
and carries the two parabolic generators to
\[
   A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
   B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix}\in\SL(2,\F_3).
\]
These generate all of $\SL(2,\F_3)$, of order $3(3^2-1)=24$, and
$\SL(2,\F_3)\cong2T$. \textbf{So $\pi_1(4_1)$ surjects onto $2T$}, and the surjection is
reduction of the manifold's own trace field modulo its own ramified prime.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[this is the one step that consumes the arithmetic]\label{rem:consumes}
\S\ref{sec:suspicious} counts one step of the construction as consuming the manifold's
arithmetic, and \textbf{Proposition~\ref{prop:mod3} is that step}. Nothing before it uses
$\Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and nothing after it uses the manifold at all: what crosses is a finite
group, and everything downstream is an invariant of the triple
$(\ee_6,2T,\text{principal }\sll_2)$ rather than of the manifold---as the abstract states
and \S\ref{sec:frame} onwards bears out.

\textbf{The geometric origin is worth naming, because it is not a numerical accident.}
The figure-eight complement is glued from \emph{two regular ideal tetrahedra}, both of
shape $e^{i\pi/3}$ with $z^2-z+1=0$, which is what makes the trace field
$\Q(\sqrt{-3})$. \textbf{The tetrahedral field yields the tetrahedral group.} We stress
\emph{regular}: every ideally triangulated cusped $3$-manifold is glued from ideal
tetrahedra, and it is regularity---the shape being a sixth root of unity---that produces
$\Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and hence $\F_3$ and hence $2T$.
\end{remark}

\begin{scope}[the two ends are not symmetric]\label{sc:asym}
The asymmetry runs against the direction the phrasing invites. At the hyperbolic end
Proposition~\ref{prop:mod3} gives a genuine group surjection
$\pi_1(4_1)\twoheadrightarrow 2T$, with exactly two such quotients. At the spherical end
there is \emph{no} surjection: $\pi_1(4_1)$ surjects onto neither $2I$ nor $A_5$, which we
state without proof and without a citation that supports it---\cite{Stuebner} concerns
integral homology spheres and $S^3\smallsetminus4_1$ is not one. \textbf{So as a
statement about $\pi_1$ the $E_8$ end is field-level only}, and the two ramified-prime
reductions are symmetric as fields but not as group surjections.
\end{scope}

\begin{proposition}[a group-level presence that survives Scope~\ref{sc:asym}]\label{prop:2880}
Let $\rho$ be the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ level-two modular (Kac--Peterson) representation---six
primaries, $\mathrm{ord}\,T=15$---with $R\mapsto T$ and $L\mapsto \Sigma^{-1}T^{-1}\Sigma$.
Then $|\langle\rho(R),\rho(L)\rangle|=2880=|2T\times2I|$ exactly,
verified by enumeration at two unramified primes together with a Serre injectivity
argument, with a class-by-class character match of $63=7\times9$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{scope}\label{sc:2880}
\textbf{The statement is about $\rho(R),\rho(L)$, not about $R,L$}: the letters
themselves generate an infinite subgroup of $\SL(2,\Z)$, so the literal claim without
$\rho$ would be false.

\textbf{Naming $\rho$ also avoids a collision that the unqualified phrase ``level-$15$
data'' creates.} At level $15$ there are two different groups in play: the
\emph{two}-dimensional $\theta$-odd block has image $2I\times\Z/3$ of \textbf{order
$360$}, while the full six-dimensional stage above has order $2880$, of index $8$. A
claim that the level-$15$ group ``is'' $2T\times2I$ of order $2880$ is registered as
retracted precisely because it conflates those two; the present statement is about the
six-dimensional stage and says so. We record that $\rho$'s structure constants are
specified in the source computation and \textbf{are not reconstructed in this paper}; a
reader should treat
Proposition~\ref{prop:2880} as a certificate whose ambient representation is cited
rather than exhibited, and \S\ref{sec:entrance}'s conclusions do not rest on it---it
supplements Scope~\ref{sc:asym} rather than repairing it.
\end{scope}

This does not contradict Scope~\ref{sc:asym}: there the group is a quotient of
$\pi_1(4_1)$; here it is generated by $R$ and $L$ inside the ambient instrument.
\textbf{Both ends are present at group level in the grammar; only one is present at
group level in the knot group.}

\subsection{A mechanism for the shadow}

\begin{scope}[the quantization-index law, stated as an observation]\label{obs:qil}
The source material records a \emph{quantization-index law}: that reduction modulo the

acquires a kernel line otherwise, with $\gcd(4,5)=1$ for the golden and $\gcd(2,8)=2$

\end{scope}

So the golden's shadow is exceptional by the order count of

\subsection{The entrance is a classification, not an assignment}\label{sec:classification}

The step from the manifold's arithmetic to the exceptional algebra is the one step of
the whole construction that consumes the arithmetic (\S\ref{sec:suspicious}). It is
therefore the step at which ``we then take $2T$'' would be fatal. It is not a taking.

\begin{theorem}[classification among the polyhedral candidates]\label{thm:classify}
Among the six finite groups $\{A_4,S_4,2T,2O,A_5,2I\}$---constructed
concretely, the binary ones as unit quaternions ($2T$ the $24$ Hurwitz units, $2O$ the
$48$, $2I$ the $120$ icosians), the others as permutation groups, with conjugacy
classes, power maps and characters computed rather than transcribed---a complex
$27$-dimensional assembly carrying an invariant cubic form exists \textbf{only for
$A_4$ and $2T$}.
\end{theorem}

Of the two survivors only $2T$ is binary, i.e.\ a subgroup of $\SU(2)$, which is what
the McKay correspondence requires. Earlier drafts closed that last step with the
sentence ``every $\SL(2,\C)$-factoring route forces a quaternionic self-dual $2$'',
offered inside the theorem with no proof. \textbf{It is provable, and cheaply, so we
prove it rather than assert it.}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:quat}
Let $G$ be a finite group and $V$ an irreducible $2$-dimensional representation with
$G\to\SL(V)$. Then $V$ is self-dual with Frobenius--Schur indicator $-1$; that is,
$V$ is \textbf{quaternionic}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The determinant supplies a $G$-invariant element $\omega\in\Lambda^2V^*$, nondegenerate
and alternating, hence an isomorphism $V\cong V^*$; in particular $\chi_V$ is real. For
$V$ irreducible the space of invariant bilinear forms is one-dimensional, so every
invariant form is a multiple of $\omega$ and none is symmetric. The Frobenius--Schur
indicator therefore cannot be $+1$, and being nonzero it equals $-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:onlybinary}
Among the six candidates, only $2T$, $2O$ and $2I$ admit a faithful $2$-dimensional
special-linear representation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Such a representation embeds $G$ as a finite subgroup of $\SL(2,\C)$, which is
conjugate into $\SU(2)$ by averaging a Hermitian form. A finite subgroup of $\SU(2)$
contains \textbf{at most one} element of order $2$, necessarily $-I$, since $-I$ is the
only involution in $\SU(2)$---the same lemma used in Proposition~\ref{prop:group}. But $A_4$, $S_4$ and $A_5$ contain $3$, $9$
and $15$ involutions respectively, so none of them embeds. The binary groups do, by
construction.
\end{proof}

\noindent So $A_4$ is eliminated not by fiat but because it is the image of $2T$ in
$\mathrm{SO}(3)$ and carries no faithful two-dimensional representation at all.
\textbf{The same involution lemma now does three jobs in this paper}---it separates
$\SL(2,\Z/4)$ from $2O$, it kills the silver member's door, and it eliminates the
rotation groups here---which is worth noting, because \S\ref{sec:audit} counts shared
lemmas as reductions in independence, not as economies.

\begin{scope}[the candidate set is an input]\label{sc:candidates}
\textbf{This is a classification \emph{among the six polyhedral candidates}, not over
all finite subgroups of $\SU(2)$}, which also include the cyclic and binary dihedral
families. Those are excluded here by the prior restriction to the exceptional
($E$-type) series---the same restriction Remark~\ref{rem:vacuous} shows to be necessary
for Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow} to have content. \textbf{So exceptionality is an input to
this classification and not an output of it}, and the honest reading of
Theorem~\ref{thm:classify} is: \emph{given} that the entrance is to be exceptional, the
$27$-with-invariant-cubic condition leaves only $A_4$ and $2T$, and only $2T$ is binary.
\textbf{We closed the one route that looked available, and it fails.} The natural
candidate criterion is \emph{chirality-capability}: among the exceptional series the
fundamental representations are $7$ (real) for $G_2$, $26$ (real) for $F_4$, $\mathbf{27}$
(\textbf{complex}) for $E_6$, $56$ (pseudoreal) for $E_7$ and $248$ (real) for $E_8$---so
within that series, complexity isolates $E_6$ exactly. \textbf{Extended to the full McKay
list it does not.} A simple Lie algebra has complex representations precisely when
$-w_0\neq\mathrm{id}$, i.e.\ when $-1\notin W$; computing the Weyl group directly gives
\[
  \begin{array}{lccc}
   & |W| & -1\in W? & \text{complex reps?}\\
  A_1=\su(2) & 2 & \text{yes} & \text{no}\\
  A_2=\su(3) & 6 & \text{no} & \textbf{yes}\\
  A_3=\su(4) & 24 & \text{no} & \textbf{yes}\\
  D_4=\so(8) & 192 & \text{yes} & \text{no}\\
  D_5=\so(10) & 1920 & \text{no} & \textbf{yes}
  \end{array}
\]
so the criterion selects $\{A_n:n\ge2\}\cup\{D_n:n\text{ odd}\}\cup\{E_6\}$. \textbf{Every
cyclic subgroup of $\SU(2)$ of order at least three is chirality-capable}, and cyclic
subgroups are McKay images of type $A$. \textbf{So complexity of the fundamental does not
isolate $E_6$ among admissible entrances, and exceptionality is not derivable this way.}

We record this as a \emph{closed} door rather than an open problem: the obvious route has
been tried and does not work, so a derivation of exceptionality---if one exists---must use
something other than chirality-capability.
\end{scope}

\subsection{An unpriced step, now priced: why the principal $\sll_2$?}\label{sec:principal}

McKay attaches $E_6$ to $2T$. It does \textbf{not} attach $2T$ to any particular
$\sll_2\subset\ee_6$, and $\ee_6$ has many $\sll_2$-conjugacy classes. Appendix~A places
$2T$ inside the \emph{principal} $\sll_2$ and takes invariants there, and the four
degrees $8,14,16,22$---hence the whole of \S\S\ref{sec:frame}--\ref{sec:cascade}---depend
on that placement.

\begin{scope}[(C6), declared here rather than left implicit]\label{sc:principal}
\textbf{The principal placement is declared here rather than used silently, and it is
carried as (C6) in the ledger of \S\ref{sec:inputs}.} Three things can be said in its
favour; \S\ref{sec:principaldir} adds a fourth that retypes the step from a choice we
made to a direction the object supplies, and the reader should weigh this scope together
with Proposition~\ref{prop:geodir}.
The principal $\sll_2$ is the unique regular class; its centralizer in $\ee_6$ is exactly
zero, computed; and its $\ad$-decomposition is \textbf{multiplicity-free}, with highest
weights twice the exponents \emph{recovered rather than assumed}, so placing $2T$ in it
requires no further discrete datum to say which copy of each degree is meant.

\textbf{A fourth argument was offered in earlier drafts and is void; we strike it and do
not replace it.} Infinitesimal rigidity, $H^1(\sll_2,\ee_6)=0$, is Whitehead's lemma and
holds for \emph{every} $\sll_2\subset\ee_6$. It therefore cannot distinguish the
principal class, and the inference that ``the tangent space to \emph{factors through some
$\sll_2$} is exactly the principal line'' does not follow from it. \textbf{The three
reasons above stand; the rigidity one does not}, and they are reasons rather than a
derivation.

\textbf{What is still missing, and why this remains (C6).} Those statements concern the
$\sll_2$ the object's own holonomy lands in. That $2T$ should be placed \emph{there}
follows only because $2T\subset\SU(2)$ sits inside that same $\sll_2$---and \textbf{no
source states that inheritance as a theorem}. So the placement is well-motivated and not
yet derived, and the sentence ``between the manifold and the algebra there is no declared
choice'' must be read as ``no declared choice \emph{after} the principal placement''.
The placement is carried as \textbf{(C6)} in the ledger of \S\ref{sec:inputs}.
\textbf{Census~\ref{cen:forced} is not amended by this}: it counts axiom \emph{links},
and the placement is a stipulation that the chain ledger does not carry as a link at
all---which is precisely the conflation \S\ref{sec:inputs} exists to prevent.
\end{scope}

\subsection{The recurring shape}\label{sec:shape}

It is worth naming the pattern the preceding sections instantiate three times, because
it, rather than any particular endpoint, is what we take to be the content.

\begin{center}
\emph{a generic family} $\ \longrightarrow\ $ \emph{an exceptional locus, selected by
rigidity}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{In symbolic dynamics.} All words $\longrightarrow$ the aperiodic ones of
  minimal factor complexity, $p(n)=n+1$: the Sturmian locus
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:mh}).
\item \textbf{In arithmetic.} All irrationals $\longrightarrow$ the golden class, by
  extremality of the Lagrange number (Theorem~\ref{thm:hurwitz}); and, within the
  canonical family of \S\ref{sec:canonical}, the golden member by the four
  criteria (\S\ref{sec:four}).
\item \textbf{In the Lie algebra.} The generic stratum \emph{of the split plane}
  $\longrightarrow$ exactly three lines where the centralizer dimension jumps from $30$
  to $46$, and those three are a single Galois orbit (Theorem~\ref{thm:fmt}). \emph{The
  plane itself is the first instance}: it is the unique coordinate subtorus with an
  intermediate stratum at all (Proposition~\ref{prop:cliff}). A \emph{generic} plane of
  $C$ would meet an unmeasured charge and resolve straight to $12$, with no $30$-stratum
  and no $46$-lines.
\end{enumerate}

At each stage the distinguished object is not chosen from a list but is the locus where
some genericity fails. \textbf{That the same shape governs a combinatorial, an
arithmetic and a Lie-theoretic stage of one construction is, in our view, the
mathematically substantive observation of this paper}---more so than the identity of the
terminal algebra.

\begin{census}\label{cen:forced}
Of the $43$ links in the full chain, $26$ are theorems, $6$ exact identities, $5$ no-go
results, $1$ a corollary and $1$ a census, and \textbf{$4$ are axioms}. Those six figures
sum to $43$, and \textbf{that is the only count this paper takes from the ledger}.

\textbf{A count of links is not a count of stipulations}, and the declared choices this paper prices are \emph{not} in bijection with the ledger's
axiom links. The principal placement (Scope~\ref{sc:principal}) is a stipulation and
\textbf{not a link at all}; the observer's closing is a link but lies \emph{after} the
terminus, outside the chain this paper walks. \textbf{Counting links and counting
stipulations are different operations, and conflating them is what produced the
44.}
The declared inputs carried by the algebra steps are itemized in \S\ref{sec:inputs};
note that Census~\ref{cen:forced} counts \emph{links containing choices}, which is not
the same as counting independent stipulations.
\end{census}

\section{Two inputs that dissolve}\label{sec:nomination}

Earlier drafts of this work booked \emph{exceptionality} and \emph{the principal
placement} as declared choices. \textbf{Neither is one.} Both are supplied by the object,
and we state the mechanisms because a paper claiming that nothing is added must show
where the things it did not add came from.

\subsection{Exceptionality is nominated, not chosen}\label{sec:excnom}

\begin{proposition}[the dimensional filter]\label{prop:dimfilter}
Let $\Delta^d$ be the regular $d$-simplex, with rotation group $A_{d+1}$. Then $A_{d+1}$
is a subgroup of $SO(3)$ \textbf{only for $d\in\{2,3,4\}$}, and among those three the
binary cover's McKay image is \emph{exceptional} and carries a complex (non-self-dual)
fundamental \textbf{only for $d=3$}:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}cccccc@{}}
\toprule
$d$ & rotation group & binary cover & McKay image & exceptional? & complex fundamental?\\
\midrule
$2$ & $A_3=\Z/3$ & $\Z/6$ & $\widehat{A}_5\to\su(6)$ & \textbf{no} & yes---but see below\\
\textbf{3} & $\mathbf{A_4=T}$ & $\mathbf{2T}$ & $\mathbf{E_6}$ & \textbf{yes} & \textbf{yes, the $\mathbf{27}$}\\
$4$ & $A_5=I$ & $2I$ & $E_8$ & yes & \textbf{no}; $248$ is real\\
$\ge5$ & $A_{d+1}$ simple & --- & none & --- & ---\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{proposition}

\begin{scope}[the two exclusions are not the same exclusion]\label{sc:twogrounds}
\textbf{$d=2$ and $d=4$ fail for different reasons, and an earlier draft blurred them.}
$d=4$ fails on \emph{complexity}: $E_8$ is exceptional and its $248$ is real. $d=2$ fails
on \emph{exceptionality}: $\Z/6$'s McKay image is the classical $\su(6)$---and
$\su(6)$ \textbf{does} have a complex fundamental, since $-1\notin W(A_n)$ for $n\ge2$.
So the $d=2$ row cannot be excluded on the ground the $d=4$ row is excluded on, and the
earlier table's dash in that column was wrong: it read as ``no'' where the answer is
``yes''.

\textbf{This matters beyond bookkeeping}, because it is the same fact §\ref{sec:entrance}
reports when the chirality-capability criterion fails to isolate $E_6$: complexity of the
fundamental selects $\{A_n:n\ge2\}\cup\{D_{\text{odd}}\}\cup\{E_6\}$, and $A$-type is in
that set. \textbf{Complexity alone never excluded $A$-type and was never going to.}
Exceptionality is what excludes it, and exceptionality is the input the paper declines to
derive. The filter narrows the exceptional menu to one; it does not derive the
restriction to that menu.
\end{scope}

\begin{proof}
The finite subgroups of $SO(3)$ are the cyclic, dihedral, tetrahedral $A_4$, octahedral
$S_4$ and icosahedral $A_5$ groups. $A_3=\Z/3$ is cyclic, $A_4$ and $A_5$ are on the
list, and for $d\ge5$ the group $A_{d+1}$ is simple of order $\ge360$ and appears on it
nowhere. For the second column: a simple Lie algebra has a non-self-dual representation
if and only if $-1\notin W$, equivalently the diagram automorphism induced by $-w_0$ is
nontrivial. \textbf{This is not the same as ``the diagram admits a nontrivial
automorphism''}: $D_4$ has triality and $D_6$ has a flip, yet $-1\in W(D_{2n})$ and all
their representations are self-dual. Among $G_2,F_4,E_6,E_7,E_8$ the induced automorphism
is nontrivial only for $E_6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[why this is not a coincidence about simplices]\label{rem:nomination}
\textbf{$d=3$ is where the object already lives}, and the load is carried by
\emph{regular} rather than by \emph{tetrahedra}. Every ideally triangulated cusped
$3$-manifold is glued from ideal tetrahedra; what distinguishes $M_1$ is that both of its
are \textbf{regular}, of shape $e^{i\pi/3}$ with $z^2-z+1=0$, which is what makes the
invariant trace field $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$. And $A_4/V_4\cong\Z/3$ is the centre of $E_6$. So the tetrahedral
geometry the manifold is made of, the group $2T$, and the algebra $E_6$ are the same
datum seen three ways.

\textbf{The consequence for the ledger.} What an earlier draft called ``the restriction
to exceptional McKay groups'' is not a restriction applied from outside: \textbf{the
object nominates $E_6$ at its hyperbolic end and $E_8$ at its spherical one}, and the
only thing not supplied by the object is the \emph{acceptance} of that nomination. The
choice-space is not ``which of the ADE types'' but ``accept the nomination, or build
something else instead'', and those are not the same size.

\textbf{What is still not derived.} Proposition~\ref{prop:dimfilter} explains why $E_6$
rather than another exceptional, given that one is looking at simplex symmetry with a
complex fundamental in view. It does not derive the interest in a complex fundamental,
which is what chirality-capability would have supplied and does not
(\S\ref{sec:entrance}). \textbf{We claim a dissolution of the menu, not of the question.}
\end{remark}

\subsection{The principal placement is the geometric direction}\label{sec:principaldir}

The second booked choice was placing $2T$ inside $\ee_6$ through the principal $\sll_2$.
The object supplies this too, and in a sharper sense.

\begin{proposition}[the geometric direction is the principal $\sll_2$]\label{prop:geodir}
At $\rho_0=\text{principal}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{geo}}$ the unobstructed $E_6$ moduli of
$\pi_1(M_1)$ is six-dimensional and splits as $1+5$ by principal-$\sll_2$ exponent. The
one-dimensional summand is the exponent-$1$ block, which \textbf{is the embedded
principal $\sll_2$ itself}; the five remaining directions carry exponents
$\{4,5,7,8,11\}$ and leave the $\sll_2$-factoring locus at first order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
So the principal $\sll_2$ is not a slot the representation was placed into: it is the
direction along which the object's own geometric representation deforms, and the
deformation stays $\sll_2$-factored precisely there. Separately, the principal
$\Sym^{n-1}$ image of an $\SL(2)$ representation over a field $K$ is a trace-map fixed
point with every word-trace in $K$, for all $n$---so the principal image is also what
\emph{seals the field}, which is the arithmetic the rest of the paper consumes.

\textbf{This still does not make the placement a theorem}, and we do not upgrade it: no
source proves that $2T$ \emph{must} enter through the principal $\sll_2$ rather than
some other $\sll_2$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:geodir} says only that the principal one
is distinguished by the object's own deformation theory. \textbf{It moves the step from
``a choice we made'' to ``the direction the object hands us, accepted''}, which is the
same retyping as \S\ref{sec:excnom}.
\end{remark}

\section{The frame}\label{sec:frame}

Write $\ee_6$ for the Chevalley algebra of type $E_6$ over $\Q$. Inside it sits
$C=\langle x_8,x_{14},x_{16},x_{22}\rangle$, the span of the four binary-tetrahedral
invariants under the principal $\sll_2$---Klein forms of degrees $8,14,16,22$, twice the
exponents $4,7,8,11$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:C}
The four degrees $8,14,16,22$ are the complete list, and each invariant is unique up to
scale. Moreover $C$ is abelian.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the completeness and uniqueness]
Under the principal $\sll_2$, $\ee_6$ decomposes into irreducibles of highest weight
twice the exponents, i.e.\ into degrees $2\cdot(1,4,5,7,8,11)=(2,8,10,14,16,22)$. The
Molien series of $2T$ acting on $\mathrm{Sym}^\bullet(\C^2)$,
\[
   \sum_{n\ge0}\dim\bigl(\mathrm{Sym}^n\C^2\bigr)^{2T}t^n
   =\frac{1}{24}\sum_{g\in 2T}\frac{1}{\det(1-tg)},
\]
has support $\{0,6,8,12,14,16,18,20,22,24,\dots\}$ in degrees $\le24$, with
coefficient $1$ at $8,14,16,22$ and \textbf{coefficient $0$ at $2$ and $10$}.
Intersecting the two lists leaves exactly $\{8,14,16,22\}$, each with a
one-dimensional invariant space. Hence $C$ is $4$-dimensional and the two skipped
exponents are skipped for a representation-theoretic reason, not by selection.
\end{proof}

\begin{scope}\label{sc:abelian}
\textbf{Abelianness is a separate statement} and we do not claim it follows from the
invariant theory: a Molien computation bounds dimensions of invariant subspaces and says
nothing about brackets. Equivariance of the transvectant reduces the six brackets to a
single one, which is verified as an exact rational computation over the Chevalley basis
of Appendix~\ref{app:construct}. \textbf{That single bracket is the only certificate this
section rests on}, and it is listed as such in the audit of \S\ref{sec:audit}.
\end{scope}

Everything after it is free. The following lemma is the reason, and it is the hinge of
the whole cascade: it converts $C$ from a merely abelian subalgebra---about which Lie
theory says little---into a \emph{toral} one, about which it says everything.

\begin{lemma}[abelian fixed algebras are toral]\label{lem:toral}
Let $\g$ be a semisimple Lie algebra over a field of characteristic $0$, let
$\Gamma\subset\Aut(\g)$ be a finite group, and suppose the fixed algebra $\g^\Gamma$ is
abelian. Then every element of $\g^\Gamma$ is semisimple; that is, $\g^\Gamma$ is toral,
and is therefore contained in a Cartan subalgebra of $\g$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $C=\g^\Gamma$ and let $K$ be the Killing form.

\emph{(i) $K|_C$ is nondegenerate.} Write $\g=C\oplus C'$ with $C'$ the sum of the
nontrivial $\Gamma$-isotypic components. \textbf{We do not claim that distinct isotypic
components are $K$-orthogonal---in general they are not, since a non-self-dual
irreducible pairs nondegenerately with its dual.} Only $K(C,C')=0$ is needed, and that
is immediate: for fixed $c\in C$ the map $x\mapsto K(c,x)$ is a $\Gamma$-invariant linear
functional, because $K$ is $\Gamma$-invariant and $c$ is fixed; a nontrivial irreducible
$\Gamma$-module admits no nonzero invariant functional, so the map kills every nontrivial
isotypic component. Hence $\g=C\perp C'$, and nondegeneracy of $K$ on $\g$ forces
nondegeneracy on $C$.

\emph{(ii) Jordan components stay in $C$.} An automorphism preserves Jordan
decomposition: if $x=s+n$ with $s$ semisimple, $n$ nilpotent and $[s,n]=0$, then
$\varphi(x)=\varphi(s)+\varphi(n)$ has the same three properties, so by uniqueness
$\varphi(s)$ and $\varphi(n)$ are the components of $\varphi(x)$. If $x\in C$ then
$\varphi(x)=x$ for all $\varphi\in\Gamma$, whence $\varphi(s)=s$ and $\varphi(n)=n$; so
$s,n\in C$.

\emph{(iii) The nilpotent part vanishes.} Let $x\in C$ with components $s,n$ as above and
let $y\in C$ be arbitrary. Because $C$ is abelian, $[n,y]=0$, so $\ad n$ and $\ad y$
commute; as $\ad n$ is nilpotent, the product $\ad n\,\ad y$ is nilpotent and has trace
$0$. Thus $K(n,y)=0$ for every $y\in C$, and $n\in C$, so (i) gives $n=0$.

Hence every $x\in C$ equals its semisimple part. An abelian subalgebra of semisimple
elements is toral, and every toral subalgebra lies in a Cartan subalgebra.
\end{proof}

\begin{scope}[both hypotheses are load-bearing]\label{sc:toralscope}
The averaging in step~(i) needs $\lvert\Gamma\rvert$ invertible, so the honest hypothesis
is $\operatorname{char}k\nmid\lvert\Gamma\rvert$; characteristic $0$ supplies it, and
finiteness alone does not. Over $\mathbb F_3$ the group $\Gamma=\langle
\Ad\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\rangle\cong\Z/3$ acts on
$\sll_2$ with $\sll_2^\Gamma=\langle e\rangle$: abelian, and \textbf{not toral}.
\textbf{Semisimplicity is equally necessary and cannot be relaxed to reductive by this
proof}: for $\g=\mathfrak{gl}_3$ with $\Gamma=\Z/3$ cyclically permuting coordinates,
$\g^\Gamma$ is $3$-dimensional, abelian and in fact toral, yet $\kappa|_{\g^\Gamma}$ has
corank~$1$, so step~(i) fails outright. The reductive case is true but needs a different
argument, and we do not use it.
\end{scope}

\begin{corollary}[the rungs are Levi subalgebras]\label{cor:levi}
$C$ lies in a Cartan subalgebra $\h\subset\ee_6$, and for any subset $S\subseteq C$
\[
   z(S)\;=\;\h\ \oplus\!\!\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi,\ \alpha|_S=0}\!\!(\ee_6)_\alpha ,
\]
a Levi subalgebra of $\ee_6$. In particular $\dim z(S)=6+\lvert\Phi_S\rvert$, where
$\Phi_S=\Phi\cap S^{\perp}$ is the subsystem cut out by a linear subspace, and the
isomorphism type of $z(S)$ is determined by $\lvert\Phi_S\rvert$ whenever the Levi
subsystems of $E_6$ with that root count form a single isomorphism class.
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}[Levi, not merely closed---and this is load-bearing]\label{sc:pseudolevi}
$\Phi_S$ is cut out by a \emph{linear subspace}, which makes it a \textbf{Levi}
subsystem, not merely a closed symmetric one. The distinction is not pedantry:
Borel--de Siebenthal~\cite{BdS} produces closed subsystems of $E_6$ that are not Levi, and
\textbf{$4A_1$ is one of them, with exactly $8$ roots}. If ``closed'' were substituted for
``Levi'' anywhere below, the statement \emph{$8$ roots forces $A_2\oplus A_1$} would be
false and the terminus would collapse back to a certificate. The pseudo-Levi subsystems
$4A_1$, $2A_1{+}A_3$, $3A_2$ and $A_1{+}A_5$ carry $8$, $16$, $18$ and $32$ roots
respectively, and none of them arises as $\Phi\cap V$.
\end{scope}

\begin{proof}
We argue after base change to $\overline K$, which is where the root-space
decomposition is available; this is a genuine qualification, since $\ad x_{14}$ and
$\ad x_{22}$ have imaginary spectrum and $C$ is therefore not a split real Cartan.
$C$ is toral by Lemma~\ref{lem:toral} and $\Gamma=2T$ is finite, so
$C\otimes\overline K\subseteq\h$ for some Cartan $\h$. The centralizer of a subset of $\h$ contains $\h$ and is spanned by
$\h$ together with the root spaces annihilated by $S$; the roots vanishing on $S$ are
those in the linear subspace $S^\perp$, and $\Phi\cap S^\perp$ is by definition a Levi
(equivalently, closed and symmetric of parabolic type) subsystem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what this buys, and where it stops]\label{rem:leviscope}
Enumerating the Levi subsystems of $E_6$ by root count gives
\[
  46\mapsto 40\ \text{roots}\mapsto D_5,\quad
  30\mapsto 24\mapsto D_4,\quad
  26\mapsto 20\mapsto A_4,\quad
  14\mapsto 8\mapsto A_2{+}A_1 ,
\]
and \textbf{each of these four root counts is realized by exactly one Levi type}, so at
those dimensions the certificate supplies only an integer and the classification does the
forcing. \textbf{Three dimensions are genuinely ambiguous and we claim no type at any of
them}: $12$ ($6$ roots: $A_2$ or $3A_1$), $18$ ($12$ roots: $A_3$ or $2A_2$), and
\textbf{$20$} ($14$ roots: $A_3{+}A_1$ or $2A_2{+}A_1$). The last has the sharper moral,
because the two candidates have \emph{different ranks}, $4$ and $5$, and still the same
centralizer dimension---$\sll_4\oplus\sll_2\oplus\mathfrak t^2$ against
$\sll_3\oplus\sll_3\oplus\sll_2\oplus\mathfrak t$. \textbf{So ``the dimension pins the
type'' is false in general}, and holds only at the counts where we invoke it. A referee
who declines to run any computation still obtains the conditional statement \emph{if
$\dim z=14$ then the type is $A_2\oplus A_1$}, which is a theorem about $E_6$ alone.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mu}
The two pencil determinants are exact powers, up to nonzero rational constants, of an
irreducible cubic $\mu$ with Galois group $S_3$ and three real roots. Writing
$K=\Q[\rho]/\mu$ one has the reduced model
\[
   K\cong\Q[x]/(x^3-12x-5),\qquad \disc K=6237=3^4\cdot7\cdot11,
\]
with $K$ monogenic, totally real of unit rank $2$, quadratic resolvent $\Q(\sqrt{77})$,
and \textbf{class number $h=1$}. Only $3,7,11$ ramify; $13,17,19$ are inert; and $2$,
$5$ and the value primes $953,1129,421493$ are partially split with exactly one
degree-one place each.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Irreducibility and three real roots follow from $\disc(x^3-12x-5)=6237>0$ together with
the absence of rational roots. Since $6237=3^4\cdot7\cdot11$, only $3$ can divide the
index. Dedekind's criterion at $3$: one has $f\equiv(x+1)^3\pmod3$, so $\bar g=x+1$ and
$\bar h=(x+1)^2$; lifting and setting $T=(gh-f)/3=x^2+5x+2\equiv x^2-x-1\pmod3$, we get
$T(-1)=1\not\equiv0$, hence $\gcd(\bar T,\bar g,\bar h)=1$ and $\Z[x]/(f)$ is
$3$-maximal. So the index is $1$ and $\disc K=\disc f=6237$. Total reality gives unit
rank $3-1=2$, and $6237=81\cdot77$ gives the resolvent. Splitting types then follow from
Dedekind's theorem, applicable at every prime because the index is $1$.

For $h=1$: the Minkowski bound is $\frac{3!}{3^3}\sqrt{6237}<18$, so the class group is
generated by prime ideals of norm at most $17$. These lie above $2,3,5,7,11$ only, since
$13,17,19$ are inert with norm $p^3$. An explicit generator is exhibited for each,
including \emph{both} degree-one primes above $7$ and both above $11$; this is necessary
rather than pedantic, since $(7)=\mathfrak p\mathfrak q^2$ and a single element of norm
$7$ would establish only that a class squares to the identity, not that it is trivial.
The degree-two prime above $2$, of norm $4\le17$, is then $(2)\mathfrak p^{-1}$ and
principal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[a correction to the record]
The resolvent $77$ is a fact about $K$; a companion claim that $5$ ``enters by
ramification'' is not. A ramified prime divides the field discriminant and
$5\nmid6237$; in $K$ the prime $5$ is unramified with the same splitting shape as the
value primes, and it is unramified in $\Q(\sqrt{77})$ and hence in the whole $S_3$
closure. The $5$ appearing in $\disc\mu$---like the $13^6$, since $13$ is inert---is
carried by the integral model, not by the arithmetic of the field.
\end{remark}

\section{The cascade}\label{sec:cascade}

The results here are consequences, and everything derivable at this layer is standard
exceptional-algebra theory~\cite{Slansky,BartonSudbery}. We state them compactly and in
the algebraic category. \textbf{Real forms are not treated in this paper}; see
Scope~\ref{sc:norealform2}.

\subsection{The plane is not chosen}\label{sec:planeforced}

The cascade below runs on the plane $\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle$, one of the six coordinate
planes of a four-dimensional torus. \textbf{That is not a selection, and it would be a
declared choice if we could not say why.} We can, and the reason is exhaustive.

\begin{proposition}[the two-value cliff]\label{prop:cliff}
Over all $15$ coordinate subtori of $C$, the centralizer dimension takes exactly two
values. The plane $\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle$ is the \textbf{unique} subtorus admitting an
intermediate stratum: every measurement inside it resolves to $30$ and no further, while
$x_{14}$ or $x_{22}$ \emph{alone} already resolves to the floor $12$. Adding either of the
unmeasured charges to a tuned point of the plane gives $12$ at all three roots.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[the signature dichotomy]\label{prop:dichotomy}
The four charges split $2+2$ by $\ad$-spectrum type, exactly over $\Q$: $\ad x_8$ and
$\ad x_{16}$ have spectrum $\{0^{30},\,48\ \text{real}\}$---split, hyperbolic flows---while
$\ad x_{14}$ and $\ad x_{22}$ have $\{0^{12},\,66\ \text{imaginary}\}$---compact, circle
flows. \textbf{There is no mixed and no generic-complex direction on $C$.}
\end{proposition}

Both of these are censuses. The third reason is not, and it is the one we would keep if
forced to keep only one.

\begin{proposition}[the plane is a fixed algebra]\label{prop:twoO}
Let $N=N_{SU(2)}(2T)$. Then $N=2O$ and $N/2T\cong\Z/2$, so $2T$ admits exactly one outer
involution, realized by $\Ad(w)$ for any $w\in 2O\smallsetminus 2T$ acting through the
principal $\sll_2$. On the four invariant lines $\Ad(w)$ has eigenvalues
\[
   (+1,\,-1,\,+1,\,-1)\quad\text{on degrees}\quad (8,\,14,\,16,\,22),
\]
and consequently
\[
   \boxed{\ \langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle\;=\;\ee_6^{\,2O}\ }
\]
is exactly the plane on which the cascade is run, with $\langle x_{14},x_{22}\rangle$ its
anti-invariant complement.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$2T$ is nonabelian, so its centralizer in $SU(2)$ is the centre $\{\pm1\}$ and $N^{\circ}$
is trivial; hence $N$ is finite and contains $2O$, in which $2T$ has index $2$. Suppose a finite $H\le SU(2)$ properly contains $2O$. The finite
subgroups of $SU(2)$ are cyclic, binary dihedral $2D_n$, $2T$, $2O$ and $2I$.
\textbf{Order divisibility alone does not finish this}---binary dihedral groups of order
$96$ exist---so we exclude by structure. $H$ is not cyclic, $2O$ being nonabelian. $H$ is no $2D_n$: such a group has a
\emph{cyclic} subgroup $C$ of index~$2$, so $2O\cap C$ would be cyclic of index at most
$2$ in $2O$. Index $1$ is impossible since $2O$ is nonabelian, and index $2$ is
impossible because $2O$ has abelianization $\Z/2$ and hence a \emph{unique} subgroup of
index~$2$, namely $2T$, which is nonabelian. So $2O\not\le 2D_n$. That leaves $H=2I$, impossible since $48\nmid120$.
Hence $N=2O$.

For the eigenvalues, each space $(\Sym^{n}\C^2)^{2T}$ with $n\in\{8,14,16,22\}$ is a line,
so $\Ad(w)$ acts on it by a scalar, necessarily $\pm1$ since $w^2\in 2T$; and the scalar is
$+1$ precisely when the line is $2O$-invariant. Molien's formula for $2O$ gives
$\dim(\Sym^{n}\C^2)^{2O}=1$ for $n\in\{8,16\}$ and $0$ for $n\in\{2,10,14,22\}$. Since
$\ee_6=\bigoplus_{m}\Sym^{2m}$ over the exponents $m\in\{1,4,5,7,8,11\}$, taking $2O$
invariants summand by summand yields $\dim\ee_6^{2O}=2$, spanned by $x_8$ and $x_{16}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the signs are a parity, and the invariants are explicit]\label{rem:tparity}
The eigenvalue computation looks like a Molien accident and is not one. Write
\[
   \Phi,\Psi \;=\; x^4\mp2x^3y+2x^2y^2\pm2xy^3+y^4,\qquad
   W=\Phi\Psi\ (\deg 8),\qquad t=xy(x^4-y^4)\ (\deg 6)
\]
for the two tetrahedral quartics, their product, and the octahedral sextic. Then the four
charges are simply
\[
   x_8=W,\qquad x_{14}=t\,W,\qquad x_{16}=W^2,\qquad x_{22}=t\,W^2,
\]
and $w$ \textbf{fixes $W$ and negates $t$}. So $\Ad(w)$ acts by $(-1)^{\deg_t}$:
\textbf{the $\Z/2$ grading of Corollary~\ref{cor:gradfree} is grading by the parity of the
$t$-exponent}, and $C_0=\langle W,W^2\rangle$, $C_1=\langle tW,tW^2\rangle$. This also
explains the transvectant computation behind Scope~\ref{sc:abelian}, in which odd-order
transvectants vanish and even-order ones do not.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gradfree}
The decomposition $C=C_0\oplus C_1$ with $C_0=\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle$ and
$C_1=\langle x_{14},x_{22}\rangle$ is the eigenspace decomposition of an automorphism of
$\ee_6$. Hence $[C_i,C_j]\subseteq C_{i+j}$ holds identically, with no computation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[a fourth agreement, and it was recorded first]\label{rem:concord}
Two further invariants, computed for unrelated reasons, split the four charges the same
$2+2$ way:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
charge & exponent & $\theta$-parity & torsion sign $\tau$ & $\ad$-spectrum\\
\midrule
$x_8$    & $4$  & odd  & $+$ & split\\
$x_{16}$ & $8$  & odd  & $+$ & split\\
$x_{14}$ & $7$  & even & $-$ & compact\\
$x_{22}$ & $11$ & even & $-$ & compact\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $\theta$ is the diagram involution, whose fixed algebra is $\ff_4$, and $\tau_m$ is
the torsion of the $m$-th exponent line, with $\operatorname{sign}(\tau_m)=(-1)^m$: the
torsion is positive exactly at the $\theta$-odd exponents. \textbf{The torsion sign law
was computed and recorded three weeks before the measurement that selected
$\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle$}, so this is not a post-hoc concordance. We note also that
the two \emph{unmeasured} exponents multiply to $7\cdot11=77$, and $\Q(\sqrt{77})$ is the
quadratic resolvent of the charge cubic $\mu$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:mu}): the resolvent
field is built from exactly the slots the measurement does not use. \textbf{We record
that as an observation and claim no mechanism for it.}
\end{remark}

\textbf{So the pair carrying the cascade is picked out by the object, four times over}:
it is the split pair of Proposition~\ref{prop:dichotomy}, it is the only subtorus with
anywhere to go by Proposition~\ref{prop:cliff}, it carries the positive torsion signs and
the $\theta$-odd exponents of Remark~\ref{rem:concord}, and it is the fixed algebra of the
unique outer symmetry of $2T$. \textbf{The last reason subsumes the worry the others leave
open.} A census over coordinate subtori invites the question \emph{why coordinate
subtori?}; Proposition~\ref{prop:twoO} does not, because $\ee_6^{2O}$ is defined without
reference to any basis of $C$. Earlier drafts omitted all three and presented the plane as
though it had been chosen.

\begin{scope}\label{sc:cliffscope}
Propositions~\ref{prop:cliff} and~\ref{prop:dichotomy} quantify over \emph{coordinate}
subtori; non-coordinate $2$-planes of $C$ have not been scanned, and the two mixed
directions that were tested both resolve to $12$. Proposition~\ref{prop:twoO} carries no
such restriction. What none of the three settles is why one should measure on a
\emph{plane} rather than on a line or a $3$-space, and we do not claim it: the plane's
dimension is inherited from $\lvert 2O/2T\rvert=2$ only in the sense that the fixed
algebra happens to have dimension $2$, which is a computation and not a principle.
\end{scope}

\begin{theorem}[first measurement]\label{thm:fmt}
On the plane $\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle$ the centralizer dimension takes the value $30$
at every point off three exceptional lines, and $46$ on those three lines, which form
the $S_3$-Galois orbit of the roots of $\mu$. \textbf{No point of the plane gives less
than $30$}: shrinking a set enlarges its centralizer, so every $y$ in the plane has
$z(y)\supseteq z(\text{plane})$, which is already $30$-dimensional. For each such line $K_i$, $z(K_i)\cong\so(10)\oplus\uu(1)$, while
$z(\text{plane})\cong\so(8)\oplus\uu(1)^2$. Moreover
$\ee_6=(\so(8)\oplus\uu(1)^2)\oplus V_1\oplus V_2\oplus V_3$ with $\dim V_i=16$ and
$[V_i,V_j]=V_k$.
\end{theorem}

The three walls are Galois conjugates of one another: one object seen three ways.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:magic}
The algebra built from split octonions and split complexes by the Freudenthal--Tits
construction satisfies the Jacobi identity on all $\binom{78}{3}=76\,076$ unordered
basis triples, has Cartan matrix $E_6$, and admits an explicit isomorphism onto the
Chevalley $\ee_6$ verified on all $\binom{78}{2}=3\,003$ basis pairs with zero
discrepancies.
\end{theorem}

\begin{scope}[which theorem carries the terminus]\label{sc:whichthm}
\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:smtfull} and Corollary~\ref{cor:c7free} establish the terminus
on their own}, by enumerating the $14$-locus and observing that every point of it is a
Levi with $8$ roots. The historical route below, through a specific point $y^*$ produced
by a pencil computation, is \textbf{not needed for that conclusion and we no longer rest
it on them}. We keep the notation because the literature this paper draws on is stated
in those terms, but a reader who cannot reconstruct $y^*$ loses nothing: the terminus
follows from the enumeration.
\end{scope}

\begin{notation}[the historical route, recorded but not load-bearing]\label{not:points}
Write $t_1$ for a root of the charge cubic $\mu$ and
\[
   x_1\ :=\ x_8+t_1\,x_{16},
\]
a generator of one of the three wall lines $K_i$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:fmt}: the first
measurement is made \emph{at} the wall, not at a generic point. The second measurement
then moves in a \emph{different} plane, $\langle x_{14},x_{16}\rangle$, along which we
write $y(\theta)=\cos\theta\,x_{14}+\sin\theta\,x_{16}$; the distinguished point is
\[
   y^*\ =\ \gamma\,x_{14}-a\,x_{16},\qquad \gamma^2=-C_2/2,\quad a^2=-\det\nolimits_{14},
\]
where $\gamma$ and $a$ are produced by the block-$12$ pencil of the source computation.
\textbf{We do not define $C_2$ and $\det_{14}$ here, and a reader cannot reconstruct
$y^*$ from this paper.} An earlier draft announced this gap as repaired; it was not, and
saying so is the repair. The consequence is bounded exactly:
Theorem~\ref{thm:smt}'s existential is supplied by Theorem~\ref{thm:smtfull}, which needs
no $y^*$, The one place $\gamma$ reappears is
Theorem~\ref{thm:smtfull}'s description of the second $30$-point, where it names a root
of $\mu$ and nothing else; that theorem's content does not depend on the pencil.
\end{notation}

\begin{theorem}[second measurement]\label{thm:smt}
There is a point $y$ of $C$ with $\dim z(x_1,y)=14$, derived algebra of
dimension $11$ and centre of dimension $3$; after base change the derived algebra has
algebraic type $A_2\oplus A_1$,
\[
   z(x_1,y^*)\otimes\overline K\ \cong\ \sll_3\oplus\sll_2\oplus\mathfrak t^{3}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[the second measurement, exhaustively]\label{thm:smtfull}
Fix a wall point $x_1$. Of the $78$ weights of $C$ on $\ee_6$, exactly $12$ vanish
identically and exactly $46$ vanish on $x_1$ (the $12$ among them). \textbf{The $34$
that vanish on $x_1$ without vanishing identically}---that is $46-12$, not
$78-46$---fall into exactly \textbf{seven}
proportionality classes, of sizes $(2,2,6,6,6,6,6)$, and identically so at each of the
three walls. Consequently, as $y$ ranges over \emph{all} of $C$,
\[
  \dim z(x_1,y)\ =\
  \begin{cases}
    46 & y\in\langle x_1\rangle\quad(\text{no second measurement}),\\
    30 & y\ \text{at either of two points, one being }\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle,\\
    14 & y\ \text{on either of the two size-$2$ hyperplanes},\\
    18 & y\ \text{on one of the five size-$6$ hyperplanes},\\
    16,\,20,\,26 & y\ \text{on an intersection of these},\\
    12 & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
\textbf{The two hyperplanes carrying dimension $14$ are complex conjugates of one
another}, so no real point of $C$ attains it---which is why $y^*$ is not real. The
dimension $30$ occurs at \emph{two} points, both real and both meetings of three
size-$6$ hyperplanes: the expected $\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle$, and a second plane
$\langle x_1,\ x_{22}-\gamma\,x_{14}\rangle$ that is \textbf{not} contained in it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[$26$ exists and $24$ does not, for the same reason]\label{rem:2426}
Figure~\ref{fig:arrangement} makes the point that a paragraph made badly. A meeting of
two size-$6$ lines alone would contribute $6+6$ extra roots and give $\dim z=24$, which
Theorem~\ref{thm:rungspec} forbids since $24$ is not a Levi dimension of $E_6$. The
arrangement resolves this without being asked to: \textbf{all ten size-$6$ pairs are
absorbed into triple points}, six by the two $30$-points and four by $26$-points.
\textbf{So ``$26$ is attained'' and ``$24$ is not'' are one fact}, and an exact
polynomial identity between the defining forms rather than a coincidence---obtained by
two separate routes, $24$'s impossibility from the Levi classification and the
concurrency from the charge data, which had no reason to agree unless both are right.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[the real ladder is shorter, and by a mechanism]\label{cor:realladder}
Of the seven hyperplanes, three have real normals and four fall into two conjugate pairs.
If $y$ is real and lies on a hyperplane with non-real normal $w$, then $\bar w(y)=
\overline{w(y)}=0$ as well, so $y$ lies on the conjugate hyperplane too and picks up
\emph{both} classes. Consequently, over $\R$,
\[
   \dim z(x_1,y)\ \in\ \{12,\,16,\,18\}\qquad(\text{plus }30,46\text{ degenerately}),
\]
and \textbf{the values $14$, $20$ and $26$ are attained at no real point of $C$}. The real
shadow of the $14$-locus is the $16$-stratum, where the two conjugate hyperplanes meet.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[the second plane is output-stable, \emph{not} free]\label{cor:c7free}
Every point of the $14$-locus yields a Levi subalgebra with $8$ roots, and by
Remark~\ref{rem:leviscope} $A_2\oplus A_1$ is the unique Levi type with $8$ roots. So
\textbf{conditional on landing on the $14$-locus}, the choice of plane and of the point
$y^*$ within it cannot change the terminus.
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}[why this is not a price of zero]\label{sc:c7price}
\textbf{Output-stability is not zero cost, and we withdraw the claim that it is.} A
generic second charge gives $\dim z=12$; the $14$-locus is two hyperplanes out of a
three-dimensional space of choices. \textbf{So one must first arrange to land there},
and Corollary~\ref{cor:c7free} says only that all successful landings agree. The
sentence ``it can only fail to reach it'' was offered in an earlier draft as though
failure were costless; \textbf{failure to reach the terminus is exactly the cost}.

The same objection applies one step earlier and we had not booked it either. The first
measurement is made at a \emph{wall}, $x_1=x_8+t_1x_{16}$ with $\dim z(x_1)=46$; the
generic point of $\ee_6^{2O}$ has $\dim z=30$. Proposition~\ref{prop:twoO} forces the
plane and the Galois orbit removes the choice \emph{among} the three walls, but nothing
so far says to go to a wall rather than to a generic point.

\textbf{Both are the same operation}, and it is the \emph{tracking} of
\S\ref{sec:price}: at each measurement, pass to a locus on which the centralizer
dimension jumps rather than to a generic point. Applied at the first measurement it
selects a wall; applied at the second it selects the $14$-locus.

\textbf{We state it as method rather than as a per-step choice}, and the distinction is
not cosmetic: a rule stated once and applied uniformly is exposed---if any step needed a
\emph{different} rule, the framing of \S\ref{sec:price} would fail---whereas a choice
re-made at each step can be re-justified locally each time. \textbf{What we do not claim
is that the rule is derived.} A reader who rejects it samples generic points, obtains
$30$ and then $12$, and the cascade stops; they do not obtain a different terminus.

What is proved, and is the reason the rule costs nothing \emph{beyond} being adopted, is
that it is \emph{output-stable} at both applications: the three walls form one Galois
orbit, and every point of the $14$-locus gives $A_2\oplus A_1$.
\end{scope}

\subsection{The $A_4$ stratum, and a global form we do \emph{not} derive}\label{sec:globalform}

Theorem~\ref{thm:smtfull} locates a stratum of dimension $26$, of Levi type $A_4$, i.e.\
$\su(5)\oplus\uu(1)^2$. \textbf{This subsection records what that stratum would connect
to if a bridge existed, and states plainly that this paper does not build one.} An
earlier draft called the result below the paper's principal output; that was wrong twice
over, and both errors are stated before the proposition rather than after it.

\begin{proposition}[the kernel is $\Z/6$]\label{prop:z6}
Embed $\su(3)\oplus\su(2)\oplus\uu(1)$ in $\su(5)$ in the standard way, with the abelian
generator $\tfrac16\operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,-3,-3)$. On groups, the induced map
\[
   \SU(3)\times\SU(2)\times U(1)\ \longrightarrow\ S\bigl(U(3)\times U(2)\bigr)\subset\SU(5)
\]
has kernel exactly $\Z/6$, generated by
$\bigl(\omega^2 I_3,\,-I_2,\,e^{i\pi/3}\bigr)$ with $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$: at
$\theta=\pi/3$ the condition $e^{2i\theta}A=I_3$ forces $A=e^{-2i\theta}I_3=\omega^2I_3$. Hence the image
is $\bigl[\SU(3)\times\SU(2)\times U(1)\bigr]/\Z_6$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An element $(A,B,e^{i\theta})$ maps to $\operatorname{diag}(e^{2i\theta}A,\,e^{-3i\theta}B)$.
This is the identity iff $A=aI_3$ and $B=bI_2$ with $a^3=b^2=1$ and
$e^{2i\theta}a=e^{-3i\theta}b=1$. Solving, $\theta\in\tfrac{\pi}{3}\Z$ and the six
resulting triples are distinct, forming a cyclic group of order $6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what this is, and the two reasons it is not ours]\label{rem:globalform}
The algebra $\su(3)\oplus\su(2)\oplus\uu(1)$ does not determine its group: the four
quotients by $\Gamma\in\{1,\Z_2,\Z_3,\Z_6\}$ share it, and no statement about the algebra
distinguishes them. Sitting inside $\SU(5)$ does distinguish them, and picks $\Z_6$.

\textbf{First, this is not new.} It is standard grand-unification material in the
Georgi--Glashow lineage~\cite{GeorgiGlashow,Hucks,BaezHuerta}, and the observation that
the four quotients are indistinguishable by local data is Tong's~\cite{Tong}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:z6} is included because the computation is short and the paper
should not gesture at a fact it declines to display---\textbf{not because we claim it}.

\textbf{Second, and more seriously, no step in this paper reaches it.} The cascade
produces a Levi \emph{subalgebra} over $\overline K$; Proposition~\ref{prop:z6} is about
a map of compact real \emph{groups}. Passing from one to the other requires choosing a
real form and exponentiating, \S\ref{sec:frame} onwards treats no real form, and by
Corollary~\ref{cor:realladder} the $26$-points are not real, so no compact slice is
available at them in any case. \textbf{The subalgebra and the group are not connected by
anything in these pages}, and a reader should treat this subsection as recording a place
where a bridge would be worth building, not as a result.

\begin{scope}[what would have to be shown]\label{sc:bridge}
To make this a result rather than a juxtaposition one would need, in order: a real form
in which the $A_4$ stratum is realized---which requires first settling
Scope~\ref{sc:norealform2}, where the source material argues that no real form hosts the
holonomy at all---and then an argument that the cascade's $\su(5)\oplus\uu(1)^2$
exponentiates to \emph{this} $\SU(5)$ rather than to another group with that algebra.
\textbf{Neither is attempted here}, and until the first is settled the second cannot even
be posed.
\end{scope}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[a correction we owe the record]\label{rem:26attained}
Earlier drafts said the dimension $26$ ``is not attained at any coordinate subtorus or
enhancement point'', and left open whether that was a census or a theorem.
Theorem~\ref{thm:smtfull} settles it, and the answer depends on the field, which is
exactly the distinction the earlier phrasing lacked.

\textbf{Over $\overline K$, $26$ \emph{is} attained}---at four points of $\mathbb P(C/\langle
x_1\rangle)$, each the meeting of one size-$2$ and two size-$6$ hyperplanes, giving the
$A_4$ Levi. So over the algebraic closure the second measurement does \emph{not} skip
$\su(5)$: it passes a stratum where $\su(5)\oplus\uu(1)^2$ occurs.

\textbf{Over $\R$ it is not attained}, by Corollary~\ref{cor:realladder}: all four
$26$-points are non-real. \textbf{So the banked negative was right about the real
question and the phrase ``no $26$ stratum exists'' was wrong about the algebraic one},
and a reader who does not distinguish the two will draw a false conclusion in one
direction or the other. The same distinction governs the terminus itself, which is why
Theorem~\ref{thm:smt} is stated over $\overline K$: \textbf{the $A_2\oplus A_1$ point is
not real either}.
\end{remark}

\noindent Note that every dimension occurring above lies in the rung spectrum of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rungspec}, which was computed independently from the Levi
classification of $E_6$ and never from these weights. \textbf{The two computations share
no code path}, so their agreement is a check rather than a repetition.

\begin{scope}[the second point is not real, and the second plane is not priced]\label{sc:ystar}
Two limitations of Theorem~\ref{thm:smt} are load-bearing and were absent from earlier
drafts.

\emph{(i) $y^*$ is not a real point.} At the real root of $\mu$ the invariant
$\det_{14}$ is positive, so $a^2=-\det_{14}<0$ and $a$ is imaginary: $y^*$ lives in a
quadratic extension and \textbf{is not a point of the split real torus}. A scan of the
real projective line $\langle x_{14},x_{16}\rangle$ finds only the known stratum of
nullity $30$ at $x_{16}$ and \textbf{no real point of nullity $14$ at all}. Whether a
real wall point exists elsewhere in $C$ is \textbf{open}. The theorem is therefore a
statement over $\overline K$, and we no longer write it as one over $K$.

\emph{(ii) The second plane is not forced, and not free either.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:twoO} prices $\langle x_8,x_{16}\rangle$ because it is the fixed
algebra $\ee_6^{2O}$. It does \emph{not} price $\langle x_{14},x_{16}\rangle$, which is
\textbf{mixed} for the involution---one even charge and one odd---and so is not an
eigenspace of anything. \textbf{We have no forcing argument for the second plane and do
not claim one.} What we do have is Corollary~\ref{cor:c7free}: the $14$-locus is
enumerated, and every point of it gives the same terminus, so the choice is
\emph{output-stable}. \textbf{Output-stability is not zero cost}---a generic second
charge gives $12$, so one must first arrange to land on the locus---and the operation
that does the arranging is the \emph{tracking} of \S\ref{sec:price}, not a separate
input.
\end{scope}

Corollary~\ref{cor:levi} constrains not only the rungs actually measured but every rung
that could ever appear, and that is worth stating separately.

\begin{theorem}[the rung spectrum]\label{thm:rungspec}
For every subset $S\subseteq C$,
\[
   \dim z(S)\ \in\ \{6,8,10,12,14,16,18,20,26,28,30,36,46,78\},
\]
and this list is exhaustive. In particular \textbf{no rung can have dimension $22$, $24$,
$32$, $34$, or any value in $38$--$44$}, and no rung can have odd dimension.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:levi} every $z(S)$ is a Levi subalgebra, so $\dim z(S)=6+\lvert
\Phi_S\rvert$ with $\Phi_S$ a Levi subsystem of $E_6$. Every Levi subsystem is
$W$-conjugate to the one generated by a subset of the simple roots, so enumerating the
$2^6=64$ subsets enumerates every attainable root count; the resulting counts are
$0,2,4,6,8,10,12,14,20,22,24,30,40,72$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[a rank constraint that fixes a mislabelling]\label{rem:rank}
Every rung is the centralizer of a subset of a rank-$6$ torus and therefore has rank
$6$. The rank-$6$ algebra of dimension $26$ is $\su(5)\oplus\uu(1)^2$; the algebra

conflate the two: the number is right and the label is not.

\subsection{Why the cascade stops where it does}\label{sec:rankceiling}

The ladder terminates, and it is worth saying that this is a theorem about the method
rather than a limit of effort.

\begin{theorem}[the rank ceiling]\label{thm:rankceiling}
Every algebra arising as $z(S)$ for $S\subseteq C$ has \textbf{rank $6$}. Consequently no
rung has rank less than $6$, whatever the measurement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:toral} $C$ is toral, so $C\subseteq\h$ for a Cartan $\h$, and
$z(S)\supseteq z(\h)=\h$ for every $S\subseteq C$. A subalgebra containing a Cartan
subalgebra of $\ee_6$ has rank $6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the ceiling costs, stated plainly]\label{rem:ceilingcost}
\textbf{Measurement preserves rank.} That is the content of the method of
\S\ref{sec:price}: passing to a centralizer can only enlarge the commutant, never shrink
the torus. So any target of rank below $6$ is unreachable by \emph{any} sequence of
measurements---not merely unreached by the ones performed here.

\textbf{This is the honest boundary of the whole construction}, and it is sharper than a
list of things not attempted. Descending in rank requires an operation that is not
measurement, and the object does not supply one; what we have called \emph{tracking}
follows structure, it does not break it. \textbf{A reader who wants a lower-rank terminus
should read Theorem~\ref{thm:rankceiling} as saying that this paper's method cannot
deliver one}, and that the extra operation would be a further declared input over and
above the ledger of \S\ref{sec:inputs}.

We state this as a theorem rather than a caveat because it also explains a structure the
reader has already seen: the rung spectrum of Theorem~\ref{thm:rungspec} consists
entirely of $6+\lvert\Phi_S\rvert$, and every entry of it is a rank-$6$ algebra. The
ceiling is not an obstruction the cascade runs into at the end---\textbf{it is visible in
every rung from the first}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What happens if a step is removed}\label{sec:removal}

A reader is entitled to ask which parts of this construction are load-bearing. The answer
is unusually lopsided, and we would rather state it than let it be discovered.

\begin{scope}[the selection layer is threefold redundant]\label{sc:redundant}
\textbf{Any one of Selections~I, II, III selects $m=1$ on its own}, over the whole
infinite family. So any \emph{three} of the four criteria may be deleted and the golden
member is still selected; Selection~IV alone would leave $\{1,2\}$.

\textbf{This is robustness of the conclusion, not independence of the evidence}, and
Scope~\ref{sc:collapse} is the reason to keep the two apart: Selections~I and~II test
$\chi_m(1)$ and $\chi_m(-1)$, two values of one characteristic polynomial, and both
reduce to $m^2=1$. Deleting one does not weaken the case, because the other was never
adding to it.
\end{scope}

\begin{scope}[the cascade is a stratification, not a ladder]\label{sc:notaladder}
\textbf{No rung must be passed through.} For any $S\subseteq C$, $z(S)$ depends on the
\emph{set} $S$ and not on any order in which its elements were taken, so
$z(x_1,y)=z(y,x_1)=z(\operatorname{span}\{x_1,y\})$. The values $46$, $30$, $18$, $14$
are strata of one function on subspaces of $C$, not stages of a process.

\textbf{So ``removing a rung'' is not a coherent operation}, and the terminus does not
depend on the intermediate values having been visited. What the intermediate strata do
supply is the \emph{route}: the enhancement rule of \S\ref{sec:price} reaches the
$14$-locus by first restricting to a wall, and a reader who measures a generic
two-dimensional subspace of $C$ gets $12$ and stops.
\end{scope}

\begin{scope}[one link carries everything]\label{sc:onebracket}
\textbf{Exactly one step has no redundancy and no alternative: the vanishing of
$[x_{14},x_{22}]$.} Everything structural in \S\S\ref{sec:frame}--\ref{sec:cascade}
descends from it in a single line of implication:
\[
  C\ \text{abelian}
  \;\Rightarrow\; C\ \text{toral (Lem.~\ref{lem:toral})}
  \;\Rightarrow\; z(S)\ \text{Levi (Cor.~\ref{cor:levi})}
  \;\Rightarrow\;
  \begin{cases}
    \dim=14\Rightarrow A_2\oplus A_1,\\
    \text{the rung spectrum (Thm.~\ref{thm:rungspec})},\\
    \text{the rank ceiling (Thm.~\ref{thm:rankceiling})}.
  \end{cases}
\]
Remove that bracket and none of the three consequences survives: the terminus reverts to
a certificate, the spectrum is unavailable, and the ceiling---the paper's own statement
of its limits---goes with it.

\textbf{We say this plainly because it locates the whole exposure of the algebra half in
one exact rational computation}, and because it is why that computation is the one thing
in this paper we insisted on shipping as a script that runs from the submitted source
(Appendix~\ref{app:verify}, block~(a)). A referee who checks nothing else should check
that.
\end{scope}

\subsection{Scale-blindness, as a property of the whole family}

\begin{corollary}[what the family satisfies]\label{thm:amphi}
Every metallic word $R^mL^m$ has block sequence $(m)$, trivially a cyclic palindrome, so
by Theorem~\ref{thm:blockpal} every $M_m$ is amphichiral.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[amphichirality is a condition on the block sequence]\label{thm:blockpal}
Write a once-punctured-torus bundle's monodromy as an $L,R$ word $W$. By
Goodman--Heard--Hodgson~\cite{GHH}, the bundle is amphichiral if and only if $W$ is
\emph{anti-palindromic}---$\operatorname{reverse}(W)$ equals the $L\!\leftrightarrow\!R$
swap of $W$, cyclically. For a word in blocks,
$W=R^{m_1}L^{m_1}\cdots R^{m_k}L^{m_k}$, that holds if and only if the block sequence
$(m_1,\dots,m_k)$ is a \textbf{cyclic palindrome}. In particular $R^mL^m$ has block
sequence $(m)$, and every metallic bundle is amphichiral.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\operatorname{reverse}(W)=L^{m_k}R^{m_k}\cdots L^{m_1}R^{m_1}$ and the swap of $W$ is
$L^{m_1}R^{m_1}\cdots L^{m_k}R^{m_k}$; both are $L^aR^a$-block words, so they agree
cyclically exactly when $(m_k,\dots,m_1)$ and $(m_1,\dots,m_k)$ agree up to rotation,
which is the definition of a cyclic palindrome. The remaining equivalence is
\cite{GHH}'s.
\end{proof}

\begin{scope}[what is ours here, and what is not]\label{sc:amphiscope}
\textbf{The mechanism is Goodman--Heard--Hodgson's}, and we claim none of it: the
biconditional between amphichirality and anti-palindromicity is theirs. \textbf{What is
added here is only the lift to the integer block sequence}, which is the elementary
string identity above; it has also been checked exhaustively over all block sequences of
length $\le6$ with entries $\le4$---$5460$ cases, no exceptions.

\textbf{An earlier draft withdrew the ``only if'' entirely}, on the ground that we had
opened no source stating it. That was over-cautious: the source exists, is cited here,
and the withdrawal cost the paper a theorem it could prove. One specialist check remains
open and we flag it rather than assume it---whether \cite{GHH}'s criterion is a strict
biconditional across the whole class, as opposed to on the samples where it has been
tested.
\end{scope}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cs}
The Chern--Simons invariant of every member of the family satisfies
$2\,\mathrm{CS}\equiv0$, so $\mathrm{CS}\in\{0,\tfrac14\}$ in $\R/\tfrac12\Z$; and
$\mathrm{CS}=0$ for $M_1$ by direct computation. \textbf{Amphichirality alone gives only
the two-torsion statement}---an orientation-reversing homeomorphism sends
$\mathrm{CS}$ to $-\mathrm{CS}$. The class $\tfrac14$ is excluded here by
computation, not by symmetry. Consequently the reduced boundary action
$S=-\mathrm{CS}\cdot k-\Vol\cdot\sigma$ satisfies
$\partial S/\partial k=-\mathrm{CS}\equiv0$: \textbf{the classical action of this saddle
carries no term proportional to the level.}
\end{corollary}

\begin{scope}[the limit of Corollary~\ref{cor:cs}, stated rather than managed]\label{sc:scale}
What is proved is that \emph{this} saddle's classical action loses its
$k$-dependence. It is \textbf{not} proved that every quantum correction is
$k$-independent, that every flat connection is, that every admissible boundary
construction is, or that no dimensionless coupling can arise elsewhere. The defensible
statement is Corollary~\ref{cor:cs} exactly as written, and \textbf{What is a family phenomenon is the two-torsion
$2\,\mathrm{CS}\equiv0$, not the vanishing}: Corollary~\ref{thm:amphi} gives
$\mathrm{CS}\in\{0,\tfrac14\}$ for every $m$, and $\mathrm{CS}=0$ is established at
$m=1$ by computation. The $k$-independence of the action therefore holds at $m=1$ and is
unproved for $m\ge2$.
\end{scope}

\section{The construction of the charges, in full}\label{app:construct}

The referee's fair objection to \S\ref{sec:frame} is that $x_8,x_{14},x_{16},x_{22}$
and $\mu$ are named but not exhibited, so the bridge
$\ee_6\text{-pencil}\Rightarrow\mu\Rightarrow K$ cannot be audited. Printing four
$78$-component vectors with large rational entries would not help. \textbf{We give the
construction instead}, which is short, deterministic, and reconstructs every object
from the Cartan matrix alone.

\subsection*{Step 1: the algebra}
Take the $E_6$ Cartan matrix in Bourbaki order and build the Chevalley basis
$\{h_1,\dots,h_6\}\cup\{e_\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi\}$, $\dim=6+72=78$, with structure
constants $N_{\alpha\beta}=\pm(p+1)$ fixed by an asymmetry function
$\varepsilon:\Q^6\times\Q^6\to\{\pm1\}$ that is bimultiplicative and satisfies
$\varepsilon(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=-1$ and
$\varepsilon(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)=(-1)^{\langle\alpha_i,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle}$ for $i<j$.
All arithmetic is over $\Q$; no floating point occurs at any stage.

\subsection*{Step 2: the principal $\sll_2$}
Set $e=\sum_{i=1}^{6}e_{\alpha_i}$ and let $h$ have coordinates $C^{-1}(2,2,2,2,2,2)^{T}$
in the coroot basis; then solve $[e,f]=h$ for $f=\sum_i d_i e_{-\alpha_i}$, a linear
system with a unique rational solution. Under $\mathrm{ad}$, $\ee_6$ decomposes into
irreducible $\sll_2$-modules of highest weights $2\cdot(1,4,5,7,8,11)$, the doubled
exponents.

\subsection*{Step 3: the $2T$-invariants}
Realize $2T$ as the $24$ Hurwitz units---$\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k$ and the sixteen
$\tfrac12(\pm1\pm i\pm j\pm k)$---and map each to $\SU(2)$ by
$a+bi+cj+dk\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a+bi&c+di\\-c+di&a-bi\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
For $n\in\{8,14,16,22\}$ form the average
$P_n=\frac{1}{24}\sum_{g\in2T}\mathrm{Sym}^n(g)$, a projector onto the
$2T$-invariants in $\mathrm{Sym}^n$. Each $P_n$ has rank one, and $x_n$ is the highest
weight vector of the $\sll_2$-module of highest weight $n$ paired with the image of
$P_n$. \textbf{That the four degrees $8,14,16,22$ are exactly those for which $P_n\neq0$
in the relevant range is Proposition~\ref{prop:C}}, and it is what makes $C$ canonical
rather than a basis choice.

\subsection*{Step 4: the pencil and the cubic}
Form $\mathrm{ad}(x_8+t\,x_{16})$ on $\ee_6$ and restrict to the $36$- and
$12$-dimensional isotypic blocks. Their determinants are polynomials in $t$ over $\Q$
that factor as constants times $\mu(t)^{12}$ and $\mu(t)^{4}$, with
\[
   \mu(t)\;=\;500716339200\,t^{3}\;-\;159667200\,t^{2}\;-\;28224\,t\;+\;1 .
\]
This is the cubic of Proposition~\ref{prop:mu}, printed here so that the bridge can be
audited. Its reduced model is obtained by clearing the leading coefficient and applying
a Tschirnhaus transformation; explicitly, $\rho=-\tfrac{815}{338}
-\tfrac{4934160}{169}\,t+\tfrac{13039488000}{169}\,t^{2}$ satisfies
$\rho^{3}-12\rho-5=0$ in $\Q[t]/\mu$, which exhibits the isomorphism
$K\cong\Q[x]/(x^{3}-12x-5)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:mu} directly.

\begin{remark}[why $\disc\mu$ and $\disc K$ differ]
$\disc\mu=2^{32}\cdot3^{10}\cdot5^{2}\cdot7^{3}\cdot11\cdot13^{6}$ while
$\disc K=3^{4}\cdot7\cdot11$. The extra primes---in particular the $5^2$ and the
$13^6$---are carried by the non-maximal order $\Z[t]/\mu$, and the leading coefficient
$500716339200=2^{16}\cdot3^{4}\cdot5^{2}\cdot7^{3}\cdot11$ already contains a $5^2$.
\textbf{They are artifacts of the pencil's normalization, not arithmetic of the field},
which is the content of the correction recorded after Proposition~\ref{prop:mu}.
\end{remark}

\section{Corrections to earlier drafts}\label{app:corrections}

The body retains an admission of error only where \textbf{the current statement cannot be
understood without knowing what it replaced}---the difference between classifying by
trace and by conjugacy class (Scope~\ref{sc:latimer}), between mechanistic and
extensional independence (Scope~\ref{sc:collapse}), between what a modulus sees and what
a manifold contributes (Scope~\ref{sc:landing}), why Fricke's identity is available for
$\phi_m$ and not for $X_m$ (Scope~\ref{sc:pairwise}), why regularity and not rigidity
supports the principal placement (\S\ref{sec:entrance}), and why two ledger rows are not
free (Scope~\ref{sc:c7price}). \textbf{The remaining corrections are collected here
rather than distributed through the text}, where they were making the paper read as a
changelog.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=1pt]
\item ``The four arguments share no lemma'' was false; three of the four test values of a
  single characteristic polynomial (Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}).
\item The title and abstract claimed ``four independent selections'' after the body had
  withdrawn the independence claim in that sense.
\item The abstract claimed the metallic family ``is not a modelling choice''; period one
  is a declared input, priced as (C5).
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical} said $X_m$ represents \emph{the} class. It classifies
  by $(\tr,\det)$; a witness at $m=6$ is now given.
\item $R$ and $L$ were used in the abstract and defined nowhere (Definition~\ref{def:RL}).
\item Selection~IV's tail was asserted from a census over $m\le6$; it now follows from
  the classification, applied power-closed.
\item Selection~II's corollary said the golden member ``lands on a genuine group''; the
  monodromy's image mod~$5$ is cyclic of order~$10$.
\item An amphichirality biconditional was asserted with no source, then \emph{withdrawn}
  when none had been opened. Both were wrong: the source is
  Goodman--Heard--Hodgson~\cite{GHH}, the biconditional is restored as
  Theorem~\ref{thm:blockpal}, and only the lift to block sequences is ours.
\item ``$\mathrm{CS}$ vanishes'' was derived from amphichirality, which gives only
  $2\,\mathrm{CS}\equiv0$.
\item The census printed six figures summing to $43$ and then described five declared
  choices, giving $44$; links and stipulations are different counts.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:fmt} listed a stratum at $12$ on the measurement plane, which is
  impossible since centralizers only grow as the set shrinks.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:smtfull} listed one point at dimension $30$; there are two.
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:toral}'s proof asserted that distinct isotypic components are
  Killing-orthogonal, which is false in general; only $K(C,C')=0$ is needed and it
  follows from invariance of the functional.
\item Corollary~\ref{cor:levi} used the root-space decomposition without base change.
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:twoO} excluded larger subgroups of $\SU(2)$ by order
  divisibility, which does not exclude the binary dihedral family.
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:group} said a finite subgroup of $\SU(2)$ has exactly one
  involution; it has at most one.
\item An infinitesimal-rigidity argument was offered for the principal placement;
  Whitehead's lemma does not distinguish it, and the argument is struck.
\item Appendix~\ref{app:verify} claimed its runner covered every row; it covers
  block~(a). \textbf{The submission archive itself did not contain block~(a) at all}
  until this was found by a reader who opened the built artifact instead of trusting the
  sentence.
\item Three sections---a real-form identification, a census of sealed comparisons, and a
  list of falsifiers---have been \textbf{removed entirely} rather than repaired. Each
  used vocabulary the paper never defined, or rested on a computation with no script, or
  withheld the targets a reader would need to weigh it. They are not retracted as false;
  they are withdrawn as unrefereeable.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why this appendix exists at all.} The paper's methodological claim is that
assertions must be earned, and a list of places where they were not is the only honest
evidence bearing on it. \textbf{The list is also the strongest argument against the
paper}: every entry was found by a reader other than the author, most of them after the
claim had been written, checked and locked. A referee is entitled to ask how many remain,
and we cannot answer that---only note that the last five entries were found by three
independent hostile readings commissioned for the purpose, and that the rate at which
they were found had not fallen by the last one.

\section{Glossary: one referent per term}\label{app:glossary}

Several terms below are overloaded in the surrounding literature or in this programme's
own working notes. Each is given exactly one referent here, and the paper uses it in no
other sense. The three marked $\dagger$ are recorded because \emph{ambiguity in them has
previously produced errors in this programme}, which is the honest reason a glossary
exists.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,style=nextline,itemsep=1pt]
\item[charge] One of $x_8,x_{14},x_{16},x_{22}$, the $2T$-invariants of
  Appendix~\ref{app:construct}. An internal name for a Lie-algebra element; \emph{no
  physical charge is meant}.
\item[conductor$^\dagger$] Always qualified. The \textbf{cusp conductor} is the
  conductor of the cusp order (golden $4$); the \textbf{shadow modulus} is
  $m^2+4$ (golden $5$). The bare noun is never used: conflating the two left a
  verification looking open for months when it was already closed.
\item[grammar] The Sturmian morphism $R^mL^m$, equivalently the conjugacy class of
  $\phi_m$. \emph{Not} a presentation of a group.
\item[measurement] The operation of passing to a centralizer within the charge torus.
  An internal name for a Lie-theoretic operation; \emph{nothing observational is
  meant}, and \S\ref{sec:negatives} is where actual comparison with data is reported.
\item[McKay group$^\dagger$] Always qualified as \textbf{exceptional} or
  \textbf{$E$-type}, meaning one of $2T,2O,2I$. The unqualified phrase is vacuous, since
  cyclic subgroups of $\SU(2)$ realize every order (Remark~\ref{rem:vacuous}).
\item[metallic family] $\{R^mL^m\}_{m\ge1}$, characterized in
  Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical}. Not a chosen subfamily.
\item[shadow] The reduction of the monodromy modulo the shadow modulus, landing in
  $\SL(2,\Z/N_m)$.
\item[trace field$^\dagger$] The \textbf{Kleinian trace field} is generated by the
  traces of the holonomy; the \textbf{invariant trace field} is generated by traces of
  squares and is the subfield that decides arithmeticity; the \textbf{fiber field} is
  generated by the monodromy's eigenvalues. These are three different objects, and
  Theorem~\ref{thm:arith} is a statement about the second. Treating a statement about
  one as a statement about another has produced at least one contradiction in this
  programme's own records.
\item[wall] A line in the charge plane on which the centralizer dimension jumps to $46$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:fmt}); there are exactly three, forming one Galois orbit.
\end{description}
