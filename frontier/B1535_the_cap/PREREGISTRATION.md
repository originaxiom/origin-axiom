## 1. The question

Take sm:B1515's frame (F-HE) at a finite-order character ν of a finite cover N of a complete finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold,
at any class c. Is the 5̄′ count bounded by the interior classes of ν³ ⊗ ρ, and the 10̄′ count by b0 + n(ν⁴), in both stacking
orders?

If so, what does that give in two places?
- On the silver squares' finite abelian covers at the pulled-back members, at every class, including the covers' own (mixed)
  classes: sL-10 item 14, first half.
- On every word state and level.

## 2. Definitions and conventions

- **The state and the cover.**
  - M is complete, finite-volume and hyperbolic, with holonomy ρ_hyp and the four ρ = h ⊗ h̄ (the SO(3, 1) vector).
  - N → M is a finite cover with cusps P_1, …, P_k. Each P_i is a parabolic ℤ² with non-real shape (sm:B1530 Lemma T).
- **The frame** (sm:B1515 §1).
  - ν is a character of π₁N of finite order; V = ν ⊗ ρ, L = ν⁻⁴ and V_η = ν⁵ ⊗ ρ.
  - For c ∈ H¹(N; V_η) non-zero, W₁ = [[V, c·L], [0, L]].
  - Λ²W₁ is an extension of Q = V ⊗ L = ν⁻³ ⊗ ρ by S = Λ²V = ν² ⊗ Λ²ρ.
  - W₂ is the other stacking order, read through W₂* = W₁(ν̄, c′) (sm:B1515 Lemma 7).
- **The index** is main's: I(E) = n(E) − n(E*), with n the interior dimension.
  - sm:B1527's Lemma E gives I(E) = h¹(E*) − h¹(E) + 2(a0 − b0) + s0 − t0.
  - The B1297 identity and the annihilator identity hold (exact_lib asserts both at every call).
- **The supplies.**
  - n(ν³ ⊗ ρ) is the interior dimension of the twisted four. It is the cuspidal cohomology of the standard representation
    (Bart–Scannell) twisted by ν³.
  - n(ν⁴) is the line's interior dimension.
  - b0 = [ν⁴ = 1].
- **Generations.** In sm:B1509's dictionary N(10′) = −I(W) and N(5̄′) = −I(Λ²W).
  - A count is g generations when (I(W), I(Λ²W)) = (−g, −g) in W₁, or (+g, +g) in W₂, g ≥ 1.
  - "Three" is g = 3.
- **The covers in Part M.** A cover is given by its deck characters B, a group of characters of π₁M (labels (w₀, w₁; k)).
  - A class of the cover's frame at p*ν is c_A = Σ_{χ∈B} c_χ, with c_χ ∈ H¹(M; ν⁵χ ⊗ ρ) (Shapiro).
  - It is pulled back when only c_1 ≠ 0, pure when one c_χ ≠ 0, and mixed otherwise.

## 3. The theorems (proved at design time)

**Lemma E′ (the index of an extension from its connecting maps).** Let 0 → S → E → Q → 0 be modules on N. Let δ⁰, δ¹ be the
connecting maps of the long exact sequence on N, and δ⁰_P the one on the cusps (h⁰(∂; ·) summed over the cusps). Then
I(E) = I(S) + I(Q) + (rk δ¹_E − rk δ¹_{E*}) + (rk δ⁰_{E*} − rk δ⁰_E) + (rk δ⁰_{P,E} − rk δ⁰_{P,E*}).

*Proof.*
- Write f(X) = h¹(X) − 2h⁰(X) + h⁰(∂; X). Then sm:B1527's Lemma E reads I(X) = f(X*) − f(X).
- The two long exact sequences give f(E) = f(S) + f(Q) + rk δ⁰_E − rk δ¹_E − rk δ⁰_{P,E}, and the same for E*, whose
  submodule is Q* and quotient S*.
- Subtract. □

**Theorem C (the cap).** Let ν have finite order and c ≠ 0. Then:
- **(i)** I(Λ²W₁) = −dim(im δ¹ ∩ K) ∈ [−n(ν³ ⊗ ρ), 0].
  - δ¹ : H¹(N; S*) → H²(N; Q*) is the connecting map of (Λ²W₁)*.
  - K is the kernel of H²(N; Q*) → H²(∂N; Q*), of dimension n(Q*) = n(ν³ ⊗ ρ).
- **(ii)** I(W₁) = dim(⟨c_i⟩ ∩ Λ(V)) − b0 + rk δ¹_W − dim(im δ¹_{W*} ∩ K_L) ≥ −b0 − n(ν⁴).
  - c_i is c's restriction to the i-th cusp where ν is trivial, placed in that cusp's slot.
  - Λ(V) is the restriction image of H¹(N; V).
  - K_L is the interior part of H²(N; L*).
- **(iii)** So in either order a count (∓g, ∓g) needs n(ν³ ⊗ ρ) ≥ g and b0 + n(ν⁴) ≥ g.

*Proof.* Five ingredients:
- (a) sm:B1515 Lemma 2: unitary pieces are conjugate self-dual, so I(S) = I(Q) = 0 for every piece, and r1 = s0 for each.
- (b) Irreducibility: H⁰(N; Q) = H⁰(N; S*) = H⁰(N; V*) = 0. The holonomy restricted to a finite-index subgroup is still
  Zariski dense.
- (c) Garland–Raghunathan: PH¹(Γ; so(3, 1)) = 0 for every non-uniform lattice Γ. Apply it on the finite cover ker ν² ⊂ π₁N and
  take the ν²-isotypic part (so(3, 1) ⊗ ℂ = Λ²ρ = Ad ⊕ Ād). Two consequences:
  - **(c1)** n(ν² ⊗ Λ²ρ) = 0: cuspidal classes are parabolic. This is sm:B1515's Lemma 3.
  - **(c2)** Λ_A ∩ π_A = 0.
    - Suppose a class z of H¹(N; ν² ⊗ Λ²ρ) restricts at every cusp to the parabolic part π_A, the classes of cocycles valued
      in (Λ²ρ)^P = n ⊕ n̄.
    - Then z(γ) ∈ n + im(Ad γ − 1) = im(I − Ad γ) = 𝔟 for every parabolic γ. So z is parabolic, hence 0.
- (d) sm:B1530 Lemma T, the torus table at every parabolic cusp and every c_P ≠ 0:
  - W has (t0, s0, t1) = (1, 2, 3) and Λ²W has (2, 3, 5);
  - e ∧ c_P ≠ 0 lies in π_A.
- (e) Lemma E′.

The argument for (i):
- **δ⁰ = 0 on both sides**, by (b).
- **δ¹ of Λ²W₁ vanishes.**
  - H²(N; S) → H²(∂; S) is onto, because H³(N, ∂; S) = H⁰(N; S*)^∨ = 0.
  - Its kernel is the image of H²(N, ∂; S), dual (Poincaré–Lefschetz) to the interior part of H¹(N; S*). So it has dimension
    n(S*) = n(S) = 0, by (a) and (c1), and the map is injective.
  - Hence δ¹ is read on the boundary, where it is the sum of the torus cups with c|_{P_i}. These vanish:
    - where ν|_{P_i} = 1 and c_i ≠ 0, by the table (t1 = 5 forces the torus δ¹ to vanish);
    - where ν|_{P_i} = 1 and c_i = 0, because the restricted extension splits;
    - elsewhere, because a module is acyclic there (both pieces are non-acyclic on P_i only if ν²|_{P_i} = ν³|_{P_i} = 1).
- **The boundary terms.**
  - At a cusp where ν is trivial and c_i ≠ 0, the table gives rk δ⁰_{P,Λ²W} = 1 and rk δ⁰_{P,(Λ²W)*} = 0.
  - Elsewhere both are 0: if ν|_{P_i} ≠ 1 then c_i = 0 or a module is acyclic.
  - So the boundary terms sum to k, the number of cusps where ν is trivial and c_i ≠ 0.
- **The composite ρ_∂ ∘ δ¹ for (Λ²W₁)*.**
  - Its cusp parts are the functionals y ↦ ⟨y|_{P_i}, w_i⟩, with w_i = e ∧ c_i = δ⁰_{P_i}(e) (adjointness of the connecting
    maps under the torus pairing).
  - On Λ_A(S*) its rank is k − dim(⟨w_i⟩ ∩ Λ_A(S)). Here Λ_A(S*)^⊥ = Λ_A(S) by the annihilator identity.
  - That rank is k, by (c2): the w_i lie in π_A.
- **The assembly.** rk δ¹ = k + dim(im δ¹ ∩ K). Lemma E′ gives I(Λ²W₁) = 0 − (k + dim(im δ¹ ∩ K)) + k.
- **The dimension of K.** By the same duality dim K = n(Q) = n(Q*), by (a). And n(Q*) = n(ν³ ⊗ ρ).

The argument for (ii):
- **δ⁰.** rk δ⁰_W = b0, since 1 ↦ [c] when L = 1. And δ⁰_{W*} = 0, by (b).
- **The boundary terms.** By the table (t0, s0) = (1, 2) at every cusp where ν is trivial and c_i ≠ 0, the boundary terms are
  again k.
- **δ¹_W.** Its boundary composite vanishes (t1 = 3). So im δ¹_W lies in K_V, of dimension n(V).
- **δ¹_{W*}.** Its boundary composite is y ↦ (⟨y|_{P_i}, c_i⟩)_i, of rank k − dim(⟨c_i⟩ ∩ Λ(V)). The rest of its image lies in
  K_L, of dimension n(L*) = n(ν⁴).
- **The assembly** is Lemma E′.

(iii) is (i) and (ii) for W₁, and for W₂ through W₂* = W₁(ν̄, c′) with n(ν̄³ ⊗ ρ) = n(ν³ ⊗ ρ) and n(ν̄⁴) = n(ν⁴). □

**Remark (the twisted terms).** The proof uses only that W₁ is an extension of a finite-order character by a finite-order twist
of ρ, with its class in H¹ of their Hom. So it holds verbatim for each term of a cover's count (sm:B1532's Lemma S), for
W₁ ⊗ χ and for Λ²W₁ ⊗ χ, with χ of finite order.
- The pieces are νχ ⊗ ρ and ν⁻⁴χ for W₁ ⊗ χ, and ν²χ ⊗ Λ²ρ and ν⁻³χ ⊗ ρ for Λ²W₁ ⊗ χ.
- k counts the cusps where both pieces are trivial on P_i and c_i ≠ 0. There ν⁵|_{P_i} = 1, so the table applies.
- The bounds read I(Λ²W₁ ⊗ χ) ∈ [−n(ν⁻³χ ⊗ ρ), 0] and I(W₁ ⊗ χ) ≥ −[χ = ν⁴] − n(ν⁴χ̄).
This is the form K1 checks on sm:B1534's terms.

**Lemma W (the line has no interior class).** Let N be a once-punctured-torus bundle with Anosov monodromy (a word state or a
level), or a finite abelian cover of one. Let ζ be a finite-order character of π₁N, trivial on every puncture loop of N's fibre.
Then n(ζ) = 0.

*Proof.*
- **The fibre.** N fibres with fibre F_D, a torus with |D| punctures, unbranched over the closed torus T̄. Abelian characters of
  F₂ kill [a, b], so the puncture loop lifts to closed loops.
- **ζ non-trivial on F_D.** Then ζ extends to a non-trivial character of T̄, and H*(T̄; ζ) = 0. The pair sequence of
  (F_D, ∂F_D) then makes H¹(F_D; ζ) → H¹(∂F_D; ζ) an isomorphism.
- **ζ trivial on F_D.** The monodromy acts on H¹(T̄) by an Anosov map, with no root-of-unity eigenvalue. So no eigenvector of
  the twisted monodromy lies in the kernel of the restriction to ∂F_D.
- **The conclusion.**
  - Wang's sequence gives H¹(N; ζ) from the twisted-monodromy invariants of H¹(F_D; ζ), plus the fibration class.
  - Every such class restricts non-trivially to a puncture circle, and that circle lies in a cusp of N.
  - So the restriction H¹(N; ζ) → H¹(∂N; ζ) is injective, and n(ζ) = 0.
- **The once-punctured case.** |D| = 1, and every character of F₂ is trivial on [a, b]. The lemma then says h¹(N; ζ) = [ζ|_P = 1].
  - The mechanism is the identity μ_u = τ_u for every φ-fixed torsion u ≠ 0.
  - μ_u is the eigenvalue of φ* on H¹(F; u) = ℂ² / ℂβ, β = (u(a) − 1, u(b) − 1).
  - τ_u is ζ(t) at ζ(t′) = 1: it is 1 for sign +, and u(ab)⁻¹ for sign −.
  - Part W reads this identity. □

**Corollary C1 (sL-10 item 14, first half).** On every finite abelian cover of m135 or m136, at every pulled-back member at every
twist and at every class of the cover (pulled back, pure, mixed), and in either order:
- the count is at most one generation;
- the 5̄′ count is at most Σ_{χ∈B} n(ν³χ ⊗ ρ) ≤ 2.

*Proof.*
- By sm:B1534's Lemma Q only finite-order ν count.
- n_N(ν⁴) = Σ_χ n_M(ν⁴χ) = 0, by Lemma W on M.
- n_N(ν³ ⊗ ρ) = Σ_χ n_M(ν³χ ⊗ ρ). A finite-order ψ = (u, κ) has h¹(ψ ⊗ ρ) ≥ 1 only at κ = 1, or at κ = −1 on m136's u₁, u₂
  (sm:B1534's K10: the eigenvalues of T_C(u) on the unit circle). Of these, exactly two per state carry an interior class, one
  each: m135's u₁, u₂ at κ = 1 and m136's u₁, u₂ at κ = −1 (sm:B1530). So the sum is at most 2.
- Theorem C (iii). □

**Corollary C2.** On every word state of any length and every level of m004, at every finite-order member and every class, in

**Lemma F (the cusps of a fibre-direction cover).** Let every χ ∈ B be trivial on t′, and let g be a coset word. On the cusp
g P g⁻¹ the class c_A, pulled back to P, is (ν⁵ρ)(g) · Σ_χ χ(g) [c_χ|_P].

*Proof.* c_χ(g p g⁻¹) = M(g) c_χ(p) + (1 − M(g p g⁻¹)) c_χ(g), with M = ν⁵χρ. The second term is a coboundary on the conjugate
cusp. □
