## 1. What is sealed

- **The question (after B1502 §5).** Witten's cubic inflow forces, at a point of an SU(N) locus, charged fields with SU(N)³ anomaly
  n = deg(L) on the link of the point. What does the link's own geometry say about those degrees, for all the loci one symmetry fixes
  at once?
- **The rule, proved at seal.**
  - A nearly Kähler link has positive scalar curvature, so its Dirac operator has no kernel and every equivariant index vanishes.
  - The Lefschetz fixed-point formula then gives, for each element γ, a sum over its isolated fixed points and its fixed curves.
  - Each curve enters with (i/8) cot(θ/2) sin^{−2}(θ/2) (d₁ − d₂), which is its cubic inflow n = (N/2)(d₁ − d₂) times a weight fixed
    by its rotation angle.
- **Consequences, proved at seal.**
  - A locus that is its element's only fixed component forces nothing, on any nearly Kähler link.
  - A cusp point is chiral through the cubic half only where its locus meets another fixed locus or point of the same element.
- **Decided at design time from B1501's banked components.**
  - S⁶'s single-sphere classes and all torus classes have n = 0.
  - S³ × S³'s 3-symmetry has a sphere with n = ±3 (SU(3)), balanced by an isolated fixed point.
- **The predictions.**
  - P1: ℂP³'s two-sphere classes pair their spheres at one angle with n = ±N (prior about 65%).
  - P2: ℂP³'s one-sphere classes have n = 0 on the sphere, the two points cancelling (prior about 75%).
  - P3: F₁,₂'s spheres all have n = 0 (prior about 80%).
