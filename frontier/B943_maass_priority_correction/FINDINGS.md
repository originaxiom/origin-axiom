## The verdict: **PRIOR ART FOUND — PARTIAL.** The claim survives on precision; its precedent number is REFUTED; two qualifiers are load-bearing and were missing.

### 1. The precedent number was wrong — and the repo's own figure was the wrong one

B922 asserted the prior state of the art was **"~10 digits"**. The true published figure
is **13 decimal places / 14 significant digits**:

> **r = 6.6221193402528** — the Picard orbifold PSL(2,ℤ[i])\\H³ ground state, read from
> the primary (Aurich–Steiner–Then, *Numerical computation of Maass waveforms and an
> application to cosmology*, arXiv:gr-qc/0404020, §8; identical value in Then,
> arXiv:math-ph/0305048).

The "~10" came from their **tables**, which print 8 decimals; the §8 text carries 13.
Only 5 decimals are independently cross-checked in the literature (against Steil 1999).

**So B922's 25 decimal places lead by 12, not by 15.** The claim's substance survives;
the number quoted for its margin did not.

### 2. Maass eigenvalues on hyperbolic 3-manifolds have been computed, abundantly

An eight-item literature, 1991–2025: Smotrov–Golovčanskiĭ (1991), Grunewald–Huntebrinker
(1996), Huntebrinker (1996), Steil (1999), Then (2003) / Aurich–Steiner–Then (2004),
Möhring (2004), Inoue (1999/2001), De Clerck–Hartnoll–Yang (2025). **The "on any
hyperbolic 3-manifold" phrasing was therefore wrong in a second way**, independent of
precision: Inoue computed Laplace eigenvalues on genuine **closed** hyperbolic
3-manifolds. The word "cusp form" is doing real work and was absent.

### 3. The pullback caveat — and the fact that resolves it, VERIFIED IN-SANDBOX

The panel raised a caveat the arc had to absorb rather than wave away: **Γ₄₁ ⊂ PSL(2,O₃)
at index 12, so every published *parent* eigenvalue already IS an eigenvalue of the
figure-eight complement, by pullback** — and published parent data exists (r ≈ 24.5033,
6 digits, De Clerck–Hartnoll–Yang 2025, which this repo already banks as a control
without having registered that it defeats "m004 has never been computed").

**This seat verified the resolving fact rather than accepting it.** Two computations,
from Humbert's volume formula and the Weyl law, using nothing from the panel:

| vol(PSL(2,O₃)\\H³) (Humbert) | 0.169156934401609 |
| vol(m004) (standard) | 2.02988321281931 |

The middle row **reproduces B791's own W = 1.010 from scratch**, confirming that
r ≈ 7.072 is the parent's **ground state**. The row that matters: the parent's Weyl count
at our r is **0.336 — well under one**. So **r = 4.9000853… lies below the parent's
spectrum and cannot be a pullback of any parent eigenvalue.** The specific claim
survives the caveat, on a fact this seat computed.

**Honest limit on that fact.** The Weyl law is a leading-order **screen**, not a
theorem-grade exclusion, and cc3's mesh scan that localized the parent ground state ran
over [6.4, 7.35] — it does not by itself exclude a parent eigenvalue below 6.4. The
non-inheritance qualifier is therefore **screened and consistent, not proven**. A
certified parent scan below 6.4 would settle it; that is registered, not claimed.

### 4. What we refuse to lean on

All the high-precision prior art is on **orbifolds**, not manifolds. Reading "hyperbolic
3-**manifold**" strictly would technically save the original sentence. **We decline that
escape.** It is exactly the necessary-read-as-sufficient move B525 was written against,
and a claim that survives only by a word its author did not intend when writing it is not
a claim that survived.
