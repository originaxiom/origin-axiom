# B1871 — One tick or two acts: the advisor seat's sealed run verified on main, and what the tick does to the weave's three

*Main, 2026-11-10 (S306o). The record's grammar has two acts, L and R. The owner's own founding rule, "a, ab, ba", is
one act: add, and the two sides change roles — the tick. The foundation takes the two acts, grades that choice "the
most fragile link", and says of the other that what it would break "is not computed". Main registered that
computation as owed (P-769). The advisor seat ran it as a sealed run (`principle_top_down/the_tick/` on its branch,
sealed before it ran, with one counting error it found and corrected afterwards). Main read its pre-registration and
result in full, ran none of its code, and recomputed everything with its own. Data-free: integers and SnapPy. 0 of
19.*

## 1. The answer first

- **The seat's result holds in every part.** Its four computations come out on main as it reports them, and one of
  them on a wider range.
- **Nothing in the grammar forbids the tick, and nothing forces it.** The computation shows what is traded.
- **What the tick explains.** LR is the tick done twice. A history with no hand, done twice, is its own mirror image.
  So the root's mirror symmetry, which the two-act grammar has as an unexplained property, becomes a consequence.
- **What it costs.** It adds about half as many histories again, each without a hand. From level 3 upward the group of
  the moves doubles. A single tick survives alone.
- **Main adds one fact the seat did not compute.** On the weave's own six-dimensional space the exchange carries the
  triplet into its conjugate. With the exchange the triplet and its conjugate are one object. So the tick gives the
  three no hand: it joins the two hands.
- **Whether the tick is an act is the owner's to rule.** The seat records an answer of the owner to it. Main treats
  that as relayed, and puts the question to the owner once, with its own opinion (§ 5), because the foundation
  depends on it.

## 3. Main's addition: the tick on the weave's six-dimensional space

The weave's triplet and its conjugate fill a six-dimensional space on which the two moves and the exchange act
(B1610's matrices, main's).

- The two acts keep the triplet. The exchange carries it into its conjugate, and the conjugate into it.
- The exchange normalises the two acts' group: it carries L and R to elements of it, and the tick done twice lies in
  it. (The exchange enters with a lift that is fixed only up to that group; these statements do not depend on it.)
- The two acts generate 96 matrices and leave two pieces, the triplet and its conjugate. With the exchange there are
  192, and one piece.

So the record's sentence that the three's hand is the sheet of the orientation cover, which the principle's own rule
exchanges (B1609, B1610), is here a statement about modules: under the tick the six-dimensional space is irreducible.
The tick does not choose between the triplet and its conjugate.

## 4. What it would change, and what is owed first

If the tick is an act:

- **The orientation stops being a choice.** Both the one-tick history (m000) and the two-tick history (m004) exist.
  The foundation's fork on this already holds that option. One input leaves the budget.
- **One of the frame's joints stops being a choice.** At level 5 the two lines are one orbit of the tick.
- **The hand is still not supplied.** Every history with a hand has a mirror partner, the added histories have no
  hand, and the triplet is joined to its conjugate. The record's wall on handedness stands, and is sharper.

**Three debts, to be computed before anything is built on the added histories:**

1. what replaces a spin structure on a history with no hand (the seat's);
2. the record's structures at odd levels — the level-5 representation, the charge and line premises — read again
   under the doubled group (the seat's);
3. every theorem the weave states for the two acts' group, restated for the group with the exchange. The triplet
   is then not a module by itself (main's, from § 3).

**Not computed by anyone:** which of the eight walls the record traces to the mirror stand or fall. That part of
P-769 stays open.

## 5. Main's opinion, for the owner

- **Admit the tick as an act of the grammar,** on the seat's three grounds, which main has checked: it is the
  founding rule; nothing forbids it; and it explains the root's mirror symmetry.
- **Build nothing on the added histories until the three debts are computed.**
- **Do not read it as supplying a hand.** It removes a choice by keeping both sides, which is the owner's own law
