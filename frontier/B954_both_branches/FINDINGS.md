# B954 — DO WE NEED BOTH BRANCHES TO UNIFY? **Yes — and the odd one cannot stand alone**

## The answer

**Yes, necessarily — and the question dissolves a misconception in B953's own framing.**

B953 described θ-even and θ-odd as "two seeds" with complementary defects, as if one could
choose between them. **One cannot.** The ℤ/2 grading forces:

| bracket | lands in | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| [even, even] | even | **the even part IS a subalgebra** — F₄, dim 52, rank 4 |
| [even, odd] | odd | the odd part is a **module** over it |
| **[odd, odd]** | **even** | **the odd part is NOT closed — it is not an algebra at all** |

So:

- You **can** restrict to the even branch. F₄ is a genuine subalgebra: rank 4, all reps
  real, achiral. **This is where the geometric point sits** (B576).
- You **cannot** restrict to the odd branch. The 26 is a module, not an algebra. There is
  no "θ-odd world" to relocate into.
- **Both branches together = F₄ ⊕ 26 = e₆**, dim 78, rank 6.

**The two "seeds" are not two alternative worlds. They are the two graded pieces of one
object, and only the even piece is self-standing.**

## Why this matters — the trap, stated exactly

Chirality lives **only** in the θ-odd component (B576). Therefore:

> **chirality requires the odd part → the odd part exists only inside the whole → the whole
> is rank 6.**

And now the sharp part. **θ is itself a rank-reducer**: it takes E₆ (rank 6) to F₄
(rank 4) — exactly the two units B952 says must go. So the object *does* possess a
rank-reducing operation, and it lands on precisely the right rank.

> **But θ reduces rank by DELETING the odd part — which is where chirality lives. The one
> rank-reducer the object has is the one operation that removes what the reduction is for.**

That is the trap in one sentence, and it is why B953's "switching seeds is not a fix" is
true for a stronger reason than B953 gave: there is no second seed to switch to.

## What this does to L133

L133 asked for "a rank-reducing mechanism that is not the θ-projection". This arc explains
**why** that constraint is the right one and not an arbitrary exclusion: **θ is the object's
only manifest rank-reducer, and it is disqualified by construction.** Any candidate must

1. reduce rank 6 → 4, and
2. **not** be a θ-projection — i.e. must not act by deleting the odd component.

The GUT chain does exactly this: E₆ → SO(10) → SU(5) sheds U(1)_ψ and U(1)_χ **by Higgs
VEVs in the 27**, keeping the 27 complex and the matter chiral. That is requirement #11 of
the GUT ledger, and it is the thing the object does not supply.

## Honest limits

1. The grading facts are **forced by the ℤ/2 structure**, not specific to E₆ — that is what
   makes the conclusion robust, but it also means **nothing here is E₆-specific except the
   dimensions and ranks** (52 + 26 = 78; rank F₄ = 4).
2. **Not excluded:** a rank-reducing subgroup that is neither F₄ nor all of E₆ and retains
   complex reps. **SU(5) is exactly such a thing.** The claim is only that the object's own
   operations — measurement (rank-preserving, B952) and θ (chirality-killing, here) — do
   not reach it.
3. This refutes nothing. B576, B953, B952 all stand; B953's conclusion is *strengthened*,
   its "two seeds to choose between" framing *corrected*.

**Verdict: YES, both branches are needed — and the odd branch is not a place one can
stand.** Chirality requires the whole; the whole is rank 6; and θ, the object's only
rank-reducer, achieves rank 4 precisely by discarding the chirality it was needed for.
