## The gap is structural, not evidential

Let **G** act on the space of pairs of 27s, **O** the G(ℚ)-orbit containing the object's pair **v**.

> **Every point of O is G(ℚ)-equivalent to v — by the definition of an orbit.**

The cubic étale algebra is an **orbit invariant**: it is *constant* on O. That is precisely why it
classifies orbits at all. Therefore:

> **No invariant built from the G-action can distinguish one point of O from another. A canonical
> point inside O is not merely unproved — it is UNREACHABLE by any orbit invariant.**
>
> Asking the orbit invariant to pick a point is asking a class function to pick a representative.

**What this rules out, and it is worth stating because it is the tempting direction:** sharpening
the *same* invariant — a finer cubic, more primes, a better splitting statistic — **cannot** close
the gap. Nor can a second orbit invariant. Every such move is constant on O.

## Exactly two routes cross it

**Route A — shrink the group.** Replace G(ℚ) by a subgroup that does **not** act transitively on O.
The natural one is arithmetic: **G(ℤ)**, or G(𝒪_K) for the object's own field. Then O breaks into
finitely many integral orbits and a canonical one may exist.

**This is B962's banked observation, and it is the reason the opening was live at all:** *"Over ℤ or
a number field homogeneity **FAILS**."* The count of G(ℤ)-orbits inside one G(ℚ)-orbit is a **class
number**-type quantity. **If it is 1, the integral orbit is a canonical point up to G(ℤ)** — which
is exactly what a VEV direction needs, since a VEV is only ever defined up to the unbroken group.

**Route B — add structure the orbit cannot see.** Impose a condition that is **not** G-invariant: a
reality/positivity condition, an integrality lattice, or compatibility with a second structure the
object independently supplies. This breaks O **by construction** rather than by invariant.
