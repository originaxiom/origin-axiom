## 3. What it shows, and where it stops

**In the limit of long golden gaps:**
- **A departure has an own value, and a long gap restores it.** Whatever else the history holds, each copy of a
  departure reads the departure's own value once the gaps beside it are long. So does the apex's real part.
- **The rate tends to φ⁴ per golden period of gap.** φ² is LR's own eigenvalue, so one period of the ground hides
  what lies beyond it by that eigenvalue squared. Post hoc: the step ratios for two copies of (6) are 6.935, 6.866,
  6.856, 6.8543, 6.8541, against φ⁴ = 6.8541.
- **Several departures read the deepest,** and copies do not change the value. Two copies tie exactly (the closure
  card's C2). Three and four differ by amounts that fall at the same rate.
- So for the value read, "one departure, not several" merges into "which is the deepest".

**At short gaps the value statements do not hold exactly** (the referee's findings, reproduced by the seat, post
hoc). Write D_g for a departure D closed by g golden periods.
- **The value moves.** The card's k = 1 has gaps of one and two periods: (6) reads half a percent off its own
  value. With one period on both sides it is nearly one percent: L⁶RLR reads 3.6515 against 3.6180. For (11) it is
  0.65%.
- **The reading does not compose as a greatest.** (6)₁(6)₂ reads 3.6371, below (6)₁ alone at 3.6515.
- **The apex can sit at the shallower departure.** A = (2,1,1,4,4) and B = (2,1,2,4,4) both keep the line, with own
  values 2.418183 and 2.423508. In A₁B₂ the apex is in A, at 2.425956. In A₂B₃, A₄B₅ and A₈B₉ it is in B; the
  referee finds the same for every gap from 2 to 12. V2's "at every k" held because the card's departures are 1.89
  or more apart in own value.

## 4. The allowed histories' readings have a least and no greatest

- The runs of 6, 11, 16, 21 and 26 all keep LR's line: L⁵ ≡ I mod 5, so Lⁿ R (LR)^k is a power of LR mod 5 exactly
  when n ≡ 1 mod 5. The control n = 7 does not.
- Their own values are (n − 1)/2 + √5/2, B1853's depth. They increase without bound. This holds at any finite set
  of levels.
- **The readings have a least and no greatest.** The least is the ground's own, √5/2 (B1875: "Every apex is at
  least √5/2."). There is no greatest: by § 3 a history reads its deepest departure in the limit, and there is
  always a deeper one that is allowed.
- **What that gives for the lightest level, under one assumption.** B1857's dE₀/dq > 0 compares classes inside one
  bundle at one fibre area. Carried across histories it needs one area for all of them, which the record has as
  unearned (P-743). Under that assumption, among the allowed histories' own lightest levels none is lowest.
- **It is not a statement about "a state of the whole".** B1875 § 7 has that as undefined: it needs "an operator, a
  clock and a statement of what its symmetries force", and none is on record. Nor is B1857's reading uniform: it is
  the apex height only as A → 0, and by P-743 the state's width grows with the height, so at a fixed area
  sufficiently deep runs are far from that limit.
- This is T:NO-PLACE in this instrument.
