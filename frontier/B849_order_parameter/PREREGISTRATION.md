**Spontaneous symmetry breaking is not a manner of speaking.** It commits to an order parameter: a

arc tries to exhibit one, and pre-commits to the reading of every outcome.

## A correction made BEFORE computing, and recorded here because it weakens my own prior

In a prior message this seat said the object's **amphichirality** makes the order parameter
"provably zero," and called that a cheap kill. **That reasoning is wrong and is withdrawn here,
before any number exists.**

In SSB the **system** carries the symmetry and the **state** breaks it. A ferromagnet's
Hamiltonian is rotation-invariant; that is the *precondition* for magnetisation, not an argument
against it. **m004's amphichirality is therefore the ℤ/2 existing, which is what SSB requires.**

So Cells 1–3 below cannot kill the reframe, and are not permitted to be reported as if they could.
What they can do is **rule out the easy answer** — an order parameter built from orientation-odd
topological invariants of the manifold — and thereby say precisely what the reframe still owes.
**Cell 4 is the cell with teeth.**

## Cells and operations

**Cell 1 — the instrument, with a positive control.** Compute the Chern–Simons invariant
(Im of the complex volume) across a panel of cusped hyperbolic manifolds, **chiral and amphichiral
both**. CS is the canonical orientation-odd invariant: reversing orientation negates it.
*Non-vacuity requirement: at least one panel member must return CS ≠ 0.* An instrument that
returns zero for everything has measured nothing, and "CS(m004) = 0" would be uninterpretable
without it. Period convention (CS for cusped manifolds is defined modulo a period) stated with the
output, not assumed.

**Cell 2 — m004 itself.** CS(m004), and its symmetry group, **exhibiting** the
orientation-reversing element rather than citing amphichirality.

**Cell 3 — the lemma, verified not assumed.** For amphichiral M, any orientation-odd invariant I
satisfies I(M) = −I(M), so 2·I(M) = 0: I is zero or 2-torsion in its value group. Checked across
the panel: every amphichiral member must land on zero-or-half-period, every chiral member is free
not to.

**Cell 4 — THE LEVEL TEST.** The β=1 transition of a Bost–Connes-type system over an imaginary
quadratic field K has its extremal KMS states permuted simply-transitively by **Gal(K^ab/K)** —
which is also the shape B700 reports for its torsor. But the chirality ℤ/2 the reframe nominates
is **complex conjugation**, and complex conjugation **does not fix K = ℚ(√−3)**: it sends
√−3 ↦ −√−3. So it lives in **Gal(K/ℚ)**, not in Gal(K^ab/K).

> **If the symmetry the reframe says breaks is not an element of the group that acts on the states
> that break it, then chirality is not the order parameter of the β=1 transition — it is the right
> object at the wrong level.**

Computable part, in-sandbox: that complex conjugation does not fix K, and that the two geometric
representations ρ, ρ̄ are exchanged by it while trace-field data is not. **Cited part, flagged in
advance: that Gal(K^ab/K) is the acting group in the CMR/Bost–Connes system.** That identification
is **NOT verified in this seat** and is declared a citation, not a computation — P5 died last week
because a gate named the right person and was asked the wrong question, and this arc will not
repeat it by assertion.
