## THE SOURCE, read not inferred

Baez & Schwahn, arXiv:2606.15235 (13 June 2026), **Theorem 1**: for Jordan subalgebras
`X ≅ 𝔥₂(ℂ)`, `B ≅ 𝔥₃(ℂ)` with `X ⊂ B ⊂ 𝔥₃(𝕆)`,
`Stab(X) ∩ Stab(B)₀ ≅ S(U(2)×U(3))` — the Standard Model gauge group. And
`Stab(B)₀ ≅ (SU(3)×SU(3))/ℤ₃`.

Dimensions, corrected: `𝔥₂(ℂ)` is **4**-dimensional (an earlier fetch said 6; that is
`𝔥₂(ℍ)`). `𝔥₃(ℂ)` is 9, `𝔥₃(𝕆)` is 27.

## T1 — FAILED

`Stab(B)` preserves `B` **setwise**, so if the 16 were `Stab(B)₀` then `B` would be a
**9-dimensional invariant subspace of the 27**. Casimir decomposition under our
`su(3)⊕su(3)`:

```
    eigenvalue -> multiplicity :  {0: 3, 183: 6, 365: 16, 911: 2}     sum = 27
```

**No 9.** Control passed (trace form on the 16 nondegenerate, rank 16; multiplicities sum to
27). So our `su(3)⊕su(3)` is **isomorphic** to `Stab(B)₀` but **not conjugate to it in `F₄`** —
theirs preserves a 9-space, ours does not.

## THE CLASS ERROR, REPEATED — the ninth instance

The whole point of this item was that Baez–Schwahn stabilise **subalgebras**, a class we had
never searched. **And I tested it with a centraliser.** I asked "does a charge-direction
*centraliser* of the right type happen to preserve a 9-space" — a proxy inside the *old*
class, not a search of the new one.

So T1's failure is a statement about **centralisers of charge directions**, not about whether
the object determines a `B`. Per `THE_RULE.md`:

> **CLASS COVERED:** `su(3)⊕su(3)` subalgebras of `F₄` arising as centralisers of a direction
> in the charge algebra `C`.
> **OUTSIDE IT:** stabilisers of Jordan subalgebras, which is the class the theorem is about
> and which remains **unsearched**.

## WHAT A REAL TEST NEEDS

carry — it would require building `𝔥₃(𝕆)` explicitly with its octonion multiplication. That
build has now been deferred twice for good reason and is the honest next step.
