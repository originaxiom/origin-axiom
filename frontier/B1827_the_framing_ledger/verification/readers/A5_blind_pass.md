# A5 — a blind pass: what the programme's framing makes impossible to find

*An outside reading, 2026-11-03. Read-only; nothing committed; no measured-data file opened; one small numerical check
run with the frame's own functions (Appendix). Independent of the other readers' work in this folder (not read). Where I
quote a measured number I use textbook values I carry in my head and say so; the record's own printed numbers are cited

## The answer first

The programme has framed itself so that the only admissible answer is "a rigid point of one punctured torus, at one
modulus, with one hypercharge Wilson line and one flavour-carrying Higgs, reproduces nine numbers to their errors". It
has then shown, carefully and honestly, that this answer does not exist (B1825). What the framing rules out in advance is
every answer flavour physics has ever actually found:

1. **Relations and exponents instead of values.** The bar grades a value at σ or nothing (`docs/THE_BAR.md`); a derived
   pattern of exponents with one fitted scale is logged as a negative ("never the scale", S245–S248). That is the
   Froggatt–Nielsen / modular-flavour form of every successful explanation of hierarchies.
2. **A Higgs that is a flavour singlet.** FR-FIB's Higgs is a flavour triplet (the adjoint, h₀ = 0), so the SM's H̃ for
   the up quarks conjugates the *flavour* vector (B1739: "under the reading S̃ the Higgs is h̄"). That single choice
   produces the orthogonality lock, the CKM corner inversion (B1826) and the three "reading" bits. In every modular
   flavour model the Higgs is a singlet and the τ-triplet is a coupling; there is nothing to conjugate.
3. **The line as a gauge-centre holonomy.** The 266-line menu hides that the record's winning lines are centre Wilson
   lines: order 5 = Z₅ of SU(5) (B1728's own "five-ality"), the odd offsets = Z₂ of SU(2)_L on exactly the doublets, and
   the colour Z₃ is a hypercharge line of order 3 by the Z₆ global form the record derived (B862, B1221). On those lines
   the frame *is* minimal SU(5) (Y_u symmetric, Y_e = Y_dᵀ; Appendix) and "c = 3" is the Georgi–Jarlskog Clebsch put in
   by hand. The principled menu (orders dividing 30) is tiny and its Z₅ × Z₃ members (orders 15, 30) sit just outside
   the sealed reach of 12.
4. **More than one locus.** One point coupling (rank ≤ 2, B1653) for all three sectors. F-theory's lesson is that
   Yukawas live at points and the CKM is the relative rotation between the up point and the down point; the record's
   own B1694 (three sectors at three τ's) and B1825's hatch (P-555) say the same.
5. **The thread, not the root.** SE1 selects the torsion-free state, which the record itself calls "the emptiest state";
   LR fixes no fibre modulus (hyperbolic) and no flat line (det(LR − 1) = −1), so the strip at Im τ ≈ 3 on an order-10
   line is a statement about a punctured torus, not about m004. The threads whose torsion equals the line's order do
   reach the strip (B1720: factor 1.80 on all six with no continuous number, 1.65 % against a 1 % bar).
6. **The strong angle as a constraint rather than a nineteenth unknown.** The frame computes arg det(Y_u Y_d) at every
   address (P-323: 0.177π at the best six-row) and files it as "an input". Measured θ̄ < 10⁻¹⁰ with J ≠ 0 is a
   zero-bit selector of Re τ, or a kill of the one-address frame without an axion.
7. **The inter-sector ratios of the third generation.** One Higgs, one normalisation: m_t : m_b : m_τ is a rigid output of
   FR-FIB (y_b = y_τ exactly on order-5 lines). The scoreboard files y_t, y_b, y_τ as "not touched", so two dimensionless
   predictions are never computed.
8. **Scales.** No normalisation, no gauge kinetic function, no potential, no comparison scale: y_t, the three gauge
   couplings, m_H, v are excluded by construction, not by result.

The record contains most of the pieces (§3). What it lacks is a frame card that names them as physics.

## 4. Assessment

The nineteen cannot all come from this object. It is a finite, amphichiral datum with no scale, no dynamics and no gauge
kinetic function: m_H, v, y_t, the three gauge couplings and the sign of θ̄ need a unit, a potential or a boundary the
object does not carry, and the record has proved as much in its own vocabulary (no dimensionful quantity, CS = 0, no
potential holds τ). What can come from it is flavour *structure*: the SU(5)-type relations the centre lines already
impose, a symmetric up Yukawa, the down–lepton transposition up to a Clebsch, a sector-dependent exponent pattern, the
corner order of the CKM, the locus where det Y is real, and the inter-sector third-generation ratios — with two or three
continuous numbers (Im τ; a second locus or h₀; a scale) fitted and declared as such. That would be a modular-flavour
model of unusual rigidity (no free O(1) coefficients at all), worth perhaps five to eight relations among the thirteen
flavour parameters, and it would be the first honest positive on the scoreboard. The single most promising path is to
reframe FR-FIB as what it is — a punctured-torus Wilson-line model whose lines are centre elements of SU(5) × SU(2) ×
SU(3) and whose Higgs is a flavour singlet — and to test it first on its rigid relations and its inter-sector outputs
(items 1, 2, 7 and 6 above, all data-free and cheap), with Im τ counted as one fitted parameter and the thread (item 5)
as the discrete datum that may replace it. If the Clebsch 3 does not come from any centre line (my expectation), the
programme will have proved that the Georgi–Jarlskog factor needs a non-abelian Higgs component, and it should then say,
in one sentence, that this object supplies the symmetry structure of flavour and not its numbers.
