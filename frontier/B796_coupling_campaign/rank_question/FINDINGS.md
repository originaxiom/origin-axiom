# PHASE 2b — THE RANK QUESTION DISSOLVES INTO THE EXOTICS QUESTION

cc3, 2026-08-10. Gate 5-Q. Structure only.

## The question

The cascade lands on **su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)³** — rank 6 (B892). The Standard
Model is rank 4. Five arcs (B952/B955/B959/B960/B962) close every route to
reducing rank, and I established two days ago that **arithmeticity forces** that
closure. But I could not settle by reading whether B864's anomaly argument
*removes the need*. Settled here.

## What B864 actually says — both halves matter

**Over the full 27, everything is anomaly-free:**

> *"Over the full 27: Tr ψ = 16−20+4 = 0, Tr ψ³ = 16−80+64 = 0, [SO(10)]²ψ = 2−2
> = 0. Over the full 16: Tr χ = Tr χ³ = [SU(5)]²χ = 0… **Nothing at the parent
> level forces a truncation.**"*

**Over the chiral core alone, two of the three u(1)s become anomalous**, cannot
be gauged, and hypercharge is the unique survivor.

**And the step between them is explicitly not derived:**

> *"**It does not derive the re-anchoring itself**… over the full 27 nothing is
> anomalous, so anomaly alone does not force keeping only the chiral core. What
> it derives is the dial/gauge split **given** the chiral matter."*

## The resolution

```
   cascade  →  su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)³              rank 6
                    │
                    │  over the FULL 27: all three u(1)s anomaly-free
                    │  → rank 6 is a CONSISTENT gauge group. Nothing to fix.
                    │
                    │  over the CHIRAL CORE (the 16 alone): ψ, χ anomalous
                    │  → not gaugeable → rank 4
                    ▼
   the SM   →  su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)_Y              rank 4
```

**So the rank problem is resolved — conditionally, by the truncation to chiral
matter, and not by rank reduction at all.** Whether the gauge group is rank 6 or
rank 4 is decided by *which matter is present*, not by breaking anything.

**Therefore the rank-reduction sequence is answering a question the framework has
already routed around.** B952/B955/B959/B960/B962 prove you cannot reduce rank by
centralizers or adjoint VEVs. That is true, and **you do not need to** — provided
the truncation happens.

## And the truncation IS the exotics question

`27 = 16 + 10 + 1`. Keeping "the chiral core" means keeping the **16** and not
the **10 + 1** — which is exactly the twelve exotic states per generation that
L134 registers and that are not observed. So:

> **the rank question ≡ the truncation ("re-anchoring") ≡ the exotics question.**
> **Three ledger items, one problem.**

That unification is the phase's real output. The framework's open item is not
*"how do we lose two units of rank"* — a question with five no-gos against it —
but *"what makes the 10 + 1 heavy"*, which has a **named candidate mechanism**:
the cubic, `27³ ⊃ 16·16·10`, whose table B884 computed exactly, with
doublet–triplet splitting external (B987, B298/B299).

**That is a strictly better place to stand.** One open question with a candidate
mechanism, instead of one open question with five proofs of impossibility plus a
separate unexplained truncation.

## What remains genuinely open, stated exactly

1. **The re-anchoring is underived.** B864 says so in its own words. Nothing in
   the corpus supplies it.
2. **No mechanism removes an anomalous gauge U(1).** Searched: **no
   Green–Schwarz, Stückelberg, or anomalous-U(1)-mass material exists anywhere in
   the corpus.** In string constructions an anomalous U(1) is removed by GS and
   becomes massive; here *"an anomalous U(1) cannot be gauged"* is used as a
   **selection principle** — it says which subgroup is *the* gauge group rather
   than describing a process that removes the others. **That may be legitimate,
   and it is not the same thing as a mechanism**, and the ledger should not let
   the two read as one.
3. **Doublet–triplet splitting stays external.**
