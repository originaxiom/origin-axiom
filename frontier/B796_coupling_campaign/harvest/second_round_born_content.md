## 1. Busch 2003: POVM-Gleason

**Statement** (P. Busch, "Quantum states and generalized observables: a simple proof of Gleason's theorem," [Phys. Rev. Lett. 91, 120403 (2003)](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.91.120403), [arXiv:quant-ph/9909073](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9909073)) [VERIFIED]:

- **Assumes:** a *generalized probability measure on effects* — a function v: E(H) → [0,1] on the full effect algebra (all positive operators ≤ I) with v(I) = 1 and finite additivity over effect sums (v(A+B) = v(A) + v(B) whenever A+B ≤ I). This is the POVM analogue of Gleason's frame function, and a *mathematically stronger* assumption than Gleason's (additivity is demanded on far more decompositions).
- **Concludes:** every such v is v(A) = tr(ρA) for a unique density operator ρ.
- **What it buys:** (i) the dimension-2 restriction of Gleason is *gone* — the theorem holds for qubits, where projective Gleason fails (exotic frame functions exist on P(C²)); (ii) the proof is elementary (no Gleason-style analysis on the sphere); (iii) a von Neumann–type no-go for noncontextual hidden variables on effects falls out as a corollary.
- **Companion:** Caves, Fuchs, Manne, Renes, "Gleason-Type Derivations of the Quantum Probability Rule for Generalized Measurements," [Found. Phys. 34, 193–209 (2004)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:FOOP.0000019581.00318.a5), [arXiv:quant-ph/0306179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0306179) [VERIFIED] — same result with variants; notably works even for vector spaces over *rational* fields, i.e., POVM frame functions are rigid in settings where projective ones are floppy.
- **The catch (relevant to "forced vs imported"):** the additivity-on-effects assumption is doing enormous work — effects A and B that sum inside I need not be co-measurable in any single PVM, so POVM-additivity is a *noncontextuality postulate across imported apparatus*, not an object-native constraint. Recent critical literature makes this explicit: ["Summing to Uncertainty: On the Necessity of Additivity in Deriving the Born Rule"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06211) [VERIFIED as existing, 2026 arXiv, content unassessed beyond abstract], and Wright–Weigert, ["General Probabilistic Theories with a Gleason-type Theorem"](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2021-11-25-588/), Quantum 5, 588 (2021) [VERIFIED], which shows Gleason-type theorems exist in *non-quantum* GPTs — so a Gleason-type derivation pins the *form* (states-as-linear-functionals) without uniquely certifying quantum Born *content*.

## 2. Gleason on von Neumann algebras: the Mackey–Gleason problem

**The generalized Gleason theorem (Gleason–Christensen–Yeadon)** [VERIFIED via Frembs–Döring, ["Gleason's theorem for composite systems,"](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1751-8121/acfbcb) J. Phys. A (2023)]:

> For a von Neumann algebra N with **no direct summand of type I₂**, every finitely additive probability measure on the projection lattice P(N) extends to a **unique state** on N.

- Case attributions [VERIFIED as attributions]: Christensen, Yeadon, Paszkiewicz for the positive-measure cases; **Yeadon specifically proved the type II₁ case**: F. J. Yeadon, ["Measures on projections in W*-algebras of type II₁,"](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1112/blms/15.2.139) Bull. LMS 15 (1983) 139, and "Finitely additive measures on projections in finite W*-algebras" (Bull. LMS 1984) [VERIFIED].
- The full **bounded/signed/Banach-space-valued** version is Bunce–Wright's solution of the **Mackey–Gleason problem**: [Bull. AMS 26 (1992)](https://www.ams.org/journals/bull/1992-26-02/S0273-0979-1992-00274-4/), [arXiv:math/9204228](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9204228), and [J. London Math. Soc. 49, 133 (1994)](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1112/jlms/49.1.133) [VERIFIED]: every bounded finitely additive measure on P(N), N without type I₂ summand, extends uniquely to a bounded linear functional.
- Survey/monograph: Maeda, ["Probability measures on projections in von Neumann algebras,"](https://www.worldscientific.com/doi/abs/10.1142/S0129055X89000122) Rev. Math. Phys. 1 (1989) 235–290 [VERIFIED]; Hamhalter, *Quantum Measure Theory* (Kluwer, 2003) [VERIFIED as existing].

**Direct answer to the II₁ question:** Yes — on a type II₁ factor (no I₂ summand), every finitely additive probability measure on projections extends uniquely to a **state**; but **not necessarily a *normal* state**. Normality is equivalent to *complete additivity* of the measure [VERIFIED via Frembs–Döring: completely additive measures extend to normal states; finitely additive ones only to states]. A II₁ factor has abundant singular states, so the finitely-additive Gleason theorem alone does not force the trace or even normality.

**What the unique trace means for "Born weights" on II₁:** a II₁ factor has a **unique faithful normal tracial state** τ, and the Murray–von Neumann **dimension function** τ|P(M): P(M) → [0,1] (continuous range) is its restriction to projections — the unique normalized normal trace-induced measure ([Anantharaman–Popa, *An Introduction to II₁ Factors*](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~popa/Books/IIun.pdf); [PNAS "Prime factors"](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.93.23.12762)) [VERIFIED]. So on the object side: among all normal states, exactly one is unitarily invariant, and its Born weights on projections are the continuous dimension. "Born weights = dimension" is object-native on II₁ in the strongest possible sense; what is *not* forced by Gleason machinery alone is the selection of the trace among all normal states — that selection needs invariance (or the maximum-entropy/KMS-at-β=0 characterization).

**Type III:** the theorem applies equally (no I₂ summand), so finitely additive measures extend to states — but there is **no trace to select** (every normal semifinite trace on a type III algebra is trivial), no dimension function, and no atoms. Probability content must come from the modular/KMS structure (Section 5).

## 3. Zurek envariance (2003–2005)

- W. H. Zurek, ["Environment-Assisted Invariance, Entanglement, and Probabilities in Quantum Physics,"](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.90.120404) PRL 90, 120404 (2003) [VERIFIED]; ["Probabilities from entanglement, Born's rule p_k = |ψ_k|² from envariance,"](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevA.71.052105) PRA 71, 052105 (2005), [arXiv:quant-ph/0405161](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0405161) [VERIFIED].
- **Assumptions:** (i) system S entangled with environment E, state in Schmidt form; (ii) *envariance*: a unitary acting on S alone whose effect can be undone by a unitary on E alone leaves the S-state unchanged — for equal Schmidt coefficients, a **swap** on S is countered by a swap on E, forcing outcome probabilities equal by symmetry; (iii) unequal coefficients handled by fine-graining into equal-amplitude branches (ancilla dilation); (iv) locality/causality (E-operations can't affect S-probabilities), probabilities are functions of the local Schmidt data.
- **Scope:** derives Born weights for Schmidt-basis outcomes of measurements on entangled pairs; does *not* apply to a pure undivided system (bipartition is load-bearing) [VERIFIED via [Schlosshauer's decoherence review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0312059)].
- **Criticisms:** Schlosshauer–Fine, ["On Zurek's Derivation of the Born Rule,"](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-004-1941-6) Found. Phys. 35(2), 197–213 (2005) [VERIFIED]: hidden assumptions — that outcomes have probabilities at all, that probabilities attach to Schmidt states and depend only on amplitudes, that the fine-graining step doesn't smuggle in Born; circularity risk in using quantum formalism (unitaries, entanglement) to derive its own probability rule. Also [Caves' notes](http://info.phys.unm.edu/~caves/reports/ZurekBornderivation.pdf) and Barnum (2003), Mohrhoff (2004) [attributions VERIFIED via review]. A no-signalling reformulation exists ([arXiv:quant-ph/0312150](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0312150)) [VERIFIED].
- **Campaign-relevant reading:** envariance is a *swap-symmetry* derivation — structurally the same move as the programme's banked "degree 2 = order of a conjugation swap" for Born FORM. Zurek's swap needs the counter-swap to exist *in the environment algebra*; whether that counter-swap is object-native or imported is exactly testable at finite stages (Section 6, T3).

## 6. Finite-stage test ideas (SL(2,p) stages, p = 5, 7, 11, 13)

## Failure modes

1. **Type-I mirage:** every finite stage is type I; II₁/III statements are limit statements. A property holding at p = 5..13 can fail in L(Γ₄₁) (weak closure adds projections). Mitigation: state all finite results as "stable along the tower," never as II₁ facts.
2. **POVM-additivity smuggling:** T1/T4 can "force" Born only because effect-additivity was assumed; the critics' point (Section 1) is that this assumption is Born-adjacent. Any positive result must name which additivity axiom carried it.
3. **GPT non-uniqueness:** Wright–Weigert shows Gleason-type forcing exists in non-quantum theories — passing a frame-function test does not certify quantum Born content, only linearity of the probability functional.
4. **Envariance circularity (Schlosshauer–Fine):** T3 presupposes that probabilities exist and attach to Schmidt data; a "swap available" result shows symmetry, not probability.
5. **2026 arXiv contamination:** items 2602.09056, 2603.24619, 2603.06211, 2604.27125 are unrefereed and known here only via abstracts; do not bank against them. The interface-gap "verified negative" is a statement about what searches surfaced, not a completeness proof; the boundary-reduction paper (2604.27125) shows adjacent framings are appearing *now* — priority risk if the gap is real.
6. **Wrong-algebra risk in T2:** the nativity claim concerns the Γ₄₁-generated algebra, not C[SL(2,p)]; conflating them changes the invariant-measure simplex and could produce a spurious match or mismatch with the 1:φ² weights.
