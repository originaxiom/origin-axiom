# #1′ ANSWERED — H₁ = ℤ *is* consumed. It closes doors, and opens none.

cc3, 2026-08-10. Gate 5-Q. Structure only.

## The question

The cornerstone campaign's #1′: **find the step that consumes H₁ = ℤ (or
knot-ness in S³), or prove none can exist.** The REID probe had traced the
**positive** chain — π₁ ↠ 2T → McKay → E₆ → cascade → SM — and found knot-ness
consumed **nowhere**, concluding the derivation selects the commensurability
class rather than the object.

That probe searched the derivation. **It did not search the no-gos.** This does.

## The answer: it is consumed, and B955 says so explicitly

`frontier/B955_l133_scout/FINDINGS.md`, quoted in full:

> *"A **knot complement has H₁ = ℤ** — cyclic. So every abelian flat connection
> has **cyclic image**, generated by **one** element, whose centralizer in
> simply-connected E₆ contains a maximal torus. **For this object, abelian
> holonomy preserves rank NECESSARILY, not generically.**"*

and, under a heading that names the scope itself —

> **"## 3. A NEW CONSTRAINT — and it is specific to knots**
> *Every quotient of a knot group has cyclic abelianization. Therefore π₁(m004)
> can **never** surject onto ℤ₃×ℤ₃ or the Heisenberg group 3^{1+2} — **the
> standard non-toral rank-reducing subgroups are not importable into this
> object.**"*

**So H₁ = ℤ is load-bearing.** #1′'s first disjunct is satisfied: a step consumes
it, and the step is named.

## But the direction is the opposite of what the campaign was looking for

Read what it is consumed **for**. In both places, knot-ness is used to **close a
route**:

- abelian holonomy **preserves rank necessarily** → one route to rank reduction
  shut;
- the standard non-toral rank-reducing subgroups are **not importable** → another
  shut.

**The object's one genuinely unique property is load-bearing exclusively in
making the physics harder.** Being the arithmetic *knot* does not supply the
derivation with anything; it removes two of the mechanisms a generic manifold
could have used.

That is a sharper and less comfortable answer than either branch #1′ anticipated.
The campaign framed the question as *"if we find where H₁ = ℤ enters, the
derivation inherits Reid's uniqueness."* It enters — and what it confers is an
**obstruction**, not an inheritance.

## The corrected statement of the cornerstone position

| | |
|---|---|
| the **positive chain** (→ E₆ → SM) | consumes the **trace field** only — a commensurability-class invariant (B803). **Generic**: ~35 % of the census, 36 % of knots carry the 2T atom |
| the **no-go layer** (rank, non-toral subgroups) | consumes **H₁ = ℤ / knot-ness** — object-specific, and it **tightens** the walls |

**Net:** the specificity is real, it is consumed, and it works against the
programme's goal rather than for it. A cornerstone built on "m004 is the unique
arithmetic knot" would be building on the property that makes rank reduction
*impossible* rather than the property that produces E₆.

## What this does to #1′, and what replaces it

**#1′ is closed.** Not by proving no step consumes H₁ = ℤ, but by finding the
steps that do and reading their sign.

**The successor question, which is better posed:** if knot-ness is what forbids
non-toral rank reduction, then the rank wall is **not** a wall of the
commensurability class — it is a wall of *this manifold*. **The sister m003 has
H₁ = ℤ/5 + ℤ, which is not cyclic**, so B955's argument does not apply to it.

> **Does m003 admit the non-toral rank-reducing subgroups that m004 provably
> cannot?**

If yes, the rank wall is an artefact of choosing the knot, and the programme has
been obstructed by its own selection criterion. If no, the wall is class-level
after all and B955's knot-specific framing is stronger than it needs to be.
Either answer is worth having, and it is a bounded computation: check whether
π₁(m003) surjects onto ℤ₃×ℤ₃ or 3^{1+2}.

**Not claimed:** that m003 is a better object, or that the programme chose
wrongly. Only that the wall's scope is now a testable question rather than an
assumption, and that today's answer to #1′ makes it the natural next one.

## The answer: neither option I posed was right

I framed this as *"m004's wall or the class's?"* It is **neither**:

- **Not m004's.** Eight other manifolds share the obstruction — including its
  sister m003 and every other one-cusped member of both rows except m207.
- **Not the class's.** **m207 sits in the golden row, same trace field ℚ(√−3),
  and admits both targets.** So the wall is not a property of the
  commensurability class.

**The real condition is `3-rank(H₁) ≥ 2`.** B955's knot argument is *correct* —
knot ⟹ H₁ = ℤ ⟹ 3-rank 1 — but it is **unnecessarily narrow**: knot-ness is one
sufficient way to fail the condition, and eight non-knots fail it too, for
reasons having nothing to do with knots (ℤ/5, ℤ/2, ℤ/10 are all coprime-to-3
torsion).

**B955's framing should be widened** from *"specific to knots"* to *"any
manifold whose H₁ has 3-rank 1"*, which is the actual hypothesis its argument
needs and which covers nine of eleven class members.

## Two of my own errors, owned

**1. A live bug, caught by a sanity check.** The first run reported m003 at
3-rank 2. That is impossible — 5 is coprime to 3 — and the check caught it.
Cause: **snappy's `homology().rank()` returns the total generator count, not the
free rank** (for ℤ/5 + ℤ it returns 2). The free rank is the number of *zero*
elementary divisors. Fixed, documented in the source, and the corrected table is
above. **Every "YES" in the first run except m207 and m129 was spurious.**

**2. A wrong framing, stated to the owner.** I said *"m003's H₁ = ℤ/5 + ℤ is not
cyclic, so B955's argument does not apply to it"* and proposed m003 as **the**
sharp test. The first clause is true and the conclusion does not follow:
B955's *argument* fails to apply, but **m003 fails the actual condition anyway**,
because ℤ/5 has no 3-torsion. **I aimed the test at the wrong manifold.** The
right one was m207, which I had in a table two arcs earlier and did not read.

## What survives, and what it means

The programme's rank wall is **real for m004 and for most of its relatives, and
breakable inside its own commensurability class**. m207 — same field, same E₆,
same everything the derivation actually consumes — admits the non-toral
rank-reducing subgroups m004 provably cannot.

So the honest statement replacing the previous one:

> **Rank reduction is not blocked by the trace field, the class, or E₆. It is
> blocked by the 3-rank of H₁, which is a property of the individual manifold
> and which the derivation never consumes.** The chain lands on a manifold with
> 3-rank 1, and that is where the wall comes from — not from anything the chain
> uses to reach E₆.

**Not claimed:** that m207 gives a working physics, or that the programme should
switch objects. m207 has volume 4.06 (twice m004's), is not a knot, and nothing
here says its cascade behaves. **Only that the wall's cause is now located** —
in a manifold invariant the derivation does not touch — rather than attributed
to the class or to E₆.

# THE WALL IS THE MEMBER'S, NOT THE FAMILY'S — exact, and it turns on 3 | m

Run 2026-08-10, following the located cause upstream into the chain itself.

## The homology of every metallic bundle, in closed form

For the mapping torus of φ on the once-punctured torus,
`H₁ = ℤ ⊕ coker(φ* − I)` on `H₁(fiber) = ℤ²`. For the metallic monodromy:

```
   φ = RᵐLᵐ = [[m²+1, m], [m, 1]]        trace = m² + 2
   φ − I    = [[m²,   m], [m, 0]]        det = −m²
   Smith:   gcd of entries = m,  d₁d₂ = m²   ⟹   d₁ = d₂ = m

   ⟹   H₁ = ℤ ⊕ ℤ/m ⊕ ℤ/m
```

**Verified against SnapPy by constructing the bundles**, not by looking them up:

| m | bundle word | manifold | H₁ | volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `b++RL` | **m004** = 4₁ | ℤ | 2.029883213 |
| 2 | `b++RRLL` | m136 | ℤ/2 + ℤ/2 + ℤ | 3.663862377 |
| 3 | `b++RRRLLL` | **s464** | ℤ/3 + ℤ/3 + ℤ | 4.813819186 |
| 4 | `b++RRRRLLLL` | t03910 | ℤ/4 + ℤ/4 + ℤ | — |

The closed form holds exactly. (m136 appearing here also confirms it *is* the
silver metallic manifold, which the class table had listed without the
identification.)

## The consequence

`3-rank(H₁) = 1 + 2·[3 | m]`, so

> **the rank wall is present ⟺ 3 ∤ m.**

Tested against B955's own two targets:

| m | manifold | ℤ₃×ℤ₃ | Heisenberg 3^{1+2} |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 golden** | **m004** | **no** | **no** |

| **3 bronze** | **s464** | **YES** | **YES** |
| 4 | t03910 | no | no |

**The bronze metallic manifold admits both non-toral rank-reducing subgroups
that m004 provably cannot.**

## What this locates, and it is the whole point

The chain's own structure, from `philosophy/P000`:

> *"The family is determined; the member is contingent."*

The **family** — the metallic bundles RᵐLᵐ — is determined by the four premises.
The **member** m = 1 is selected by three criteria (systole, expansion threshold,
arithmeticity), of which P000 says plainly *"no single criterion collapses the
family to a member without importing extra structure."*

**So the rank wall is a property of the contingent choice, not of the determined
family.** It exists because m = 1, and it would not exist at m = 3.

That is a sharper statement than anything the rank-reduction literature in this
corpus contains. B952/B955/B959/B960/B962 close route after route; every one of
those closures is downstream of a choice the programme's own philosophy labels
**contingent**, and none of them says so.

## Scope — stated tightly, because this is the kind of result that gets overread

- **Computed:** the closed form `H₁ = ℤ ⊕ (ℤ/m)²`, verified on four bundles;
  the 3-rank criterion; the surjection tests, by enumeration.
- **Not claimed:** that s464 gives a working physics. **Nothing here computes its
  trace field, its cascade, or whether the rest of the chain survives at m = 3.**
  The bronze member may fail everything else — P000's three criteria pick m = 1
  for reasons unrelated to rank, and B125's arithmeticity selector is an
  {m = 1, m = 2} filter that **excludes m = 3 outright**.
- **In particular:** if arithmeticity is required, m = 3 is already out, and the
  wall is then *forced* by the selection rather than merely caused by it. **That
  is the next thing to check and it is bounded** — compute s464's invariant trace
  field and ask whether it is arithmetic.

**The honest headline is therefore conditional:** the rank wall is caused by the
member choice; whether that choice was itself forced by arithmeticity — making
the wall unavoidable after all — is not settled here.
