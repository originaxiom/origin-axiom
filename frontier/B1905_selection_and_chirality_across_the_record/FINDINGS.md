# B1905 — Selection and chirality across the whole record: what was settled, by which kind of principle, and what was forgotten

*Main, 2026-11-12 (S306p). The owner asked that evening, entered here in plain words: whether the record should be
swept once more for what it in fact settled on selection and on chirality; whether anything keeps crucial mathematics
and principles from being forgotten; and that the kinds of principle be sorted by the cases they work for, so that
the record learns from its own walls. He then asked that it be made the priority. A reading of the record by six
read-only readers, one reader an arc, with main's machine check of every quotation. The classes are the readers' and
are not independently checked. No measured value is read or compared. 0 of 19.*

## 1. The answer first

- **Neither is solved, and no page says it is.** 636 of 1,644 arcs have selection or chirality in their verdict line
  or title; 511 are about one of them on reading. Each of the six readers reports independently that no page in its
  slice claims either solved outright.
- **The record had already drawn the lesson, in two arcs of August 2026, and one of them is in no governing page.**
  - B1204 (2026-08-29) read the record's own successes: exactly two families have ever forced anything, a finite
    classification with a membership test, and a consistency condition that a candidate can violate. Both can fail;
    neither is a symmetry; a criterion that cannot fail cannot select. It is cited in a handful of later arcs and
    in none of the working rules, the foundation, the law map, the theorem registry, the plan or the closure map.
  - B1225 (2026-08-31): no symmetry that can be read off the object selects within its own canonical class. This one
    is in the foundation, the working rules, the registry and the map.
- **Main proposed this morning that rates of cancellation would select.** That is a principle of the kind with the
  worst record here, and B1204 already said why. It was refuted by computation; it should have been stopped by the
  record.
- **Which kind of principle settled what** is § 3. In one line: uniqueness, arithmetic and census settle questions
  about the object's own structure; symmetry settles what cannot be split; an extremum, a flow or a measure has not
  settled a wall.

## 4. What is not shown

- That the classes are right arc by arc: one reader each, not checked by a second.
- That nothing outside the 636 bears on the two questions: the sweep read verdict lines and titles.
- Whether the one success of a consistency condition on a continuum (the hypercharge, B1160, as B1204 reads it) has
  a counterpart for the hand. **Read before it was asked as new (the rule of § 5):** the record holds failable
  conditions that touch the hand, and at the object's symmetric points each of them forbids it. B1674: if the moves
  act on the fermions as a symmetry, the mixed anomaly with colour vanishes exactly when they act on left- and
  right-handed quarks alike, and the one with the weak group never vanishes (binding if that group is gauged). B1296:
  under the theta-equivariance, every face of the chamber is vector-like or anomalous in the colour cubic, never
  chiral and anomaly-free. B1340: the one coefficient that can fail is the cubic of a direction that is not the same
  for every family. B864: hypercharge is the one abelian direction that can be gauged over chiral matter. So the
  question is not whether such a condition exists. It is which symmetry has to be absent for a hand to be allowed,
  and whether its absence follows from the object or is chosen (B1298 prices the road without the equivariance at
  the lift, I-28).
