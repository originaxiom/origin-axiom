# B1989 card — listening 6: the whole's bound states over ℂ, the finite orbits of the moves and the frustration levels they sit at

Written 2026-11-25, before any run, unattended, on the listening line. Data-free. 0 of 19.

## Why

- **B1988:** the one self-consistent outcome is the common point (κ = −2). Values, if any, live in the flow away from
  it.
- **B1983:** over 𝔽_p the whole leaves small orbits on many leaves. Over ℂ, an orbit of the whole that closes up is a
  bound state: a finite set of readings the moves only permute.
- **The question, defined by behaviour:** which finite orbits does the whole have, and at which frustration levels κ?
  Those levels would be numbers the whole fixes, with nothing chosen.

## The system and the method

- **The moves** L: (x, y, z) ↦ (x, z, xz − y) and R: (x, y, z) ↦ (z, y, yz − x) on the readings
  (tr a, tr b, tr ab), as in B1983 to B1988.
- **A source of finite orbits that is certain:** a representation of the two records into a finite group has finitely
  many images under the moves, so its reading has a finite orbit.
  - The method takes every pair (a, b) in each finite subgroup of SU(2): Q₈, the binary tetrahedral 2T (24), the binary
    octahedral 2O (48) and the binary icosahedral 2I (120), with exact entries in a cyclotomic field.
  - The binary dihedral groups are left out: they form infinite families.
  - Readings are exact algebraic numbers, and orbits come from the moves themselves.
- **Not claimed:** that these are all the finite orbits. The classification of finite orbits is in the literature, cited
  from memory and not checked.

## What is watched (described)

- **For each group:** the distinct readings of its pairs; the moves' orbits on them; each orbit's size and κ.
- **The set of κ values carrying a bound state,** and which fields they live in.
