## 1. The question

**L242 (b), made exact.** Let M_n be the n-fold cyclic cover of m004 and π_n = F ⋊ ⟨z = mⁿ⟩ its group.
- **A vacuum** of the harmonic family on M_n is V = ν ⊗ ρ_q restricted to π_n. Here q > 0 with q ≠ 1, and ν = (ν_F, λ) is a
  character of H₁(M_n) = ℤz ⊕ T_n: ν_F a character of T_n = coker(Φⁿ − 1), λ = ν(z) ∈ ℂ*.
- At an exceptional point the split sum V ⊕ L is a vacuum too. It is fixed exactly when V is (Krull–Schmidt, with L determined
  by ν).
- **The question:** for which (n, ν) is V fixed by a count-odd map?
- **All allowed states.** The harmonic family off its hyperbolic point is on record only for m004 (GENESIS v1.3 §8). Lemma U
  says what holds at the hyperbolic point on every state. The family on other states is registered as the next arc (§6).

## 2. Definitions

**The symmetries.** Isom(M_n) consists of lifts of m004's eight isometries: SnapPy gives 8n for n ≤ 8, and the lifts are 8n.
They are generated by four automorphisms of π, given as words (B1512's table):
- ι: m ↦ mnm⁻¹, n ↦ mn⁻¹mnm⁻¹;
- ε: m ↦ mn⁻¹m⁻¹, n ↦ mnm⁻¹n⁻¹m⁻¹;
- α: m ↦ mnm⁻¹, n ↦ nmn⁻¹m⁻¹n²m⁻¹;
- τ = conjugation by m (the deck).

In the fibre model (B1511's basis x = nm⁻¹, y = mnm⁻², Φ = [[0, −1], [1, 3]]), characters are row vectors v = (a, b) mod N, and the
pullback is v ↦ vB, λ ↦ λ^σ:

| map | B | σ | ρ_q∘β | d (dualises) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ι | −I | +1 | ρ_q | 0 |
| ε | S = [[0, 1], [1, 0]] | −1 | ρ_{1/q} | 1 |
| α | M = [[2, 1], [−1, −1]] | +1 | ρ_{1/q} | 1 |
| τ | Φ | +1 | ρ_q | 0 |

**Count-odd maps.** V ↦ (V∘β)* for β with d = 1 (L1, L2: the index changes sign). They may be followed by complex conjugation of
the twist, which is count-even and allowed because ρ_q is real.

## 3. Lemmas (proved now)

**Lemma Z (no cross term).** For each of the four maps, the fibre part of β(z) lies in im(Φⁿ − 1). So in the splitting
H₁ = ℤz ⊕ T_n, β(z) = z^σ exactly, and ν∘β = (ν_F∘B, λ^σ).
- *Proof.* The fibre parts are ±(1 + Φ^{±1} + ⋯ + Φ^{±(n−1)})e for e ∈ {e_x, e_y}, up to a power of Φ. Since Φ − 1 ∈ GL₂(ℤ),
  (1 + ⋯ + Φⁿ⁻¹)ℤ² = im(Φⁿ − 1) (B1512 (iii)).
- *Independent check:* route R computes the cross term c of β(z) on its own and finds c = 0 on every level n ≤ 12 (C4).

**Lemma C (the criterion).** For q ≠ 1, V(ν, q) is fixed by a count-odd map exactly when:
- **(E)** some element with d = 1 and σ = −1 has vB = v, when λ is not on the unit circle (generic or real λ);
- **(E or A)** that, or some element with d = 1 and σ = +1 has vB = v, when |λ| = 1 (λ = ±1 included).

*Proof.*
- Non-dualising β with dualising: (V∘β)* ≅ (ν∘β)⁻¹ ⊗ ρ_{1/q}. This is never a vacuum at q, because the longitude's eigenvalues
  {q, q, q, q⁻³} and {q⁻¹, q⁻¹, q⁻¹, q³} differ for q ≠ 1, and every character kills the longitude.
- Dualising β: (V∘β)* ≅ (ν∘β)⁻¹ ⊗ ρ_q. It is fixed iff ν∘β = ν⁻¹, i.e. vB = −v and λ^σ = λ⁻¹.
- With complex conjugation: iff vB = v and λ^{−σ} = λ̄.
- For λ off the unit circle only σ = −1 survives. For |λ| = 1 both σ do.
- ι (B = −I, count-even, σ = +1) commutes with all and absorbs the sign of B, so "vB = ±v" may be read as "vB = v".

**Lemma G (golden, after B1279 and B1303).** charpoly(M) = t² − t − 1, M² = Φ⁻¹, SMS = −M⁻¹ and SΦS = Φ⁻¹ (C7). So:
- on T_n ⊗ ℤ_p with p split, M acts on two eigenlines by φ_p and φ̄_p = −φ_p⁻¹, and **S exchanges them**;
- the group is {±M^j S^e}, j mod 2n;
- the count-odd elements are of two kinds:
  - the **golden Galois reflections** ±M^{2k}S (σ = −1, d = 1), each exchanging φ with φ̄;
  - the **golden rotations** ±M^{2k+1} (σ = +1, d = 1), each preserving every eigenline.
- At generic λ only the reflections act. So a vacuum at generic λ loses every count-odd symmetry iff its twist is invariant
  under no golden Galois reflection. In that sense its twist chooses a golden sheet.

**Lemma SP (split primes).** At a split prime p | |T_n|, a character whose p-primary part is a nonzero vector on one eigenline is
fixed by no reflection: the reflection carries that part to the other line, and the two lines meet only in 0 because 5 is a unit
mod p. So it is unfixed at generic λ. Hence unfixed vacua at generic λ exist on every level whose T_n has a split prime: for
n ≤ 12, n = 5, 7, 9, 10, 11.

**Lemma F (closed counts).** Let n be odd and p = L_n a split prime: n = 5, 7, 11 give p = 11, 29, 199, and T_n = F_p².
Write a character as v = a·u + b·uS, with u on the φ-line. Then:
- the reflections fix v iff a/b ∈ H = ±⟨φ̄²⟩, of order 2n;
- the rotations fix a single-line v always, and a mixed v never, because φ² has odd order n.

So, with no other input:
- unfixed at generic λ = (p − 1)(p + 1 − 2n);
- unfixed at unitary λ = (p − 1)(p − 1 − 2n).

The values are **n = 5: 20 and 0; n = 7: 448 and 392; n = 11: 35 244 and 34 848.**

**Lemmas L1, L2.** T₁ = 0, so every vacuum on M₁ is fixed (sm:B1520). T₂ = ℤ/5, ramified: every symmetry acts as ±1, so every
character is fixed by a reflection.

**Lemma U (every allowed state, at the hyperbolic point).** Let M be any cusped hyperbolic word state, ρ₁ its holonomy into
SO(3,1) ⊂ SL₄(ℝ), and ν unitary. Then (ν ⊗ ρ₁)* ≅ ν̄ ⊗ ρ₁ = conj(ν ⊗ ρ₁), since ρ₁ is real and preserves a form. So the
dualising-then-conjugating map, which is count-odd, fixes every unitary vacuum at the hyperbolic point of every state. This is
B1297 §5.1's unitary remark: there is nothing to select there. Off the hyperbolic point a family must first exist (§6).

**The order over a firing vacuum (cited, not recomputed).** At every firing member, both orders exist over the same split
vacuum V ⊕ L, and they count opposite signs: W₁ (V the sub) and W₂ (L the sub), I(W₁) = −I(W₂) (sm:B1511, sm:B1512). This is
main's L241, B1438's slope law. So **no vacuum selects a chirality, whatever its stabiliser.** This arc decides where the
count-odd mirror breaks, not whether a chirality is selected.
