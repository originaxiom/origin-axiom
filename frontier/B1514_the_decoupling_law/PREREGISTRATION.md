## 3. Proved now (design time)

**Lemma 1 (the cusp).**
- ℓ is a commutator in F, so every fibre character is trivial on it, and ρ_q(ℓ) has eigenvalues q, q, q, q⁻³ (B1513 Lemma 1).
- Every case-(b) λ lies in μ₄, so L(t) = λ⁻⁴ = 1 and L is trivial on P.
- Hence on P:
  - V, V*, V_η and V ⊗ L have ℓ-eigenvalues among q^{±1} and q^{∓3};
  - Λ²V and Λ²V* have q^{±2};
  - Λ²W₁ is filtered by Λ²V and V ⊗ L, and Λ²W₁* by their duals.
  None of these has an eigenvalue 1 when q, q², q³ ≠ 1, which D3 checks at every reading. So all of them are acyclic on P. L, W₁ and
  W₁* are not.
- Poincaré–Lefschetz duality therefore pairs H¹(M; Λ²W₁*) = H¹(M, ∂M; Λ²W₁*) perfectly with H²(M; Λ²W₁), and likewise with the
  stars exchanged.
- So B′ vanishes for every 5̄′_H iff [a ∧ a′] = 0 in H²(Λ²W₁) for all a, a′ ∈ H¹(W₁). Likewise B vanishes for every 5′_H iff
  [f ∧ f′] = 0 in H²(Λ²W₁*).

**Lemma 2 (the 10̄′ lives in the sub).** At a firing member, every 10̄′ class has a representative with values in V ⊂ W₁.
- H⁰(L) = 0, since L is non-trivial on F, and H¹(L) = ℂx (banked h¹(L) = 1).
- The connecting map H¹(L) → H²(V) is x ↦ ±x ∪ c, which is non-zero (banked I(W₁) = +1, B1511 Theorem A).
- In 0 → H¹(V) → H¹(W₁) → H¹(L) → H²(V) the last map is injective. So H¹(V) → H¹(W₁) is an isomorphism.

**Lemma 3 (the sub-wedge lemma).**
- Take a, a′ ∈ H¹(W₁) with representatives a = i∘a_V and a′ = i∘a′_V (Lemma 2).
- V is a submodule, so g·a′(h) ∈ V. Hence a ∪_∧ a′ takes values in Λ²V ⊂ Λ²W₁, and equals i_*(a_V ∪_∧ a′_V).
- So [a ∧ a′] is the image of [a_V ∧ a′_V] ∈ H²(M; Λ²V).
- **If H²(M; Λ²V) = 0, every 10̄′·10̄′·5̄′_H coupling of the member vanishes,** whatever 5̄′_H classes exist. (Lemma 1 turns the class
  statement into one about the coupling.)
- *Across an orbit (remark, not used for a claim).* Suppose Hom_{G_n}(V_i ⊗ V_j, V_k ⊗ L_k) = 0. Then a form's map W_i ⊗ W_j → Λ²W_k
  sends V_i ⊗ V_j into Λ²V_k, and the cross coupling factors through H²(Λ²V_k) in the same way. The census computes cross couplings
  directly.

**Lemma 4 (the Higgs filtration).** Λ²W₁ sits in 0 → Λ²V → Λ²W₁ → V ⊗ L → 0, with V ⊗ L = ν⁻³ ⊗ ρ_q. Its t-value is λ⁻³ = λ,
since λ⁴ = 1.
- **M₄ (ν³ = 1 on F, λ = 1).** V ⊗ L = ρ_q on M₄, and Shapiro gives H*(M₄; ρ_q) = ⊕_{μ⁴=1} H*(m004; μ ⊗ ρ_q).
  - B1509 T3's polynomial Q(q, μ) at μ = 1, −1, ±i is −(q − 1)²/q, (q² − 34q + 1)/(−q) and (q² − 14q + 1)/q.
  - At q² − 7q + 1 = 0 these numerators are 5q, −27q and −7q, none zero. So V ⊗ L is acyclic.
  - **If Λ²V is acyclic, Λ²W₁ is acyclic: no Higgs class on M₄.**
- **M₆ (ν⁸ = 1).** ν⁻³ = ν⁵ and λ⁻³ = λ⁵ (λ = ±1), so V ⊗ L = V_η, whose H¹ is ℂc (banked).
  - **If Λ²V is acyclic, H¹(Λ²W₁) ≅ H¹(V_η) = ℂc:** one 5′_H, the lift of the background's own class.
- **M₅ (ν¹¹ = 1, Φ-eigenline).** The orbits are ν^{κʲ} with κ of order 5 mod 11, over the quadratic residues {1, 3, 4, 5, 9}.
  - So ν_F⁻³ (exponent 8, a non-residue) lies in the deck orbit of ν_F⁻¹ = ν_F∘ι (B1512 Lemma I). For example ν_F = (1, 5) gives
    ν_F⁻³ = (8, 7), in orbit 19 with ν_F⁻¹ = (10, 6).
  - The deck and ι are automorphisms of G_n that fix λ and preserve ρ_q up to conjugacy. So h*(V ⊗ L) = h*(V): 1, or 2 at w = 7.
  - **If Λ²V is acyclic, H¹(Λ²W₁) ≅ H¹(V ⊗ L) has dimension h¹(V).** These classes come from the member's image under ι and the
    deck, not from its own c.
- **In every case** h¹(Λ²W₁*) = h¹(Λ²W₁), by Lemma 1, duality and χ = 0.

**Lemma 5 (the deck and ι).**
- The members of one orbit agree on every reading: the deck is conjugation by m, which fixes λ and preserves ρ_q.
- A class's two orbits, ν and ν⁻¹, agree by ι.

**Lemma 6 (the sector count).**
- n(W₁) − n(W₁*) = I(W₁) = +1 at every member (banked), and n(W₁) = h¹(V) (Lemma 2 and the banked r₁ = 0).
- That is one 10̄′ at a simple point. At M₅'s double point it is two 10̄′ and one 10′: a vector-like pair plus the chiral state.
- B′ is read on all of H¹(W₁) = H¹(V), so it covers every 10̄′ class, chiral or paired.
