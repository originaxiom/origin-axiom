# B1438 — ADDENDUM 2 (2026-10-01): the slope law is the first order of a curve of flat connections

B1444 finds what the slope law is the first order of. The reducible non-split representation that a background
carries is the end of a curve of irreducible flat PSL(2) connections of the level — a twisted-periodic curve of the
trace map through the torsion point of the extension character on κ = 2 — and along that curve:

- **the slope is the cusp shape at the end.** Theorem D's "cusp shape of the affine representation" is the limit of
  the cusp shape of irreducible representations (146 of 146 backgrounds of a sealed run; a theorem).
- **the coupling is a first-order torsion.** The torsion of the sector with characters α = ℓβ, β is
  (s(α) − s(ℓ))(s(β) − s(ℓ))·e + O(e²), e = 2 − κ (1 223 of 1 223; a theorem).
- **the firing condition (Theorem A) is the vanishing of that term**, and the index it counts is zero at every other
  point of the curve: it is a property of the reducible end.

**One simplification of this arc's statements.** s(χ⁻¹) = s(χ) (s is real and commutes with complex conjugation;
checked on 164 characters of seven levels in B1444). So Theorem A reads

    I(ℓ, α) = [s(α) = s(ℓ)] − [s(β) = s(ℓ)],

and the β⁻¹ of the text above and of B1443 can be read as β. Nothing computed changes.

Nothing here is withdrawn.
