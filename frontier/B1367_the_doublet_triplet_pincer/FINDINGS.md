G ⊗ S_D and G ⊗ S_T with the same generation matrices G = 6λ·⟨N⟩, G_ν = 6λ·⟨ν^c⟩ and unit single-27 structure constants: their
generation-ranks are equal, so light up-Higgs doublets = light exotic triplets, in every vacuum, for every coupling pattern, at every
order (higher operators are E₆-invariant too). A light D has the cubic's diquark and leptoquark couplings (B1276 §3) and proton decay
needs it above 10¹⁵ GeV; a light Higgs needs it light. The pincer closes unless something breaks E₆ on the matter itself. A Wilson

## 2. The theorem

*Let the E₆-charged fields be n copies of the 27 (and any number of 27̄s) with a superpotential whose couplings are E₆-invariant up to
holonomy factors acting on whole 27s. In every vacuum in which only the Standard-Model singlets N_g, ν^c_g take VEVs, the number of
light up-type Higgs doublets equals the number of light exotic colour triplets D, and each ν^c VEV pairs as many lepton doublets with
up-Higgs doublets as it pairs D with d^c. Hence a vacuum with a light Higgs has a light D.*

*Proof.* The doublet and triplet blocks are G ⊗ S_D + G_ν ⊗ S^ν_D and G ⊗ S_T + G_ν ⊗ S^ν_T with identical generation matrices G, G_ν
(the same λ contracted with the same VEVs), and the single-27 structure constants are unit maps H_u → H_d, H_u → L, D → D̄, D → d^c
(the table): the generation-rank of [G | G_ν] governs both. 27̄s do not couple to 27·27 (no E₆ invariant), higher operators are
E₆-invariant, and a holonomy factor is one E₆ element on all components of 27_g, a change of basis within each generation that
preserves both ranks. ∎

The light D couples through det Q, det Q^c and Tr(Q L Q^c) to quarks and leptons with the cubic's coefficient (B1276 §3's diquark and
leptoquark rows), so with the deck's order-one coupling a TeV-scale D gives τ_p ~ 10⁻¹² s; the observed lifetime needs m_D ≳ 10¹⁵ GeV.

## 3. What it means

1. **Every apex design of the object is excluded**, independently of the neutrino sector, of 27̄s and of the line: point-localised
   27s are full 27s with E₆-symmetric couplings, and the pincer has no gap. The three-apex closing (B1355–B1364) and its anti-apex
   extensions (L215) fall together. This is the strongest of the three exclusions and the one that explains the other two.
2. **B1276 §2 is a theorem, not a tree-level tension**; B1283 §3's "one light Higgs pair, one light D pair" and B1302's contrast
   (Y₁₂'s one-triplet vacua, where the line acts on the h¹ matter) are its two faces.
3. **The door.** Splitting needs E₆-breaking on the matter (a line acting on bulk modes, B1302); chirality needs an index that closed
   closings cannot supply (B1351); the two meet only for chiral bulk matter, i.e. on the cusped object through its ends — B1351's escape
   (a cusp-fixed weight with a disc-type partition), empty on the V₁₀ deformations (B1352) but not yet asked for Standard-Model-reaching
   connections of the object itself, and the physical-bridge lane's conditional net three from the ends. Registered as L216.
4. The three faces: the identity of the two blocks is the arithmetic of one E₆ invariant; the point-localisation is the geometry of
   the apex; the pincer is the quantum theory's mass matrix.

## 4. Caveats

1. The couplings are taken E₆-invariant up to whole-27 holonomy factors, which is what point-localisation gives; a coupling that acts
   differently on the components of one 27 would need an E₆-breaking field at the apex, and the design has none.
2. Soft supersymmetry-breaking terms give the light D a mass at the soft scale — that is the "light" of the theorem, and it is what the
   proton-decay bound forbids.
3. The bulk of B1364's line carries a vector-like triplet pair and no doublet (B1365), so it cannot supply the Higgs either.
