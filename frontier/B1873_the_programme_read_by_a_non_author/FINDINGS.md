  package; the order of work has no circle and respects every dependency the map records; the budget's totals are
  what its file says.
- **Its one cause is real.** The programme's rule for sealing a value is that nothing open may lie upstream of it.
  "Upstream" is read from the map's edges. Three ledgers were not joined to the map: the audit's findings, the open
  debts, and the unearned identifications. So the rule could not be evaluated where it matters.
- **Repaired now, each with a refusal in the builder and a planted control:**
  1. every finding names what it bears on, and a finding that names nothing is refused;
  2. the sealed card waits on every package that holds something no single value's list can show: a finding about
     the repository as a whole, or a debt the map joins to nothing;
  3. a package marked started while a package it cannot be worked before has not started is refused;
  4. "fixed" stays main's word, and the re-audit's verdict is a separate field; a finding the re-audit did not accept
     cannot be marked fixed.
- **Corrected in main's own record:** one finding main had marked fixed is partly fixed (the correction was not
  carried to four places); one finding main had half-contradicted stands in full (the script the foundation cites
  does fail); one input was one choice where main's own newest arc counts two; the count of exits on the table was
  12 and is 8.
- **Owed, and registered (P-773):** the debts and the unearned identifications joined to the map; every bit and
  discrete choice carrying its alternatives; one price printed instead of three.

suite, the look ledger, the nulls of the comparison, the verdict files' fields. Thirty-nine name arcs, nodes of the
map or pages. The first kind cannot be shown clean by any one value's list of what it uses, so the packages that
hold them must be done before any seal. The second kind is what the per-value certificate will read.

marks fixed are accepted there, three of them with residuals that are a debt of their own (P-772).
