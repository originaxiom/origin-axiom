# Addendum (2026-11-10, B1859) — the uniqueness clause is false at m = 4; the bound stands

**Not an edit of the arc. A correction of one clause, carried from the sweep of 2026-10-23 (P-318) and verified at B1859.**

## What this arc proved, and what it identified

- **Proved, and standing:** |SL(2, ℤ/N)| is a McKay order only for N ∈ {3, 4, 5}; the metallic shadow modulus is
  m² + 4; the two meet at m = 1.
- **Identified without saying so:** that the grammar's own-conductor shadow is all of SL(2, ℤ/(m² + 4)).
- **That identification fails when gcd(m, 4) > 1.** The shadow is the group the two letters Rᵐ and Lᵐ generate. At
  m = 2 it has order 32, not 384 (B1019). At m = 4 the letters are trivial mod 4 and generate SL(2, 5) mod 5: the
  shadow is 2I, the same E₈ door as the golden.

## The corrected statement

- **The door set is {1, 4}, for every m** (B1859 § 2: three branches, checked on m ≤ 60).
- **The golden is unique among primitive grammars** (m odd), where this arc's bound applies as written.
- **The golden is the only metallic grammar with both the own-conductor door and a quotient of π₁ onto 2T.** π₁ of the
  bundle of R⁴L⁴ has none (B1859 § 3).

## What stands and what is fenced

- **Stands:** the bound, its completeness by proof, the verdict PROVED, and the earlier addendum on N = 4.
- **Fenced:** "the unique metallic grammar whose own-conductor shadow is a McKay group" now reads "the unique primitive
  one". "Uniqueness over an infinite family with a proof" holds for the primitive family.
- **The class of the error:** a proxy for the object's own group (the ambient group in place of the generated one).
