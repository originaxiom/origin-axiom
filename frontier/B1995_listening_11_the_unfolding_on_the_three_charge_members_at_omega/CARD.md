## Why

- **B1994:** ω's own symmetry, the move LR⁻¹, tells the three apart. On the triplet around the common point it has
  eigenvalues 1, e^{2πi/3} and e^{−2πi/3}: one neutral member (along (1, 1, 1)), two charged.
- **B1986:** the whole's unweighted unfolding on the triplet is W₊ = dL + dR, with eigenvalues 1 and (1 ± i√7)/2, and
  W± with inverses has eigenvalues 2, 2 and 0.
- **The question:** what does the unfolding do to each charge member? Does it keep charge, which persists most, and
  how do its own modes sit against the charges?

## The checks (exact, sympy)

- **U1:** does W₊ commute with ω's symmetry s = d(LR⁻¹)? Does W±?
- **U2:** W₊ and W± written in the charge basis (s's eigenvectors): the diagonal entries (each member's
  self-persistence per move) and the off-diagonal ones (charge mixing).
- **U3:** the overlaps |⟨charge member | mode⟩|² between the charge members and W₊'s and W±'s eigenvectors.
- **U4:** the long-run share of each charge member: the limit of |⟨c|(W/‖top‖)ⁿ|c⟩| as n grows, for W± (symmetric,
  so the limit exists).
