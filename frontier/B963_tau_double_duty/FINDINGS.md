  *This is why the 27 is complex.*

> **So the same τ both certifies that the 27 is complex and is the only involution that can

## 2. Is it an uncertainty principle? **No.**

| an uncertainty relation needs | what we have |
|---|---|
| a conjugate pair (non-commuting / Fourier-dual) | **one object with two roles** |
| a quantitative bound ΔA·ΔB ≥ c | **a dichotomy — no bound, no trade-off curve** |
| both meaningful, neither sharp | **mutually exclusive outcomes** |
| a state space carrying the variances | **none** |

There is no "rank 5, half-chiral." Reality type is **discrete** (Frobenius–Schur ±1, 0), so
there is no continuous *amount* of chirality to trade against rank. The resonance is
suggestive and the phrase would immediately start doing work it has not earned — which is
what the firewall is for. **Recorded as a rejected analogy, with the mechanism kept.**

## 3. THE SCOPE CORRECTION — what I nearly banked, and why it was wrong

I was about to bank the sharper-sounding claim: **"the object has no intermediate regime"** —
that any chirality at all forces full E₆ closure, so there is no middle ground. The owner
asked whether we were rushing. **We were.**

**What B576 actually says**, read carefully:

> *"Along any integrable deformation **of the geometric representation** with a nonzero θ-odd
> component, the Zariski closure is forced to full E₆(ℂ)."*

Its mechanism is the **block-sum lemma**, which works precisely because such deformations
**contain the principal sl₂** (whose centralizer in e₆ is zero, with multiplicity-one
isotypic blocks). So:

- **HOLDS for:** deformations containing the principal sl₂.
- **NOT established for:** arbitrary representations of π₁ into E₆ — and specifically **not**
  for the **finite-image** representations (A₄, D₅, S₅) that L133 was about, since a finite
  image contains no principal sl₂ at all.

**Does the conclusion survive? Yes — but by a different argument.** The finite-image case is
closed **independently** by B959's torality step (elementary abelian rank ≤ 2 ⟹ toral ⟹
rank 6), not by any threshold. **So the no-go stands; the unified "no intermediate regime"
framing does not.** Two arguments, not one, and saying otherwise would have been a
false unification — the most attractive kind of error this programme makes.

**It solves a meta-problem (why one class of attempts kept failing), not the problem.**
