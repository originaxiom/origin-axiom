## 0. Seen from above

The generated state space (B1384) makes every state reached by native moves part of the architecture. The owner asks whether
several of them, taken together, can supply the missing ingredient. "Together" can mean only three things:
- one state made from several (a join);
- one state that carries another's data exactly (a cover: pullback and induction, B1384 S3's Shapiro identity);

## 1. The frame, and what "together" can mean

- **The ingredient (B1368, B1369, B1370).** A spin-0 sector cusp-fixed on cusp c has its Higgs class in ann(P_c) ⊂ H¹(M; ℝ). If
  P_c has full rank the character is of finite order, unitary and vector-like, so a chiral half-generation needs a free cusp. There
  the sign of the leading Fourier shell F of the harmonic form partitions the torus into ∂⁺ = {F > 0} and ∂⁻, and the cusp
  contributes N = −χ(∂⁺).
  - An isometry fixing c that negates the class gives N = 0 (B1369's parity).
  - A single-direction leading shell gives an annulus and N = 0 (B1370).
  - The spin-½ half has its own conditions (B1372, B1373). This arc addresses the spin-0 half only.
- **The native arrows (B1384 §1, the web seats' transition table).**
  - Word extension and concatenation: reachability.
  - Covers: refinement.
  - Decks and isometry quotients: symmetry.
  - Fillings: closure, which makes the manifold closed and every closed closing vector-like (B1351).
- **Only one arrow is an exact transport of physical data between states: the cover.** Flat connections pull back, and Shapiro's
  lemma identifies cohomology on a cover with induced cohomology below (B1384 S3). Concatenation builds a new state, but no theorem
  of the record carries a connection or an index from M(u) and M(v) to M(uv).

## 3. The statements

**T1 (no free cusp on a word state).** Let φ ∈ SL(2, ℤ) be hyperbolic and M_{±φ} the once-punctured-torus bundle. Then
H₁(M) = ℤ ⊕ ℤ²/(±φ − I)ℤ², with det(±φ − I) = 2 ∓ tr φ ≠ 0, so b₁ = 1. The puncture gives one cusp. Its peripheral subgroup is
generated by λ = ∂F = [a, b], which is 0 in H₁, and a transverse loop μ mapping onto the base ℤ; its image has rank 1 = b₁, so the
cusp is not free. Concatenation (M(uv) from M(u), M(v)) and the fibre-cyclic covers (M(wᵏ)) are word states. *The word architecture,
under its own joins, never contains a free cusp.* Proof as written; 154/154 checked (S1).

**T2 (the swap is the mirror).**
1. P L P = R, and P reverses the fibre's orientation. So conjugation by P identifies M(swap w) with M(w) by an orientation-reversing
   map.
2. For A ∈ SL(2), A⁻¹ = J Aᵀ J⁻¹ with J ∈ SL(2, ℤ), and w(L, R)ᵀ = rev(swap w). So M(rev swap w) ≅ M(φ⁻¹) ≅ −M(w).
3. Combining 1 and 2, M(rev w) ≅ M(w), orientation kept.
4. Hence M(w) is amphichiral when swap(w) ~ w or swap(w) ~ rev(w) cyclically. The converse is the standard one, from b₁ = 1 (the
   fibration is unique up to isotopy and sign) and the correspondence between hyperbolic conjugacy classes and cyclic words.
5. Both directions were checked against SnapPy's symmetry groups on 154/154 states (S2).

*On the word states the mirror is P, and a chiral word state and its mirror are a P-torsor, like LR and RL (B1384 §1, the
invariant-selection theorem).*

**T3 (the divide, to length 8; computed).**
- The word states whose invariant trace field is ℚ(√−3) are exactly ±(LR)ᵏ, k = 1…4, all P-fixed. That is m004's field and the
  founding ratio's (K021).
- No chiral word state lies in m004's commensurability class.
- The arithmetic word states are those listed in S4. Every one has integral traces and an integral volume ratio to its Bianchi
  orbifold (S3, S3b).
- m010 and m009 are arithmetic over ℚ(√−7), commensurable with PSL(2, O₇) at volume ratio 3.

Beyond length 8: Bowditch–Maclachlan–Reid (Math. Ann. 302 (1995), Thm 4.2, as quoted in arXiv:1408.0348 Thm 5.1) prove that the
arithmetic mapping tori of a punctured surface fall into finitely many cyclic-commensurability classes. The divide can therefore fail
at greater length only through finitely many primitive words. To length 8 the primitive arithmetic words are LR, L²R and L²R²
(d = 3, 7, 1), and none of them breaks it. The direct test "every shape in ℚ(ω)" (S4) is independent of the degree certification, so it
covers the 24 uncertified states too.

**T4 (the joins).** The invariant trace field is a commensurability invariant (Maclachlan–Reid, *The Arithmetic of Hyperbolic
3-Manifolds*, §3.3). Two consequences:
- No state with a field other than ℚ(√−3) has a common finite cover with m004. By T3 this includes every chiral word state to
  length 8, and m010.
- The only exact transport (pullback and induction along covers, B1384 S3) therefore joins m004's data only to members of its own
  class. That class contains all of B1186's family, all covers of m004 and m003, and every cover of the Bianchi orbifold
  PSL(2, O₃) \ ℍ³, which is reached from m004 by a cover followed by an isometry quotient.

**Lemma L1 (the Eisenstein cusp lemma).** Suppose:
- a free cusp has a hexagonal lattice;
- an isometry fixing the cusp acts on its torus with linear part of order 3 (or 6, then through its square);
- the Higgs class is fixed by that isometry. This is forced if the class spans an invariant line, since an order-3 map has no
  eigenvalue −1.

Then in coordinates centred at the rotation's fixed point the leading shell is F = 2|c| Σⱼ cos(2πkⱼ·y + φ), with k₁ + k₂ + k₃ = 0.
- Its critical points are three extrema of value 3cos(φ + 2πm/3) and three saddles of value −cos 3φ.
- Hence χ(∂⁺) = #{m : cos(φ + 2πm/3) > 0} − 3·[cos 3φ < 0], which is **+1 if |φ mod 2π/3| < π/6 and −1 otherwise**.
- So N = ∓1, except at six phases, provided c ≠ 0 and no fixer's translation part kills the shell.

Topologically, the rotation has no fixed line on H₁(T²), so an invariant region is never a union of essential annuli.

An isometry negating the class forces φ = ±π/2, the degenerate phase, consistent with B1369's parity. *In B1370's cusps a non-zero N
needed a Fourier coefficient to vanish; at an Eisenstein cusp N ≠ 0 whenever the leading coefficient does not vanish.*

**Lemma L2 (the transfer lemma).** Let an isometry R fix cusp c and rotate its torus by order 3. Then every R-invariant class f in
H¹(M; ℚ) vanishes on P_c, because 1 + R + R² = 0 on H₁(T_c) gives 3f(x) = f(x + Rx + R²x) = 0. So the admissible Higgs classes of L1
are exactly V = H¹(M; ℚ)^R = H¹(M/⟨R⟩; ℚ), the first Betti number of the rotation's quotient orbifold. Proof as written; asserted
on every rotation cusp computed (S8).

**Lemma L3 (the global parity).** Let g be an isometry of M, preserving or reversing orientation, with g*[ω] = −[ω] for the Higgs
class. Then the total index vanishes: N = −χ(∂⁺M) = 0, where ∂⁺M is the union of the positive regions over every cusp on which the
sector is cusp-fixed.
- The harmonic representative is natural, so g*ω = −ω.
- g maps the cusps where the sector is cusp-fixed onto themselves, since ann(P_{g(c)}) corresponds to ann(P_c).
- g carries the partition at c onto the negated partition at g(c), so χ(∂⁺_{g(c)}) = χ(∂⁻_c).
- Summing, and using χ(∂⁺_c) + χ(∂⁻_c) = 0 at each cusp (transverse zero sets), gives χ(∂⁺M) = χ(∂⁻M) = −χ(∂⁺M).

B1369's region-swap lemma is the case g(c) = c. L3 is the invariant-selection principle (B1384 §1) applied to the index: a non-zero N
selects a sign, and a symmetry exchanging the signs forbids the selection. Orientation-preserving isometries that fix the class
instead carry N_c to N_{g(c)} with the same sign.

**Corollary (only m004's class).**
- For an arithmetic cusped manifold with field ℚ(√−d), every cusp shape lies in ℚ(√−d). A hexagonal cusp has shape ≡ ω, so it
  requires d = 3.
- Among arithmetic classes, L1's mechanism exists only in m004's class. Square cusps, with a two-direction and degenerate first
  shell, occur only for d = 1.
- On the ℚ(√−7) ladder (S5's free cusps) the leading shells are one- or two-directional, annular or degenerate, so N = 0
  generically.

**The pilot (computed).** In B1186's family and in the covers searched (S8), every free cusp with an order-3 rotation is closed in
one of two ways:
- the rotation quotient has b₁ = 0 (V = 0);
- or V ≠ 0 and an isometry of M negates it (L3).

At a census cusp that isometry is a mirror of the cusp. The locally open cusps found are on two members, ocube06_08812 (S9: chiral)
and ocube06_05532 (amphichiral, dim V = 2). On both, an orientation-preserving isometry swaps the pair and negates the class. No open
Eisenstein cusp is known.

**Why, on o10_150725's covers.** On the base, the twelve isometries act on the class v = (1, 1, 1) by ε = (orientation sign) ×
(cusp-swap sign):
- the rotations, ε = +1;
- the reflections fixing the cusp, ε = −1;
- the orientation-preserving swaps of cusps 0 and 2, ε = −1;
- the orientation-reversing swaps, ε = +1.

Every cover found inherits an isometry with ε = −1. The target is therefore sharp: a cover to which the rotation lifts and only ε = +1
isometries lift, namely the rotations and the orientation-reversing swaps, a subgroup of order 6.

## 4. What it means — the owner's question

**Can more objects together supply what m004 withheld?** Only through a relation that transports physical data, and there is one
such relation: the cover. That confines "together" to m004's own commensurability class.
- The word states are all excluded by T1, even though they carry chirality of the 3-manifold freely.
- The chiral ladder is excluded by T3 and T4. m010 included: its field is ℚ(√−7).

Inside the class, the shift does real work, because it removes two limits.
- The class is no longer bounded by a census. B1186's 112 members were the part of it with at most ten tetrahedra. The architecture
  contains all its covers.
- The class is no longer an excursion "from the object". It is the object's own refinements.

And the arithmetic that makes m004 what it is — the Eisenstein face — turns out to be exactly what makes a chiral index *generic*
rather than accidental at a free cusp (L1 and its corollary). It works only where no symmetry negates the class. So the ingredient
m004 withholds has a precise address:

> **a member of m004's class with a free hexagonal cusp that an order-3 isometry rotates, whose rotation quotient has b₁ > 0, and
> whose invariant class no isometry of the member negates — neither a mirror of the cusp nor a swap of Eisenstein cusps.**

Every such cusp computed so far fails at the last clause or at b₁ = 0. The nearest miss, ocube06_08812, fails only globally: its
cusps are open one at a time, and a swap closes the pair. That is sL-6, the Eisenstein cusp.

**Does it strengthen the chain?** Yes, in three places, and it exposes one seam.
- **The orientation fork, the order bit and matter chirality are one element.**
  - P's semantic type (B1384 S0b: redescription or move) was already the orientation fork and A7.
  - It is now also the mirror on the word states (T2).
  - It is also the kind of symmetry whose presence kills the index at an Eisenstein cusp (S8a: the cusp's mirrors; S9: the swap
    that takes their place on a chiral member). L3 says why: an index is a sign, and a symmetry that exchanges the sign forbids it.
  - B1383's spin bit (the deck's role) belongs to the same question.
- **The founding identity is located.** The golden face can go chiral (S6), but the Eisenstein face, as the state's own trace field,
  stays on the P-fixed states and on m004's class (T3).
- **The joint region is fixed by theorem, not by choice.** It is the commensurability class, and the architecture's refinements
  enlarge it. sL-1 is thereby re-derived from the genesis rather than from the owner's direction of 2026-09-16.

**What it does not do.** The shift supplies no values: the 19 stay at 0. It does not license disjoint "sectors" as a Standard Model.
Concatenation is not a physical join, since no theorem transports data along it. The spin-½ half of a generation has its own record
(B1372, B1373) and is not addressed here. An open Eisenstein cusp would still need three things:
- the leading coefficient non-zero;
- the shell not killed by a translation part (B1370's instrument);
- the spin-½ half and the Standard-Model conditions.
