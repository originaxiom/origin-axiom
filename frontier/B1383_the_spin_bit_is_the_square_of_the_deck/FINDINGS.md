## 1. The physics inputs, verified at their sources (quoted, not derived)

1. **M-theory is Pin⁺, with nothing to shift it.** Freed–Hopkins, *Consistency of M-theory on nonorientable manifolds*
   (arXiv:1908.09916, Q. J. Math. 2021), §2: *"Eleven-dimensional M-theory has both time-reversal symmetry and fermionic fields, and
   no additional global symmetries, so the Wick-rotated symmetry group is one of the two Pin groups. Because time-reversal squares in
   Minkowski spacetime to (−1)^F, the appropriate group is H₁₁ = Pin⁺₁₁ … we require that X have a tangential pin⁺ structure."* Their
   convention is B1382's: *"Projective 4-space RP⁴ has w₂ = 0, so admits a pin⁺ structure"* (§5), i.e. Pin⁺ is obstructed by w₂.
   The clause "no additional global symmetries" matters: there is no internal symmetry that could redefine T and flip T².
2. **T² = (−1)^F ⟺ Pin⁺, and the construction of Pin⁺ structures from a deck.** Witten, *Fermion path integrals and topological
   phases* (arXiv:1508.04715, Rev. Mod. Phys. 88 (2016) 035001), §1: *"we only consider time-reversal symmetries with T² = (−1)^F …
   This also means that the square of a spatial reflection R … is +1, and that when we work on an unorientable manifold, we use a
   Pin⁺ structure, not a Pin⁻ structure … In section 5, all statements are reversed: T² = +1, R² = (−1)^F … Pin⁻."* §4.2 is B1382's
   exact situation. For an unorientable Y = Ŷ/ℤ₂ with the ℤ₂ generated by τ: *"Suppose that we are given a τ-invariant spin structure
   S on Ŷ. This means in particular that we have … an action of τ on ψ̂, obeying τ² = 1. In this case, we can define a pin⁺ structure
   P on Y."* Footnote 1: *"In Euclidean signature, all symmetries act on the fermions in a linear fashion … a Euclidean signature
   symmetry CR continues to either the antilinear symmetry T or the linear symmetry CR in Lorentz signature."* So every statement
   below is made in Euclidean terms, where T and R are the same reflection.
3. **The Standard Model's CP, when it is a symmetry, is Pin⁺.** Yonekura, *The absence of global anomalies of CP symmetry*
   (arXiv:2602.11475, 2026), §2.2: *"Pin⁺(d): (Rₙ)² = 1, Pin⁻(d): (Rₙ)² = (−1)^F … if we require Rₙ to map a Weyl fermion to (the
   hermitian conjugate of) itself … we automatically have Rₙ² = 1 … the relevant group is Pin⁺(d). On the other hand, to realize
   Pin⁻(d) in four dimensions, we need to have an even number of fermions."* §2.4: *"the symmetry group is Pin⁺(d) ⋉ G."* Abstract:
   *"the standard model matter content does not have anomalies"* after gauging CP. S6 below shows the Pin⁺ choice is **forced** for
   the SM's content: a Pin⁻ CP would need X·X̄ = −1 on the flavour space of the lepton singlet, which is impossible in dimension 3.
4. **The Standard Model itself has no exact T or CP.** The CKM phase gives J ≠ 0 (of order 3 × 10⁻⁵; PDG 2024, *CKM quark-mixing
   matrix*). So the SM as it stands cannot be put on an unorientable manifold at all. A CP symmetry exists only as a gauge symmetry
   broken spontaneously. That is the string-theory situation: *"CP is a gauge symmetry in string theory … all θ angles arise due to
   spontaneous CP violation"* (Dine–Leigh–MacIntire, PRL 69 (1992) 2030, hep-th/9205011; also Choi–Kaplan–Nelson, Nucl. Phys. B391
   (1993) 515, hep-ph/9205202). Item 3 is about that situation.

B1382 §4 said *"T² = (−1)^F, the Standard Model's"*. That phrase is corrected here: the SM has no T. What is true is items 1–3.

## 3. The theorem and the table

**Theorem (the sign law).** Let τ be the Gieseking deck on m004 = Ŷ, σ a spin structure, and ε the Pin convention. The deck paired
with the reversal of k further (flat) directions lifts to the spinors with square **q = ε^{k+1}(−1)^{k(k+1)/2}σ**. The mapping torus
of τ carries every (ε, σ), and its double tick lifts to **εσ**.

*Proof.* (λW, c)² = (εA, 0) (B1382); the element (σA, 0) acts trivially on σ's spinors, so the deck squares to εσ. Pairing it with
U = f₁⋯f_k in the graded product gives the extra sign (−1)^k U² = (−1)^k(−1)^{k(k−1)/2}εᵏ. For the mapping torus, s̃²ã⁻¹ =
(εA·(σA)⁻¹, translation by 2) = εσ·(translation by 2). ∎ S2 checks the Clifford half against Stiefel–Whitney classes in a case where
they decide; S3 checks the graded product against the single Clifford algebra at a fixed point.

**What physics buys, and what it does not.** It buys the Pin type, which is the bit B1382 relocated to: Pin⁺ is required by M-theory
(with no internal symmetry that could shift T²), by every T² = (−1)^F theory, and by the SM's own CP, with three generations
excluding Pin⁻. So B1382's relocated bit is paid. The spin bit then becomes **the square of the deck on fermions**: ρ₁ ⟺ τ̃² = 1 (the
deck is an honest involution on fermions and can be gauged), ρ₂ ⟺ τ̃² = (−1)^F. Which one physics wants depends on what the deck
*is*:

- a gauge symmetry, when the parent itself is physical: ρ₁;
- the CP of the Standard Model on an oriented carrier: ρ₂;
- the tick of B1104's four-manifold: the double tick's periodicity.

So the bit moves once more, from "which Pin type" to "which role the deck plays", and there it meets the orientation axiom. **Read
physically, A6 says the carrier is the oriented cover, not the parent.** That excludes role (a). The only role that selects B1141's
lift is therefore the one the chain's own orientation axiom sets aside. In the role most consistent with A6 — the deck as the carrier's
CP — the Standard Model selects the other lift. The weakest genesis axiom and the spin bit are one question in physical form.

**A consequence of role (a) worth knowing.** If the Gieseking manifold were the spatial universe with a gauged CP (role (a)), every
CP-odd quantity would be a section of the orientation line bundle L. J is one of them. Since w₁(N) ≠ 0, L has no nowhere-vanishing
section, so J would have to vanish on a hypersurface Poincaré-dual to w₁: a CP domain wall somewhere in the universe. That is the
standard line-bundle fact, noted here and not computed further.

spinors, and it needs no deck. Witten 2016, eqs. (2.58)–(2.60): *"its eigenvalues have even multiplicity because of a Euclidean
