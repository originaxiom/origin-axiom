## The question, as the record had posed it

MENU-1 established that the value arm **cannot be won by value-matching**: the menu of
type-respecting maps is dense — **11,720 values, median gap 3.53×10⁻⁵, min 1.63×10⁻⁹** — so any

> *"it needs a **forcing theorem that selects one map before looking**. That theorem does not exist.
> It is new mathematics, and it is the single thing standing between the programme and a computed
> SM number."*

And the ledger's other open rows have the identical shape: the object presents a set, and cannot
supply a point of it — the CS bit's {0, ¼}, the Higgs line, the hypercharge ray, the finite menus,
the colour frame.

## The argument

Five steps, each citing a banked result:

1. **B1168, the parity×dimension law**: object-canonical ⟺ β-even **and** dimensionless.
2. **MENU-1 / B1203**: the menu's 17 tier-1 atoms are object-canonical, and every menu value is
   built from them by {+, −, ×, ÷, √}.
3. **GC-15 / B1191**: the admissible group is **G = Stab_Aut(D)|_T, read off D and never chosen** —
   that restriction is the whole content of "expressible from the object".
4. An Aut(D)-invariant is fixed by every subgroup of Aut(D), hence by **G**.
5. {+, −, ×, ÷, √} are G-equivariant, so **G fixes every atom, hence every menu value.**

> **G acts trivially on the menu.** All 11,720 are fixed points.

GC-15's regime (i) needs G **transitive** to give a unique invariant measure and a cost of log₂|T|
bits. A **trivial** action is the opposite extreme: every subset is invariant, no measure is
distinguished, and no selection is possible.

## THE RESULT

**No symmetry readable off the object can select within the menu — and the cut is zero for the same
reason in every case.**

B1203 discovered this for **c** and reported the cut as *exactly 0 of 11,720*, noting the structural
reason: *"the menu lies wholly inside the c-even class already — requiring what every candidate
satisfies selects nothing."* **That reason is not special to c.** It applies to every G that is
readable off D, because the menu is built from the invariant class and **invariance cannot
distinguish among invariants.**

So the missing "forcing theorem" is not missing. **It is impossible in the form sought**, and the
impossibility is a one-line consequence of B1168's own law.

## What this does and does not close

**Closes:** the search for a forcing theorem *built from the object's own symmetries*. That search
is over, and it ended in a proof rather than exhaustion. It also explains, without further work, why
two years of value-matching produced ten honest negatives.

**Does not close:** whether a selector exists using data that is **not** an object-invariant. B1168
says such data is β-odd or dimensionful — the reader's column. **A selector there is not forbidden
by this argument; it is simply not the object's.** That is the Heartbeat Atlas's thesis, arrived at
from the opposite direction, and it is where any remaining prediction arm must live.

**The honest fence:** step 2 rests on the menu's atoms being object-canonical, which is MENU-1's own
construction rule and is cited, not re-derived here — cloud's enumerator is the primary. If a tier
admitting non-canonical atoms were used, the argument does not apply to that tier, and B1203 already
notes the ω-tier case behaves the same way for c.
