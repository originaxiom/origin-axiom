at most 2 of the 3 torsor bits.  P16.4 (no F_2^3 fixed point) requires
3 independent bits.  With 2 bits, the pointer automatically HAS a fixed

ATTACK E: THE THETA PROBLEM
========================================================================

The attack: sigma(a)=ab ALWAYS writes 'a' first.  Parent tracking =
"follow the original 'a'" is a CONTENT choice, not a spatial choice.
Content choices are not affected by spatial reversal.  Therefore
tracking is not theta.

Counter-evidence: A1 Section 7 EMPIRICALLY refutes this attack.
Reversal DOES swap parent and child, with zero mismatches at every
position and every level tested (k=3..8, up to 34 positions per level;
multi-step trajectories to k=15, all 5 starting positions).

Resolution (B1 Section 4): the tracking choice is not purely about
content.  It is about the ALIGNMENT between the observer's tracking
direction and the substitution's intrinsic writing direction.  Theta
reverses sigma's direction via conjugation (sigma -> sigma_mirror =
R.sigma.R), which flips the alignment.  The content label
(parent/child) switches because it is correlated with the spatial
position (offset+0/offset+1), and reversal swaps which content sits
at which position.

This attack is ANSWERED by the data.  Tracking = theta is the one
part of the proposal that survives.

However, note the CAVEAT (from B1):  "theta swaps tracking" means
"conjugating sigma by reversal swaps which content label sits at which
position."  This is theta acting on SIGMA BY CONJUGATION, not theta
acting on the WORD DIRECTLY.  The word-level theta (reverse the word)
and the sigma-level theta (conjugate the substitution) are related but
distinct.  The tracking correspondence is with sigma-level theta, not
with word-level theta directly.

RESULT: This attack is the WEAKEST of the seven.  P16.1 survives.
