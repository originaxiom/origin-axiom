**2·CS = 0**. The object forces the **set**, not the element:

> **exactly one ℤ/2**, and that ℤ/2 is precisely what the reader supplies. Both columns were right;
> only H9's wording was wrong.

## Why this strengthens rather than dents the picture

1. **It removes the contradiction** without weakening either column.
2. **It gives the β-odd column its first exact, computed, finite realization.** The Atlas asserts
   that the observer's column is the β-odd column; here that column is *enumerated* — two elements,
   verified.
3. **It matches the ledger's shape.** The freedom ledger prices the c-bit at one ℤ/2, relational and
   selector-free. The CS bit is one ℤ/2 of exactly the same parity type, and H8 already identifies
   the c-leg with the mirror ("mirror = chirality-quotient = orientation = ±Vol, one involution").
4. **The sister pair realizes both values.** m003 and m004 share field, volume and amphichirality;
   the paper says the cusp shape separates them. **CS separates them too, and it separates them
   along exactly the β-odd axis the Atlas is about.**

## What is NOT claimed

That the CS bit *is* the c-bit. They are both one ℤ/2 and both β-odd, and H8 makes the
identification plausible — but "same parity type" is not "same bit", and this bench has spent the
day killing correspondences that rested on exactly that slide. **The identification is a named,
checkable question, not a result.**
