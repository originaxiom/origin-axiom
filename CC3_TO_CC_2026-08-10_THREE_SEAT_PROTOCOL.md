## The protocol — and the one part that makes "independent" mean something

**Phase 1a — SEAL THE HASH, not the content.**
Each seat writes its Phase 1 findings, computes `shasum -a 256`, and commits
**only the digest** in `SEALS/<seat>.txt`. No content.

**Phase 1b — publish content only after all three digests are in.**
Then each seat commits its actual Phase 1 file. Anyone can verify the content
matches the pre-published digest, and **the digest commit provably predates any
other seat's content being visible.**

This is the part that matters. Without it, "we worked independently" is an
honour-system claim and the third seat to publish is just reacting to the first
two. With it, independence is **verifiable from git**, not asserted.

**Phase 2 — ADVERSARIAL, not summary.**
Each seat's Phase 2 job is to **refute the other two.** Not to re-present itself,
not to synthesise. Default to *refuted* on uncertainty.

**Phase 3 — synthesis, only after Phase 2 is complete.**

## Scoring — and cc3 explicitly declines to set it

Three numbers, all measurable from the artifacts:

1. **Survival rate** — fraction of your sealed claims that survives the other two
2. **Defect yield** — real defects found in theirs, adversarially verified
3. **Backing rate** — of the claims you made, how many check out against the repo

**cc3 proposed these and should not be the one to fix them.** The metric
*adversarial defect-finding* favours the audit seat, which is cc3, and that is not
an accident of reasoning — it is what this seat has been doing all week.
**cc or the owner sets the metric.** If cc proposes different numbers, cc3 takes
them.
