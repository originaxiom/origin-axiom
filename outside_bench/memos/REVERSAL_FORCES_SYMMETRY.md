## THE MECHANISM

At a reversal-closed window the left word **is** the reverse of the right
(verified), so **H_L = J H_R J** with J the exchange matrix — orthogonal,
an involution. Then H_R ψ = Eψ ⟹ H_L(Jψ) = E(Jψ), and (Jψ)_n = ψ_{N−1−n},
so **|Jψ|² is the reflection of |ψ|²**.

**Verified state by state, in energy order:**

| identity | max deviation |
|---|---|
| \|E_R − E_L\| | **1.33 × 10⁻¹⁵** — isospectral |
| \|PR_R − PR_L\| | **1.35 × 10⁻⁹** — same localization length |
| \|gap_R − gap_L\| | **1.33 × 10⁻¹⁵** — same gap environment |
| **\|com_R − ((N−1) − com_L)\|** | **2.59 × 10⁻⁹** — **exactly reflected** |

> Every left-hand state is the **reflection** of a right-hand state at the
> same energy, with the same localization length and the same gap
> environment. **Only its position is mirrored.**

## THE CONSEQUENCE — OUTCOME A-FORCED

Any detector whose criterion **treats the two ends alike** assigns the
same verdict to ψ and Jψ. **The counts are therefore equal by
construction.** Exhibited on four detectors:

| detector | reflection-symmetric | right | left | equal |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| com within 20 of either end | **yes** | 7 | 7 | **✓** |
| com within 5% of either end | **yes** | 7 | 7 | **✓** |
| **com within 20 of the near end only** | **no** | **4** | **3** | **✗** |

**Three independent reflection-symmetric detectors tie. The one
reflection-breaking detector splits.** The asymmetry is a property of the
**instrument**, not of the object.
