## 1. The statement, precisely — and the paraphrase drops the load-bearing word

**Definition (standard).** A **Jørgensen group** is a non-elementary Kleinian group **that can be
generated by TWO elements** for which **equality** holds in Jørgensen's inequality. The Jørgensen
number is defined for a **two-generator** subgroup:

```
J(G) = inf { |tr²A − 4| + |tr[A,B] − 2|  :  G = ⟨A,B⟩ },     J(G) ≥ 1   [Jørgensen 1976]
```

**Callahan (2009), as the literature states it:**

> **"the only *torsion-free* Jørgensen group is the figure-eight knot group."**

B1345 paraphrases this as *"the only **orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold** with `J = 1` is the
figure-eight complement"*, and **memo 225 relayed that paraphrase and built on it.** What the
paraphrase drops:

* **"TWO-GENERATOR" is part of the definition.** A manifold whose group needs three generators has
  no Jørgensen number in this sense — it is **outside the theorem's domain**, not a manifold the
  theorem rules out.
* **"orientable" is not an added hypothesis.** A **Kleinian** group already lies in `PSL(2,ℂ)`,
  which acts by **orientation-preserving** isometries. So **m000 is outside the domain**, not a
  counterexample — the same conclusion B1345 reached, by a different route, and its instinct there
  was right.
* **"torsion-free"** (no torsion *elements*) is what makes it a manifold rather than an orbifold.
  That part of the gloss is fair.
