## 2. THE CELLS — two outcomes each, fixed now

**CELL 1 — is the resolvent plane-invariant?** Build the pencil at all six charge pairs; for every
cubic factor, compute the squarefree part of its discriminant.
* **A** — every plane yields the **same** resolvent `ℚ(√77)`. Then `77` is an invariant of the
  object's charge system, **not** of the measurement choice, and B1077's bare-form split result and
  the 77 coexist as statements about two different layers. **The twist-source is not a choice.**
* **B** — the resolvent **varies** with the plane. Then `77` is a property of *which two charges
  were measured*, the (4,8)-plane has no privileged status, and **B882's door closes on its own
  circularity**: the dressing that produces K is selected by a choice, not by the object.

**CELL 2 — is B888's echo a LAW or a coincidence?** For each plane, compare the resolvent's
squarefree part against the product of the two **complementary** (unmeasured) exponents.
* **A** — the identity holds at **all six** planes. B888's *"observation, unweighted"* becomes a
  computed law: *the resolvent remembers exactly what was not measured.*
* **B** — it fails at any plane. The (4,8) ↦ 7·11 = 77 instance is then a coincidence of one plane,
  and is recorded as such.

**CELL 3 — is the factorization SHAPE special to the measured plane?** B886 banks
`P = F₁¹ · F₂⁸` — two cubics, multiplicities 1 and 8, `3 + 24 = 27`.
* **A** — the same shape at every plane. The shape is a fact about the 27 and the charge system.
* **B** — the shape differs. Then the (x₈,x₁₆) plane is structurally distinguished, and the
  distinction itself is the finding — it would be the first object-intrinsic reason to prefer one
  plane, which is exactly what a non-circular twist-source needs.
