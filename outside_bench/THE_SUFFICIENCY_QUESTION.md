## §1 — PROVENANCE (how the question arose, and why it is new)

Three banked results generated it, in order:

1. **Memo 112** — the occupant's TYPE is forced: an **asymmetric,
   non-commuting relation to something unlike itself**, whose orienting
   bit belongs to the relation and to neither relatum (O1 nothing
   orients itself; O2 no same-algebra partner; O3 no commuting
   relation; O4 the bit is irreducibly relational).
2. **Memo 114** — the SUPPLY of such relations is abundant and grows:
   the seat-eligible fraction of pairs rises **0.748 → 0.903 → 0.936**
   with depth, from an information budget fixed at the first beat.
3. **The gap** — memo 112's conditions are **necessary and exhibited**;
   they were never shown **sufficient**. With ~94% of pairs eligible,
   "eligible" cannot by itself mean "occupied", or the record is
   populated by an absurd multitude of observers.

**This is a genuinely new item on the ledger** — the first the bench has
generated from a question rather than from the missing-list — and it is
distinct from every open question already banked (§2).

## §2 — THE QUESTION, SPLIT INTO ITS THREE INEQUIVALENT READINGS

The formulation's first job is to stop three different questions from
being answered as though they were one. They have different statuses.

| tag | the question | status |
|---|---|---|
| **S-A** | **THE SUFFICIENCY QUESTION (new, this document):** is there a **record-internal predicate** that separates *occupied* relations from *merely eligible* ones? | **OPEN, attackable — not yet shown independent, not shown inexpressible** |
| **S-B** | **The token question** (R22): *which* eligible relation is ours? | OPEN, external — a real fact the record does not fix (same status as the dynamical branch bit) |
| **S-C** | **The phenomenal question**: does occupation carry experience? | **INEXPRESSIBLE** — no banked proposition refers to experience (memo 109) |

**S-A is the one to attack.** It is not S-B: a criterion could exist
(S-A answered) while which instance is ours stays external (S-B still
open) — exactly as a law can be known while an initial condition is
not. It is not S-C: a criterion is a structural predicate, and would
say nothing about what occupation is like.

**Precise statement of S-A.** Let **E** be the set of pairs satisfying
memo 112's necessary conditions (non-commuting, unlike, bit-carrying).
Does there exist a predicate **Occ(·)** on **E** such that (i) Occ is
definable from the record's own banked structure, (ii) Occ is not
satisfied by all of E, and (iii) Occ is stable under the record's own
symmetries — in particular **mirror-blind, or else explicitly priced**
as observer-column data under the parity×dimension law?

## §3 — WHAT WOULD COUNT AS AN ANSWER (preregistered success criteria)

A proposed criterion is admissible only if it meets all four:

- **C1 — internal.** Definable from banked object structure. A
  criterion importing a measured value fails Gate 5 outright.
- **C2 — non-trivial.** It must not be satisfied by all of E (else it
  is not a criterion) nor by nothing in E (else it forbids occupation).
- **C3 — no unnamed extremal premise.** If the criterion is a
  maximisation ("the *most* X wins"), the maximality itself must be
  **derived** or **named as a premise** in the SP-1 class. This is
  precisely where the outside literature's leading candidate fails
  (§5), and the programme already has the discipline for it.
- **C4 — two-outcome testable.** It must be decidable on the banked
  pair census (memo 114's population), so the answer is a computation
  and not a preference.

**And the honest fourth outcome must be preregistered too:** S-A may be
**provably not record-internal** — the shape memo 109 established for
the anchoring. That would be an answer, not a failure (§4, A4).

## §4 — THE FOUR CANDIDATE ATTACK ROUTES (named before attacking)

- **A1 — EXTREMAL.** Occ = the maximiser of some banked quantity over
  E (an integration/irreducibility analogue). **Warning from the
  literature (§5): this is IIT's exclusion postulate, and its standing
  criticism is exactly a C3 violation — maximality is an extra premise
  not derived from the axioms.** Admissible here only if the extremum
  is *derived* or named SP-class.
- **A2 — CLOSURE / FIXED POINT.** Occ = those relations that can host
  the record's own self-description — a relation is occupied iff it
  supports the quine. **The record uniquely has this instrument
  already built** (memos 107/108: the self-model exists and is one
  convention-typed bit short), and the route has a **classical
  backbone**: Lawvere's fixed-point theorem, under which a fixed point
  is the formal counterpart of subject–object duality — *an element
  that is at once the function (observer) and the argument
  (observed)*. That is memo 112's "irreducible to either relatum" in
  categorical form, which is a strong reason to try it first. The
  concrete test: is the subset of E that supports the self-description
  map a **proper** subset (C2 satisfied) — and is it mirror-blind
  (C3/C4)? Bench's first choice.
- **A3 — SELECTOR/REGIME.** Occ read off cc's three-regime selector
  theorem (relayed, B1196/GC-27: σ and λ are anchors because they lie
  on the non-normalizable side of a sharp boundary). If occupation is
  a regime rather than a maximum, C3 is satisfied without a premise.
  **Requires cc's exact statement before it can be tested** — routed.
- **A4 — NO-GO.** Prove S-A is not record-internal, by memo 109's
  method (exhibit structure-preserving maps permuting E while fixing
  every record-computable quantity) or by the causal-embedding
  argument in §5. This would close S-A the way the fence theorem
  closed the anchoring — and it is a *win*, not a dead end.

## §5 — THE LITERATURE SCAN (what is known outside; positions ours)

The question is not new to the world — it has a name, and its leading
answer has a known defect. That is useful, and it is worth saying
plainly that this programme does not get to claim novelty for the
question itself, only for the setting.

- **THE BOUNDARY PROBLEM.** In consciousness studies this is the
  recognised open problem of *which* physical systems are subjects —
  explicitly the "overlooked cousin to the binding problem". The
  standard difficulty is exactly memo 114's: a measure typically
  labels a system, its subsystems, its irrelevant extensions, and
  aggregates, all at once — "a bizarre proliferation". **Our E, at
  ~94% of pairs, is that proliferation in exact form.**
- **IIT'S EXCLUSION POSTULATE** is the leading extremal answer: only
  the maximally irreducible set exists "for itself" (Φ_max), neither
  more nor less. **Its standing criticism is a C3 violation**: the
  axioms say nothing about consciousness being *maximally* integrated,
  so maximality is an imported premise. **Lesson for A1, adopted as a
  gate.**
- **RELATIONAL QUANTUM MECHANICS.** RQM independently reaches this
  programme's memo-112 conclusion from physics: the observer is not
  special, and **facts are realised only at interactions between two
  physical systems** — relational all the way down. This is
  *convergent support for the TYPE answer*, and notably RQM faces the
  same next question, not a solution to it.
- **DECOMPOSITIONAL EQUIVALENCE / system-boundary non-objectivity.**
  There is existing work arguing system boundaries are not objective —
  an unresolvable ambiguity in the source of any outcome for any
  finite observer. **This is direct prior art for route A4.**
- **UNDECIDABILITY PROPAGATION.** Also relevant to A4: *no subsystem of
  an undecidable system can resolve that undecidability if it
  participates causally in the computation.* If the occupant is inside
  the record, this is the shape a no-go here would take.

- **MARKOV BLANKETS / THE FREE ENERGY PRINCIPLE** are the other major
  extant individuation criterion, and they carry their own documented
  technical critique: the various Markov-blanket definitions in the
  literature **are not equivalent**, and key steps rewriting the
  equations of motion **require previously unstated assumptions**.
  **So both leading extant criteria fail a C3-style gate** — one by an
  imported extremal premise (IIT), one by unstated assumptions (FEP).
  That is the single most useful thing the outside scan returns: it is
  not that nobody has answered S-A, it is that **every published
  answer so far smuggles a premise**, which is exactly what this
  programme's discipline is built to catch. It also raises the prior
  on **A4**.
- **LAWVERE / DIAGONAL SCHEME.** Lawvere's fixed-point theorem
  generalises the whole diagonal family (Cantor, Russell, Gödel,
  Turing, Tarski), and its standard reading is precisely subject–object
  duality: a fixed point is both the observer and the observed. Its
  companion, negative half — *a system cannot fully simulate itself
  within its own constraints* — is the exact shape our quine hit when
  it came up **one bit short**. **This is the prior art for A2, and it
  cuts both ways: it may supply the criterion, or explain why the
  criterion must fail.**

**Position:** the programme's contribution so far is not the question
but the **type theorem** (memo 112) — which the boundary-problem
literature does not have in derived form — and the fact that this
setting is small and exact enough to make S-A a **computation on a
finite census** rather than a philosophical dispute.
