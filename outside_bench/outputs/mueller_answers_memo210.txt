THE SCOPE, and it is the part that matters
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [Mul] Theorem 1.1: "Let X be a CLOSED, oriented hyperbolic 3-manifold."
  [MFP] prove the CUSPED case, and their theorem is the LEADING term only:
    "lim_{k->inf} log|T_{2k+1}(M)| / (2k+1)^2 = - Vol(M) / 4pi"
  The figure-eight complement is CUSPED. So the m(m+1) refinement is NOT
  established for it by these two papers.

AND THE RESIDUAL IS NOT A CONSTANT -- it is the Ruelle tail, converging
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Transposing [Mul]'s formula (signs opposite: his torsion is the reciprocal
  normalisation of B581's Delta'(1)), R_m = -c - sum_{k<=m} log|R_{2k}(k)|,
  so a CONSECUTIVE pair of exponents gives one Ruelle term directly:
    m=4 -> 5:  R_5 - R_4 = -0.0104577255   => log|R_{10}(5)| = 0.0104577255
    m=7 -> 8:  R_8 - R_7 = 0.0002468702556   => log|R_{16}(8)| = -0.0002468702556

    |increment at 5| / |increment at 8| = 42.361221  over 3 steps
    systole of 4_1 (SnapPy): l_0 = 1.087070144995739
    e^(3 l_0) = 26.081088;  e^(3.5 l_0) = 44.913818
    => the increments shrink at a rate between e^(-l_0) and e^(-1.3 l_0) per
       step -- the decay a Ruelle-zeta tail has, NOT a fitted constant.
    the SIGN also flips (-0.01046 then 0.0002469):
    the geodesics carry imaginary parts (+-1.7228 at the systole), so the
    terms oscillate. A constant cannot do that; a Ruelle sum must.
