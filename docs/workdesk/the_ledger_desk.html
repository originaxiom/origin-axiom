The Ledger Desk

A read-only workdesk over the record. The repository stays the only place anything is written. The desk is rebuilt from every commit, and every mark on it links back to a file, a line and a commit. Each view exists to answer a question a researcher actually asks of the record. Pictures of the mathematics are computed, labelled weave or thread, and never stand in for a result.

What the research says

How other scientists map large research records

Six agents read the literature and surveyed the repository. The full sourced report is docs/workdesk/RESEARCH_REPORT_2026-10-28.md. These are the findings that shaped the design.

Counts, homepages and cones

Programs that navigate well do not start from one big graph. Tao's Equational Theories Project, the LMFDB, the Stacks Project and the Lean blueprints use three layers, all regenerated by code from the canonical record: counts against a stated end condition, a permanent page per object with its links and provenance, and scoped subgraphs such as one result's dependency cone.

ETP paper (arXiv 2512.07087); LMFDB knowls and completeness flags; Stacks Project tags

Generated, never hand-kept

Status typed by hand drifts. When blueprints began to be generated from the Lean code itself, the conversion exposed unused nodes, missing edges and results marked wrongly, including an AI-written proof that skipped the lemmas it claimed to use.

LeanArchitect (ITP 2026, arXiv 2601.22554); Carleson project paper

Big pictures mislead

Above about twenty nodes, matrices beat node-link drawings on most reading tasks in controlled studies; only path-finding favours node-link. Layout alone changes what viewers judge important. In mathlib, 74% of dependency edges are invisible in the source and the most central nodes are trivial infrastructure.

Ghoniem et al. 2004; McGrath et al. 1997; Network Structure of Mathlib (2026)

Provenance has a standard shape

W3C PROV's plan, activity and entity match an arc's rule, run and output almost one to one. A preregistration can be withdrawn but never edited. The ETP let its continuous integration be the only thing that moved cards on its task board.

W3C PROV-DM; OSF registrations; ETP §4

Pictures earn trust

Pictures of hyperbolic manifolds have found real mathematics: cohomology fractals were discovered while illustrating Cannon–Thurston maps. The trustworthy ones print the command that regenerates them and disclose their artefacts. Wolfram-style pictures of rule evolution were received as "at best, qualitative".

Bachman, Goerner, Schleimer, Segerman (2022); Goerner (arXiv 2512.16181); Kindlmann and Scheidegger (2014)

Usefulness is testimony

No controlled study shows that dependency graphs, boards, 3D or interactive stories help mathematicians do research. The evidence is the builders' own accounts. So a view should be judged by the insights it produces over weeks of real use, and cut if it produces none.

North 2006 (insight-based evaluation); Shneiderman and Plaisant 2006 (long-term case studies)

Our bottleneck is data

The record already guarantees what most projects lack: citations that resolve, sealed rules and commit-ordered lifecycles. It lacks one identity per arc across seats (18 B numbers name different arcs on different branches), a verdict file for the W arcs, and a scope field: weave or thread is recorded on 2 arc verdicts on this branch and 186 on main.

this session's read-only survey of both branches, 28 October 2026

The design in one picture

The repository is the back end; the desk only reads it

An extractor runs on every push. It reads both branches, because much of the record lives only on main, and writes one index with a manifest: the source commit, the schema version and a checksum per table. The desk is a static site over that index. It has no edit buttons. A card moves on a board only because a commit changed the record.

Working view A · stage 2 of the story

The weave stage: every thread on one surface

Each thread is a cyclic word in the two moves. Its matrix is hyperbolic, so it has an axis in the upper half-plane, and the word closes into one geodesic on the modular surface. The stage draws every lift of every thread up to the length you choose, over the Farey tessellation, in the Poincaré disk. Nothing is picked by hand: the rule is all primitive cyclic words with both letters, length at most n. Click a curve to focus one thread. The badge then says THREAD.

weave

threads per length

Exact, computed in this page from L = [[1, 1], [0, 1]] and R = [[1, 0], [1, 1]] with the record's period rule (CONVENTIONS §3: a word acts as the standard rule on its reverse). The full stage is the same under either rule; only a single thread's label changes. A matrix and its negative act alike on the plane, so the sign of a thread (LR for m004, −LR for m003) is not visible here. The cusp ∞ is at the top and 0 at the bottom.

Working view B · the record's own data

How well does this seat predict its own runs?

Every W rule states its cells with a prior before the script exists. Every output records which cells held. Joining the two gives a calibration curve, a check on the record's honesty that no single arc can give. A point on the diagonal means the stated priors meant what they said.

Outcomes are as computed in each arc's one run. Later post hoc readings, such as "failed as worded, holds post hoc", are not counted. 290 of 377 prior rows join to a machine-readable outcome; the rest record theirs in prose. Bars are 95% Wilson intervals.

The visual story

The mathematics in five stages

Most of the mathematics does not live in three dimensions, so the story does not force it into one space. Each stage gets the surface where its objects actually live, and the stages are linked: select a thread at stage 2 and it stays selected at stages 3 to 5. Three dimensions are used only where the object is three-dimensional.

The rule

The two moves L and R and the relation they fail, ab ≠ ba, shown acting on the Farey tessellation: L slides triangles along the cusp at ∞, R along the cusp at 0. Words grow as paths in the Stern–Brocot tree.

The waves

The tick walk. At tick n a thread acts as φⁿ, and on the parities as (φ mod 2)ⁿ: a static matrix of ticks against every state to a stated length, carrying the results that hold at every tick (the trichotomy, Theorem S). The field. The owner's seed and field: the wave as the seed's counterpart, whose state would supply the nineteen sizes. It is drawn as the seed's space of vacua with the record's candidate potentials over it (W59, W172–W176), marked plainly as not built and not forced. Plate: the matter's wavefunctions, the zero modes on the torus fibre, one density per line. (The owner's ruling, 29 October.)

The towers

The gauge towers (P-415, the owner's question of 28 October): does reality emerge from what is allowed on all the towers together? The chain su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊂ su(5) ⊂ so(10) ⊂ E₆ ⊂ E₇ ⊂ E₈ is drawn whole, none chosen, with each frame's verdict on each rung: B727 (E₆ across three faces is generic), B1805 (Witten's G₂ frame: SU(5) keeps the mechanism and lacks the parity, Spin(10) the reverse), B1812 (the 7-brane frame: LR's class is E₁₀'s; only so(10) to E₈ can collapse to a point; the object fixes the total class, not where a tower sits). Untested frames are marked open. Plates: each thread's tower of closed universes (W125) and the congruence tower of lines of order N (W140, W176, W153's ladder). Later: the closings at every tick of every thread. (The owner's ruling, 29 October.)

Guardrails

What keeps the desk scientific

  • One writer. The desk never edits the record. Boards move because commits moved them.
  • Every mark has a source. Each node, cell and picture carries file, line and commit, and the build fails if one does not resolve.
  • Weave by default. Every overview shows all objects under a stated rule. A view of one object, or of its covers, says THREAD on its face.
  • Pictures are tested. A picture of a thread must not change when its word is rotated or its domain is changed; CI checks that before a picture is published.
  • Status on two channels. An arc's own status is its colour. Whether it rests on a READING or an OPEN arc upstream is its frame. A result is only as firm as what it rests on.
  • No unsourced answers. If a language model ever answers questions on the desk, every claim in its answer must cite file, line and commit, and the desk checks each citation before showing it.

Build order

What this seat would build first

The index

The extractor, the manifest and the determinism check, over both branches. Arc ids keyed by seat, since 18 bare B numbers name different arcs on different branches. No views yet.

The ledger, the lifecycle strip and calibration

The three views whose data is already machine-readable, so the desk is useful in its first week.

The atlas and the board

After the seats agree a small status vocabulary and a weave-or-thread field, so the board is derived, not guessed.

The story, stages 1 to 4

Two-dimensional, exact, in the browser.

The towers in 3D

Last, with exports from SnapPy in CI, because it is the costliest and the least tied to a result.

Not built: a force-directed map of all twelve thousand files as the home page, editing in the browser, and three dimensions for objects that are not three-dimensional.
