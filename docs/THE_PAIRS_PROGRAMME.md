## 1. Purpose

Every choice still standing in the read-out on the whole is a choice between two options that an operation of order
two exchanges (an involution σ). The programme probes each such pair until it **settles itself**, by the pair's own
relation, with no rule added from inside and no data from outside, or until it is shown to be **physical**, a real
two-way input that stays on the scoreboard as one.

## 2. The outcomes, fixed before any probe (a closed list)

| outcome | what it means | how it is shown |
|---|---|---|
| **SAME** | the two options give the same read-out | the outputs agree under σ to the computation's precision (threshold stated per probe, at most 1e-9 in log ratio) |
| **SEEN FROM THE OTHER** | one option is the other read through a map of the record (a cover, a move of the act, a change of view) | the map is written down and the outputs agree through it, to the same threshold |
| **ONE PICKS** | one option is itself a selection between two (a max or min over two branches, a choice of sheet or of generators); the other is not | the selection is exhibited (a corner, a sheet dependence, a generator dependence), and the other option is shown free of it |
| **PHYSICAL** | none of the above | every candidate map fails by more than 1e-3 with the controls passing; it stays a counted bit |

- **BOTH TAKE PART** is not an outcome by itself: using each option in a different role (one triplet for quarks, the
  other for leptons) counts only if the assignment, itself a pair, settles by one of the four.
- **Anything between the thresholds** is reported as found and given no outcome.
- **No new outcome** is added after a probe is run.

## 3. Rules for every probe

1. **Expectations first:** the pair, σ as a formula, each outcome's numerical signature, and the failure condition,
   committed before the run.
2. **Two controls:** a positive control (a pair known to be SAME must come out SAME) and a negative control (a pair
   known to differ must come out different).
3. **Two computations:** no outcome moves the scoreboard until a second, independent computation agrees (the rule of
   2026-11-13, extended to every pair outcome).
4. **The firewall:** no output is compared with any measured value; outputs are named by position, not by particle.
5. **A change of frame is a new frame.** If a pair settles only in a changed frame, its values are committed as a new
   frame beside frame C. The choice between the frames is itself a pair and goes through this list; never by fit.

## 6b. The count (adopted from the web seat's W-F16, 2026-11-14)

Each remaining choice is a two-way bit. **Free bits = the number of bits − the rank of the relations − the rank of the
symmetries** (over F₂, every symmetry preserving every relation). The programme's accounting uses this count.

- **The first block** {hand, tick parity, undoing}: the relation h = o + e (proved for every odd m; computed to m = 41)
  and the symmetries J and K leave **0 free bits**. The hand, the arrow and the tick parity settle among themselves.
- **The second block** {scar order, Higgs reading, hypercharge sign}: with the relation q + y (charge), it settles only
  if C (S ↔ S̃) or D∘C (D the scar duality) is a symmetry of the frame's output. **In frame C neither is**, on three
  computations:
  - main's mirror test;
  - agy-exp's X-SCAR (no element of GL(2, ℤ) and no scar duality exchanges the worlds);
  - main's direct run of W-F16's test: world 1 at (a, b) against world 2 at (−a, −b) differs by 3.06 in log, with matter's
    own parity holding to 6e-14.
  So **frame C carries one free bit here**, and frame D (the Higgs's charge as matter's) is the probe that can remove it.
- **Pre-seal:** the channel weight's truncation moves four outputs by about 1 %. Theorem 90's leading form fixes it to
  3e-5 (norm gap about 1e-5), so frame C's values are computed with N_T90 on B1928's grid before anything is committed.
