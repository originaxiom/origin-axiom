# Selection and the dictionary: main's own view (2026-11-15)

*Main, alone: no subagent, and no seat's answer read. The author put the same question to every seat in his own words.
This file is committed before any seat's answer is read, so the views can be compared independently. Each statement
says whether it is **established** (a banked arc, cited) or **judgement** (main's opinion). No measured value is
compared here. 0 of 19.*

## 1. The obstacle, as I see it

**Judgement.** Selection and the dictionary are not two problems. They are the two outputs of one thing the programme
lacks: a stated law of what is computed on the object. A physical theory supplies both.
- It says which states exist and what they are called: the dictionary.
- It says which configuration is realised, by an extremum, a dominance, or a protected count: selection.

The programme has a principle and an object, but no such law. The frames were dictionaries built by analogy, and they
had nothing to select with. So every frame needed hands-on choices (`docs/TOTAL_INPUTS_SCOREBOARD.md`: 12 to 14
borrowed joints per frame, none earned).

## 2. The principle already names a law, if it is read literally

**Judgement.** "Existence is what remains when cancelling to nothing cannot complete" (GENESIS, PF1) is, word for word,
what an index is: the signed count of what cannot pair off and cancel. Read that way, the principle answers both
questions.
- **Observables** are the protected, index-type quantities of the object. They are integers, or rigid numbers, with no
  parameter to set.
- **Selection** is non-vanishing: what is observed is what does not cancel.
- **The dictionary** is the protected states, carrying the quantum numbers their holonomy gives them.

This is an interpretation. The record's own formal face of PF1 is a commutator trace (κ ≠ 2, Fricke–Vogt; GENESIS §1).
The two are compatible, but neither derives the other.

## 4. What has gone wrong (judgement)

1. **The physics was done on the fibre, which has a modulus, not on the rigid object.** The selection problem for τ is
   largely self-inflicted.
2. **The read-outs were non-cancelling averages.** That is the opposite of what the principle says survives.
3. **The dictionary was built by analogy** to string model building: magnetised tori, modular flavour symmetry, a
   weight-3/2 Higgs. It imported those models' free choices: moduli, readings, lines.
4. **Numbers were chased before the structure was forced.** Chirality and representation content are still borrowed.
   Forty-five looks have spent the credibility that a later match would need (`docs/LOOK_LEDGER.md`).

## 5. How I would attack it

1. **Write the law down.**
   - The programme's partition function is the signed, covariant sum over the ledger: every allowed history, with a
     flat connection over them so that cancellation is meaningful.
   - Its observables are declared to be its protected parts.
   - First test, data-free: does this sum at the act's level produce a non-vanishing chiral index, and does it live in
     the bulk or at the cusp?
   - Success: a non-zero net chirality with no unit chosen.
   - Failure: zero for every principled boundary datum. Then chirality is an input of the programme, and it is said
     plainly.
2. **Read the dictionary off the protected states.** Find the representation content of what does not cancel, under
   the holonomy. The record already ties hypercharge mod 5 to the tick's prime: 6Y mod 5 is constant on SU(5)'s
   multiplets, which is SU(5)'s centre (B1934, B1942).
   - Success: one generation's content, 10 + 5̄ + 1 of SU(5), chiral, with multiplicity three, derived and not
     assigned.
   - Failure: vector-like or wrong content.
3. **Select among flat connections by what the object maximises.**
   - The geometric representation uniquely maximises the volume among representations: volume rigidity, due to
     Gromov, Thurston and Goldman for closed manifolds, and Dunfield and Francaviglia for cusped ones. This is cited,
     not verified on main.
   - It also dominates the volume-conjecture asymptotics, which the record holds (B1116 to B1124).
   - That is an extremum the object supplies. PF5's remark that "the greatest" fails applies to the fibre's flat-line
     instrument (B1881), not to this.
4. **Values only after 1 to 3,** as rigid quantities of that theory: overlaps of protected modes, eta invariants,
   torsions, and spectra of canonical operators on the rigid manifold. None of these needs a modulus.

## 6. What is closest to making a real difference (judgement)

**The index layer.** It comprises:
- B1290's index formula, which says chirality is a cusp datum;
- B1929's eta invariant, a rigid hand candidate;
- the computed 3D index (B1428, B1431);
- the chiral three's index (B1894).

It is rigid and parameter-free, it fits the principle's own wording, and it touches the most physical fact we have to
explain: the world is chiral, and has three generations.

Suppose the principle's cancellation, written as a law, gives a non-vanishing chiral index with the Standard Model's
representation content and no unit chosen. That would be a physics claim others would have to engage with: why a
minimal amphichiral object yields a chiral world with three generations. It would be falsifiable on structure, before
any number is compared.

## 7. The risks, stated

- **Spacetime is not yet in the dictionary.** Nothing in the record says, without a choice, what part of the object is
  3 + 1 dimensional spacetime (B1104: no canonical fourth dimension). Without that, even a perfect index result is a
  count that matches: a strong hint, not a theory.
- **The literal-index reading of PF1 is main's interpretation, not a derivation** (§ 2).
- **The root's vanishing chiral index may be the final answer.** Then the honest result is that chirality, and with it
  the hand, is an input of this programme, counted on the scoreboard. That would still be worth knowing exactly.
