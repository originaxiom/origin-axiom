## Entry 1 — the entrance is unique over an infinite family

**Statement, corrected.** Let $R = \binom{1\,1}{0\,1}$, $L = \binom{1\,0}{1\,1}$, and for $m \ge 1$
let the *metallic grammar* be the word $R^mL^m$, whose trace is $m^2+2$ and whose level is
$\Lambda(m) = m^2+4$. Then **$m = 1$ (the golden grammar) is the unique member of the infinite
family for which $\mathrm{SL}(2,\Z/\Lambda(m))$ is a binary polyhedral group.**

**Proof shape.** (i) $|\mathrm{SL}(2,\Z/N)| = N^3\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2}) \ge 6N^3/\pi^2 > 120$ for
$N \ge 6$, so no modulus above 5 can reach a binary polyhedral order — completeness by bound, not by
search. (ii) $N \in \{3,5\}$ give $2T$ and $2I$; **$N = 4$ gives order 48 = $|2O|$ but is NOT $2O$**
(seven involutions against one — see the B997 addendum of 2026-08-31, and B1019's own sibling test).
(iii) $\Lambda(m) = 3$ has no integer solution; $\Lambda(m) = 4$ forces the degenerate $m = 0$;
$\Lambda(m) = 5$ gives $m = 1$.

**Banked:** B997 (+ 2026-08-31 addendum), B1002 (the conductor identification discharged).

**The prior-art question, stated so a specialist can answer it in one reading:**
> *Is it recorded anywhere that, among the words $R^mL^m$, the golden one is singled out by the
> condition that $\mathrm{SL}(2,\Z/(m^2+4))$ is binary polyhedral?* The ingredients are entirely
> classical — the order formula, the ζ(2) bound, $\mathrm{SL}(2,3) \cong 2T$, $\mathrm{SL}(2,5)
> \cong 2I$. **The question is whether the conjunction has been drawn.**

**Verdict:** ☐ pending

## Entry 3 — the exit is rule-independent

**Statement.** Impose *registerability* (the derived criterion, B860/B871) on a maximal-subalgebra
descent from $E_6$. The registerable options per step are $[3,2,1]$. **Enumerating every
registerability-respecting selection function gives six chains, and all six terminate at the
Standard Model algebra** — while the intermediates differ (max-dim and first-listed pass through
$SO(10)\times U(1)$; min-dim and last-listed through $SU(3)^3$ and Pati–Salam).

**The content.** The endpoint is robust to the ranking rule; the ranking selects the **path**, not
the destination. Combined with B869's control — the cascade rule is not an SM-generator from a
different starting group — the robustness is evidence that the **menu structure** forces the
endpoint rather than the ranking.

**Banked:** B994, with B863's termination theorem (the SM is the *terminal* registerable algebra,
each proper descent killed by a named mechanism, the SM's own chirality as positive control).

**The prior-art question:**
> *Has a maximal-subalgebra descent from $E_6$ been enumerated over the whole space of selection
> rules, with a chirality-type terminality criterion, and the endpoint shown rule-independent?*
> Descents from $E_6$ are ancient; **an enumeration over rule-space with a terminality theorem is
> the part to check.**

# SWEEP RESULTS — first pass, 2026-08-31 (literature search from this bench)

**Two of the four questions now have partial answers, and the crux of the fourth is located.**

### Entry 1, part (i): **KNOWN.**
The classification is standard. A direct source states it plainly: *"These are the only two values of
$n$ for which $\mathrm{SL}(2,\Z/n)$ is isomorphic to binary polyhedral groups"* — namely
$\mathrm{SL}(2,3) \cong 2T$ and $\mathrm{SL}(2,5) \cong 2I$.

> **This independently confirms the N = 4 correction.** The literature says **two**, not three. The
> external referee, our recomputation, and now the standard classification all agree that
> $\mathrm{SL}(2,\Z/4) \not\cong 2O$. B997's "whole exceptional series" clause was wrong on a point
> the literature had already settled, and we had banked the refuting instrument (B1019) ourselves.

### Entry 1, part (iii) — the conjunction: **NO PRIOR ART FOUND** (not a certificate).
Searches for the metallic word $R^mL^m$ against level $m^2+4$ and the binary-polyhedral condition

drawing the conjunction**. The lean is NOVEL-CANDIDATE for the *statement*, while every ingredient
is classical. Under B659's vocabulary this is at best **ROUTINE-to-NOVEL-CANDIDATE**: the value is
the conjunction, and a specialist may still place it in minutes.

### Entry 4 — the global form: **the $\Z_6$ half is KNOWN; the selection half is the live question.**
*"It has been known since 1980 at latest that it really is $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times
\mathrm{U}(1)/\Z_6$ which is actually a subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(5)$."* The paper already says this
is decades old, and it is right to.

**But the surrounding question is live and current**, which raises rather than lowers the stakes:
the admissible global forms $G_p$, $p \in \{1,2,3,6\}$, and how to determine which one nature uses,
are the subject of active 2024--2025 work on generalized symmetries, one-form global symmetry, and
fractionally charged states at the energy frontier. **This is not a settled backwater; it is a
question people are currently trying to answer.**

> **THE CRUX, now sharp enough to be decided.** $\Z_6$ *given* $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ is old. The
> programme's claim is that nothing here assumes $\mathrm{SU}(5)$. B994 is what makes that testable
> and also what complicates it: of the six registerable chains, some pass through
> $\mathrm{SO}(10)\times\mathrm{U}(1) \to \mathrm{SU}(5)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$, and others through
> $\mathrm{SU}(3)^3$ and Pati--Salam, **which do not pass through $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ at all**.
>
> So the question that decides Entry 4 is exactly this: **is the $\Z_6$ obtained on the chains that
> never enter $\mathrm{SU}(5)$?** If yes, the global form is forced by the descent rather than
> inherited from an assumed embedding, and that is a genuinely new claim in a live area. If it is
> obtained only on the $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ chains, the result restates a 1980 fact.
>
> **This is a computation we can run on banked material, not a literature question.** It is the
> single highest-value open cell in the programme, and it was never asked.
