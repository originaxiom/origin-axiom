## 0b. Disclosed (decided or seen before the seal)

- **Design facts about SL(n, 𝔽₂)** (`before_the_seal/design_facts.py`, run). The fraction of elements acting on some
  line as a 3-cycle of its three points is 1/3, 1/3 and 0.311 for n = 2, 3, 4. Higher ranks add 7-cycles (n ≥ 3) and
  5- and 15-cycles (n = 4). So the line's three persists in every rank, but cycling alone does not single it out.
- **Counting, by hand.** Epi(F₂, Q₈) has 24 elements and Aut(Q₈) has order 24, so there is one class. Epi(F₃, Q₈) has
  336 elements (8³ minus the 176 triples in a maximal subgroup), which is 14 classes.
- **The theory that bears on it.** Every finite subgroup of SU(2) is 2-generated. For solvable G and n > d(G), the
  Nielsen moves act transitively on Epi(F_n, G) up to Aut(G) (Dunwoody 1970), so no class is fixed when there is more
  than one. **So C1 and C2 below are expected for the solvable groups** (cyclic, binary dihedral, 2T, 2O) and are
  verification there; **the binary icosahedral group 2I (order 120, not solvable) is the case theory does not decide.**

## 1. The objects

- **The weave of n records.** Aut(F_n) generated by the elementary Nielsen moves (the unit shears a_i ↦ a_i a_j^{±1},
  a_i ↦ a_j^{±1} a_i, i ≠ j), together with the inversions and permutations of the letters. Its image in GL(n, ℤ)
  is the weave of n records; for n = 2 it is the record's weave (B1672: the four shears and their inverses generate
  Aut⁺(F₂)).
- **A common point.** A character of F_n in SU(2), with finite non-abelian image, that every move fixes: ρ∘φ is
  conjugate to ρ for every generator φ. Equivalently, a class of Epi(F_n, G)/Aut(G) whose 2-dimensional faithful
  characters the moves fix, for G a finite non-abelian subgroup of SU(2).
- **The groups.** The binary dihedral groups Dic_m (order 4m, m ≤ 15), 2T (24), 2O (48) and 2I (120).

## 2. Predictions (to be sealed)

- **C0, the control (n = 2).** Q₈'s character (a ↦ i, b ↦ j) is fixed by every move: its class is the only one. List
  every common point at n = 2 among the groups; the record's T2 says the quaternion point is one, and the census says
  which others, if any.
- **C1 (n = 3).** No common point: for every group, the moves' orbit on Epi(F₃, G)/Aut(G) has no fixed class.
- **C2 (n = 4).** The same, with orbits explored from seeds and compared against the class count from Hall's formula
  where full enumeration is too large (2I).
- **C3, the bite.** If C1 or C2 fails, the instrument names the group, the class and the character, and the multiplicity
  it carries.

## 3. What it would mean

- **If C0–C2 hold: two records is the unique count at which the weave has a common point in SU(2).** GM1 then follows
  from one weaker, stated input: that the weave shares a persistent point, in SU(2), with finite image. The three
  parities and the 2T triplet follow from that point (the paper's T2). The input "SU(2), finite image" stays priced.
- **If C1 or C2 fails, a larger register has its own common point.** The count of two is a choice, and the
  multiplicity the larger point carries is named.
- **Either way, the line's three on 𝔽₂ is rank-independent** (the design facts). Whether matter attaches to lines is a
  separate question, left open.
