## 0b. The intellectual architecture (the owner: "it has to be intellectually rich, not just technical … people
should follow the concept, the philosophy through and behind the math")

The reader should be able to follow the paper twice: once through the mathematics, and once through the idea. The idea
is never decoration and never a claim. It is the reason each step is taken.
- **The paper asks an old question precisely:** why is there something, and why this something? It answers the first
  half with a principle (non-cancellation) and a grammar. Its honest answer to the second half is that the structure is
  forced but its "which" is not.
- **Every section has three movements:**
  1. **the question, in words:** what the principle asks at this step, and why this step is the next one;
  2. **the mathematics:** the theorem, its proof or cited proof, and its scope;
  3. **the reading,** in a marked box: what the result means for the idea. Readings are labelled as readings and kept
     apart from claims, so the richness never leaks into the rigour (the record's firewall, applied to prose).
- **The conceptual spine, as the sections carry it:**
  - **Something rather than nothing:** a description that does not generically cancel (κ ≠ 2) is the minimal content
    of existence. The seed is what such a description must look like first (A = LR).
  - **Not one thing but a family:** the principle allows many objects. Asking what holds for all of them at once (the
    weave) is the honest form of "what is forced".
  - **Three, from parity:** the records' own parities, cycled by every move, are where three appears. Nothing is put in
    to make it.
  - **Symmetry is modular:** the moves the grammar allows are exactly the symmetry of a classical family of theta
    functions. The flavour triplet is a modular object because the records are.
  - **Couplings have a geometry:** isotropy at every point makes one generation massless at leading order, and the
    hierarchy becomes a question of how far a direction sits from a cone.
  - **The boundary is a theorem about symmetry itself:** an invariant law can pick only symmetric points (B990's law),
    and symmetric points carry no hierarchy. So whatever chooses the world's numbers is not a symmetry.
  - **What would choose:** a dynamics, a history, or a reading. The paper states the selection problem as the next
    question and does not answer it.
- **The method is part of the philosophy:** inputs are priced, computations are sealed before they are run, and the
  record keeps its corrections. A programme that asks what is forced must show what it assumed. A short section says
  this in prose, not only in an appendix.
- **The closing section is an essay with its claims marked:** "What the principle forces, and what it leaves". It
  covers forced existence against unforced selection, the shape of a selection principle, and where the observer and
  the reading could enter. The firewalled speculation appears there only as a question.
- **Accessibility:**
  - definitions in words before symbols;
  - a glossary;
  - one running example (the root LR) used across the sections, with the weave statement beside it;
  - figures that carry concepts (the chain and its prices; forced against free), not only data.
