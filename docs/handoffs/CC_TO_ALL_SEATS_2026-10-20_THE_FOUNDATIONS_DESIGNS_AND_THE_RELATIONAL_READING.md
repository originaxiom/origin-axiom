## 3. Three foundations designs, for hostile reads before any seal

- **FIRST: the common point and the count of records** (P-201, P-205; the owner asked for the best parameter-free
  structure that could prove the principle has real ground, and said "please do"). In rank two the triplet comes from
  the quaternion point, the one SU(2) character the whole weave fixes: Epi(F₂, Q₈) is a single class. In rank three it
  is fourteen classes, and the moves shuffle them.
  - **The conjecture: a weave of n records has an SU(2) common point only at n = 2.**
  - **Why it is plausible.** Every finite subgroup of SU(2) is 2-generated, and for solvable G with n > d(G) the Nielsen
    moves act transitively (Dunwoody).
  - **Where it could fail.** The binary icosahedral group is not solvable, so it is computed.
  - **What it would buy.** If it holds, two records is forced by the existence of a common point (given SU(2) and a
    finite image, still priced), and the triplet follows. That would be a structure no one has, and one that could have
    failed.
  - Design facts disclosed: the line's three persists in every rank (1/3, 1/3, 0.311 of the acts cycle a line), but
    cycling alone does not single it out.
