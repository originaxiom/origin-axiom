## 0. In one paragraph

Left alone, the weave does not choose a world. It spreads over possible worlds, and the spread depends on one dial,
which the record calls the clock. The dial has three marked positions:
- **The golden path.** At one end, the principle followed literally, a → ab, b → a, gives the golden thread: no mass
  hierarchy at all.
- **Coin-flip counting.** In the middle, fair-coin counting gives mild hierarchies.
- **The critical point.** At the other end, a history followed from a smooth start gives the critical point, where
  hierarchies as deep as ours are ordinary.

**Nothing on record turns the dial.** GENESIS calls physical time NOT DERIVED, and the measured six cannot turn it
either: B1814 had about 19% power, and one world carries about one nat. The outside literature has met this exact
problem, as the measure problem of cosmology. It never derived the answer, but almost every independent principle it
found points to the critical end. The next attack is to decide the dial from the program's own structure, and the record
now has three concrete routes to try. The selection of our world's specific depth, the strip at Im τ ≈ 3, is a separate
and still open question: no combination of the clocks picks it.

## 3. The clock, fully informed

### 3.1 One dial, three marked points

Reader A's result (`A_the_programs_time.md` § 4, C2) is that B1813's parameter s is a single axis, measured by the

### 3.3 What the outside world says (reader B)

- **The cosmological measure problem has the same shape.**
  - In eternal inflation, the choice of time cutoff (proper time, scale factor, causal patch, light cone) changes which
    universes are typical. Garriga et al.'s family of time variables is a one-parameter family of clocks, like B1813's s.
  - That field narrowed its measures by outside criteria (pathologies, invariance, global–local duality) and never
    derived one. CHOSEN is the honest label.
- **The cosmologists' strongest consistency test** (Bousso–Yang) is that counting and following must agree.
  - Counting by length gives Liouville measure, and one typical history followed also gives Liouville: only the critical
    clock passes.
  - The move clock is singular, carried by a Lebesgue-null set of histories.
- **Independent principles mostly pick the critical clock:**
  - the physical (SRB) measure;
  - absolute continuity (Malament–Zabell);
  - stochastic stability;
  - arithmetic counting (Duke; Hecke points);
  - horocycle invariance (Dani 1978).

  Only maximal entropy per letter picks the move clock, and only once the letter is taken as the unit of time.
- **Counting flux vacua already gives the critical clock.** In Ashok–Douglas's one-modulus model, counting by tadpole
  gives hyperbolic area. Realistic finite-tadpole models (Ishiguro–Kobayashi–Otsuka) instead pile up at τ = ω and avoid
  the cusp: finite cutoffs matter.
- **The closest physics precedent is the Mixmaster (BKL) singularity.** There a history is a continued fraction. Counting
  per epoch gives a non-normalisable measure; counting per era gives the Gauss measure. The coordinate dependence was
  settled with clock-free invariants (Cornish–Levin), which is our d.
- **The sharpest form of the question:** is the weave's initial law absolutely continuous? Any smooth start flows to the
  critical clock. Only a law singular from the start (fair coins over letters) stays thick.
