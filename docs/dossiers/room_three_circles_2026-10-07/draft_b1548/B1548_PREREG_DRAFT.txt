## 2. Definitions and conventions

As sm:B1547 §2 (N₄₅, the frame, r, s, t, k, b0, the keys, the strata, the routes), with:
- **The circles.** A circle is a homomorphism φ: π₁(N₄₅) → ℤ, trivial on every cusp and primitive. Its points are the
  characters exp(2πi·j·φ/m), j a unit mod m, of exact order m.
  - Its values on the 46 common loops, W ∈ ℤ⁴⁶, name it in both routes.
  - The population's circle is the one whose order-2 point is χ0. Its values W_CIRCLE are sealed in `run.py`, found by route
    R's census (orders 2 and 3, the five circles' directions with entries in {−1, 0, 1}).
  - Each route solves for φ in its own lattice basis from W_CIRCLE. It checks that φ is primitive, that its order-2 point is
    the route's own χ0, and that its order-3 points are room-three in the route's own census.
- **The members**: the circle's points of exact order m for m in ORDERS = (3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 20, 24, 30, 40, 60), taken in
  the order (m, key). There are Σ φ(m) = 84. Both routes' primes are 1 mod 120, so every order here has its roots of unity
  there.
- **The Galois classes**: the orbits of ν ↦ ν^k over the automorphisms that fix √3 (Lemma Γ): every unit k mod m when 12 does
  not divide m, and k ≡ ±1 mod 12 when it does. So one class per order, and two at 12, 24 and 60.

## 3. What is proved or found at design time

**3.1 The circles (structure; control K9, and the dossier).** At orders 2, 3, 4 and 5, the cusp-trivial characters of N₄₅ with
n ≥ 3 are exactly the points of five circles, each with n = 3. In route R's lattice basis their directions are (0, −1, −1, −1),
(−1, 0, 1, 0), (0, 0, −1, 0), (0, −1, 1, 0) and (−1, 0, 0, −1), up to sign. τ permutes them (K3).

**3.2 The members satisfy sm:B1547's hypotheses.** Let ν be a point of the circle of order m, 4 ∤ m.
- ν is trivial on every cusp (φ is), so all five cusps are trivial.
- ν⁴ = exp(2πi·4j·φ/m) is a point of the same circle of order m/gcd(m, 4) ≥ 2. So ν⁴ ≠ 1, b0 = 0, L = ν⁻⁴, and n(L) = 3
  (checked at every member, P4: n(ν⁴) = 3 and n(L) = 3).
- So sm:B1547's Corollary 1 (Theorem C (ii) with sm:B1545's Lemma F′) and Lemma G hold verbatim. Three at ν lies in a
  distinct stratum X_U, |U| ≤ 2, with support U, and the generic class of each has the least counts on the classes of
  support U.

**3.3 Lemma Γ at these orders.** The four is defined over ℚ(√3) (sm:B1547 §3.4, control K4). An automorphism of
ℚ(ζ_{lcm(m, 24)}) that fixes √3 sends ν to ν^k exactly for the units k above. Galois-conjugate members over such k read alike
in characteristic 0. Both routes read every member, so the verdict does not rest on Γ. P3 tests it.

**3.4 The other order, the deck symmetry.** As sm:B1547 §3.4.
- The other order at ν is W₁ at ν̄ = ν^{m−1}, a member of the same order on the same circle.
- τ carries χ0's circle to the other four with the same counts (K3 at four sample members).

**3.5 What a NEGATIVE covers.** No class at any point of order 3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 20, 24, 30, 40 or 60 of any of the
five room-three circles carries three, in either order. Points of other orders are not read. P9 checks Theorem C's cap and
Lemma F′ at a generic class of K0^ν and of H¹ at every member.
