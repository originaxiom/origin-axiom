## Why this route

- **The owner chose it** (2026-10-08, "Six-dim object"): search for a six-dimensional object the weave forces, where the
  standard heterotic dictionary applies (net generations = an index) and the SU(5) shape can be read.
- **Three findings point here.**
  - Main's B1604 (T-NO-INDEX-IN-THREE): no count of flat-module cohomology on a thread or a cover is an index. A count
    needs an even-dimensional object the weave forces, carrying a non-flat bundle.
  - W22: the fibre, of dimension two, is such an object. Its index is ±3, but under the weave's group, not under a gauge
    group (W21's qualification).
  - W23: the record's E₈ frames on the fibre give at most two complete generations.
- **The web seat's A1** (verified in main's B1478): a bundle is told from its conjugate on a smooth closed bulk only in
  dimension 2 mod 4. After two, the next such dimension is six.

## The criteria (a candidate passes only if it meets all five)

- **S1, forced.** It is built from the joint action of every move on the shared fibre. No free parameter, and no thread or
  cover chosen by hand.
- **S2, the right kind.** Real dimension six, with a complex structure and a trivial canonical class (or an SU(3)
  structure). Without that, the heterotic dictionary does not apply.
- **S3, a count exists.** It is compact, or its ends carry conditions that the weave fixes, as W22 did on the fibre.
- **S4, a bundle with a hand.** It carries a non-flat holomorphic bundle that the weave forces. The structure group must
  have complex representations, so that the commutant in E₈ can be chiral.
- **S5, the count.** Net generations = the index. N(10) and N(5̄) (or the 16s, or the 27s) are read off.

## The candidates

- **K1, the fibre's character variety.** 𝒳 = ℂ³, with coordinates (x, y, z) = (tr a, tr b, tr ab). Every move acts on
  it. Its level sets κ = tr [a, b] = x² + y² + z² − xyz − 2 are cubic surfaces. The common point is (0, 0, 0), at κ = −2.
- **K2, the weave's local model at the common point.**
  - The linear parts of the moves act on T₀𝒳 = ℂ³.
  - The finite linear symmetries of 𝒳: the parities (sign changes) and the moves of finite order.
  - The compact orbifolds E³/G that these groups define.
- **K3, main's frame spaces** (L250). Γ_s\SL(2, ℂ) for every thread s.
- **K4, the universal families over the weave's moduli.**
  - The Kuga–Sato threefold E ×_M E. It was set aside on 2026-10-08 for a free twist of the base.
  - The isomonodromic total space (ℍ × X_{−2}) / SL(2, ℤ): the Markov surface X_{−2} carried over the modular curve by
    the moves.

### A, the object (K1)

- **A1.** Every move acts on 𝒳 by a polynomial map that preserves κ. L and R have Jacobian determinant +1, so they
  preserve Ω = dx ∧ dy ∧ dz; the swap P has −1. So the weave acts on a complex threefold with a holomorphic volume form.
  **Prior 99%.**
- **A2.** The points every move fixes are exactly (0, 0, 0) (the common point, κ = −2) and (2, 2, 2) (the trivial
  character, κ = 2). **Prior 95%.**
- **A3.** The linear parts at the common point generate the cube's rotations (order 24). Their character at (S, L, SL)
  is (−1, 1, 0), which is W21's P (3′).
  - So the flavor triplet of W21 is the tangent space of the six-dimensional object at the common point:
    T₀𝒳 = H¹(F₂; ad ρ_Q) = ⊕_p H¹(F₂; χ_p).
  - **Prior 90%.**
- **A4.** κ has five critical points in ℂ³, all of Morse type: (0, 0, 0) at κ = −2, and the four points with
  entries ±2 and xyz = 8, at κ = 2.
  - The Euler characteristic of the level sets is read two ways:
    - from the compactification: 9 for a smooth projective cubic, minus 3 for the triangle at infinity;
    - from the critical points: one less per node.
  - Prediction: χ = 6 generically, 5 at κ = −2 and 2 at κ = 2. The total is χ(ℂ³) = 6 − 1 − 4 = 1.
  - **Prior 90%.**
- **A's verdict on S3 and S4.** 𝒳 is contractible and non-compact. The moves form an infinite group with a common
  fixed point, so they do not act properly. T𝒳 is trivial. Prediction: K1 fails S3 and S4. **Prior 95%.**

### B, the local model (K2)

- **B1.** Each parity fixes one line of 𝒳, its own axis. The three axes meet only at the common point. On the Markov
  surface X_{−2}, each parity fixes only the common point. **Prior 99%.**
- **B2.** The finite linear symmetries of 𝒳 that preserve κ form T_d ≅ S₄, of order 24. They are the even sign changes
  (the parities) and the permutations.
  - The part with determinant 1 is A₄ (order 12). It is generated by the parities and the move of order three
    (a ↦ b, b ↦ (ab)⁻¹), which acts as a 3-cycle.
  - The swap P supplies the odd permutations.
  - **Prior 90%.**
- **B3.** The node of X_{−2} at the common point is A₁, with tangent cone x² + y² + z² = 0 = ℂ²/{±1}. Lifted to SU(2)
  through that cone, the groups give:
  - the parities → Q₈ (order 8): the D₄ singularity;
  - A₄ → 2T (order 24): E₆;
  - the moves' linear parts, the cube's rotations → 2O (order 48): E₇. These act only linearly.
  - **Prior 99%** (McKay; the check is of orders).
- **B4.** The compact orbifolds E³/G, for any elliptic curve E, with G = the parities V₄, A₄, and the rotations O. The
  orbifold Euler numbers, by the string formula (1/|G|) Σ_{gh = hg} χ(Fix g ∩ Fix h):
  - V₄: 96 (the textbook value);
  - A₄: 32;
  - O: 28.
  - With discrete torsion, −96, −32 and −28.
  - Net generations under the standard embedding: 48, 16, 14.
  - **Prior 90%** for the values; **97%** that none is three. The curve E is free (fails S1).

### C, the frame spaces (K3): stated, not computed

### D, the gauge side: what a six-dimensional object would have to supply

- **D0, an audit of W22, found while writing this rule.**
  - W22 read the end condition block by block. By D1 the three parity blocks are the same doublet. An end condition
    may therefore mix them: it is any subspace Λ₊ of the six-dimensional space of local solutions.
  - The moves' lifts act on that space as A_φ: block u∘φ → block u, by the lift g ∈ 2O. W22 used the same g.
  - **The prediction.** Under these lifts the six dimensions split as 2 ⊕ 4 (the spinor and the four of 2O), so the
    commutant has dimension 2. The conditions every move keeps are then 0, the 2, the 4 and all six, with index −3,
    −1, +1 and +3. Index 0 never occurs: chirality is forced. The magnitude three is forced only when the condition
    keeps the parity blocks apart, as W22 assumed. **Prior 80%.**
  - **The control.** Adding the parity grading (the block-diagonal signs χ_u(x)) to the lifts leaves commutant 1.
    Only the block-diagonal conditions survive, which is W22's ±3. **Prior 95%.**
  - If the prediction holds, W22's sentence "index −3 or +3, never 0" is qualified in the record. It becomes: never 0;
    three when the parities stay separate sectors at the puncture.
- **D1.** For each parity p, χ_p ⊗ ρ_Q ≅ ρ_Q. The intertwiner is conjugation by the quaternion unit that matches p. So
  𝕎 ≅ ρ_Q ⊗ ℂ³: three copies of the spin doublet, with the parities labelling the copies. **Prior 99%.**
- **D2.** 𝕎 has rank 6 and trivial determinant, so it fits SU(6)′ ⊂ E₈. The commutant of SU(6)′ is SU(3) × SU(2),
  the Standard Model's non-abelian group.
  - 𝕎's holonomy is only Q₈, so its centralizer in E₈ is larger. The dimension is the average of the 248's character
    over Q₈, computed through the branching
    248 = (8,1,1) + (1,3,1) + (1,1,35) + (3,1,15) + (3̄,1,15̄) + (3,2,6) + (3̄,2,6̄) + (1,2,20).
  - Prediction: 55, that is F₄ × SU(2). **Prior 85%.**
- **D3.** The multiplicities in the 248: the spin doublet ρ appears 56 times, each parity character 27 times.
  - Under F₄ × SU(2), these are (26, 2) + 2(1, 2) and (26, 1) + (1, 1).
  - The check: 55 + 2·56 + 3·27 = 248.
  - **Prior 85%.**
- **D4, the theorem the cells feed** (proved in the note, not by the code).
  - ρ is quaternionic and the 248 is real, so the multiplicity space U_ρ is pseudoreal under the centralizer.
  - A chiral reading needs W22's end condition on the gauge factor. That is a Lagrangian half U′ ⊂ U_ρ (the modes of one
    hand), because the reality of the 248 sends the condition to its conjugate.
  - The (26, 2) is irreducible, so F₄ × SU(2) preserves no Lagrangian half of it. Every chiral gauge reading of the
    weave's bundle therefore breaks F₄ by a choice.
  - That choice is the dictionary that the stop hook names (FK11). What a six-dimensional object must supply is a forced
    complex structure on the gauge side, meaning a bundle whose holonomy has complex representations (S4).
