## 2. The symmetric-phase pattern, and why it is close to a theorem

- **The pattern (COMPUTED across arcs; first named in the reading A1–W61, point 2).** Every dynamics the record has
  computed lands on a symmetric point:
  - τ at ω or the cusp (W51, W59, W62, W67; B1686);
  - the line at a half-period (W60);
  - the Higgs's direction on the cone or the real locus (B1646), on O's special lines (W64);
  - every vacuum W67 selects has the second generation massless or degenerate.
  - The reading's words: "the dynamics picks the most symmetric state, and the most symmetric state has no flavour."
- **Why (the literature; a READING).** Michel's critical-orbit theorem (L. Michel, 1971; stated in G. Gaeta,
  arXiv:math-ph/0207002): for a compact group acting smoothly, an orbit is critical for every smooth invariant function
  exactly when it is isolated in its stratum. For a linear action it applies on the unit sphere, since a line's whole
  ray shares one stratum. O's 6- and 8-orbit lines on M's sphere are orbits of that kind, and the real points
  (1, 0, 0) and (1, 1, 1) too. The modular group is discrete, not compact, but the same conclusion holds at i and ω
  through their finite stabilizers: ∂_τV has weight (2, 0), so it vanishes there (section 9a). The cusp is a boundary
  point, where the growing functionals run, not a critical orbit. Every invariant potential is stationary at those
  orbits, whatever its coefficients, and with few terms the minima fall there. B990 ("an orbit invariant is constant on
  the orbit, so it cannot pick a point") is the record's half of the same fact.
- **What it implies.** A minimum away from those orbits sits in a stratum of positive dimension, and its position moves
  with the coefficients. So a vacuum away from the symmetric points costs coefficient ratios, unless the coefficients
  are themselves forced, for instance by a loop over a forced content. GENESIS FK9's v1.46 scoping says the same: B990
  concerns the orbit of one point, and B1655's minima are an exception, with chosen coefficients (P-139).
- **Pre-mortem.** Michel's theorem says critical, not minimal: which critical orbit is the minimum still depends on the
  signs of the coefficients (W64 K7's c). "Isolated in its stratum" has to be checked for each orbit named; W149 checks
  it for M.

### 6a. The cone is the level-4 modular curve (classical; read against the record by hand)

- **The fact.** Γ(4) has index 24 in PSL(2, ℤ), genus 0, and no elliptic points. X(4) → X(1) is branched over i, ω and
  the cusp with orders 2, 3 and 4, and Γ/Γ(4) ≅ S4 acts on X(4) ≅ ℂP¹ as the octahedral group: 12 points over i,
  8 over ω, 6 cusps. Riemann–Hurwitz checks it: −2 = 24 · (−2) + 12 + 16 + 18.
- **The same group acts on M's isotropic conic,** the projective lines with h·h = 0, also as the octahedral group. Its
  orbits with stabilizers ℤ₂, ℤ₃ and ℤ₄ are W64's 12-, 8- and 6-orbit lines. So i, ω and the cusp sit over the 12-,
  8- and 6-orbit lines, as the record found case by case:
  - the forms tend to h₀ at the cusp (W64 K3, W146);
  - every Higgs section lies at ω on one isotropic line (W65);
  - the 26 special lines (W64).
- **The two actions are intertwined by a single Möbius map,** since the octahedral group has no non-trivial centralizer
  in PGL(2, ℂ). An antiholomorphic one also exists: compose with complex conjugation h ↦ h̄, which commutes with O's
  real rotations and exchanges h₀ and h̄₀, as CP does (W64 K1). The choice between the two is the hand again.
- **R's line.** R·R = 0 identically by Jacobi's identity, so R(τ) is an exactly isotropic line for every τ: an
  equivariant map from X(4) to the conic. W64 K3's exponents on (e_i, v₊, v₋) are 1/8, −1/8 and 3/8. Relative to v₊
  the e_i part goes like q^{1/4} = q₄, X(4)'s coordinate at the cusp, and the v₋ part like q₄², as the cone requires.
  So R's map has local degree 1 at the cusp, as the intertwiner does. Whether its global degree is 1 is for W149.
- **The target on the cone (by hand, from B1743's table).** On the conic through h₀, a line is h₀ + s e₁ − (s²/2) h̄₀,
  with h₀·h̄₀ = e₁·e₁ = 1, so h·h = 2(ε + δ²/2) for h = h₀ + δ e₁ + ε h̄₀. On B1743's best row (0.4375 + 2.1i),
  δ = 0.074 + 0.308i and −δ²/2 = 0.045 − 0.023i, against ε = 0.048 − 0.032i. The target is the cone's point s = δ,
  moved off the cone by ε + δ²/2 ≈ 0.003 − 0.009i. That reproduces the row's I, 0.0177. S204's constant gives that
  row's δ as 0.075 + 0.309i.
- **R on the same chart** (checked numerically in scratch, not an arc, and locked in the dossier's test): R·R vanishes
  to 10⁻⁴¹, |s_R| = 2√2 |q₄|, and R's h̄₀ part is |s_R|²/2. The last two hold to 7 × 10⁻⁶ and 1.4 × 10⁻⁵ at Im τ 2,

## 9. The literature (the fourth sweep)

Every result below is about one modulus and one or two triplets, taken one at a time: thread-type input, never a
weave result. The sweep read about 35 papers in full. This seat checked each claim used here against the paper's own
text, and corrected one (9a).

### 9a. Holding τ

- **i and ω are stationary for every modular-invariant potential.** ∂_τV has weight (2, 0), so it vanishes at the
  fixed points (Cvetič, Font, Ibáñez, Lüst and Quevedo 1991; Leedom, Righi and Westphal, arXiv:2212.03876, eq. 2.27).
  This is Michel's theorem in τ (section 2). Symmetry does not say whether the point is a minimum.
- **A minimum near, not at, a fixed point needs the fixed point to be a maximum, or a term that is not stationary
  there.** Novichkov, Penedo and Petcov (arXiv:2201.02020, eq. 3.2) give a heterotic-type potential where ω is a local
  maximum and the minimum sits at |u| ≈ 0.0145/(m + 0.0625), u = (τ − ω)/(τ − ω²). The departure is set by an
  integer m.
- **A finite Im τ from one loop.** Higaki, Kawamura and Kobayashi (arXiv:2402.02071, eq. 3.7) hold Im τ with a
  Coleman–Weinberg potential from heavy vector-like states whose mass is a modular form of weight k and T-charge t.
  With p = 2πt/N, μ = M̃_Q and p = 2π/3, the minimum is at 2 Im τ = 3.9, 7.9, 12 and 16 for k = 6, 8, 10 and 12.
  - **The correction:** the sweep said "Im τ ~ 3 is reachable". The paper shows it for k = 8, and Im τ ≈ 2 for k = 6.
    The location is set by the integers k and t, with a weak dependence on μ/M̃_Q.
  - **Against the record:** W59 and W62 found every computed content running to a cusp, but those contents are the
    fibre's own spectrum. Heavy states whose masses are T-charged forms of the frame have not been tried. The 27's
    exotics (W139, W140) are the frame's candidates.
  - **Pre-mortem.** Whether the frame's exotics have such masses, and with which weights, charges and signs, is not
    known.

### 9b. Hierarchies near the fixed points

- **The power-counting rule** (Novichkov, Penedo and Petcov, arXiv:2102.07488). Near a fixed point the residual
  charges fix the power of the small parameter in each entry. At level 4 their Table 2 lists (1, ε, ε²) near ω and
  (1, ε, ε³) near i∞, and every viable case there has one side reducible. Their Higgs fields are trivial singlets.
- **The record's three are alike** (W49), one irreducible triplet on each side, and its Higgs is a triplet in M. So the
  record sits outside that table. The hierarchy it needs has to come from the Higgs's direction, which is where every
  fit puts it.
- **ε at level 4 is e^{−π Im τ/2}:** 0.043, 0.027 and 0.017 at Im τ 2.0, 2.3 and 2.6. The target's I (0.015 to
  0.019) is not ε. Its slope is +0.24 per unit Im τ (W146), and ε's is −π/2. This is stated here so that nobody
  matches the two by eye.
- **Pre-mortem.** Table 2 lists the patterns viable under the paper's filters. It is not a no-go for 3 ⊗ 3.

### 9c. One triplet's alignment

- **For a triplet of S4 with the phase symmetry, the global minima are exactly (1, 0, 0), (1, 1, 1), (±1, ω, ω²) and
  (0, 1, i), with their images** (Ivanov and Nishi, arXiv:1410.6139, eq. 22; Degee, Ivanov and Keus,
  arXiv:1211.4989). (0, 1, i) is the minimum when the coefficient of Σ(Im φ_a†φ_b)² is negative and dominant, and
  (1, ω, ω²) when the competing condition holds.
- **So W64 K7 is a known result in the singlet case.** Its 6-orbit lines are the (0, 1, i)-type and its 8-orbit lines
  the (1, ω, ω²)-type. Both are isotropic and rigid: the minimum does not move under moderate changes of the
  coefficients. Under A4 alone the fourth point becomes (0, 1, e^{iα}), with α set by a coefficient ("flexible").
- **What it does to H-ALIGN.** The record's family group is O ≅ S4 (W136). With one triplet and a quartic potential,
  the minimum is h₀ exactly, with I = 0, and it does not move. A departure needs one of four things:
  - a second field;
  - a term that breaks O;
  - a higher-order term (B1655's octic moved the isotropy defect to 0.447);
  - τ itself, which falls exponentially.
  S212's free second doublet is the first of these.
- **Pre-mortem.** The classification is for quartic potentials of a triplet of doublets. A gauge-singlet triplet
  without the phase symmetry has more invariants, and sextic terms can add minima in generic strata. The basis also
  needs checking: W64's O acts by signed permutations of the units, which is the papers' basis, and h₀ = (0, 1, i)/√2
  in W146's.

### 9d. CP

- **One S4 or A4 triplet allows neither explicit nor spontaneous CP violation** (Ivanov and Nishi, citing their
  ref. 13). With Higgs triplets and exact A4 or S4 Yukawas, no alignment gives non-zero quark masses together with a CKM
  phase (González Felipe, Serôdio and Silva, arXiv:1302.0861).
