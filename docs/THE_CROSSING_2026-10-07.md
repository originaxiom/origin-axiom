## 2. What blocks the two nearest approaches — one object

- **The chirality count.** On a smooth closed bulk a bundle is told from its conjugate only in dimension ≡ 2 (mod 4)
  (GENESIS v1.12, the web seat's A1). The record's class index contains no spinors and does not follow the bit (B1480).
- **The weak mixing angle.** If the couplings meet, (1/α₂ − 1/α₃)/(1/α₁ − 1/α₂) = (b₂ − b₃)/(b₁ − b₂): measured 0.717; the
  desert of B915, 0.528; Higgs doublets light and colour triplets heavy, 0.714; matter kept by invariance, 0.500. And
  in the 27 of E₆ the triplet's weights are −2 times the quark doublet's, so no flat abelian line removes the triplet
  on any state (B1484).
- **Both need matter counted by an index rather than kept by invariance** — a frame of dimension ≡ 2 (mod 4) with a
  bundle that is not flat. Lead **L250**; the candidate is the six-dimensional frame space X_s = Γ_s\SL(2,ℂ).

**What a reader reported about that candidate (2026-10-07; a pointer — main has not opened the sources, and nothing
here rests on them).** On a compact quotient: every holomorphic bundle has c₁ = 0, and every homogeneous or flat bundle
has all Chern classes zero (Winkelmann's memoir on complex parallelizable manifolds, Thms 8.13.1, 7.14.4; Cor 6.6.3) —
so the record's flat bundles can never count there either. The holomorphic tangent bundle is trivial, hence
χ(X, E) = c₃(E)/2 when c₁ = c₂ = 0: a bundle counts exactly when its Euler characteristic is not zero. The only
non-flat bundles known are rank-two bundles built from the elliptic curves that closed geodesics trace in X; all their
Chern classes vanish. **No holomorphic bundle with c₃ ≠ 0 is known, and no theorem forbids one outside the homogeneous
class.** Every known solution of the heterotic equations on these spaces uses a flat or trivial gauge bundle (no net
generations). For a cusped manifold X is not compact and a count needs boundary data — which is where B1483 finds the
spin structure (the curve E_s).
